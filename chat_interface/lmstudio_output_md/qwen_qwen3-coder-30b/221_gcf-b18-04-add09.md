## PROJECT / PROGRAMME SUMMARY

### A.2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

In the past 50 years the Buenos Aires Province (PBA, for its acronym in Spanish) has suffered rainfalls of great intensity adversively impacting the Lujan River Basin where 11.4% of households fall within at least one of the unsatisfied basic needs indicators (a proportion larger than that of the Buenos Aires Province, which is 8.2%). During the last years the extent of flooding has increased resulting in waterlogging of soils in urban and rural areas as well as roads and trails.

The general purpose of this Project is promote measures aimed at reducing and/or preventing river flooding, as well as controlling stream-flows and mitigating the impact of floods in the Lujan River Basin, thus causing more resilience vis-à- vis climate change in the basin.

Floods in the Lujan River Basin are mainly caused by: (i) meteorological and climate factors resulting in increases in rainfall frequency and intensity; (ii) physical characteristics of the basin such as: slope, contour, water-storage of the soil and water conveyance of the river; and (iii) anthropogenic factors such as changes in land-use, growing urbanization and the features of road and water infrastructure.

To address the issue, the Buenos Aires Province has structured the Lujan River Comprehensive Plan (LRCP) with an estimated cost of USD $315,3 MM to solve the aforementioned problems. The plan envisages the development of an integral project to implement structural measures together with non -structural measures such as implementation of an early warning system (EWS), actions aiming at implementing land-use management and the strengthening of institutions.

The scope of the project thus defined fully complies with the priorities set out by Argentina under the Intended National Deterred Contributions (INDC) regarding climate change. Those priorities include implementation of early warning systems and application of structural and non -structural measures to face extreme events. Structural measures are the following: feasibility studies and civil works to increase the conveyance capacity of the river, construction of retention basins and substitution of inflatable dams for lock gates.

Funding of the project has been structured as follows: 29.13% PBA; 52.3% CAF and 18.56% GCF. This funding structure enables PBA to have the required cash flow and ensures that the project may be fully and successfully implemented within the timeframe agreed upon, ensuring the early implementation of structural and non -structural adaptation measures.

### C.1. Strategic Context

The Republic of Argentina is made up of 23 Provinces and the autonomous city of Buenos Aires. Argentina has a population of nearly 40 million inhabitants, 90% of which is located in urban areas. The Metropolitan Area of Buenos Aires, which includes the city of Buenos Aires and 26 municipal jurisdictions, accounts for 0.5% of the national territory and concentrates 31.92% of the population.

Between 2004 and 2008, Argentina was a leader in poverty reduction, but since 2008 this trend has slowed down. In 2014, 12.7% of the population was considered poor (living with less than USD $4 a day). One third of the population of Argentina lives with an income between USD $4 and USD $10 a day, and are at risk of falling into poverty.

In the last decade the main concern of the Federal Government was economic recovery and economic growth. However, this policy brought about some important distortions in the economy. The new administration, inaugurated in December 2015, is introducing adjustments to eliminate economic distortions responsible for hindering economic growth and for increasing the inflation rate. Argentina is now living in a context of changes expected to bring about results in the mid term with the reduction of fiscal deficit and increases of debt capacity later on.

### The Republic of Argentina and the Climate Change:

In 1993 Argentina ratified the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) and, in 2001 the Kyoto Protocol. In 2002, a presidential decree replaced the Secretariat of Environment and Natural Resources with the Secretariat of Environment and Sustainable Development under authority of the Head of the Cabinet of Ministers. By December 2015 it became the Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development vested with significant institutional duties. Its core purpose is to provide assistance to the President and the relevant Ministries, as well as «coordinating all and any public policies having impact on the environmental sector, by implementing a strategic planning of environmental policies and programmes adopted by the national government».

The new Minister relies on the Secretary for Environmental Policy, Climate Change and Sustainable Development that hosts the Directorate for Climate Change and is responsible for the adoption and coordination of policies and actions relating to climate change. Additionally, there is a government committee for climate change with the involvement of both Federal and Provincial governments.

In the Third National Communication to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (November 2015), Argentina highlighted the country's vulnerability vis-à-vis climate change, analyzed greenhouse gas emissions (GHG) and put forward a set of planned mitigation measures and the need for adaptation measures to address problems created by frequent heavy rains, such as floods, with subsequent harm to the population and the agricultural production.

Furthermore, prior to the Paris negotiations in 2015, Argentina submitted to the UNFCCC, its Intended Nationally Determined Contributions (INDC), where it made clear that adaptation to climate change is a major concern, whereby it has implemented adaptation measures. Nevertheless, to further such adaptation actions, international financing, technology transfer and institutional strengthening are required. Two out of the main seven adaptation measures considered in the INDC include early warning systems and implementation of structural and non-structural measures to face extreme events.

Additionally, the Inter-jurisdictional Cabinet on Climate Change was established on July 25 2016 for the purposes of articulating policies on climate change and increasing the society's awareness of their relevance and significance. Among these core duties is aligning the national and provincial governments with regard to actions on mitigation and adaptation to climate change; propose a National Plan on Response to Climate Change, as an input to discuss a core comprehensive national strategy and contribute to capacity-building in the field of prevention and handling of emergency and disaster situations arising from extreme climate events, among others.

## DETAILED PROJECT / PROGRAMME DESCRIPTION

In 1994 Argentina introduced in its Constitution the obligation to protect the environment granting to its population the fundamental right of a healthy environment. In addition, the Constitution gave the Provinces the right to regulate and introduced the notion of "Minimal Budgets for Protection" to ensure a minimal threshold of environmental quality across the national territory.

The two laws currently enacted on "Minimal Budgets" for the Lujan River Project are the following:

- Law 25.675, General Law on Environment (Law 25.675). This Law sets out the notion of land-use as a tool for environmental management. It also establishes guidelines for the environmental policy and the Congruence Principle to align the domestic legal framework with the goals and targets of sustainable development.
- Law on Minimal Budgets for the Integrated Water Resources Management (Law 25.688). This Law is the basis for creating agencies devoted to river basin management.

At the national level, the Federal Agreement on Water Resources and the Guiding Principles for the Integrated Water Resources Management (Law 26.438) seek integrated management of water resources through the notion of territorial areas in river basins. The Province of Buenos Aires has already ratified this Law.

## Provincial Framework

At the Provincial level there is a large number of regulations for the integrated river basin management:

- Law 11.723 (amended by Law 13.516) is the General Law on Environment, which is the environmental regulatory framework for the Province of Buenos Aires. This Law sets out guiding principles governing the Province's environmental policy. The purpose of this Law is "the protection, preservation, improvement and restoration of natural resources and environment in the Province of Buenos Aires, to preserve life in the broadest sense, ensuring present and future generations the preservation of biological diversity and the quality of the environment".
- Law 5.965, which sets out the framework for water resources protection. This Law is the basis to fight against water pollution in the Province and to grant waste water discharge permits into receiving waters.
- Law 12.257 (Water Code). The Water Code of the Province of Buenos Aires, with its amendments, is the most important piece of legislation for water regulation and water management for river basin units in the Provincial territory.
- Law 14.710, which creates the Committee of the Lujan River Basin Committee (COMILU). This is a major body for the implementation of the Lujan River Project.

Additionally, with regard to planning and management of water resources, the government of the Province of Buenos Aires approved in 2007 the Provincial Water Management Plan contemplating differentiated actions for the metropolitan area, the rural area and the coastal area. The plan calls for investments of USD $1.000 millions which benefit 8 million of people in Lujan, Salado, Reconquista, Matanza-Riachuelo, Areco and Rio V river basins and the region adjacent to the capital (La Plata, Berisso and Ensenada).

## C.2. Project / Programme Objective against Baseline

In the last century the Southeast region of South America experienced changes in the rainfall pattern affecting the water balance of the region, particularly in La Plata River Basin.

Floods in the Lujan River Basin have both direct and indirect impact over near 8 million people of the Province of Buenos Aires. In a timeframe of two years around 10.017 precarious dwelling became recurrently flooded and it is estimated that in a timeframe of 100 years this figure would rise to 15.085. The floods affect mainly single-family dwelling (87% of floods).

The Lujan River Basin is large and narrow. Water from heavy rains reaches the main river course very quickly, thus raising abruptly the river's water level and bursting the banks of the river. Floods in the river basin occur rather fast, on a large-scale and unexpectedly while people become defenceless in face of such natural phenomena.

Over time this problem has become more acute due to the following reasons:  

- Climatic Factors.
- Physical Factors.
- Anthropogenic Factors.

### Climatic Factors

Rainfall and temperature are the main variables influencing the region's climatic characteristics. In wet years, the increase in rainfall results in a disproportionate increase in the streamflow because water infiltration (absorption capacity of soil and subsoil) is low and water evaporation decreases. On the other hand, in dry years, the reduction in rainfall results in a proportionally larger reduction of streamflow.

In short, rainfall variations in the zone result in a magnified river basin response. The lack of water brings about restrictions in water provision for both human consumption and agriculture, whereas the excess of water results in floods.

Streamflow is also affected by the spatial distribution of rainfall. The larger the rainfall intensity and frequency over a zone of the river basin with low interception capacity and/or over a zone with good drainage capacity, the larger the magnitude of river flooding.

### Physical Factors

During 1989-1994 conventional farming in the river basin was based on terrace farming. Thereafter, the region changed the modality of land preparation for direct seeding (cereals, oilseeds). This type of land preparation for cultivation modifies the runoff of the surface whereby a larger part of the rainfall infiltrates, resulting in a larger subsurface runoff.

Intensive use of land for agricultural purposes gives rise to soils with sparse vegetation and low level of interception resulting in an acceleration of the runoff and a reduction of temporary retentions. As a result, the volume of water in the river becomes larger in shorter spans of time.

On the other hand, channelization in the river basin should rely on technical justification and the subsequent approval from provincial or municipal authorities. Clandestine channelization generally lacks of technical planning, and adversely affects the drainage network of creeks, channels and rivers.

### Anthropogenic Factors

Urban growth brings about a decrease in interception capacity and an increase of soil impermeabilisation, which increases the volume of water and the speed at which such water flows into river courses. In addition, the increases in residential density calls for more infrastructure works, more roads, more public utilities, etc., in other words, changes in land-use increase the levels of interception of runoff and, also, increase vulnerability in the region.

### Components of the Project

The Project has 8 components. Components 1, 2, 3 and 4 seek to address problems derived from physical factors; component 5 seeks to address problems derived from anthropogenic factors adversely affecting the river basin and component 6 seeks to mitigate the issues relating to abrupt occurrence of floods.

Performance of executive projects and preparation of tender documents for civil works under the Project needed in order to finalize engineering designs and to prepare the tendering process for the engineering works considered in Components 2, 3 and 4.

#### Component 2: Water conveyance works and enlargement of the river channel

These will improve the conveyance capacity of the Lujan River through construction of supplemental channels, enlargement of channels and crossing works:

- Enlargement of Lujan River channel with trapezoidal sections over a distance of 9.5 Km, from RN 8 until the railway bridge of Belgrano North line.

Construction of areas for temporary retention of surplus water (ARTEH) - This component is complementary to Components 2 and 4. The retention basins, built on the higher part of the river, are meant to remain empty, when the river floods they retain water thereby allowing lower parts of the river to evacuate its excess water.

- Areas for temporary retention of surplus water (ARTEH) in the following creeks: Los Leones, Mayan, Leguizamón, Grande, del Oro and other two over the Lujan River.
- Substitution of inflatable dams for lock gates in Lujan and Mercedes.

The lock gates replacement will allow adequate water conveyance thereby being complementary works of Component 2.

#### Component 4: Works for bridges replacement and enlargement

Works for replacement and enlargement of bridges to improve current runoff conditions, removing river obstructions and detritus. These works will be in addition to the construction of channels. In 1993 Argentina ratified the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) and in 2015 the Kyoto Protocol. Thus far, Argentina has submitted three communications under the United Nations Framework Convention describing Argentina's vulnerability to climate change, providing the inventory of gas emissions and advancing Argentina's position regarding adaptation measures, giving high priority to the implementation of such measures.

The following are Argentina's priorities under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change:

- Introduce the expected climate change effects into parameters for designing infrastructure works, particularly those related to water management and the construction of embankments to avoid flooding.
- Update laws and land-use plans taking into account expected climate changes, introducing statutes on land-use based on flood risk maps under current and forecasted climate conditions.
- Aim at having a land-use plan that takes into account climate changes to avoid or mitigate undesired consequences over human settlements. This is relevant for developments along maritime coastlines and riverbanks.

Furthermore, prior to the Paris negotiations in 2015, Argentina, through the INDC, put forward its contributions on the matter of mitigation and adaptation measures making it clear that adaptation measures are an ongoing priority. Two out of the core seven adaptation measures to climate change are the following: a) intensifying and broadening early warning systems for heavy rains, floods and hot weather, and systems of response to and recovery from climate disasters; b) implementing structural and non-structural measures to address extreme events. Additionally Argentina's INDC calls for international financing to implement adaptation measures.

The Lujan River Project falls within Argentina's regulatory framework and PBA's field of competence. It is worth mentioning that Law 14.710, whereby the Committee of the Lujan River Basin is created, seeks to modify existing institutional arrangements enacted by the Water Authority more than a decade ago.

Law 14.710 vested COMILU with implementing powers for river basins management with legal authority to make and implement general interest decisions. Prior to the enactment of Law 14.710, Committees were of consultative nature to coordinate or arrange initiatives.

The purpose of Law 14.70 is the preservation of water resources and the sustainable management of the basin. To fulfill this objective COMILU has been vested with powers to formulate environmental policies of the river basin together with other competent organisms and powers for the integrated management of the river basin, including issuance of land-use plans and recommendations on related expropriations and resettlements.

Successful implementation of the Lujan River Project under Law 14.710 would constitute a regulatory framework replicable for projects on adaptation to climate change.

The following are expected climate changes in Argentina:

- **Temperature rise**: It is likely that in the period 2020-2040 temperature rises 1°C in Argentina. This temperature rise will be more significant in the North resulting in greater evaporation and, subsequently, in increases in both aridity and desertification.
