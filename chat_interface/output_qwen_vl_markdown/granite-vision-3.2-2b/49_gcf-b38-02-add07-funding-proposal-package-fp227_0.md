# Green Climate Fund FUNDING PROPOSAL V3.0 | PAGE 4

## Introduction

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) is a global initiative that aims to mobilize $100 billion annually by 2020 to support climate action in developing countries. This proposal outlines the GCF's funding proposal for the period 2021-2030, focusing on three key areas: climate change mitigation and adaptation, clean energy, and sustainable land use.

## Climate Change Mitigation and Adaptation

The GCF will provide $50 billion in funding for climate change mitigation and adaptation projects in developing countries. This includes support for renewable energy projects, such as wind and solar power, as well as projects that reduce greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture, industry, and transportation. The GCF will also provide funding for early warning systems and disaster risk reduction measures to help communities prepare for and respond to climate-related disasters.

## Clean Energy

The GCF will provide $30 billion in funding for clean energy projects in developing countries. This includes support for the development of renewable energy technologies, such as wind and solar power, as well as projects that reduce greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture, industry, and transportation. The GCF will also provide funding for energy efficiency measures to help communities reduce their energy consumption and lower their greenhouse gas emissions.

## Sustainable Land Use

The GCF will provide $20 billion in funding for sustainable land use projects in developing countries. This includes support for the development of agroforestry systems, which combine trees with crops and livestock to improve soil health and reduce greenhouse gas emissions. The GCF will also provide funding for reforestation and afforestation projects to help restore degraded lands and sequester carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.

## Conclusion

The Green Climate Fund is a critical tool for mobilizing the resources needed to address climate change in developing countries. This proposal outlines the GCF's funding proposal for the period 2021-2030, focusing on three key areas: climate change mitigation and adaptation, clean energy, and sustainable land use. By providing $100 billion annually to support these projects, the GCF can help developing countries reduce their greenhouse gas emissions, improve their resilience to climate-related disasters, and promote sustainable development.

### B.1. Climate context (max. 1000 words, approximately 2 pages)

Climate baseline: Madagascar's geographical location, maritime influence and wind regime result in a great deal of rainfall received between December and January, and a cooler, dry season (May to October), which is the least wet season. The southern coast receives more than 1000 mm per year.

Climate change: Climate change has been observed in Madagascar since the 1970s. Temperatures have increased from 22°C to 24.8°C from 1970 to 2005, and another increase of 3.6°C between 2005 and 2010. The highest temperatures were recorded in the west coast (19°C - 21°C) and the lowest in the east coast (14°C - 16°C).

Temperature trends: Average annual temperatures range from 22°C to 24.8°C between 1970-2020. The highest average annual temperature was recorded in 2015, and the lowest in 1970.
