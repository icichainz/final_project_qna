# IV. GENDER ISSUES IN RESPONSE TO IMMEDIATE CLIMATE-INDUCED DISASTERS AND CLIMATE RISKS

## Introduction

In Timor-Leste, rural populations are greatly exposed to a range of hazards, including flash floods and droughts. Socio-economic factors and intensification of these climate-induced hazards over time have led to increased vulnerability among women and men. This report examines the gender issues in response to immediate climate disasters and climate risks, focusing on the impact of climate change on women's access to resources, their ability to respond to disaster, and their vulnerability to climate-related hazards.

## Gender Roles and Responsibilities

Gender roles and responsibilities play a significant role in shaping women's vulnerability to climate risks. Women are often responsible for managing household resources, including food, water, and energy. In the context of climate change, this responsibility can be exacerbated by increased demands on these resources due to changes in precipitation patterns and extreme weather events.

## Gender Disparities in Climate Change Adaptation

Gender disparities are also evident in climate change adaptation efforts. Women are more likely to be affected by climate-related hazards, such as floods and droughts, due to their reliance on natural resources for livelihoods. Additionally, women's access to information about climate risks and adaptation strategies is often limited, making it difficult for them to make informed decisions about their safety and well-being.

## Gender Inequality in Climate Change Mitigation

Gender inequality also affects the effectiveness of climate change mitigation efforts. Women are more likely to be involved in agricultural activities that contribute to greenhouse gas emissions, such as deforestation and livestock farming. Additionally, women's access to education and training on climate change mitigation strategies is often limited, making it difficult for them to participate fully in these efforts.

## Gender-Responsive Climate Change Adaptation Strategies

To address the gender disparities in climate change adaptation, a gender-responsive approach is necessary. This approach involves engaging women and girls in decision-making processes related to climate change adaptation and mitigation strategies. It also involves addressing the specific needs and vulnerabilities of women and girls, such as their access to resources, their ability to respond to disaster, and their vulnerability to climate-related hazards.

## Conclusion

Gender issues play a significant role in response to immediate climate disasters and climate risks. Women are more likely to be affected by climate-related hazards due to their reliance on natural resources for livelihoods, and they face barriers to accessing information about climate risks and adaptation strategies. A gender-responsive approach is necessary to address these issues and ensure that women and girls are fully included in climate change adaptation efforts.
