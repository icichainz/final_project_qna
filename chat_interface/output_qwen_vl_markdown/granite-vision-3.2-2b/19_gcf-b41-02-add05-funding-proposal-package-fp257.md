## Funding Proposal

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) is pleased to announce the awarding of funding for the RE GAIN project, which aims to scale solutions for food loss in Africa. The project will focus on improving the resilience of smallholder farmers and rural communities to climate change impacts, with a particular emphasis on reducing post-harvest losses and increasing access to markets.

The RE GAIN project will be implemented in the following countries: Burkina Faso, Kenya, Malawi, Uganda, and Zambia. The project will involve a range of activities, including farmer training and extension services, improved storage facilities, and market access support. The project is expected to have a significant impact on food security in the region, with potential benefits for both farmers and rural communities.

The funding proposal includes a detailed budget breakdown, which outlines the costs associated with each activity. The budget will be used to support the implementation of the RE GAIN project over a period of three years, from 2024 to 2026.

## Executive Summary

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) is a global initiative that aims to mobilize $100 billion annually by 2020 to support climate change mitigation and adaptation efforts in developing countries. This proposal outlines the GCF's objectives, strategies, and expected outcomes for the next five years.

## Objectives

The GCF aims to:

1. Mobilize $100 billion annually by 2020 to support climate change mitigation and adaptation efforts in developing countries.
2. Enhance the resilience of vulnerable communities to climate-related hazards and disasters.
3. Promote sustainable development and economic growth while reducing greenhouse gas emissions.
4. Foster international cooperation and knowledge sharing on climate change mitigation and adaptation.

## Strategies

The GCF will implement the following strategies to achieve its objectives:

1. **Climate Change Mitigation**: The GCF will provide financial support for projects that reduce greenhouse gas emissions, such as renewable energy, energy efficiency, and sustainable agriculture.
2. **Climate Change Adaptation**: The GCF will support projects that enhance the resilience of vulnerable communities to climate-related hazards and disasters, such as early warning systems, disaster risk reduction, and climate-resilient infrastructure.
3. **Sustainable Development**: The GCF will promote sustainable development by supporting projects that improve access to clean energy, water, sanitation, and nutrition, while reducing poverty and inequality.
4. **International Cooperation**: The GCF will foster international cooperation and knowledge sharing on climate change mitigation and adaptation through partnerships with governments, private sector, civil society, and academic institutions.

## Expected Outcomes

The GCF expects to achieve the following outcomes:

1. **Reduced Greenhouse Gas Emissions**: The GCF will mobilize $100 billion annually by 2020 to support projects that reduce greenhouse gas emissions, contributing to the global effort to limit global warming to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels.
2. **Enhanced Resilience**: The GCF will enhance the resilience of vulnerable communities to climate-related hazards and disasters, reducing the impact of climate change on human health, livelihoods, and economies.
3. **Sustainable Development**: The GCF will promote sustainable development by supporting projects that improve access to clean energy, water, sanitation, and nutrition, while reducing poverty and inequality.
4. **International Cooperation**: The GCF will foster international cooperation and knowledge sharing on climate change mitigation and adaptation through partnerships with governments, private sector, civil society, and academic institutions.

## Conclusion

The Green Climate Fund is a critical initiative that can help developing countries mobilize the necessary resources to address the challenges of climate change. 6 Programme application
====================================================

To ensure consistent application of gender responsive and socially inclusive planning and implementation, per component and across all countries, several important principles and approaches are outlined below. These comply with AGRA’s organizational commitments and globally accepted EAS test practice.

6.1 GENDER INTEGRATION APPROACH
-------------------------------------------------

In keeping with AGRA’s five Year Gender Inclusion Strategy (2022-2027), the RE-GAR4 Programme will adopt the Reach, Empower and Engage framework across each of its three Outcomes. This approach will ensure that women and youth are reached by actively ensuring that they are included as core Programme participants in all activities, including meetings and trainings, and number of women and youth only events and through targeted efforts to identify and deliver benefits that women and youth place explicit value on or be these time savings.

6.2 GRIEVANCE MECHANISM, SAFEGUARDING TOOLS AND STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT
-------------------------------------------------

Stakeholder engagement measures will work proactively towards identifying and addressing issues before they become of the scope of AGRA’s activities. However, it is important to note that no issue can be resolved without the involvement of all stakeholders. Therefore, we will ensure through the EAS Due Diligence that the grantor will establish a mechanism to receive and facilitate resolution of stakeholders’ concerns, complaints, and grievances about the project’s performance. Procedures stipulated by AGRA’s Grievance Mechanism (Items 6 of AGRA’s ESMS Manual) will be followed and stakeholders will be informed about their rights and obligations under this mechanism.

The mechanisms, this includes the Grievance Mechanism, are designed to be transparent projects that allow for gender experianza, cultural appropriateness, and needs assessable to all segments of the stakeholders at no costs and without retribution. Furthermore, the actions are applicable for EIC’s risk Category A, B and C projects.

6.2.4 Stakeholder Engagement
Stakeholders are persons or groups who are directly or indirectly affected by a project, as well as those who may have interests in the project's success or failure. They can be identified through various methods such as surveys, interviews, focus group discussions and community consultations. The stakeholders should be involved in all stages of the project including planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

The outcomes of the overall and country level strategic risk assessments should be formally taken into consideration for any engagement guideline or programme plan updates.
