## 1. Introduction

The Indian Ocean is a critical region for global food security, economic development, and biodiversity conservation. However, it faces significant threats from climate change, including sea-level rise, ocean acidification, and increased frequency of extreme weather events. Ecosystem-based adaptation (EbA) strategies can help mitigate these impacts by enhancing the resilience of coastal communities to climate change.

## 2. Methodology

Additionally, the study conducted focus group discussions with community leaders and stakeholders to gather insights on their experiences and perceptions of climate change impacts and EbA strategies.

## 3. Results

The focus group discussions highlighted the importance of EbA strategies for enhancing the resilience of coastal communities to these impacts.

## 4. Discussion

The study identified several key challenges related to EbA in the Indian Ocean, including limited access to resources, lack of awareness and knowledge about EbA strategies, and insufficient capacity for implementation. The study also highlighted the importance of integrating gender perspectives into EbA strategies to ensure that they are inclusive and equitable.

## 5. Recommendations

Based on the findings of this study, the following recommendations are made:

1. Increase access to resources for coastal communities in the Indian Ocean to implement EbA strategies.
2. Conduct awareness and knowledge-raising campaigns to educate community members about climate change impacts and EbA strategies.
3. Strengthen the capacity of local governments and institutions to implement EbA strategies.
4. Integrate gender perspectives into EbA strategies to ensure that they are inclusive and equitable.
5. Encourage public-private partnerships to support the implementation of EbA strategies in the Indian Ocean.

## 6. Conclusion

This study provides valuable insights into the challenges and opportunities related to EbA in the Indian Ocean. The findings highlight the need for increased access to resources, awareness-raising campaigns, capacity building, gender integration, and public-private partnerships to enhance the resilience of coastal communities to climate change impacts.

## Abstract

This report presents a gender assessment and programme level action plan for ecosystem-based adaptation in the Indian Ocean. The study aims to identify the current state of gender equality, assess the impacts of climate change on women's livelihoods, and propose strategies to enhance women's participation in decision-making processes related to ecosystem management.

## Introduction

The Indian Ocean is a critical region for global food security, biodiversity conservation, and sustainable development. However, the region faces significant challenges, including climate change, overfishing, and pollution, which disproportionately affect women's livelihoods and access to resources. This report provides an overview of the current state of gender equality in the Indian Ocean, assesses the impacts of climate change on women's livelihoods, and proposes strategies to enhance women's participation in decision-making processes related to ecosystem management.

## Gender Assessment

The gender assessment was conducted using a mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative data from surveys and qualitative data from focus group discussions with women and men involved in ecosystem management. The survey collected data on women's access to resources, their participation in decision-making processes, and their perceptions of climate change impacts on their livelihoods. The focus group discussions provided insights into the cultural and social factors that influence women's participation in ecosystem management.

## Impacts of Climate Change on Women's Livelihoods

The report found that climate change has a significant impact on women's livelihoods, particularly in terms of access to resources such as fish, water, and land. The study identified several factors that contribute to this impact, including changes in rainfall patterns, sea-level rise, and increased frequency of extreme weather events. The report also found that women are more vulnerable to these impacts due to their dependence on natural resources for their livelihoods and their limited access to education and training opportunities.

## Strategies to Enhance Women's Participation in Decision-Making Processes

The report proposes several strategies to enhance women's participation in decision-making processes related to ecosystem management. These include:

1. Increasing access to education and training opportunities for women, particularly in the fields of ecology, conservation biology, and sustainable development.
2. Promoting gender equality in decision-making bodies, such as fisheries committees and coastal management boards.
3. Encouraging the participation of women in research and monitoring activities related to ecosystem management.
4. Developing policies and programs that support women's access to resources and their involvement in decision-making processes.

## Conclusion

The report concludes that gender equality is a critical factor in enhancing women's participation in ecosystem management in the Indian Ocean. The study identified several factors that contribute to this impact, including changes in rainfall patterns, sea-level rise, and increased frequency of extreme weather events. The report also proposed several strategies to enhance women's participation in decision-making processes related to ecosystem management.
