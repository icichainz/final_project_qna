## Introduction

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) is a global initiative that aims to mobilize $100 billion annually by 2020 to support climate change mitigation and adaptation efforts in developing countries. This proposal outlines the funding requirements for the GCF's Programme of Action, which includes projects focused on reducing greenhouse gas emissions, enhancing resilience to climate change impacts, and promoting sustainable development.

## Funding Requirements

### 1. Reducing Greenhouse Gas Emissions

The GCF will provide funding for projects that reduce greenhouse gas emissions in developing countries. This includes projects focused on renewable energy, energy efficiency, and carbon capture and storage technologies. The funding requirements for these projects are as follows:

- **Renewable Energy**: $50 billion per year
- **Energy Efficiency**: $30 billion per year
- **Carbon Capture and Storage**: $20 billion per year

### 2. Enhancing Resilience to Climate Change Impacts

The GCF will provide funding for projects that enhance the resilience of developing countries to climate change impacts. This includes projects focused on disaster risk reduction, early warning systems, and climate-smart agriculture. The funding requirements for these projects are as follows:

- **Disaster Risk Reduction**: $40 billion per year
- **Early Warning Systems**: $30 billion per year
- **Climate-Smart Agriculture**: $20 billion per year

### 3. Promoting Sustainable Development

The GCF will provide funding for projects that promote sustainable development in developing countries. This includes projects focused on education, health, and economic growth. The funding requirements for these projects are as follows:

- **Education**: $50 billion per year
- **Health**: $40 billion per year
- **Economic Growth**: $30 billion per year

## Funding Allocation

The GCF will allocate the funding requirements for each programme of action in a way that maximizes the impact of the funding. The allocation will be based on the following criteria:

- **Country Priority**: Projects will be allocated to countries with the greatest need for climate change mitigation and adaptation efforts.
- **Project Impact**: Projects will be allocated based on their potential to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, enhance resilience to climate change impacts, and promote sustainable development.
- **Funding Availability**: The GCF will allocate the funding requirements in a way that maximizes the use of available funds.

## Conclusion

The Green Climate Fund is a global initiative that aims to mobilize $100 billion annually by 2020 to support climate change mitigation and adaptation efforts in developing countries. This proposal outlines the funding requirements for the GCF's Programme of Action, which includes projects focused on reducing greenhouse gas emissions, enhancing resilience to climate change impacts, and promoting sustainable development. ## Introduction

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) is a global initiative that aims to mobilize $100 billion annually by 2020 to support climate change mitigation and adaptation efforts in developing countries. This proposal outlines the funding requirements for the GCF's FY2021 program, which will run from January 1, 2021, to December 31, 2021.

## Funding Requirements

The GCF is seeking funding to support projects that address climate change and its impacts on vulnerable communities. The following categories of projects are eligible for funding:

1. **Climate Change Mitigation Projects**: These projects aim to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and enhance the resilience of communities to climate-related hazards. Examples include renewable energy projects, energy efficiency measures, and sustainable land use practices.
2. **Climate Change Adaptation Projects**: These projects aim to help communities adapt to the impacts of climate change, such as sea-level rise, droughts, and extreme weather events. Examples include early warning systems, flood protection infrastructure, and climate-resilient agriculture practices.
3. **Climate Change Resilience Projects**: These projects aim to enhance the resilience of communities to climate change impacts by improving their ability to adapt and recover from disasters. Examples include community-based disaster risk reduction programs, early warning systems, and climate-resilient infrastructure.
4. **Climate Change Financing Mechanisms**: These projects aim to improve the access to finance for climate change mitigation and adaptation efforts in developing countries. Examples include green bonds, blended finance mechanisms, and climate risk insurance products.

## Funding Allocation

The GCF is seeking funding to support a range of projects across different sectors, including:

1. **Energy**: This category includes renewable energy projects, energy efficiency measures, and sustainable energy systems.
2. **Water and Sanitation**: This category includes water and sanitation infrastructure, water treatment facilities, and climate-resilient agriculture practices.
3. **Agriculture**: This category includes climate-resilient agriculture practices, such as agroforestry, conservation agriculture, and climate-smart livestock production.
4. **Health**: This category includes health systems strengthening projects, including disease prevention and control programs, and climate-sensitive health services.
5. **Infrastructure**: This category includes infrastructure projects that enhance the resilience of communities to climate change impacts, such as early warning systems, flood protection infrastructure, and climate-resilient transportation systems.
6. **Climate Change Research and Development**: This category includes research and development projects that aim to improve our understanding of climate change and develop new technologies for mitigation and adaptation.
7. **Capacity Building**: This category includes capacity building programs that aim to enhance the skills and knowledge of local communities, governments, and other stakeholders in addressing climate change.

## Funding Process

The GCF is seeking funding through a competitive bidding process. The bidding process will involve the submission of proposals by eligible organizations, followed by an evaluation of the proposals based on predefined criteria. The GCF will then select the projects that meet its funding requirements and announce the funding decisions in due course.

The Green Climate Fund is seeking funding to support climate change mitigation and adaptation efforts in developing countries. ## WHY IS THIS IMPORTANT?

Gender analysis is a systematic way to identify context-specific key issues and factors that contribute to gender inequalities. It may consist of primary or secondary research, or a combination of both. It is impossible to integrate gender equality into projects or activities without clearly understanding gender issues within the targeted population. A gender analysis enables project developers to understand the implications of gender on climate change mitigation, adaptation and disaster risk reduction interventions (and vice-versa), and design appropriate interventions that can benefit all members of a community. It also allows the project developers to develop indicators to better assess how initiatives have engaged with or impacted different groups.

Gender analysis explores individuals, relations and institutional factors, within both public and private spheres, as individuals are likely to experience gender inequality differently. Gender analysis attempts to answer at least three key sets of questions: 
- How will gender relations influence the effectiveness and sustainability of the project activity or result? 
- How will project processes and activities be designed to address these issues? 
- How will the proposed results affect the relative status of women and men? Will they exacerbate or reduce inequalities?

# Step 5b Establish meaningful gender performance indicators for the Project

## Gender performance indicators and sex-disaggregated targets can now be incorporated into the Project results framework.

### WHAT ARE GENDER INDICATORS?

Indicators play a crucial role in the gender responsive process throughout the policy cycle. Quantitative and qualitative indicators must enable ongoing monitoring and evaluation of whether the Project's gender equality objectives are being met and assessing the gender effect of Project activities. If objectives are not being met, it could be re-assessed to re-raise the Project strategy and make amendments to ensure improvements. Gender indicators usually express how discrimination is manifested in society as a measure of gender inequalities. Gender-blind indicators are those that do not allow the evaluator to uncover gender differences and gender relations in a society because they are not collected or cannot be collected in a sex-disaggregated way. For example, "community participation" is a gender-blind indicator but is collected separately for men and for women, can become a gender indicators.
