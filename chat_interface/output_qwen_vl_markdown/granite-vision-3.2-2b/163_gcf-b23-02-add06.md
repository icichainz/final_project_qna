## Key Issues Related to Gender Equality

The following are some of the key issues related to gender equality:

1. **Access to Water Resources**: Women often have less access to water resources than men, particularly in rural areas and for marginalized groups such as women with disabilities and elderly people. This can lead to a lack of knowledge about water management practices and the ability to manage water resources effectively.
2. **Water Management Practices**: Women are often responsible for managing household water resources, which can be time-consuming and labor-intensive. They may also have limited access to information on water management practices and technologies that could improve their efficiency and effectiveness.
3. **Decision-Making Processes**: Women are underrepresented in decision-making processes related to water management at all levels, from local communities to national policy levels. This can lead to a lack of representation of women's perspectives and experiences in the development and implementation of water policies and programs.
4. **Health and Sanitation**: Women have a higher risk of waterborne diseases due to their exposure to contaminated water sources and limited access to safe sanitation facilities. Improving water quality and supply can help reduce this risk and improve women's health and well-being.
5. **Economic Opportunities**: Access to clean water is essential for economic development, particularly in rural areas where it can lead to increased agricultural productivity and improved livelihoods. Women who have access to water resources may be able to participate more effectively in the economy and contribute to their communities' development.

## Proposed Interventions

1. **Capacity Building**: Provide training and education on water management practices and technologies for women at all levels of society. This can help improve their knowledge and skills in managing household water resources and participating in decision-making processes related to water management.
2. **Inclusive Decision-Making Processes**: Ensure that women are represented in decision-making processes related to water management at all levels, from local communities to national policy levels. This can help ensure that their perspectives and experiences are taken into account in the development and implementation of water policies and programs.
3. **Access to Water Resources**: Improve access to clean water resources for women, particularly in rural areas and for marginalized groups such as women with disabilities and elderly people. This can help reduce their exposure to contaminated water sources and improve their health and well-being.
4. **Health and Sanitation**: Implement interventions aimed at improving water quality and supply, particularly in rural areas where it can lead to increased risk of waterborne diseases. This can help reduce this risk and improve women's health and well-being.
5. **Economic Opportunities**: Provide training and education on economic opportunities related to water management, such as the development of small-scale agricultural projects or the creation of new businesses in the water sector. This can help increase women's participation in the economy and contribute to their communities' development.

## Conclusion

The consultation process of respondents has highlighted the importance of involving women in decision-making on water management matters at community level. Based on the results of this gender analysis, it is clear that improving water quality and supply is both a social and economic priority for both men and women. The proposed interventions aim to address key issues related to gender equality and promote inclusive decision-making processes related to water management.
