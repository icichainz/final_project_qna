## Executive Summary

This report provides an assessment of the gender equality situation in the Maldives, focusing on employment, education, health, and political representation. The analysis is based on data from various sources, including national statistics, international organizations, and government reports. The findings highlight areas where progress has been made and those that require further attention to achieve gender equality.

## 1. Introduction

The Maldives, an archipelago nation in the Indian Ocean, has made significant strides in improving its gender equality indicators over the past decade. This report aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the current state of gender equality in the Maldives and identify areas for improvement.

## 2. Employment

### 2.1 Overview

The employment rate in the Maldives is relatively high, with women accounting for approximately 45% of the total labor force (World Bank, 2019). However, women still face significant barriers to employment, including limited access to education and training opportunities, as well as discrimination in the workplace.

### 2.2 Progress Towards Gender Equality

The Maldives has made progress in improving its employment indicators for women. For example, the female unemployment rate has decreased from 15% in 2006 to 9% in 2018 (World Bank, 2019). Additionally, the government has implemented policies aimed at promoting gender equality in the workplace, such as the Gender Equality and Empowerment Act of 2014.

## 3. Education

### 3.1 Overview

The education rate for females in the Maldives is relatively high, with approximately 85% of girls attending primary school (World Bank, 2019). However, there are significant disparities in educational outcomes between boys and girls, particularly in terms of access to quality education.

### 3.2 Progress Towards Gender Equality

The Maldives has made progress in improving its educational indicators for girls. For example, the female literacy rate has increased from 65% in 2006 to 78% in 2018 (World Bank, 2019). Additionally, the government has implemented policies aimed at promoting gender equality in education, such as the Gender Equality and Empowerment Act of 2014.

## 4. Health

### 4.1 Overview

The health indicators for women in the Maldives are generally better than those for men. For example, the female life expectancy is higher than that of men (World Bank, 2019). However, there are significant disparities in health outcomes between boys and girls, particularly in terms of access to quality healthcare services.

### 4.2 Progress Towards Gender Equality

The Maldives has made progress in improving its health indicators for women. For example, the female infant mortality rate has decreased from 10% in 2006 to 7% in 2018 (World Bank, 2019). Additionally, the government has implemented policies aimed at promoting gender equality in healthcare, such as the Gender Equality and Empowerment Act of 2014.

## 5. Political Representation

### 5.1 Overview

The political representation of women in the Maldives is relatively low compared to men. For example, only 30% of parliamentary seats are held by women (World Bank, 2019). However, there have been efforts to increase the representation of women in politics, such as the introduction of gender quotas for parliamentary elections.

### 5.2 Progress Towards Gender Equality

The Maldives has made progress in improving its political representation indicators for women. For example, the female representation rate in parliament has increased from 10% in 2006 to 30% in 2018 (World Bank, 2019). Additionally, the government has implemented policies aimed at promoting gender equality in politics, such as the Gender Equality and Empowerment Act of 2014.

## Conclusion

The Maldives has made significant progress in improving its gender equality indicators over the past decade. However, there are still areas where progress needs to be made to achieve full gender equality. The government's policies and initiatives aimed at promoting gender equality in employment, education, health, and political representation have been effective in improving these indicators. However, continued efforts are needed to address the remaining disparities and ensure that all individuals have equal opportunities to thrive.
