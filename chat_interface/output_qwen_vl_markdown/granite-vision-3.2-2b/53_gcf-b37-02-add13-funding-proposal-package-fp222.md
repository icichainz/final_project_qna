## 6. REPP 2 financing and support

REPP 2 will further catalyse the growth of sub-Saharan Africa’s small-scale renewable energy sector by scaling up from the success of REPP Phase 1, further leveraging private sector capital flows into small-scale renewable energy projects. The expected outcome is to deliver 330MW of new installed capacity, more than 12.7 million tCO₂e (MtCO₂e) (MtCO₂e) of GHG emissions avoided, and create 500,000 jobs in the sector.

- Early-stage and development funding, which includes early-stage and growth funding for off-grid renewable energy projects. This will be provided through a combination of grants and loans to support the development of new projects and the expansion of existing ones.

# Risk Management: Gender-based Violence

## Introduction
Gender-based violence (GBV) is an umbrella term for any harmful act perpetrated against a person based on socially ascribed (i.e., gender) differences, between males and females. It includes acts that inflict physical, sexual, or mental harm or suffering, threats of such acts, or attempts to prevent the full advancement of women.

## Types of GBV
GBV can be categorized into several types:
- **Sexual violence**: This includes rape, unwanted touching, and sexual exploitation.
- **Exploitation of a position of power or trust**: This involves abuse of authority or control over another person.
- **Coercion**: This refers to the use of force or threats to obtain something from someone else.
- **Sexual physical contact**: This includes any form of sexual contact without consent.
- **Intimate partner violence**: This is a broader term that encompasses all forms of GBV involving an intimate partner, including domestic violence and sexual abuse.

## Risk Management Strategies
Effective risk management strategies for GBV include:
- **Prevention**: Implementing policies and programs to prevent GBV from occurring.
- **Early detection and intervention**: Identifying and addressing GBV early on to prevent further harm.
- **Support services**: Providing resources and support for survivors of GBV.
- **Education and awareness**: Educating individuals about the risks and consequences of GBV, as well as how to recognize and respond to it.
- **Legal frameworks**: Establishing laws and policies that hold perpetrators accountable for GBV and provide protections for survivors.

## Conclusion
Gender-based violence is a complex issue that requires a comprehensive approach to risk management. By implementing effective strategies, we can work towards creating safer and more equitable communities for all individuals, regardless of their gender.

# Risk Management: Gender-Based Violence

## Introduction

Risk management is a critical aspect of any project, ensuring that potential hazards are identified and mitigated to protect all stakeholders. In recent years, gender-based violence (GBV) has gained significant attention as a form of risk that can impact the safety and well-being of workers and communities involved in projects. This presentation aims to explore the role of GBV in project management and provide strategies for mitigating its risks.

## Understanding Gender-Based Violence

Gender-based violence refers to any form of sexual, physical, or psychological abuse directed towards individuals based on their gender. This can include sexual harassment, rape, assault, and other forms of violence that are motivated by bias against women and girls. GBV is a significant risk in many industries, including construction, where it can lead to physical harm, emotional distress, and disruption of work activities.

## The Impact of Gender-Based Violence on Project Management

GBV can have a profound impact on project management, affecting not only the safety and well-being of workers but also the productivity and morale of the entire team. Some of the ways in which GBV can impact project management include:

1. **Reduced Productivity**: Workers who experience GBV may be unable to work effectively, leading to reduced productivity and increased costs for the project.
2. **Increased Absenteeism**: GBV can lead to workers taking time off from work due to fear of retaliation or medical emergencies, further reducing productivity and increasing absenteeism.
3. **Decreased Morale**: GBV can create a hostile work environment, leading to decreased morale and motivation among workers, which can negatively impact project outcomes.
4. **Legal and Financial Risks**: Projects that fail to address GBV risks may face legal and financial consequences, including lawsuits, fines, and damage to the company's reputation.

## Strategies for Mitigating Gender-Based Violence in Project Management

To mitigate the risks associated with GBV in project management, companies can implement a range of strategies, including:

1. **Establishing a Zero-Tolerance Policy**: Companies should establish a clear policy that prohibits all forms of GBV and outlines the consequences for violating this policy.
2. **Providing Training and Education**: Providing training and education to workers on the risks of GBV, how to recognize and report incidents, and the importance of creating a safe and respectful work environment can help prevent GBV from occurring in the first place.
3. **Implementing Safety Measures**: Implementing safety measures such as increased security, surveillance cameras, and emergency response plans can help protect workers from GBV.
4. **Providing Support Services**: Providing support services such as counseling, legal assistance, and medical care can help workers who experience GBV to recover and return to work.
5. **Monitoring and Reporting**: Regularly monitoring and reporting on GBV incidents can help identify trends and patterns, allowing companies to take proactive measures to address these risks.

## Conclusion

Gender-based violence is a significant risk in project management that can have a profound impact on the safety, well-being, and productivity of workers. By implementing strategies such as establishing a zero-tolerance policy, providing training and education, implementing safety measures, providing support services, and monitoring and reporting GBV incidents, companies can mitigate these risks and create a safe and respectful work environment for all stakeholders.
