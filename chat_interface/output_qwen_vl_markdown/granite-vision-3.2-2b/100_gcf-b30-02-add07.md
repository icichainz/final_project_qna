## Climate context (max. 1000 words, approximately 2 pages)

### Kenya's vulnerability to climate change

Kenya was ranked 7th on the 2018 Global Log-Term Climate Risk Index published by Germanwatch and it is expected to experience almost twice as many extreme weather events in the coming decades. The country's vulnerability is linked to seasonal rains. Between March and July 2019, Kenya experienced almost twice as many extreme weather events compared to the same period in 2018. In addition, Kenya experienced a significant increase in the number of people affected by droughts, floods, and wildfires during this time.

### Kenya's location and climate

Kenya is located in East Africa, in the Horn of Africa region. It is situated on the eastern coast of the Indian Ocean and shares borders with Ethiopia to the north, Uganda to the east, Tanzania to the south, Rwanda to the west, Burundi to the southwest, and South Sudan to the northeast. Kenya's climate is characterized by a tropical savanna with two distinct seasons: rainy season (June to August) and dry season (September to November). The country experiences high temperatures throughout the year, with average annual temperatures ranging from 25°C to 30°C.

## Gender Analysis for Enhancing the Resilience of Communities and Ecosystems in the Athi River Catchment Area

The National Environmental Management Authority (NEMA) and the Korea Environmental Industry and Technology Institute (KEITI) have collaborated to conduct a gender analysis for enhancing the resilience of communities and ecosystems in the Athi River catchment area. This report outlines the findings and recommendations based on the analysis.

## Background

The Athi River is a vital water source for the region, providing freshwater for drinking, irrigation, and hydroelectric power generation. The river's catchment area encompasses fourteen falls in Klambu County, Tunay Africa. However, the region faces several challenges, including climate change, deforestation, and human-wildlife conflict. Gender analysis is crucial to understanding the impacts of these challenges on women and men and to developing effective strategies for enhancing resilience.

## Methodology

The gender analysis was conducted using a mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative data from surveys and statistical analyses with qualitative data from interviews and focus group discussions. The study focused on the following aspects:

1. **Demographic Characteristics**: Age, sex, marital status, education level, and employment status of women and men in the catchment area.
2. **Livelihoods**: Agricultural practices, fishing, and other livelihood activities.
3. **Access to Resources**: Access to water, sanitation, healthcare, and education facilities.
4. **Climate Change Impacts**: Changes in rainfall patterns, temperature, and extreme weather events.
5. **Human-Wildlife Conflict**: Incidents of crop damage, livestock loss, and human injuries due to wildlife encounters.
6. **Decision-Making Processes**: Participation in decision-making processes related to water resources management and conservation.

## Findings

The gender analysis revealed several key findings:

1. **Disparities in Access to Resources**: Women are more likely to have limited access to water, sanitation, and healthcare facilities compared to men. This disparity is exacerbated by their involvement in agricultural activities that require manual labor and exposure to environmental hazards.
2. **Climate Change Impacts**: Both women and men are vulnerable to the impacts of climate change, including changes in rainfall patterns and extreme weather events. However, women are more likely to experience food insecurity due to crop failures and livestock losses.
3. **Human-Wildlife Conflict**: Women are more likely to be involved in human-wildlife conflict incidents, particularly when they are collecting water or tending to crops near wildlife habitats. This is often due to limited access to resources and the need for food security.
4. **Decision-Making Processes**: Women are less likely to participate in decision-making processes related to water resources management and conservation compared to men. This is partly due to their involvement in domestic tasks and the lack of awareness about the importance of gender equality in these areas.

## Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following recommendations are proposed:

1. **Improve Access to Resources**: Governments and NGOs should invest in infrastructure development, such as water treatment facilities and irrigation systems, to improve access to resources for both women and men.
2. **Promote Gender Equality**: Policies and programs should be implemented to promote gender equality in decision-making processes related to water resources management and conservation. This includes increasing the participation of women in these areas and providing them with education and training on environmental issues.
3. **Enhance Climate Change Resilience**: Governments and NGOs should develop and implement strategies to enhance the resilience of communities and ecosystems to climate change impacts. This includes promoting sustainable agricultural practices, improving water management systems, and providing early warning systems for extreme weather events.
4. **Reduce Human-Wildlife Conflict**: Governments and NGOs should implement measures to reduce human-wildlife conflict incidents, such as providing education on how to coexist with wildlife and promoting the use of non-lethal methods for pest control.

## Conclusion

The gender analysis highlights the disparities in access to resources, vulnerability to climate change impacts, and participation in decision-making processes related to water resources management and conservation among women and men in the Athi River catchment area. The recommendations proposed aim to address these challenges and enhance the resilience of communities and ecosystems in the region.

# Business Overview

## Key Information

- **Revenue**: Over $100 monthly for residents of Machakos County.
- **Employment**: Informal employment ($30), crop farming, and the dairy sector at $10 each.
- **Household Spending**: Food, education, housing, healthcare, clothing, and social functions.

## Main Areas of Household Spending

- **Food**: Leading component, with food being reported to be costly due to recurrent nature and high depletion capitalization costs.
- **Education**: High expenses for children's education, including school fees and extracurricular activities.
- **Housing**: Significant expenses for housing, including rent, utilities, and maintenance.
- **Healthcare**: Costs of healthcare are high, with medical expenses being a significant portion of household spending.
- **Clothing**: High expenses for clothing, including workwear and leisure clothing.
- **Social Functions**: Expenses for social functions, such as entertainment and travel, are relatively low but still significant.

## Challenges Faced by Households

- **Water Supply**: Water is supplied only twice a week, forcing households to buy from vendors at Kamu.
- **Healthcare Costs**: High healthcare costs, including medical expenses and dental care, are a significant burden for households.
- **Food Insecurity**: Food insecurity is a major issue, with high expenses for food due to recurrent nature and high depletion capitalization costs.
- **Social Functions**: Social functions, such as entertainment and travel, are relatively low but still significant.

## Conclusion

The main areas of household spending in Machakos County include food, education, housing, healthcare, clothing, and social functions. The highest expenses are for food, education, and housing, while the lowest expenses are for social functions. Water supply is a significant challenge, with water being supplied only twice a week. Healthcare costs are high, and food insecurity is a major issue due to recurrent nature and high depletion capitalization costs.

## Methodology

The study was conducted in Machakos County, Kenya. The sample consisted of 100 men and 100 women aged 15-64 years. The data were collected through face-to-face interviews using a structured questionnaire. The interviews were conducted in both English and Swahili.

## Results

The results showed that the majority of women spent their time on housework, with an average of 2 hours per day. This was followed by childcare activities, which took up an average of 1 hour per day. The remaining time was spent on other economic activities such as shopping and cooking.

The findings suggest that the majority of women in Machakos County are spending their time on housework, which is a major source of income for them. However, this also means that they have little time for their economic activities, which can perpetuate their low income status. The study suggests that there is a need to address the issue of time poverty among women in Machakos County.

The study provides valuable insights into the daily activities of women in Machakos County. ## Gender-responsive budgeting (GRB)

Gender-responsive budgeting is a method of determining the extent to which government expenditure has achieved its intended goals and objectives. It involves collecting data on gender equality in all areas of public policy and using that information to inform decision making.

## Gender action plan (GAP)

A Gender Action Plan (GAP) is a key gender mainstreaming tool and mechanism for ensuring gender-inclusive development. It is used by governments to identify, analyze, and address the needs of women and men in all areas of public policy. A GAP forms the basis for operationalizing the results and recommendations of the gender analysis.

## Gender disaggregation data

Gender disaggregated data refers to any data that is collected separately for women and men, or by sex. It reflects the realities of the lives of women and men and policy issues relating to gender. Data collected and tabulated separately for women and men are used in the development of GRBs and GAPs.

## Gender action plan during the project cycle

The gender action plan is developed during the project cycle, with input from stakeholders including women's groups, civil society organizations, and government agencies. The plan is reviewed and updated as needed to ensure that it remains relevant and effective.

## Conclusion

Gender-responsive budgeting and Gender Action Plans are important tools for ensuring gender equality in all areas of public policy. By collecting data on gender equality and using that information to inform decision making, governments can create more inclusive and equitable policies that benefit both women and men.
