### Social and Environmental Context

As public transport is the backbone of urban transport it is therefore key to a sustainable mobility system. According to the latest urban air quality database, 98% of cities in low- and middle-income countries with more than 100,000 inhabitants are not meeting World Health Organization (WHO) air quality guidelines. The transport sector is a major source of air pollution.

### Country Conditions and Ownership

Latin America has strong enabling conditions in terms of clean transport and e-mobility. The region has one of the cleanest electricity generation portfolios in the world with the median of Program countries contributing to 10% of their total energy consumption from renewable sources (LOI, 2020). However, there is a lack of clear regulatory frameworks for e-mobility and the transport sector.

### Intervention Strategy

The Program goal is to accelerate Electric Vehicle (EV) deployment and enable a large-scale regional transition to low-carbon mobility by 2030. The Program will implement interventions to kick-start EV market deployment significantly earlier than under a BAU scenario avoiding a lack of long-lived assets and technical assistance. The key strategy value is that the function of the Program is that it functions as an accelerator for the transition to low-carbon mobility, thereby creating new jobs and stimulating economic growth.

### Conclusion

The Program has been developed in close coordination with CAF whose FPF25 has been approved by the U.S. Department of Commerce (DOD) as a key component for modernizing and upgrading the public transport system of Latin America. ### Introduction

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) is a global initiative that aims to mobilize $100 billion annually by 2020 to support climate action in developing countries. This proposal outlines the funding requirements for the GCF's EV Deployment and Program Approach to Overcome Barriers project, which focuses on improving electric vehicle (EV) deployment and addressing barriers to overcoming them.

## Project Overview

The EV Deployment and Program Approach to Overcome Barriers project aims to improve EV deployment in developing countries by addressing barriers such as high upfront costs, lack of charging infrastructure, and limited access to financing. The project will focus on improving the grid resilience, reducing emissions from transportation, and promoting sustainable urban mobility.

## Funding Requirements

The GCF is seeking $100 billion annually for the EV Deployment and Program Approach to Overcome Barriers project over a five-year period (2020-2024). The funding will be allocated as follows:

* Year 1: Project design, implementation planning, and initial activities
* Year 2: Implementation of key activities, including grid resilience improvement, charging infrastructure development, and financing mechanisms
* Year 3: Expansion of key activities, including transportation sector reform, urban mobility promotion, and capacity building for local stakeholders
* Year 4: Monitoring and evaluation, reporting, and finalization of the project
* Year 5: Transition to a new funding cycle

## Project Activities

The EV Deployment and Program Approach to Overcome Barriers project will focus on the following key activities:

1. Grid Resilience Improvement: The project will work with local governments, utilities, and other stakeholders to improve grid resilience by enhancing the reliability, efficiency, and sustainability of the electricity supply chain. This will involve upgrading transmission infrastructure, improving energy storage systems, and promoting renewable energy sources.
2. Charging Infrastructure Development: The project will work with local governments, utilities, and other stakeholders to develop charging infrastructure for EVs, including public charging stations, fast-charging corridors, and home charging solutions. This will involve conducting feasibility studies, designing and building charging infrastructure, and promoting the adoption of EVs through incentives and subsidies.
3. Financing Mechanisms: The project will work with local governments, financial institutions, and other stakeholders to develop financing mechanisms for EV deployment, including low-interest loans, grants, and public-private partnerships. This will involve conducting feasibility studies, designing and implementing financing mechanisms, and promoting the adoption of EVs through incentives and subsidies.
4. Transportation Sector Reform: The project will work with local governments, transportation authorities, and other stakeholders to reform the transportation sector by promoting sustainable mobility modes, such as public transit, cycling, and walking. This will involve conducting feasibility studies, designing and implementing transportation policies, and promoting the adoption of sustainable mobility modes through incentives and subsidies.
5. Urban Mobility Promotion: The project will work with local governments, urban planners, and other stakeholders to promote urban mobility by improving the accessibility, affordability, and sustainability of public transportation systems. This will involve conducting feasibility studies, designing and implementing urban mobility policies, and promoting the adoption of sustainable mobility modes through incentives and subsidies.
6. Capacity Building for Local Stakeholders: The project will work with local governments, utilities, and other stakeholders to build capacity for EV deployment by providing training, technical assistance, and financial support to local stakeholders. This will involve conducting feasibility studies, designing and implementing capacity building programs, and promoting the adoption of EVs through incentives and subsidies.

## Conclusion

The Green Climate Fund's EV Deployment and Program Approach to Overcome Barriers project is a critical initiative for improving electric vehicle deployment in developing countries. The project will focus on improving grid resilience, reducing emissions from transportation, and promoting sustainable urban mobility. The GCF is seeking $100 billion annually for the EV Deployment and Program Approach to Overcome Barriers project over a five-year period (2020-2024).

2. **Energy Efficiency**: Projects that improve energy efficiency in buildings, transportation, and industry.
3. **Climate-Smart Agriculture**: Projects that promote sustainable agricultural practices and support the development of climate-resilient food systems.
4. **Disaster Risk Reduction and Management**: Projects that enhance the resilience of communities to natural disasters and other hazards.
5. **Climate Change Adaptation**: Projects that help countries adapt to the impacts of climate change, such as sea-level rise and extreme weather events.
6. **Sustainable Water Management**: Projects that promote sustainable water management practices and support the development of water-resilient infrastructure.
7. **Climate Change Mitigation**: Projects that reduce greenhouse gas emissions and promote the transition to a low-carbon economy.
8. **Climate Justice**: Projects that address the disproportionate impacts of climate change on vulnerable populations, such as women, children, and indigenous peoples.
9. **Capacity Building**: Projects that build the capacity of countries to implement climate policies and programs.
10. **Research and Development**: Projects that support research and development in climate-related fields, such as clean energy technologies and climate modeling.

# Green Finance Initiative (GFDI)

## Overview

The Green Finance Initiative (GFDI) is a global initiative that aims to mobilize $100 billion in sustainable finance by 2030. The GFDI brings together governments, financial institutions, and other stakeholders to create an enabling environment for the transition to a low-carbon economy.

## Key Components of the GFDI

The GFDI consists of several key components:

1. **Green Finance Framework**: This framework provides a common language and set of standards for green finance products and services. It includes guidelines on climate change, sustainability, and environmental impact.
2. **Green Finance Pillars**: The GFDI focuses on three main pillars: Climate Change Mitigation, Sustainable Infrastructure, and Green Economy Transition. These pillars provide a framework for identifying and prioritizing green finance opportunities.
3. **Green Finance Platforms**: The GFDI establishes platforms to facilitate the flow of green finance, including online marketplaces, credit facilities, and investment schemes.
4. **Capacity Building**: The GFDI provides capacity building programs to enhance the skills and knowledge of financial institutions, policymakers, and other stakeholders in green finance.
5. **Monitoring and Reporting**: The GFDI establishes a monitoring and reporting framework to track progress towards the $100 billion goal and ensure accountability.

## Benefits of Green Finance

Green finance offers several benefits, including:

1. **Climate Change Mitigation**: Green finance can help reduce greenhouse gas emissions and mitigate the impacts of climate change.
2. **Sustainable Infrastructure Development**: Green finance can support the development of sustainable infrastructure, such as renewable energy projects and green buildings.
3. **Green Economy Transition**: Green finance can facilitate the transition to a low-carbon economy by supporting the growth of green industries and jobs.
4. **Financial Inclusion**: Green finance can help increase access to financial services for underserved populations, including small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and developing countries.
5. **Risk Management**: Green finance can help manage risks associated with climate change, such as extreme weather events and natural disasters.

## Conclusion

The Green Finance Initiative (GFDI) is a global initiative that aims to mobilize $100 billion in sustainable finance by 2030. The GFDI consists of several key components, including a green finance framework, green finance pillars, green finance platforms, capacity building programs, and monitoring and reporting.
