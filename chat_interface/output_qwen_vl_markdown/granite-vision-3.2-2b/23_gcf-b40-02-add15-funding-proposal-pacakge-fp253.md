## Project Programme title: Greening Financial Systems: Delivering Climate Finance for All

## Funding Proposal

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) is committed to mobilizing $10 billion annually from the private sector and public donors to support climate change mitigation, adaptation, and financing in developing countries. This funding proposal outlines a project titled "Greening Financial Systems: Delivering Climate Finance for All," which aims to strengthen financial systems and promote access to climate finance for all countries.

## Project Description

The project will focus on the following key areas:

1. Strengthening financial sector capacity: The project will provide training and technical assistance to financial institutions, including banks, insurance companies, and investment firms, to enhance their understanding of climate change risks and opportunities, as well as their ability to manage climate-related risks and investments.
2. Promoting access to climate finance: The project will work with financial institutions to develop and implement innovative products and services that enable them to provide climate finance to developing countries. This may include green bonds, blended finance instruments, and other innovative financing mechanisms.
3. Enhancing transparency and accountability: The project will work with financial institutions to improve the transparency and accountability of their climate finance activities, including the use of climate risk disclosure standards and the establishment of independent climate risk registers.
4. Fostering public-private partnerships: The project will facilitate the development of public-private partnerships that enable financial institutions to provide climate finance to developing countries while also promoting sustainable economic growth and job creation.

## Expected Outcomes

The expected outcomes of this project include:

1. Strengthened capacity of financial institutions to manage climate-related risks and investments, leading to increased access to climate finance for developing countries.
2. Development of innovative products and services that enable financial institutions to provide climate finance to developing countries.
3. Improved transparency and accountability of financial institutions' climate finance activities, including the use of climate risk disclosure standards and the establishment of independent climate risk registers.
4. Fostering of public-private partnerships that enable financial institutions to provide climate finance to developing countries while also promoting sustainable economic growth and job creation.

## Budget

The estimated budget for this project is $10 billion over a five-year period, with the following breakdown:

* Year 1: Capacity building and training of financial institutions (25% of total budget)
* Year 2: Development of innovative products and services (37.5% of total budget)
* Year 3: Promotion of access to climate finance (25% of total budget)
* Year 4: Enhancement of transparency and accountability (25% of total budget)
* Year 5: Fostering of public-private partnerships (12.5% of total budget)

## Conclusion

The Green Climate Fund is committed to mobilizing $10 billion annually from the private sector and public donors to support climate change mitigation, adaptation, and financing in developing countries. This funding proposal outlines a project titled "Greening Financial Systems: Delivering Climate Finance for All," which aims to strengthen financial systems and promote access to climate finance for all countries. The expected outcomes of this project include strengthened capacity of financial institutions to manage climate-related risks and investments, development of innovative products and services that enable financial institutions to provide climate finance to developing countries, improved transparency and accountability of financial institutions' climate finance activities, and fostering of public-private partnerships that enable financial institutions to provide climate finance to developing countries while also promoting sustainable economic growth and job creation.

# Female participation in the labour force

## Key indicators: Gender gaps in the economy and financial sector

In terms of employment, around half of Uzbek women participate in the labour force, and there is a significant gender gap of 35% in labour force participation in 2020 [1]. Women employment is concentrated in certain industries such as education, health care, and social work. While there are no restrictions on women's employment, women are more likely to be employed in these sectors than men.

## Gender gap in wages

The gender gap in wages is 26% of the total wage gap between men and women [1]. This means that, on average, women earn 74 cents for every dollar earned by men. The gender gap in wages is higher among women with lower levels of education and employment status.

## Gender gap in economic activity

Women's participation in the labour force has been increasing over time, but it still lags behind men's participation [1]. Women are more likely to be involved in informal economic activities such as housekeeping and childcare, which can limit their access to formal employment opportunities.

## Gender gap in political representation

Women make up 26% of the Uzbek parliament, but they hold only 10% of the seats [1]. This means that women are underrepresented in political decision-making positions.

## Gender gap in access to education and healthcare

Women have made significant progress in accessing education and healthcare, but there is still a gender gap in these areas [1]. Women are more likely to drop out of school than men, and they face barriers to accessing quality healthcare services.

## Gender gap in entrepreneurship

Women's participation in the labour force has been increasing over time, but women still face significant barriers to entrepreneurship [1]. Women are more likely to be involved in informal economic activities such as small-scale business ownership.

## Gender gap in access to credit and financial services

Women have made progress in accessing credit and financial services, but there is still a gender gap in these areas [1]. Women are less likely to have access to formal banking services than men.

## Conclusion

The gender gap in the labour force, wages, economic activity, political representation, education and healthcare, entrepreneurship, and access to credit and financial services is significant and persistent. Addressing these gaps requires a multi-faceted approach that includes policies to promote women's participation in the labour force, improve their access to education and healthcare, and address barriers to entrepreneurship and access to credit and financial services.
