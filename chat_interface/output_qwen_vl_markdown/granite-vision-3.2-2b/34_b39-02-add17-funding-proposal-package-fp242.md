## Green Climate Funding Proposal V3.0 | Page 48

### Introduction

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) is a global financial mechanism established under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). The GCF aims to mobilize $100 billion per year from developed and developing countries by 2020 to support climate change mitigation, adaptation, and financing. This proposal outlines a funding proposal for the Green Climate Fund under the GCF's V3.0 framework.

## Objectives

The primary objectives of this funding proposal are:

1. To mobilize $100 billion per year from developed and developing countries by 2020 to support climate change mitigation, adaptation, and financing.
2. To ensure that the funding is used effectively and efficiently to achieve the GCF's objectives.
3. To promote transparency and accountability in the use of GCF funds.
4. To support the development and implementation of climate-resilient infrastructure and technologies.
5. To enhance the capacity of developing countries to adapt to and mitigate the impacts of climate change.

## Funding Sources

The funding sources for this proposal are:

1. The Green Climate Fund (GCF) itself, which is a global financial mechanism established under the UNFCCC.
2. Additional contributions from developed and developing countries through bilateral agreements and other mechanisms.
3. Voluntary contributions from private sector entities, civil society organizations, and other stakeholders.

## Funding Allocation

The funding will be allocated as follows:

The Dom Rep has made significant progress in promoting financial inclusion, but there are still challenges to overcome. The Dom Rep has implemented several policies and laws to promote financial inclusion. The National Bank of the Dom Rep (Banco Nacional de la República) was established in 1985 to regulate and supervise banks, ensuring that they operate in a fair and transparent manner. The Banco also provides financial education and training programs to help individuals and businesses understand the importance of financial inclusion.

The Dom Rep has also implemented policies to promote gender equality in the financial sector. For example, the government has established the National Council for Women's Economic Empowerment (CONWE) to promote women's economic empowerment and access to credit. The council provides training and mentoring programs to help women start their own businesses and access credit.

## Challenges and Opportunities

Despite the progress made, there are still significant challenges to overcome in promoting financial inclusion in the Dom Rep. One of the main challenges is gender-based discrimination, which limits women's access to credit and financial services. To address this challenge, the government has implemented policies to promote gender equality in the financial sector.

Another challenge is limited access to formal financial institutions, particularly for rural and marginalized communities. The government has implemented policies to promote financial inclusion by providing access to microfinance institutions (MFIs) and other alternative finance providers. These institutions offer credit and savings products to individuals who do not have access to traditional banking services.

## Conclusion

Financial inclusion is a critical component of economic development and poverty reduction in developing countries, including the Dom Rep. While there are significant challenges to overcome, the government has implemented policies and initiatives to promote financial inclusion. By addressing gender-based discrimination, promoting access to formal financial institutions, and providing training and mentoring programs, the Dom Rep can continue to make progress in promoting financial inclusion.
