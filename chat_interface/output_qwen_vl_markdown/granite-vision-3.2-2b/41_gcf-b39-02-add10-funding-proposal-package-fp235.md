### Funding Proposal

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) is pleased to announce the funding proposal for the project titled "Mangroves for climate Public, Private and Community Partnerships for Mitigation and Adaptation in Ecuador." This project aims to support the development of mangrove ecosystems as a natural and sustainable solution to mitigate and adapt to the impacts of climate change.

### Project Description

Mangroves are coastal wetlands that provide numerous ecosystem services, including carbon sequestration, flood protection, and habitat for a diverse range of species. However, mangrove ecosystems are under threat from human activities such as deforestation, pollution, and climate change. This project aims to support the development of mangrove ecosystems in Ecuador through partnerships between public, private, and community organizations.

The project will focus on the following key areas:

1. **Research and Monitoring**: Conduct research and monitoring activities to assess the current state of mangrove ecosystems in Ecuador and identify potential threats and opportunities for mitigation and adaptation.
2. **Capacity Building**: Provide training and capacity building programs for local communities, government agencies, and private sector organizations to enhance their understanding of mangrove ecosystems and the importance of conservation efforts.
3. **Policy Development**: Develop and implement policies and regulations that support the conservation and sustainable use of mangrove ecosystems in Ecuador.
4. **Community Engagement**: Engage local communities in the development and implementation of mangrove conservation projects, providing them with opportunities for economic benefits and environmental protection.
5. **Monitoring and Evaluation**: Establish a monitoring and evaluation framework to track the progress of the project and ensure that it is achieving its objectives.

### Budget

The total budget for this project is $10 million, which will be allocated as follows:

- Research and Monitoring: $2 million
- Capacity Building: $3 million
- Policy Development: $2 million
- Community Engagement: $2 million
- Monitoring and Evaluation: $1 million

### Conclusion

The Green Climate Fund is committed to supporting the development of mangrove ecosystems in Ecuador through partnerships between public, private, and community organizations. This project aims to support the conservation and sustainable use of mangrove ecosystems, providing numerous ecosystem services and benefits for local communities and the environment.

#### 8.1. Climate context (max. 500 words, approximatley 2 pages)

Ecuador's complex coastal geography comprises 3,631 km of coastline, which is home to seven million people and plays a central role in Ecuador's economy. Guayaquil, the country's capital, is located on the Pacific coast and is one of the most populous cities in South America. The city is also an important center for both export-oriented industries and local livelihoods by providing habitat to a wide range of species and maintaining healthy coastal zones. Coastal areas are where a significant portion of Ecuador's biodiversity is found, including many endemic species.

Economically, the coastal regions of Ecuador are important for fishing, tourism, and agriculture. The country has a long history of coastal fishing, with the Pacific Ocean providing a rich source of seafood for both local consumption and export. Tourism is also an important sector in the region, with popular destinations including the Galapagos Islands, the Andes Mountains, and the coastal cities of Quito and Manaus. Agriculture is also significant in the region, with crops such as coffee, sugarcane, and vegetables being grown along the coast.

The coastal regions of Ecuador are also important for their role in climate change mitigation and adaptation. The country has a high vulnerability to the impacts of climate change, including sea level rise, increased frequency and intensity of storms, and changes in precipitation patterns. However, the coastal regions of Ecuador are also important for their role in carbon sequestration and other climate-related processes. For example, the mangrove forests along the Pacific coast provide a natural barrier against storm surges and help to reduce the impacts of sea level rise on coastal communities.

The Green Climate Fund is committed to supporting projects that address the climate change challenges facing Ecuador's coastal regions. The fund will prioritize projects that are focused on reducing greenhouse gas emissions, enhancing resilience to climate change impacts, and promoting sustainable development in these regions.

# Independent Technical Advisory Panel's assessment of FP235

## I. Assessment of the Independent Technical Advisory Panel

### 1.1 Overview

The Mangroves for climate Public, Private and Community Partnerships for Mitigation and Adaptation in Ecuador project is a cross-cutting project aimed at strengthening the resilience of Ecuador's coastal areas to the migratory impacts of climate change. This small-scale project will bring together the public, private, and community sectors to develop and implement strategies for mitigating and adapting to climate change in Ecuador.

### 1.2 Project Interventions

The project interventions are centered around three main components:

(a) Community empowerment: Enhance the management and restoration of mangroves through community-based initiatives, such as training programs for local communities on sustainable use of mangrove resources.

(b) Private sector engagement: Mobilize the shrimp farming sector to adopt and finance sustainable practices that enhance the resilience of mangroves to climate change impacts.

(c) Create and enabling environment: Develop and enforce policies that integrate mangrove conservation into broader climate strategies, supported by improved monitoring and evaluation systems.

### 1.3 Project Execution

The execution of the proposed project is expected to protect and manage 156,635 ha of mangroves, reduce GHG emissions by 4.6 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent through the restoration of degraded mangrove forests, and create jobs in Ecuador's coastal communities.

### 1.4 Budget

The total budget for the proposed project is USD 45.9 million. A substantial portion of this budget will be provided by Ecuador's Ministry of the Environment, Water and Ecological Transition (Ministerio del Ambiente, Agua y Transición del Ecuador (MAATE)) and Conservation.

### 1.5 Project Duration

The project is expected to run for five years, from 2023 to 2027.
