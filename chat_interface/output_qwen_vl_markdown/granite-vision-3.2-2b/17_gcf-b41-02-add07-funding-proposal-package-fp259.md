## Re: Funding proposal for the GCF by Conservation International regarding "Adapting to climate change" in Pacific island communities and economies

Dear Sir/Madam,

We are writing to express our views on the funding proposal submitted by Conservation International (CIF) for the Green Climate Fund ("GCF") under the programme titled "Adapting to climate change" in Pacific island communities and economies.

## Background

The GCF aims to support projects that contribute to reducing greenhouse gas emissions and enhancing adaptation to climate change, particularly in vulnerable regions such as Pacific island communities. The proposed project by CIF focuses on the development of a national climate change adaptation plan for Tonga, which aligns with the GCF's objectives.

## Our Position

While we appreciate the efforts made by Conservation International to address climate change in Pacific island communities, we have some concerns regarding the proposed funding proposal. Specifically, we believe that the following points should be considered:

1. **Eligibility Criteria**: The eligibility criteria for the GCF programme are not clearly defined in the proposed funding proposal. We recommend that the proposal include a detailed description of the eligibility criteria to ensure transparency and fairness in the selection process.
2. **Project Scope**: The scope of the proposed project is not explicitly stated in the funding proposal. We suggest that the proposal should provide a clear outline of the project's objectives, activities, and expected outcomes to facilitate evaluation and monitoring.
3. **Budget Allocation**: The budget allocation for the proposed project is not specified in the funding proposal. We recommend that the proposal include a detailed breakdown of the costs associated with the project, including personnel, equipment, travel, and other expenses.
4. **Monitoring and Evaluation**: The monitoring and evaluation framework for the proposed project is not explicitly stated in the funding proposal. We suggest that the proposal should include a clear description of the indicators used to measure the project's progress and impact.
5. **Transparency and Accountability**: The funding proposal does not provide information on how the funds will be allocated or spent, which raises concerns about transparency and accountability. We recommend that the proposal include a detailed breakdown of the proposed expenditures and a plan for monitoring and evaluating their effectiveness.

## Conclusion

We appreciate the efforts made by Conservation International to address climate change in Pacific island communities, but we have some concerns regarding the funding proposal submitted to the GCF. We recommend that the proposal include a detailed description of the eligibility criteria, project scope, budget allocation, monitoring and evaluation framework, and transparency and accountability measures.

We look forward to your response and hope that you will consider our views in the selection process for the GCF programme.

# Green Space Fund

## Introduction

The Green Space Fund (GSF) is a program designed to promote sustainable development and environmental conservation in developing countries. The GSF aims to support projects that enhance access to green spaces, improve air quality, and foster community engagement in environmental stewardship.

## Program Overview

The GSF supports various programs and initiatives that focus on the following key areas:

1. **Access to Green Spaces**: The program aims to increase public access to green spaces, such as parks, gardens, and urban forests, in underserved communities. This includes the development of community-led green space projects and the provision of funding for infrastructure improvements.
2. **Air Quality Improvement**: The GSF supports projects that improve air quality by promoting the use of clean energy sources, such as solar and wind power, and reducing emissions from transportation and industry. This includes the development of renewable energy projects and the implementation of green building standards.
3. **Community Engagement**: The program aims to foster community engagement in environmental stewardship by supporting grassroots initiatives that promote sustainable practices, such as waste reduction, recycling, and conservation. This includes the provision of funding for community-led projects and the development of educational programs.
4. **Climate Change Adaptation**: The GSF supports projects that enhance the resilience of communities to climate change impacts, such as sea level rise, drought, and extreme weather events. This includes the development of early warning systems, disaster risk reduction strategies, and climate-resilient infrastructure.
5. **Economic Development**: The program aims to support projects that promote economic development through sustainable use of natural resources, such as forestry, fisheries, and agriculture. This includes the provision of funding for sustainable livelihoods programs and the development of green business models.

## Funding and Partnerships

The GSF is funded through a combination of government grants, private sector investments, and international donations. The program partners with various organizations, including non-governmental organizations (NGOs), private companies, and community groups, to support its programs and initiatives.

## Conclusion

The Green Space Fund is a critical tool for promoting sustainable development and environmental conservation in developing countries. By supporting projects that enhance access to green spaces, improve air quality, foster community engagement, adapt to climate change impacts, and promote economic development, the GSF aims to create more livable, resilient, and sustainable communities around the world.

Women are often more vulnerable to these impacts due to their roles in resource management, food security, and economic activities.

2. **Gender Equity in Adaptation Strategies**: Gender equity is a critical component of effective adaptation strategies. The assessment highlights the importance of involving women in decision-making processes related to climate change adaptation. This includes access to resources, leadership positions, and knowledge sharing.

3. **Best Practices for Gender Equity in Climate Change Adaptation**: Several best practices have been identified for promoting gender equity in climate change adaptation. These include:
   - Incorporating gender perspectives in climate change assessments and planning.
   - Providing training and capacity building programs for women in climate change adaptation.
   - Establishing community-based organizations that empower women to participate in decision-making processes.
   - Ensuring equal access to resources, such as funding, technology, and information.

4. **Challenges and Opportunities**: The assessment identifies several challenges related to gender equity in climate change adaptation, including limited access to education and training, cultural barriers, and economic constraints. However, it also highlights opportunities for leveraging women's knowledge, skills, and experiences to enhance adaptation efforts.

## Access to Water

This issue is particularly prevalent in rural areas and among marginalized groups such as women, children, and the elderly.

## Gender Based Violence and Access to Water

GBV can have a significant impact on women's access to water. For example, women may be forced to travel long distances to collect water, which can expose them to violence and exploitation. ## Addressing Gender Based Violence and Access to Water

To address the intersection of gender based violence and access to water, it is important to adopt a holistic approach that addresses both issues simultaneously. This can include:

1. Providing education and awareness programs on GBV and its impact on women's access to water.
2. Implementing policies and programs that promote gender equality and provide women with equal access to resources such as water.
3. Supporting community-based initiatives that address the root causes of GBV, such as poverty and social exclusion.
4. Encouraging men's participation in gender equality efforts and challenging harmful gender stereotypes.

## Conclusion

Gender based violence is a complex issue that requires a comprehensive approach to address its root causes and provide support to those affected. By addressing the intersection of GBV and access to water, we can work towards creating a more equitable and just society for all.
