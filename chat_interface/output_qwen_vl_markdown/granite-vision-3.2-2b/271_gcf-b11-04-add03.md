## 1. Introduction

This document provides a detailed description of the Green Climate Fund (GCF) project, titled "Green Climate Solutions for Smallholder Farmers in Sub-Saharan Africa." The project aims to support smallholder farmers in developing countries by providing them with access to climate-smart technologies and practices that will help them adapt to the impacts of climate change.

## 2. Project Objectives

The primary objective of this project is to develop and implement a set of climate-smart agricultural practices that will improve the productivity, resilience, and sustainability of smallholder farms in Sub-Saharan Africa. The project aims to achieve the following objectives:

1. To provide smallholder farmers with access to climate-smart technologies and practices that will help them adapt to the impacts of climate change.
2. To improve the productivity, resilience, and sustainability of smallholder farms in Sub-Saharan Africa.
3. To support the development of a market for climate-resilient agricultural products and services.
4. To promote the integration of climate change considerations into national policies and programs.
5. To enhance the capacity of smallholder farmers, farm extension workers, and other stakeholders to access and use climate-smart technologies and practices.

## 3. Scope and Deliverables

The project will focus on the following areas:

1. Climate Change Assessment and Adaptation Planning
2. Development of Climate-Smart Technologies and Practices
3. Capacity Building for Smallholder Farmers
4. Market Development and Promotion
5. Policy and Programmatic Support
6. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning

The project will deliver the following outputs:

1. A comprehensive assessment of the climate change impacts on smallholder farms in Sub-Saharan Africa.
2. A set of climate-smart technologies and practices that will be adapted to the needs of smallholder farmers in Sub-Saharan Africa.
3. Training materials for smallholder farmers, farm extension workers, and other stakeholders on the use of climate-smart technologies and practices.
4. Market development plans and strategies for the promotion of climate-resilient agricultural products and services.
5. Policy and programmatic support for the implementation of the project.
6. Monitoring, evaluation, and learning reports on the progress and impacts of the project.

## 4. Methodology

The project will use a participatory approach that involves smallholder farmers, farm extension workers, other stakeholders, and policymakers in the design, implementation, and monitoring of the project. The project will be conducted in collaboration with local partners, including governments, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), and private sector companies.

The project will use a combination of field research, laboratory testing, and modeling to assess the climate change impacts on smallholder farms in Sub-Saharan Africa and to develop climate-smart technologies and practices that will be adapted to the needs of smallholder farmers in Sub-Saharan Africa. The project will also use a participatory approach to engage with stakeholders and to build their capacity to access and use climate-smart technologies and practices.
