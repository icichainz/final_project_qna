## Ocean acidification
Ocean acidification is measured based on the ocean aragonite saturation state. Aragonite is the most common form of calcium carbonate found in tropical reefs and is used as a bulk marine raw material for steelstone, including concrete. Increasing ocean acidification leads to decreasing aragonite saturation. The best conditions for coral to grow is at an aragonite saturation state above 4; below 3, the conditions to grow coral are critically limited (1)

## Ocean acidification and deoxygenation. The projected increase in atmospheric CO₂ concentration is expected to lead to a decrease in the pH of the oceans (pH < 8.3) by 2100 under the RCP6.5 scenario, which represents the highest emissions scenario. This will result in a significant reduction in the capacity of marine organisms to build and maintain their shells and skeletons, leading to a decrease in biodiversity and ecosystem functioning.

## Projected changes in the tropical Pacific Ocean anoxic zone saturation state were also estimated under the low-emissions scenario (B1). The results indicated that:

* Magnoliade and rate of ocean acidification is proportional to atmospheric CO₂ concentration.
* Anoxia saturation state from 2051 – 2100 will decline in the entire Pacific island region, with the greatest changes observed under the highest emissions scenario.
* The lowest anoxia saturation state values are to occur in the eastern equatorial Pacific, to the east of the equator, and in the South Equatorial Current region, approximately between 5°S and 20°S (Cook Islands and Tuvalu).
