The MARENA program focuses on three main areas:
1. **Marine Protected Areas (MPAs):** The establishment and management of MPAs to protect marine ecosystems and biodiversity.
2. **Sustainable Fisheries:** The promotion of sustainable fishing practices and the development of alternative livelihoods for coastal communities.
3. **Marine Pollution Control:** The reduction of marine pollution through the implementation of waste management systems and the promotion of clean technologies.

1. **Needs Assessment:** A comprehensive assessment of the marine environment and biodiversity in the target countries is conducted to identify the most pressing conservation challenges.
2. **Policy Development:** Based on the needs assessment, policies are developed to support the implementation of the MARENA program. These policies may include the establishment of MPAs, the promotion of sustainable fisheries practices, and the reduction of marine pollution.
3. **Capacity Building:** Capacity building programs are implemented to strengthen the institutional capacity of target countries to manage and conserve their marine resources.
4. **Monitoring and Evaluation:** Regular monitoring and evaluation of the program's progress is conducted to ensure that it is achieving its objectives and making a positive impact on the marine environment and biodiversity.

### Conclusion
The MARENA program is an important initiative undertaken by the GCF to support marine conservation efforts in developing countries. The Green Climate Fund project described in this document aims to restore degraded lands by planting native vegetation and improving soil health in Nicaragua. The project will involve the use of local materials and traditional farming techniques, as well as the involvement of local communities in the planning and implementation process. The project has several objectives, including enhancing resilience to climate change impacts, promoting sustainable development, and supporting local communities. The project budget is $50 million, with the following breakdown: $20 million for landscape assessment and planning, $30 million for planting native vegetation and improving soil health, $10 million for monitoring and evaluation, and $5 million for capacity building and knowledge sharing. The project will be implemented over a period of five years, with the following timeline: Phase 1: Landscape assessment and planning - Year 1, Phase 2: Planting native vegetation and improving soil health - Year 2, Phase 3: Monitoring and evaluation - Year 3, Phase 4: Capacity building and knowledge sharing - Year 4, Project completion - Year 5.
