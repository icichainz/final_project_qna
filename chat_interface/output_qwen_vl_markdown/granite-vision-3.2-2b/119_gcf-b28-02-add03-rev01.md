## Introduction

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) is a global initiative that aims to mobilize $100 billion annually by 2020 to support climate action in developing countries. This funding proposal outlines the GCF's approach to financing projects that address climate change and promote sustainable development.

## Financial and Economic Criteria and Analysis

At a high level, all ACDF (African Climate Fund) GCP (Green Climate Project) projects will be financially viable, after support from the program. The ACDF Investment Criteria are designed to ensure that projects meet certain financial and economic standards. These criteria are expected to be reflected in a cross-a bankability threshold (CBT) that is used to assess the feasibility of each project.

The CBT is based on several factors, including the estimated investment and operations costs, as well as cash inflows. The estimated investment and operations costs are calculated using a realistic approach, taking into account the potential risks and uncertainties associated with climate change projects. Cash inflows are also considered, as they represent the revenue that can be generated from the project over its lifetime.

The CBT is expected to be used to assess the feasibility of each project and to identify those that meet the financial and economic criteria for financing. Projects that do not meet these criteria will not be eligible for funding.

## Financial Analysis

All projects will also benefit from ADB's (Asian Development Bank) Financial Management and Analysis of Projects guidelines, which provide a framework for conducting financial analysis of projects. These guidelines are expected to help ensure that projects are financially viable and that the GCF is able to make informed decisions about which projects to fund.

## Conclusion

The Green Climate Fund's approach to financing projects is based on several key principles, including the use of financial and economic criteria to assess project feasibility, the use of a cross-a bankability threshold to identify eligible projects, and the use of ADB's guidelines for conducting financial analysis. These principles are expected to help ensure that the GCF is able to mobilize the necessary funding to support climate action in developing countries.
