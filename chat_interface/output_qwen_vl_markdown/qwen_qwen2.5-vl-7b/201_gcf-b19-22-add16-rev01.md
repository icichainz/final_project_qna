The Project Development Objective (PDO) of the proposed project is to strengthen the adaptive capacity and climate resilience of communities and economic activities in areas of infrastructure development, including hydromet services, through the development of climate-resilient pathways. Bufuka Faso already experiences large weather and climate variability. Extreme weather and climatic events are also frequent. The project will address this by strengthening the adaptive capacity and climate resilience of communities and economic activities in areas of infrastructure development. In areas of infrastructure development, hydromet information can inform the design of projects that are more resilient to extreme weather events and climate change. Private companies and investors will benefit from better infrastructure development related to water management for their operations.

Private companies and investors will benefit from better infrastructure development related to water management for their operations.

# C1. Strategic Context

## Sub-Saharan Africa – Regional Context

Africa accounts for a very small fraction of the greenhouse gas emissions, but it is bearing the brunt of a disproportionate share of the effects of climate variability and volatility. It is currently experiencing temperatures in some parts that range from below 2°C to above 40°C, which is a very substantial risk of heat-related damages. In Sub-Saharan Africa, the Sahel region is among the most vulnerable hot spots from a climate and disaster risk perspective.

Stretching from Mauritania to Sudan, the Sahel has faced repeated drought with the most severe occurring during the 1960s and early 1970s. The region is characterized by high vulnerability due to its exposure to extreme climatic conditions, poor accessibility to food, high grain prices, environmental degradation and displacement of populations. The region also experiences frequent flooding in some parts of the Sahel, especially during heavy rains. The countries' economies are highly exposed to climate risks due to their dependency on agriculture, which accounts for a large share of GDP (15% in Burkina Faso), employment (60%), food security and export earnings.

The African Hydro-Meteorological Framework Programme, which is jointly developed by the African Development Bank (AfDB), World Meteorological Organization (WMO) and World Bank Group, responds to this challenge supporting the most vulnerable countries in Sub-Saharan Africa to build their capacity for climate change monitoring and early warning systems. The African Hydro-Meteorological Framework Programme aims at improving the resilience of communities against climate change and increasing the overall resilience to climate risks within the Sahel region Burkina Faso plays a central role due to its geographical location, which makes it vulnerable to droughts and floods.

### Country Context – Burkina Faso

Background
Burkina Faso is a landlocked country located in the middle of the West African Sahel region and occupying over 274,000 square kilometers. Located in the transition zone between the Sahara Desert to the north and the humid coastal areas at the Gulf of Guinea, Burkina Faso is prone to chronic drought, flash floods, windstorms, and disease outbreaks. With limited rainfall (150-300 mm per year), poor soils, and a population density of 84 people per square kilometer, Burkina Faso contributes roughly a third of Burkina Faso's GDP. The country's soils tend to be poor in nutrients, have low water-holding capacity, and are highly susceptible to erosion. Any reduction or delay in rainfall results in reduced crop yields and supplies/yields are immediately affected. The country suffers from 'quasi-drought conditions' since the early 1970s.

# Detailed Project / Programme Description

## Green Climate Fund (GCF)

From the Mouhoun River (Black Volta) in the western part of the country rivers are intermittent. The impacts of climate change are projected to increase both the frequency and severity of these events.

As a result of this fragility, Burkina Faso remains at the bottom of the UN Human Development Index, ranking 162 out of the 187 countries in 2013. The country is also one of the most vulnerable to climate change with 45% of its population living on less than $1 a day and 61.7% of the population is at risk of multiple hazards.

Measures taken by the government to increase resilience to climate variation have started, but remain local and small scale. The improving of weather and climate forecast capacity, the development of early warning systems, encompassing both meteorological and hydrological aspects, are among these measures. These measures aim to mitigate impacts of current climate variability and better adapt to climate change. It is predicted that the expected changes in climate variability and average conditions will further exacerbate climate impacts, particularly on rural and poorest populations.

### Climate and Disaster Risk Profile

Due to its geographical position, Burkina Faso is characterized by a dry tropical climate, which alternates between a short rainy season (June–September) and a long dry season. Burkina Faso's climate is prone to strong seasonal and annual variation due to its location in the hinterland and its pronounced location in the Sahel belt. The country has three climatic zones: the Sahelian zone in the north with an average annual rainfall between 500mm and 600mm; the Sudanese zone in the center, which has an average annual rainfall between 600mm and 900mm; and the southern Sudanese zone in the south with an average annual rainfall of more than 1200mm.

In Burkina Faso, droughts and floods are the most severe hazards. Since 1989 Burkina Faso has experienced several major droughts and floods. During the period 1963 to 2014, droughts affected a cumulative number of 12.4 million people including livestock in 57 out of 134 districts. The effects of droughts have become more pronounced over time with regular impacts on production. ## Project Scope
The project design is based on two main principles: (i) Investments need to be at scale to support and sustain national hydro-meteorological services, preparedness and response; and (ii) Support needs to be targeted and provided end-to-end improved hydro-meteorological services as well as disaster response along the entire "information value chain" from weather and climate information to early warning systems, preparedness and response capacity. The project will further target its finance towards the additional costs of improved weather and climate information services, preparedness and response capacity.

Lessons learnt from projects and programs targeted at strengthening weather and climate services indicate that support has in the past often been scattered, e.g., by procuring a number of automatic weather stations or building capacity within a specific sector. Such approaches have not always led to sustainable results as they do not address the entire service these improvements and receive the required recognition from government and beneficiaries. While in the past many projects have focused on improving weather and climate services, the overall institutional framework is reinforced and service delivery has successfully improved. The Africa Hydro-Met Program therefore targets its project design (i) at scale to support and sustain national hydro-meteorological services, preparedness and response at scale; and (ii) by supporting sustainable weather and climate service improvements.

### D.1. Value Added for GCF Involvement

Please specify why the GCF involvement is critical for the project/programme, in consideration of other alternatives.

GCF support for the Burkina Faso Hydromet Modernization and Strengthening Project under the Africa Hydromet Program is crucial. Most NMRSs in Africa are unable to meet the basic needs for weather and climate information. Less than 20% of African countries have a national meteorological service (NMS) that provides reliable, timely, and accurate weather forecasts and early warning services, which are critical for disaster risk reduction, food security, health, and economies. A recent WMO survey shows that 54 percent of the surface and 71 percent of the upper air weather stations in Africa are not operational or have been abandoned due to lack of funding, maintenance, and skilled personnel.

The investment needs for system-wide modernization of hydromet services in Sub-Saharan Africa are substantial, and also at the core of building climate adaptation capacity as well as climate-resilient development. Funding from governments and agencies is often insufficient. In addition, it pays limited attention to the system architecture and sustainability aspects of NMRSs. Meeting operating budget constraints remains a challenge, despite the benefits provided by hydromet and early warning services. Even as the cost-benefit ratio of investing in hydromet services is high, the lack of investment in capacity building and end-user connectivity has been a major obstacle for effective transformative change, is difficult for most African governments.

Coordination, sustainability and last-mile connectivity are major challenges in the optimal performance of NMRSs and EWS. The limited availability of trained personnel and the poor maintenance of equipment have been maintained because of lack of trained staff and/or operating budget. Often, the support is small-scale and has a project approach, while the financing must be obtained from different sources. This makes it difficult to ensure sustainability.

Burkina Faso is facing the impacts of climate change. With projected increases in climate variability, and desertification, it is an urgent need for Burkina Faso to build its capacity in hydromet services. The country believes that the country takes urgent action to cope with shocks and stresses such as drought, flooding and food security.

Without GCF involvement, the Government of Burkina Faso cannot take adequate steps to help vulnerable communities and the population at large. It is also important for the country to ensure the sustainability of its hydromet services in terms of cost and adequacy of support to Burkina Faso for hydromet strengthening. Investment needs for system-wide modernization of hydromet services, capacity building and end-user connectivity are significant. The Government is struggling to set aside resources from its stretched national budget. Even as the cost of delivering climate-resilient development in Burkina Faso is high, it is not possible to meet these costs with the limited resources available at least developed country. Because funding is insufficient, it pays limited attention to the system architecture and sustainability aspects of national systems. Investments in climate information and early warning will have life-saving impacts on vulnerable populations.

As the project will yield adaptation benefits that reduce the impacts of floods and droughts (including secondary impacts on food security) on vulnerable populations, it is fully in line with GCF’s objective to invest in climate resilient development at the national level. The Project will also contribute to the achievement of Burkina Faso’s National Development Plan objectives: increasing productivity, the Project will directly support climate-resilient development. Investment in hydromet services is a “no regret” climate adaptation investment, particularly for a vulnerable LSC like Burkina Faso.

Production related vulnerability is among others often related to the dependency of women on natural resources (e.g. water, land, forests), which are subject to climate change impacts. Women also face barriers in accessing sectors, insecure or limited access to land as well as insurance products and services. Gender norms also influence agricultural practices and survival mechanisms such as food intake. Livestock, typically owned by women and youth, is sold first during times of crisis.

Violence is a universal phenomenon, however, it tends to spike in the context of natural disasters, domestic violence, and sexual violence. Women are more vulnerable than men when they lack access to information and when social structures experience failures.


Lack of agency is related to the understanding and perceiving of risks as well as coping and response strategies. Gender imbalance in level of education and literacy, limited rights and political representation, and limited access to social infrastructure. Limited rights and low social status for women limits their knowledge of risks.

Hydro-met services and effective early warning systems can play an important role to reduce the vulnerability of women and children to climate change impacts. The project will implement a gender action plan in line with the national policies of Burkina Faso. The project's gender actions are informed by the existing World Bank gender strategy, the Gender Action Plan (GAP) for Burkina Faso, the National Strategy on Gender Equality in Burkina Faso; Daham and Hanner, 2015: The Identification for Development (D4D) Agenda; Its Potential for Empowering Women and Girls in Burkina Faso, and the World Bank's Gender Action Plan (GAP) for Burkina Faso. It will also take into account the findings of the Gender Assessment Report on Climate Change and Water Resources Relations Towards Gender Equality in Burkina Faso, which will be updated during project preparation. It will make current gender information available to all stakeholders involved in the project design and implementation. Examples of relevant user needs include for example providing hydro-meteorological series and forecasting for women farmers communities, small women owned business as well as involving schools in hydro-met monitoring.

In preparation of the project, a gender focused assessment of the hydromet information users and beneficiaries' needs was conducted in the Sahel countries focusing notably on three axes of gender related vulnerability for key livelihood groups. The results will be used to inform the design of the project's gender action plan. A Gender Action Plan will be undertaken to ensure that early warning messages to women and vulnerable groups including disabled are targeted. The Gender Action Plan will be approved by the Board of the GCF in March 2017 upon approval of the proposal by the board of the GCF.

The project will support DGGE, DGMG, DGPC, SAP and CONSEUR to target and communicate weather forecasts and climate information to women and vulnerable groups. The dissemination of weather forecast and climate information is communicated through dedicated communication channels, which are relevant for women and men, and particularly for women in rural areas. This can be done by using mobile phones, radios, or by selling food from wood lots or forest houses gardens. Similarly, the project would support the government to better target their disaster response efforts towards women and vulnerable groups including disabled. The project will also provide training of food shelters, which are safe and secure for women or that contingency plans and women specific evacuation exercises will be developed. This will maximize the reach of early warning systems and minimize the potential negative impact of disasters on women.
