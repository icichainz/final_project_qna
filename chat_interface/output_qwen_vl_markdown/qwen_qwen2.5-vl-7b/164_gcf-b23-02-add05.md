A.2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

Please provide a brief description of the proposed project/programme, including the objectives and primary measurable benefits (see section E). The detailed description can be elaborated in section A.

According to Germainitch's 2018 Global Climate Risk Index, Honduras ranks first on the list of countries most affected by negative impacts from extreme climate-related events over the last two decades. In particular, the country was severely impacted by the 2016-2017 El Niño event, which triggered a severe Pine-bark Beetle outbreak that destroyed 480,000 Ha of pine forest (about 10% of the total national forest cover and 25% of coniferous forests). According to established climate models, the frequency of extreme events is expected to increase in Honduras over the next fifty years, increasing the probability of similar types of severe pine-bark beetle outbreaks. This is expected to impact water availability and food security for a large proportion of the Honduran population.

Models show that, if no measures are taken, the impacts of climate change by year 2050 will double the area affected by severe Pine-bark Beetle outbreaks in 29 basins which represent the main source for water supply to the country. This would result in a significant reduction in the availability of surface and groundwater by an estimated 40%, affecting the livelihoods of a large proportion of the Honduran population, especially groups that are highly dependent on environmental resources for their economic activities.

The project aims to address more acute issues like the called "environmental migration", both to other areas in Honduras and abroad. To avoid this scenario, the project main goal is to transform the management of coniferous forests from a reactive model to a proactive one. Agroforestry practices and adaptive forest management are critical areas for the provision of water services.

The project fosters a paradigm shift in forest management towards a model based on active participation of local communities and the private sector, the adoption of agroforestry practices, and the implementation of an integrated approach to better governance of the forest sector and its financial sustainability, shifting from a reactive model to a preventive one in the face of climate change.

The project will produce the following impacts:

- Increased water security, especially during the dry season, for 450,000 men and women in the central zone of Honduras with an improved resilience to climate change.
- Protection of 270,000 Ha of pine forests with adaptive forest management and an increased renewable resource base.
- Reduction of 10.5 million CO2e from poor forest and land use.
- Economic and social benefits for 27,000 households (male and female headed) due to the adoption of diversified livelihoods and improved access to alternative livelihood options.

The project is aligned with the framework of forest and climate change policies, including the National Development Plan, the Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC), the Water, Forest, and Soil Master Plan, and the REDD+ National Strategy, among others. Honduras has made significant progress in the preparation of the National REDD + Strategy; which has developed its participation in the Forest Carbon Partnership Facility (FCPF). This project will

### C.1. Strategic Context

1. In 2016, the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) of Honduras was approximately USD 21 billion, with a share of USD 2361 GDP per capita for the 9.1 million population. This makes Honduras the country with the highest poverty rate in Central America and the Caribbean. It is also one of the countries with the highest rate of poverty among 14 countries in Latin America and the Caribbean*. In addition to the high levels of widespread poverty, according to Germanwatch, Honduras was ranked as the third most vulnerable country on a list of the countries most affected by negative impacts from extreme climate-related events such as hurricanes, floods, droughts, and heat waves.

At the same time, it is the Central American country with the largest forest cover of 5.4 million Ha (ICF Forest Map, 2014), equivalent to 48% of the national territory. The forests in Honduras are of great ecological importance due to their location and capacity to generate key ecosystem services such as water for the largest part of the country's population. Due to the strategic importance of its forest resources, the Government of Honduras has committed to restore 1 million Ha of forest by 2030 in its National Forest Conservation (NFC) Law.

As many as 12 species of Dendroctonus bark beetles have been reported in Central America as well as several species of pine beetles of the genus Ips. Most economically important outbreaks are caused by Dendroctonus frontalis and Ips confusus. In July 2016, a new outbreak was detected of Dendroctonus beetles (Coleoptera: Curculionidae, Scolytinae), particularly those in the genus Dendroctonus, are important pests throughout the range of pine forests in Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Belize, and Nicaragua (Blings and Clarke).

Rain gauges and flow meter will be installed in the watersheds and real-time data will be collected. The data of rain gauges and flow meters will be used to estimate the water availability using the Water Assessment Tool that was used to design the program. The model will use the average number of curves improved as a result of project intervention, that explain the infiltration capacity of the soil. The average number of curves improved as a result of the project intervention will be utilized to model the increase in the amount of water available in all the area of intervention using SWAT. The results will be shared with the Local Councils and the population.

The monitoring of the ecosystem service of Tonnes of Carbon equivalent (CO2eq) avoided will be carried out by the Centro de Información y Patrimonio Forestal (CIPF) from ICF. The project will provide them training and financial support to measures the ecosystem service in the parcels in year 1-3, as well as the technical personnel for the social component of the project (component 2 of the project). After the end of the project the CIPF will continue monitoring the indicator, as part of its responsibility of monitoring the status of the forest and the carbon stock. The University of Granada has a specific budget for the technician personnel for satellite information analysis and the Forest Management Unit of the University of Granada is the unit responsible for monitoring emissions as part of the National REDD + Strategy, so that project measurements will be aligned with this strategy.

The monitoring of the ecosystem service of water regulation will be carried out through an agreement between the National Forestry University and the Francisco de Vitoria University. The agreement includes the installation of rain gauges and flow meters in the selected watersheds. When the WF will be constituted, the agreement will be extended to include the Francisco de Vitoria University to continue the monitoring and modelling as the water availability part of the social and research projects of the University.

In average, the income of salaried employed women and freelance is equivalent to the 92.2% and 92.9% of men's income, in 2007 and 2016, respectively.

Between 2009 and 2013, the informal employment rate of men decreased from 73% to 71.3%. In that same period, the percentage of women with the same type of employments increased from 74.8% to 75.6%.[Chart 4]

## Business sectors
In 2016, women were more represented in non-agricultural area activities, such as wholesale and retail trade (28.2%), as well as in community, social, and personal service activities (25.5%).[13], while their male peers were occupied in primary activities, such as agriculture, livestock, forestry, and fishery, mainly (41%), and in wholesale and retail trade (15.7%)[Chart 4].

Almost half of the businesses in Honduras are female-owned or the main partner is a woman.[13][14]. According to the IIB, in 2014 there were 137,330 small, medium and large enterprises (MIPYMES) from which 52% were female-owned or the main partner is a woman.
