# A2 Project / Programme Executive Summary

The Poverty Reduction, Energy and Climate Change Project (PROEZA) will improve the resilience of 30,000 households that are highly vulnerable to the impacts of climate change (CC) in 64 municipal districts located in eight administrative regions of Bolivia. The vulnerability of these communities is high due to their low adaptive capacity and exposure to CC. The overall vulnerability is rated as extremely high. For IPCC climatic scenarios and the baseline information on the impacts of CC on people's livelihoods and ecosystems (see Section C2), it is estimated that climatic variations would have significant effects on the agricultural sector, with a reduction in yields of 10-35% for maize, 40-60% for soybeans, and 70-80% reductions in productivity in crops such as corn, beans, cassava, sesame and sugarcane. Reduced productivity leads to a change in the food basket composition, with an increase of starchy staples and a decrease of fruits and vegetables, as well as social, environmental, economic, and cultural effects in the rural sector that can ultimately impact resilience to CC.

In the selected municipalities, most poor and extreme poor households receive support from the public social protection program (PROGRESA) and are therefore more vulnerable than other households. These communities have low agricultural sustainability. Furthermore, these rural households are highly dependent on their natural environment for energy and food security; most cook with firewood and depend on local water sources. PROEZA will provide these households with technical and financial support to establish agroforestry systems that will help them diversify their livelihoods by providing a source of income through the sale of timber, fruits, nuts, and other products from the trees in the system as well as firewood and valuable high-quality timber from native tree species. These systems will provide shade, conserve soil, store CO2 and regulate water flows, thus improving food security and reducing vulnerability to CC. The agroforestry systems will also help these communities reduce their dependence on firewood and highly valuable timber from native tree species to create an important long-term family asset. PROEZA will establish 10,564 ha of new agroforestry systems in the project area. These households will be supported by PROEZA to do this by introducing environmental incentive payments (E-CTC), conditioned on the successful establishment and care of the agroforestry production systems promoted by the project, and accompanied by technical and legal advice on how to manage these systems.

In addition to establishing new agroforestry systems, PROEZA will also establish 24,600 ha of CENIFAGROforestry systems through this innovative incentive system that blends a social protection program with environmental incentives. These households will be supported by PROEZA to do this by introducing environmental incentive payments (E-CTC), conditioned on the successful establishment and care of the agroforestry production systems promoted by the project, and accompanied by technical and legal advice on how to manage these systems.

To complement this and promote a holistic landscape approach to ensure CC resilience in target areas, PROEZA will promote the entry of small and medium land owners in the regional bioenergy market. To achieve this, concessional credit will be provided for the establishment of 24,000 ha of New Generation Forest Plantations (NGFP) that will include more than 8,800 ha of riparian protection forests to protect watercourses.

The above-mentioned interventions will be supported and sustained through PROEZA's strategic institutional interventions over a period of years, designed to generate a profound transformation. The Government plans a 10-year program for the implementation of the Forest Law (2015) that provides intensive institutional, legal aid and enforcement support to forest land use, environment and energy regulation entities to ensure compliance with project safeguards, environmental, land use and bioenergy regulations. After Year 6 onwards the Government will provide technical assistance to the project in the form of a dedicated team of forestry services regime to secure project institutionalization and sustainability. In this context, PROEZA's support is crucial to enabling the Government to implement its forest policy and to ensure that it has the capacity for effective planning and decision-making framework. The project itself will provide the testing grounds for this transformative change through its governance structure. Specifically, the six line agencies responsible for agriculture, forestry, social protection, indigenous peoples' rights, environment and energy regulation will be represented in a single institution that ensures inter-sectoral coordination, swift execution and transparent management of the project's interventions. These institutions will be supported by the Executive Committee (EC), chaired by the National Executive Secretary, the Executive Secretary of Planning (STP), FAO will provide the EC with technical assistance, project supervision, implementation and fiduciary support.

*PROEZA means "colossal achievement" in Spanish and emphasizes the importance of this project.
*FAO is the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations. It provides technical assistance to governments in developing countries for food security, agriculture, forestry, fisheries and rural development.
*Of 108,957 poor and extreme poor households surveyed by SAG in February 2017, 67% declared they have access to land. The average plot size estimated in the project is 3.8 hectares/tenant.

# C1. Biogeographic Context

## Country Context

Paraguay finds itself in a vicious cycle of poverty, unsustainable agriculture, forestry and energy practices that exacerbate vulnerability to CC.

### Socioeconomic Context

While poverty rates in Paraguay grew 22 percent between 2003 and 2011, extreme poverty remained persistently high. In 2011, three out of ten people were living below the poverty line and 48% could not afford the basic food basket (the extreme poor), placing the country among the poorest in Latin America. Since 2011, there have been substantial welfare improvements with poverty rates falling to 17 percent by 2013 and extreme poverty dropping from 48% to below 35%. The poverty rate dropped below 0.4% for the first time in the last fifteen years. However, two main challenges to the sustainability of poverty reduction are: (i) a large proportion of the population still lives on the edge of poverty; and (ii) although economic opportunities have expanded, a large share of the population still faces a volatile economic environment. In rural areas, more than two-thirds of the extreme poor live in households engaged in agriculture, a highly volatile sector. A sizeable proportion of those above the poverty line but under moderate poverty lines have incomes that are vulnerable to shocks and changes in the economy or climate shocks could push them back into poverty.

Nonetheless, especially for cash transfers through the social protection program "Sembrando Oportunidades", which includes Teleférico, Teletránsito, and Teleagro Misiones Magallanes accounts for one third of the decrease in rural poverty. The conditional cash transfer program, Teleférico, targets poor families with school aged children and offers a sizeable resource providing them with food assistance, health care services, education, and other social benefits. In 2013, Teleférico reached approximately 414,180 households, approximately 10% of the country's population. To stabilize vulnerable households economically and help them to escape poverty, the program provides cash transfers in exchange for school attendance and health care services. The program also aims at increasing the resilience of these households by increasing their productive, financial, human and social assets. These cash transfers may not be a major driver behind the change in the incidence of poverty but still are an important role in protecting households from falling into extreme poverty.

Paraguay's indigenous people and female-headed households continue to be the most vulnerable social groups in Paraguay. There are about 20 different indigenous ethnic groups comprising around 115,000 people (1.7% of the country's total population) living on or near the borders with Brazil and Argentina in the Chaco region and in the northernmost territories, only 2.5% have access to potable water and 31-36% to electricity. To address this, Paraguay’s National Development Fund (FONDEP) has been working since 2008 on a comprehensive strategy for indigenous peoples’ development that includes services (water, sanitation and health, decent housing and electricity), and ensuring legal titling of indigenous lands. Many of Paraguay's indigenous people live in remote areas with limited access to basic social services and infrastructure, and rely on their natural environment. It is these households who derive most of their economic and social benefits from the forests and land that are most vulnerable to environmental degradation and in need of most sustainable use options regarding the natural resources they depend on.

### Sectoral Context

Despite Paraguay's substantial production of hydropowerlectricity, nearly half (45%) of national energy consumption comes from fossil fuels. Paraguay’s expanding agricultural frontier and its heavy dependence on biofuels have contributed to globally high rates of deforestation, reducing native Atlantic Forests in Paraguay by 95% (see Fig E7 Deforestation risk in Annex IV). Firewood and charcoal are used to dry for grains, export produce, ceramic plants, and as the primary energy source in the southern part of the country. The use of firewood is also a major cause of deforestation. In addition, Paraguay has been identified as the primary energy source for 88% of rural households.

Paraguay's reliance on fossil fuels represents both an opportunity and a challenge. Paraguay will continue to rely heavily on non-renewable energy sources in the short term but is moving away from one that deflates native forests to one that relies exclusively on sustainable sources of wood from planted forests and sustainable management of natural forests. Paraguay is poised to become a leader in the development of renewable energy, with a number of projects already under way or planned for implementation. # C2: Project Programme Objective against Baseline

**Project Description:** The project aims to reduce poverty among poor households vulnerable to the impacts of CC and to increase the forest cover in environmentally sensitive areas of Eastern Paraguay.

The National Program to Reduce Extreme Poverty "Sombrales Oportunidades" has surveyed 360,002 households nationally. Of these, 15% are located within the environmental boundaries of Eastern Paraguay selected by PROEZA for their high social and environmental vulnerability (see Section C.3 in annex A). The vulnerability of the ecosystems and resources is directly related to the poverty level of the population living there.

The targeted poor households are classified as "poor" or "extreme poor", totaling 82,099 households. On average, 42% of these are female-headed households (see Section C.3 in annex A). The project will benefit 15,675 families from 10 municipalities and 1 Department. The Project's target beneficiaries consist of 30,000 of those poor and extremely poor rural households that are highly vulnerable to the impacts of CC in 46 municipal districts.

The data shows that 82% of the targeted poor households in the project area are highly dependent on the natural environment for their daily domestic needs and food security. Currently, 32% of the rural poor and extreme poor households depend on water wells, and 30% depend on other open water sources including rainwater for all domestic purposes. This makes them vulnerable to the impacts of CC such as droughts and floods.

An average of 95% of the poor and extreme poor households in the targeted Departments use firewood to cook their food and 84% use it for heating, lighting, and other household activities. The project will provide improved stoves that ensure that each family consumes around 12.5 kg of firewood per year and that poor households spend up to US$270/year to buy charcoal and firewood. This represents a significant reduction in the consumption of firewood by these families, which will bring about positive environmental and economic effects. PROEZA will also promote the establishment of multi-functional stoves for cooking and heating purposes as well as the use of alternative energy sources such as solar power or firewood surplus to local/regional biomass energy markets, as well as create an opportunity for financial savings and time for other productive activities. # D1. Value Added for GCF Involvement

## a. Accelerate Paraguay’s planned shift towards a low-emissions, sustainable development path.

Paraguay is poised to shift rapidly from depletion of native forests to a reliance on sustainable sources of fuelwood from planted trees and other renewable energy sources. This will be achieved through the implementation of a new program that creates substantial platform and clear delegation of authority to launch this fundamental transformation. With GCF support, Paraguay can accelerate its planned shift towards a low-emissions, sustainable development path by focusing on climate-resilient development. GCF support will catalyze this shift by supporting a change in incentives for market participants by reducing costs and risks, eliminating barriers to the deployment of climate-resilient solutions, thereby enabling a rapid transition.

## b. Larger scale of impact

GCF investments will enable Paraguay to implement this project (i) more efficiently through the proposed public/private execution and implementation arrangements, and (ii) on a larger scale than would be possible with existing resources. The project will benefit 105,839 families in the establishment of 24,460 ha of multifunctional, climate resilient planted forests (0.8 ha per family). The inclusion of concessional loan financing for medium and large landowners will allow the project to engage innovative approaches such as “climate-resilient agroforestry systems” or “sustainable forest management plantations” that protect both riparian zones and remnant native forests by shifting fuelwood demand toward sustainable sources. This will also provide proof of concept for a second much larger phase on investment in growing forests for bioenergy.

## c. Increased focus on the most vulnerable populations

The GCF grant resources will be directed to financing afforestation activities in areas of high social and environmental vulnerability. The GCF grant financing will be derived from the World Bank’s Global Environment Facility (GEF) Trust Fund, which has demonstrated that indigenous groups are more likely to reside (including indigenous and women-headed households). As reported in the Project Implementation Manual, the project will build upon and include steps already developed by INFORNA and STP to support implementation and MAE. Georeferencing of activities will be embedded since inception into the Project Management Manual and will be part of the M&E plan.

The project will use a Geographic Information System (GIS) for data collection, analysis, and reporting. This will be supported by PRODEA. - All georeferenced maps will be taken in a known projection with preference for WGS84 and in decimal degrees.

The full data set of coordinates and KMZ files will represent the geographical distribution and relevance of the Project's interventions on the project areas and will form the "Project's Implementation Atlas". The database will be managed by the M&E unit and made available to the Executive Committee and FAO at any given moment. Training will be provided by FAO to the M&E unit staff. All map outputs will be provided in digital format (shapefiles or equivalent) with the metadata and sources of information. All maps shall be reported precisely on Google Earth and delivered as well as KMZ/KML files formats.

In terms of reporting, the project will ensure annual reporting to the GCF according to GCF covariants, rules and standards. Reporting will consist of four phases:

- **Technical Reports (TRs) prepared by Service Providers**: TRs will describe implemented activities and involved beneficiaries. The PMU will review these reports and provide comments and information from the service providers will ensure Georeferencing of each executed activity and will present TRs on a quarterly basis to PMU.
- **Quarterly reports (QRs) prepared by the PMU**: QRs will present the work and achievements of the involved beneficiaries. The PMU will review these reports and provide comments and information from the beneficiaries and other involved stakeholders. QRs are prepared by the PMU and transmitted to GCF quarterly.
- **Annual reports (ARs) prepared by the Executive Committee and FAO**: ARs will present the work and achievements of the project, including a summary of the results achieved in terms of poverty alleviation and vulnerability reduction from the two supervision reports (SRs) prepared by FAO as part of their commitment to the project. ARs will also include the "Project's Implementation Atlas" — presenting the maps and charts obtained thanks to the georeferencing of activities, which will be provided by the M&E unit. The AR is reviewed and approved by the Executive Committee and FAO and are transmitted to the GCF by FAO. The annual report presented to GCF by FAO will also contain a clear and articulated replenishment plan.
