### A.2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

Please provide a brief description of the proposed project/programme, including the objectives and primary measurable benefits (see investment criteria in section E). The detailed description can be elaborated in Annex 1.

The project intersects several national strategies: the overarching Plan Manco Vice (Green Morocco Plan, GMP), the regional strategy for the development of the Boudouib Valley (BVDV), the National Water Strategy, and the new agriculture resilience and water preservation strategy. It is aligned with Morocco's adaptation strategy for 2020 as described in its national climate change plan.

The project area, the Boudouib Valley, is located in the semi-arid Tafilalet region (see map in Annex 1 and photos in Annex 2). In the South-East part of the Kingdom, characterized by a high vulnerability to the effects of climate change (increased temperatures and water scarcity), The local population relies on oasis-based agriculture along the Guir wadi river. However, this area has been affected by severe flooding events since 1980, which has caused destruction of fields and houses. Most of the oases farmers rely on agriculture combined with other activities such as livestock rearing (sheep, goats), fishing in the oasis lakes, and exploitation of natural resources like wood (mahdouf tree) and olives. These investments take place outside the oasis areas and are permitted by the exploitation of underground water resources. This economic dynamic is to a large extent supported by the state which will benefit the local population through job creation (qualified and unskilled labor) as well as stimulation of a local investment dynamic in similar, smaller scale investments for the production of quality dates, highly valued on local and international markets.

The sustainability of this economic dynamic depends on both the long-term preservation of the underground water resources (in the context of increased aridity), as well as balanced development between highly profitable agriculture outside of the oasis areas and the oases themselves.

Building on the opportunity created by the construction of a new dam upstream of the Boudouib valley, the project's purpose is to secure the benefits and development potential of the current economic dynamic on the date value-chain, through the resilience of a highly vulnerable area with regard to climate change. The project is organized in three components:

C1: Connecting to the dam and transfer of surface water to the Boudouib Valley (45.8 MEUR)

C2: Building the climate - resilience of oasis communities through a holistic approach (12.5 MEUR)

C3: Cross-cutting sustainability measures (technical assistance, groundwater preservation, environmental and social impact assessment) (20 MEUR)

C4: Project Management (2.4 MEUR)

## C.1 Strategic Context

Please describe relevant national, sub-national, regional, global, political, and/or economic factors that help to contribute to the proposal. Highlight opportunities and challenges and analyses.

It is now widely recognized that the Mediterranean region, of which Morocco is part, will be a hot spot for climate change. One of the areas that will be most affected in the coming decades. According to the IPCC, an increase in temperatures of 2 to 3 °C is expected in the Mediterranean region by 2050 and to 3 ° C - By c/o 2100. Summer precipitation could decrease by up to 40% over the next few decades.

Morocco is already facing major problems of water stress, desertification, loss of biodiversity and extreme climatic events such as floods and droughts that will be aggravated by climate change.

Climate change threats to impact the Moroccan economy and will exacerbate existing vulnerabilities. Since sub-decades and now, temperatures have increased by 0.5°C per decade and future increases are expected to be even higher than the average global temperature increase. Precipitation is already decreasing in some areas of Morocco and this trend is expected to continue.

The Government of Morocco (GOM) cannot afford to ignore them. The World Development Report (2010) places Morocco among the countries that, at a worldwide level, will suffer the most due to the negative effects of climate change on its economy. In addition, the country has a very high vulnerability index in terms of economic development. Droughts are extremely costly: the 1994/95 drought caused agricultural GDP to fall by 45 percent and GDP to fall by 20%. The GMP (Green Morocco Plan) is an ambitious government strategy designed to make agriculture a driving force for equitable economic growth. Launched in 2008, the GMP has the goal to double agriculture's value added within a decade.

The Green Morocco Plan (GMP) aims at integrating small farmers into the global economy and increasing their integration into the world economy; the Gulf has recently signed various free trade agreements, culminating in the recent "Advanced Status" awarded by the European Union. Pillar II focused on the inclusion of subsistence farmers into the global economy through the development of a market for agricultural products.

The GMP is based on two pillars: the first pillar is the "aggregation" approach aiming at bringing together smallholders and other actors in order to increase their competitiveness, while the second pillar aims at supporting those who are already competitive. The transversal actions include a comprehensive overhaul of the various sectors (land tenure, water management, rural infrastructure), and institutional reforms.

The originality of this strategy lies on its:

- Its articulation on 2 Pillars targeting respectively the already productive and "well off" agribusinesses with a mix of public and private investments, and the small farmers who will benefit from a more concessional and detailed support system (2nd Pillar).
- The "aggregation" approach aiming at bringing the two Pillars through technology transfer, credit (renewable energy), training, etc. to increase the competitiveness of products by small scale farmers; nucleus-plasma model), etc., in order to accelerate the modernization of agriculture and create win-win situations on the fields.
- The integration of sustainable development dimensions (and a focus on preservation of scarce resources) into the two Pillars.

Realizing that climate change risks regarding the impacts of the GMP, the Ministry of Agriculture, with support of World Bank, the GEF and SCCF (Special Climate Change Fund), has undertaken a project to Integrate Climate change adaptation in the GMP.

This is a key assumption that we made in our initial analysis, in an economically sustainable way over a 20 year period with [the] project scenario.

The construction of the Kadoussa dam upstream the Gour wadi will allow the production of 39 million cubic meters of surface water and its storage in the reservoir. The project will define the technical characteristics of this dam and define its management to allow transfer and delivery of this water (in priority to oases communities and to larger scale farms) towards the extension systems, which are located at a higher altitude than the Gour wadi. This action will tackle two issues key to the sustainability of the current economic transformation of the Boudouf Valley: i) preserve oasis-based agriculture and enhance its resilience to climate change (i.e. secure its economic and social existence against the effects of global warming); ii) provide a new source of water for extension systems, which are currently collected in the dam.

Component 3 will focus on projects aimed at enhancing the resilience of vulnerable populations in the oases through a holistic approach which combines i) modernization of oases hydraulic infrastructure and water resources mobilization; ii) agro-ecological practices as adaptation measures to CC (e.g. crop diversification, adoption of drought-resistant crops, rotational farming and iii) potential rural development activities that are to be elaborated through a participatory planning process. ### Sub-component 3.2: Contribute to groundwater preservation and to the establishment of an Aquifer Contract in the Oasis

All forms of agriculture present in this semi-arid region, and particularly outside the oases, rely or will rely on a combined management of two resources (surface and groundwater), the availability of which will vary during a given year at different times. The lack of regulation of these resources is one of the causes of their overexploitation; needs and calls for regulations tools necessary to ensure the sustainability of the exploitation of these resources.

More generally, groundwater, as a common and pooled resource, is subject to the "tragedy of the commons", which leads to its over-exploitation. The lack of regulation of this resource, in particular through the creation of aquifers (generation I), causes also, endangering the private and public investments made in the area. Aquifer contracts are one way of setting-up rules and regulations, constraints, inclusion and exclusion criteria for the use of water, from a bottom-up perspective. The objective is to ensure that all users have access to this resource while ensuring that no user is left behind or is trapped, by making information on the situation of the aquifer available and organizing a debate and a decision-making process in which all stakeholders are involved.

The following link provides more information about the principles of managing groundwater resources: <http://www.oncommons.org/magazine/etienne-8-principles-managing-communes>

Aquifer contracts are part of Morocco's Water Law and are currently implemented in a number of irrigation schemes in the country. The project will contribute to the implementation of an aquifer contract for the Tafraout groundwater and irrigation scheme. Apart from an important participatory process and dialogue amongst the water users, to which the project will contribute, this subcomponent will also include an import modeling dimension based on new investments for deep measuring, as well as engineering of an aquifer model.

The following link provides more information about the Tafraout Water Law: <http://www.oncommons.org/magazine/etienne-8-principles-managing-communes>

The Agence de Bassin Hydraulique du Quir-Ziz - Rhéas have the lead on the overall preparation of the Aquifer Contract; the project will allow the Ministry of Agriculture and the ORMAT to contribute effectively to this process.

### D1. Value Added for GCF Involvement

Please specify why the GCF involvement is critical for the project/programme.

The current trends in the project area will lead to a relatively pessimistic scenario, whereby patrimonial (i.e., necessary for future generations) underground water resources may be depleted due to an unsustainable agricultural business model and climate change. The main drivers of this trend are described below:

- The high level of climatic vulnerability of oases because they are dependent on irregular access to surface water.
- The increasing scarcity and irregularity of the wadi's spates, due to climate change, will have cumulative negative impacts on agricultural productivity and overall ability of the oasis system to sustain demographic growth and human development.
- The acceleration of the mobilization of underground water resources, because of increasing plant demand in the event of climate change.

The adaptation rationale of the project builds on the future availability of surface water, upon completion and filling of the dam. In order to:

- Target the most climatically vulnerable by securing regular access to water for agriculture (compare to baseline of increased scarcity and irregularity of access) and promoting development through more efficient and adapted water management practices.
- Put into place the appropriate institutional and legal frameworks, by which these additional and renewable resources (surface water) directly alleviate the pressure on the underground aquifer, thereby reversing the overexploitation trend:

# E.2. Paradigm Shift Potential

## Degree to which the proposed activity can catalyze impact beyond a one-off project/programme investment

### E.2.1. Potential for scaling up and replication (Provide a numerical multiple and supporting rationale)

Describe expected contributions to global low-carbon/or climate-resilient development pathway through a potential scaling-up of impact (e.g., in terms of geographical scope, types of the proposed project/programme).

Increased water scarcity in semi-arid and rural areas of the Southern Mediterranean, as a consequence of climate change, threatens economic life in these territories and, more specifically, the promotion of a balanced and sustainable development. This is due to the disappearance of traditional livelihoods (e.g., agriculture) and the appearance of new ones in the disappearing of agriculture at all territord and of the associated livelihoods; it will generate polarized and conflict-prone situations. The project aims to test a new model for development downstream of a water storage infrastructure based on an integrated approach and its three pillars:

- Transport surface water and build an efficient irrigation water distribution infrastructure to insure "more crop for the drop".
- Ensure an inclusive access to surface and underground water making sure that the local communities benefit both from the public investment and from the technological transfer allowed by the presence of agro business.
- Promote climate-smart agriculture within the cases and extensions (improving water efficiency of cropping systems, introducing drought resistant varieties, increasing added value per unit of land and per drop, protecting soil and environment).
- Creating and securing self-regulating institutions, in which users take responsibility for the management of a common pool resource, in this case water.

This is a shift towards a more holistic approach to improving the adaptation of agriculture and rural territories to climate change. The project breaks from a more classical approach focusing on infrastructure. This approach and the tools it will develop will constitute a "package" that could be easily replicable in other irrigation schemes of the country. In particular, this holistic approach to water management for agriculture in an environment of increased scarcity may also reveal as an important tool to conflict prevention and stabilization of poor rural areas.

### International waters:

The Gour basin is a cross-border basin shared between Morocco and Algeria. For that matter, the Gour basin may be subject to the Convention on non-navigational uses of international watercourses, adopted by the Assembly General of the United Nations in 1997 (Article 2). The convention offers States neighboring cross-border watercourses to cooperate and manage such water sources with an agreement or a protocol. It also provides for the establishment of local agreements. This agreement is based on two main principles as follows:

- *Right to use and benefit from water resources* (Article 5) and,
- *Obligation not to cause significant harm* to other States of the stream. (Article 7).

The status of this international convention is as follows:
- Adoption by the UN General Assembly on 21 May 1997 in New York
- Morocco signed the convention on August 18, 2014.
- Setting Force of the convention on August 17, 2014.

Also, it is subject to the Moroccan law (Article 3).

In the context of the Gour basin and at the stage of the detailed design and impact studies, the downstream impacts of the construction of the Kaddousa dam and of the development of the irrigated area downstream are evaluated as negligible. The reason for this evaluation lies in the fact that the project is located upstream of the Moroccan-Algerian border; only exceptional cases cross the border) and therefore, the notification procedure has not been considered as a requirement of the Convention (with reference to Article 7, paragraph 2). # H.2 Arrangements for Monitoring, Reporting and Evaluation

Please specify institutional setting and implementation arrangements for monitoring and reporting. Please indicate how you will organize mid-term and final evaluations.

## Mid-term evaluation (at least one) and annual reports will be elaborated by the Implementing Agency during the lifespan of the project, i.e., between the signing of the financial agreements and the Technical Closing Date (when the project is declared as closed).

Mid-term evaluation and annual reports will highlight any concerns or risks that require the attention of AE and the GCF. In addition, the evaluations/reports should recommend corrective actions in order to ensure that the climate investment criteria are met.

A final evaluation will be implemented 6 months after project closure and will examine the overall performance of the project against the investment criteria and other evaluation criteria that will be defined in the evaluation guidelines.

The objective of the final evaluations is to learn lessons and apply those lessons in order to upscale and replicate successful projects.

The mid-term evaluation will be financed through the project. The end line evaluation will be financed through AFD’s own resources.

Subsequent revisions headed by a Senior Program Manager will take place twice a year for the first two years, then once a year. Please provide methodologies for monitoring and reporting of the key outcomes of the project/programme investments. These should not only explain what was done but also evaluate how the project implementation has performed against the agreed outcomes and investment criteria (both financial and those that caused unintended impacts and other co-benefits).

Project-level evaluation reports will be submitted by AEs to the Secretariat.

Evaluations will be guided by the following principles:

(a) Independence and impartiality;
(b) Transparency;
(c) Participation of all relevant stakeholders (first and foremost local/disadvantaged communities, women and people with disabilities);
(d) Adherence to ethical standards within the evaluation with respect for the beliefs, culture and customs of the beneficiaries; and
(e) Objectivity.
