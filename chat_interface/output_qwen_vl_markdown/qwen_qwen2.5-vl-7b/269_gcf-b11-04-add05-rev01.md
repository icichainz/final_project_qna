# Brief Programme Information

## 1.1 Programme Executive Summary

Acumen is raising a new impact fund, KewKull Ventures Fund (the “Fund”), that will seek to catalyze a thriving off-grid energy ecosystem in East Africa, demonstrating that nations can leapfrog fossil-fuel grids to clean energy. The fund will be comprised of portfolios companies in the clean-energy sector in East Africa that provide access to energy services for low-income populations and are aligned with the GCF’s goals and will have overlapping impact with the adaptation goals of the GCF. The Fund will initially focus on Rwanda and Kenya, but will seek to expand across East Africa over time.

We are requesting $20M of equity capital from GCF that will be invested in the Fund and $5M of grant capital to be used for technical assistance funding. Acumen is seeking a total of $25M in equity investment to allow it to receive grant capital from GCF detailed in the sections below. Acumen will seek to upgrade Acumen’s credit rating.

Acumen is targeting a $103M total size for the Fund and expect that GCF’s anchoring investment will help to catalyze the remaining investment capital into the Fund. Acumen will invest at least $5M and up to $7M into the Fund, with the balance of the equity coming from other investors.

Acumen is targeting a first close of $40M by the end of the year, providing a strong foundation to raise the remaining balance of the Fund in a second close. 

The Fund would be one of the first, if not the first, of its kind in the sense that it is a climate change focused fund with an explicit focus on social impact and financial inclusion. The Fund will invest in companies that serve bottom-of-the-pyramid (“BOP”) customers. The Fund will seek to bring off-grid solar power and clean energy services to 10-15 million people by the end of its first close, which is expected to be achieved through a blueprint to a clean-energy future that can be followed by other nations. The Fund expects that its investment capital will have an impact on over 20M people in East Africa.

The Fund will seek to invest in ~10 – 15 companies. 60-70% of investments will be in businesses that have proven solutions to bring energy access to the masses such as solar lanterns, solar home systems and solar mini-grids, while the remaining investment capital will be used for other clean-energy technologies.

Examples of investments in the latter category include consumer financing vehicles, mobile payment and remittance services, and other financial inclusion products.

The Fund is unique in that its social impact is built into the operations and incentive structure of the Fund. The Fund Manager will only achieve a share of the Fund’s profit if both a financial hurdle rate and Impact Target are met. If either or both of these targets are not met by the end of the Fund’s first close, then the Impact Target, the Fund Manager’s share in profits will be adjusted downward in proportion to the percentage of the Impact Target that is missed.

The Fund will track multiple social impact metrics of the portfolio companies and report those to the GCF and its Investors. Acumen will report on the number of lives impacted, number of individuals with improved energy access, poverty levels of the households that have been reached, gender impact of investments, and number of jobs of US$10 reduced. The Fund may also report on other metrics as it sees fit.

## Strategic Context

### Rwanda

In Rwanda, over 70 percent of the population (~8.5M people) live off the grid, and the current best estimate is that cutting this figure to 30 percent will cost between $700 million and $1 billion per year for the next several decades. This represents a significant opportunity to improve access to electricity for millions of people in the dark. Moreover, the government's current best plan reluctantly calls for 50 percent of the desperately needed grid expansion to be powered by diesel generators.

On the other hand, Rwanda has unique advantages that no other country has: 1) Abundant, undeveloped renewable natural resources, including solar and hydropower; 2) A progressive government that has focused sharply on energy development in recent years. However, despite these advantages, only about one-third of the half of the power infrastructure planned for 2018 is not yet financed or under construction, so the generation source is not yet locked in.

Households with no electricity rely on kerosene lanterns and candles to meet their lighting needs. But these sources of light are dangerous, low-quality and expensive. Consumers pay $1 a week for kerosene for lighting and $1 more for phone charging. In Rwanda, electricity costs are among the highest in the world, at roughly $0.20/kWh compared to $0.12 in U.S. (high tariffs, heavily subsidized). In addition to high costs, power supply is often unreliable due to poor infrastructure and insufficient and unreliable supply as the number one barrier to stronger business and industrial growth.

## Kenya

In Kenya, only 20% of the population is connected to the grid, leaving 85 million people without access to affordable and reliable electricity. This lack of grid connectivity hinders the productivity as it limits daily activities such as cooking, lighting, and charging mobile phones. The country's energy sector faces slow rates of electrification coupled with high population growth; the grid supply versus demand crisis will only be exacerbated by the projected increase in energy consumption. Kenya has a low per capita income and a large proportion of its national budget goes to energy subsidies. Rural Kenyan households spend around 24% of their income on lighting, or roughly $4.2 per kg on kerosene fuel at a far higher rate per hour of lighting than if the same was provided by the grid.

The Environment and Policy Management Component under the Natural Resource Management Programme (NRM) supports the Government of Kenya in the implementation of the first medium-term plan (MTP), 2013-2017, with general focus particularly in relation to strategies and goals for environmental planning and governance.

The mandate of the Ministry of Environment and Mineral Resources (MEMR) is "to 'promote, conserve, protect, monitor and sustainable manage the environment and natural resources for the benefit of present and future generations'".

**(Baseline Scenario)**

The United Nations Sustainable Development Goals identify electricity as essential to eradicating poverty. The goal of the Sustainable Development Goals is to ensure universal access to affordable, reliable, and modern energy services.

More than 1.5 billion people lack access to electricity, mostly relying on kerosene lanterns and candles for their lighting needs. But these sources of light are dangerous, low quality and expensive. Kerosene lamps produce carbon dioxide (CO2). It is estimated that each kerosene lantern with a weekly fuel consumption of one liter of kerosene produces 0.1 tons of CO2 each year. In general, fuel-based lighting in the developing world accounts for about 5% of all CO2 emissions to the atmosphere each year. The portfolio will seek to address the lack of electricity and high kerosene use through affordable household clean energy products and services, such as solar powered lanterns, solar home systems and mini-grids. The cost of a solar home system that costs between $5 - $40, consumers can buy these products with cash upfront and receive a payback period just five months after purchase. In the case of mini-grids, the cost is higher but still affordable. The customer pays for this product over time because they don't have enough money to pay for the entire system upfront. 3.3 Program Description

The Fund intends to target solutions that provide access to clean, safe and affordable energy for those living at the BoP. The Fund will invest in approximately 10-15 companies, with investment range of approximately $25 million to $75 million per company. Investments will be made in a single portfolio company or in a series of related investments. Specific documents, systems and processes used to carry out the above monitoring include:

a) **100-Day Plan**
Portfolio Managers for Fund investments will develop a "100-Day Plan" at the time of an investment in collaboration with the management of the relevant portfolio company. This document identifies the key near-term actions that need to be taken by both parties and outlines the challenges that the portfolio company may face and the actions that can be taken to mitigate those challenges. The Fund's internal processes will require a 100-Day Plan for each investment, with the plan being reviewed at least once every quarter. The 100-Day Plans are four key priorities that can be broken down into two to three specific tasks with clear timeframes and lines of accountability. These include: financial management, including cash flow forecasting, budgeting, and capital structure; operations and information management; sales and marketing; and management capacity building.

c) **Annual Review Process**
The annual review process is a culmination of the monthly and quarterly reviews that occur throughout the year. The purpose of the annual review is to assess the performance of the Fund's portfolio companies on financial, operational, strategic, and ESG matters. This will include an assessment of those portfolio companies that are facing significant challenges as well as those that have seen superior performance; to foster detailed discussion with each portfolio company regarding its progress against its 100-Day Plan; to assess how the portfolio companies going forward; and ultimately to inform strategic decisions related to the overall portfolio strategy.

The annual review document is supplemented by a Post-Investment Management Plan ("Post Plan") that outlines the investment strategy with respect to each investment. The Post Plan is developed along the same timeline as the annual review document and is discussed in the same course.
