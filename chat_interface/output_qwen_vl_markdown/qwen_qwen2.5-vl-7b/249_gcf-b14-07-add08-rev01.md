# A2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

The EBRD-GEF SEFF Co-Financing programme ("the Programme") has been developed to deliver this scale in the EBRD region through the use of an innovative approach that builds on a decade of experience and an existing pool of USD 3.5 billion of climate finance mobilized through Partner Financial Institutions (PFIs).

The Programme will achieve its paradigm shift by creating new and significantly scaling up existing markets for commercial sustainable energy, energy efficiency and climate resilience financing. By doing so it will contribute to the aim as stated in the Paris Agreement "to make finance flows consistent with a pathway towards low greenhouse gas emissions and climate-resilient development". The Programme will do this through combination of financial support, capacity building and technology transfer and supported by a deep level of country ownership.

The Programme will deliver scale over the next three years by financing, via local PFIs, more than 20,000 scalable and replicable renewable energy, energy efficiency, and climate resilience projects across the industrial, commercial, residential, transport and agriculture sectors in the EBRD region, including Central Asia and South Caucasus and Eastern Europe. It will address multiple market barriers along the technology supply chain and unlock the potential of private sector finance by:

- Building the capacity of all actors along the climate technology supply chain, in particular by encouraging local PFIs to establish and grow climate financing solutions for RE, EE and CR that currently either do not exist at all, or are underserved.
- Stimulating demand for best-available climate technologies by providing much-needed long-term finance that more closely matches the financial characteristics of RE, EE and CR projects;
- Facilitating the creation of new markets by demonstrating the profitability and enhanced competitiveness of climate technologies and ultimately de-risking climate investments to leverage a growing level of funding from the private sector over time.

# C.1. Strategic Context

## 1. Common priorities as stated in the INDC and the role of the private sector

The participating countries (the “Countries”) highlighted the crucial role of energy efficiency and renewable energy deployment in mitigating climate change, through the Intended Nationally Determined Contributions (INDCs) for each nation. The INDCs placed particular emphasis on improving demand-side energy efficiency in the industrial sector and buildings, an objective that has traditionally been difficult to achieve due to the diverse nature of these two sectors, and the need for private-sector involvement. A major focus was also given to renewable energy deployment, with a specific emphasis on solar power generation. In the case of the countries in Central Asia, the Middle East and North Africa, reducing water demand in water-intensive technologies is a top priority in their national adaptation agenda.

Some policy measures together with limited public spending are being planned in the Countries to create a more conducive environment for energy efficiency, renewable energy and climate resilience. This indicates a recognition and support of the private sector’s important role in deploying investments to support the INDCs, and its potential to contribute to the paradigm shift to a low-carbon, resilient climate economy.

## 2. Strategic rationale for increased investment in climate technologies in the proposed regions

Investments in energy efficiency and renewable energy will have to deliver about 70% of the global greenhouse gas emissions reductions required to shift the world to a below 2°C warming path.* The private and financial sectors are integral for achieving the investment volume required at this time available.

Existing infrastructure, particularly in the industrial sector, exists in the Countries, some of which are among the most energy- and carbon-intensive economies in the world. In these regions, technologies that improve energy efficiency can help businesses optimise their production processes and improve product quality to boost profitability and increase competitiveness. In combination with renewable energy technologies and services, these technologies can contribute to substantial enhanced well-being and productivity as well as reduced utility bills.

### Renewable Energy

The Countries have abundant renewable resources that present commercial opportunities to generate clean electricity at low cost while reducing exposure to volatile fossil fuels and securing energy supply through diversified energy sources. The deployment of renewable energy technologies in the Countries enables access to smaller investors, and in particular, distributed and/or building-integrated renewable energy production.

### Climate resilience

Many of the Countries are also characterised by their vulnerabilities to the impacts of climate change, in particular water scarcity and water resources. The Programme covers some of the world’s most water scarce regions such as Central Asia, the Middle East and North Africa. These countries face a dual challenge: not only to reduce greenhouse gas emissions but also to maintain and enhance business competitiveness. Nevertheless, the level of technologies deployed is often very basic. Significant opportunities for water efficiency investments on a cost-benefit basis exist in these regions. Opportunities include energy and water efficient technologies with payback ranging from 1-5 years, as well as water-efficient irrigation systems. Using local financial institutions to facilitate investments in these technologies will access smaller clients and dispersed investors, especially in the agricultural sector – the main water user in many countries.

## 3. Targeted sectors and technologies

The Programme will provide the mechanisms needed to effectively and efficiently address the wide range of barriers to scaling up the deployment of innovative, higher performing climate technologies.* Implementing the Programme will allow Countries to tap into the potential for financially attractive energy efficiency, renewable energy and climate resilience project opportunities.

# DETAILED PROJECT / PROGRAMME DESCRIPTION

## GREEN CLIMATE FUNDING PROPOSAL | PAGE 14 OF 48

Without addressing these key barriers, the development of a market for sustainable energy and climate resilience is likely to remain limited and the EBRD regions will continue their energy inefficient practices while relying heavily on fossil fuels in their energy mix. By addressing multiple barriers through its design, the Programme aims to scale up private sector investments in energy efficiency, renewable energy and climate resilience projects.

The four specific objectives of this Programme are:

1. **Demonstrate and scale-up commercially viable sustainable energy and climate resilience financing**
   The Programme will help to develop and to demonstrate effective and efficient financing mechanisms for energy efficiency and renewable projects through new business models designed for industrial, corporate and residential clients. The Programme has important potential to contribute to the development of a market for sustainable energy and climate resilience by demonstrating how energy and climate resilience lines scale up to the potential impact of the project. Through increased financing vehicles, faster market penetration of low carbon technologies is expected.

2. **Lower the risk perceptions towards sustainable energy and climate resilience projects**
   The Programme will provide technical training on risk analysis and will actively develop these new markets and reduce the perceived high risk of financing sustainable energy and climate resilience projects. Extensive training on risk analysis will enhance PFI loan officers' understanding in these new sectors, and as a result, the risk margins related to the technical risk of projects are expected to be reduced. The Programme will also provide a pass-through mechanism of GCF concessional funding from local financial institutions.

3. **Transfer skills for sustainable energy and climate resilience projects**
   The Programme will provide technical training for sustainable energy and climate resilience financing to loan officers from various departments in the EBRD, including project finance, legal, credit, risk management, head offices and regional branches at partner financial institutions. At the end of the programme, partner financial institutions will have increased internal capacity to deliver energy efficiency, renewable energy and climate resilience investments.

4. **Raise public awareness of benefits, financial viabilities and positive impacts on the livelihoods of affected communities that climate resilient projects can bring**
   The Programme will launch regional or country specific projects through which sustainable energy and climate resilience (mainly water efficiency) technologies, appliances and equipment to the proposed regions above the current market practice by raising public awareness, building capacity to civil society organisations and leveraging their networks. In addition to strengthen demand driven approaches, the programme will also provide training for financial institutions on how to assess projects that help potential clients to better understand requirements of financial institutions for sustainable energy and climate resilience lending.

# C3. Project / Programme Description

## COMPONENT I: CREDIT LINES (SUSTAINABLE ENERGY AND CLIMATE RESILIENCE FINANCE) – NON-GRANT COMPONENT

The first component of the programme provides for credit lines to be extended by Participating Financial Institutions (PFIs) for sustainable energy and climate resilience lending. The financing will support investments in high performance climate technologies and supply chains/local manufacturing to ensure the availability of these technologies.

In principle, all PFIs must comply with EBRD criteria on eligibility. Eligibility criteria include:

- PFIs financials, integrity and ownership
- PFIs’ client base and market base in relation to identified market demand/extent that client base would benefit from higher energy performance technologies
- PFIs outreach practices/familiarity with financing core items of equipment/executive management enthusiasm for new financing products

The use of credit lines should be in accordance with a ‘Policy Statement’ as part of the Loan Agreement between EBRD and PFIs. PFIs will only lend the funds to their clients (and beneficiaries) after investing in (A) pre-approved technologies from the Best Available Technologies Selector or (B) technologies and equipment validated by the experts procured by EBRD.

## COMPONENT 2: TECHNICAL SUPPORT – GRANT COMPONENT

The technical support component of the programme will include investment assistance, training and marketing activities.

## D1. Value Added for GCF Involvement

The Programme can help the GCF to achieve a paradigm shift to a "low-carbon" path by contributing to creating demand drivers, sufficient market size and energy and climate resilience in markets of 500 million people. Sustainable water and climate resilience financing in the proposed regions is still in a very early development stage with few banks offering dedicated loan products or actively marketing the benefits of such investments. Further efforts to demonstrate the benefits of sustainable water and climate resilience financing are needed, as well as mechanisms to accelerate the commercial financing of these projects.

The Fund will effectively fill the gap in the market through a structured commitment mechanism that provides long-term financing and technical support to accelerate the adoption of climate technologies.

The Programme will help the GCF to "accelerate the operationalization of the adaptation mitigation window", and to ensure adequate funding for adaptation, as well as to provide additional resources for adaptation, as requested by the GCF Board during COP 20. By participating in the proposed Programme, the Fund can leverage the EBRD's wide range of networks with financial institutions and deliver a large number of projects to reach critical mass for the creation of a competitive market.

For EBRD, components such as capacity building, which is an essential component of SEF to address capacity bottlenecks, can only be provided by donor funding. The Programme targets various areas of the GCF strategic impacts and cross-cutting investment priorities. Through the mix of investment priorities and results areas, the Programme aims to deliver on the balanced, cross-cutting approach to mitigation and adaptation.

Nevertheless, the SEF Programme's component with the highest value added for GCF involvement is the opportunity to partner with the private sector and harness EBRD's implementation capacity, to catalyse investment in the results area and maximize the impact of the GCF. The SEF will provide a platform for the mobilization of private sector resources through public-private development and unlock private sector investments in adaptation, responding to different country financial contexts and availability of capital. It will support mitigation by scaling up increased supply of clean energy, through the combination of EBRD and GCF funding, as well as by supporting the transition from fossil fuels to renewable energy sources and other more climate-compatible business practices.

The SEF also boosts private sector investment in adaptation projects, which until now have suffered from a lack of private capital. The GCF is also the ideal partner for this Programme due to the combination of concessional lending and grant for technical assistance required to achieve the GCF objectives and consequent paradigm shift in the targeted countries.

# Monitoring

Implementation of each Facility under this Programme will be managed and monitored at project and Facility level by both EBRD’s in-house staff and SEF operation team procured by the EBRD.

## 1. EBRD in-house staff oversight and quality assurance

The EBRD has dedicated staff in its headquarters and regional offices involved in engaging PFI, selecting qualified PFIs, structuring project agreements, managing project implementation, approving project eligibility, and monitoring financial, technical, and other risks.
- EBRD SEF team managers will oversee and mitigate factors that can affect the performance of the Programme. SEF “project managers” responsibilities may include the following: provision of standardized project development procedures from sub-loan application to eligibility and compliance screening, implementation supervision, marketing guidance and mitigation of negative impacts, and reporting on progress and achievements from inception through Facility closure.
- EBRD will conduct due diligence, monitor financial and integrity risks, and prepare mitigation measures throughout the Programme lifecycle. Accompanying the technical performance of the Facility that lead to climate objectives will be verified by EBRD SEF managers.

## 2. Monitoring by the SEF operation teams procured by the EBRD

SEF operation teams on the ground will assist and monitor PFIs along the entire project lifetime. SEF operation team’s roles include the following:
- Triggering and standardizing Project procedures along the project lifecycle from project screening to monitoring
- Project term due diligence: screening project eligibility and compliance prior to approval
- Follow-up after financial close: verifying the implementation of project and performance of the project
- Ongoing level monitoring: monitoring the progress and implementation of the Facility as a whole.

## Reporting

### 1. Reporting of the PFI to the EBRD

As specified in the Loan Agreement between PFI and EBRD, the PFI is obliged to report its portfolio on the use of proceeds of the Programme to the EBRD.
- Inception report: As soon as a team of local and international consultants (SEF operation team) or Project implementation team has been appointed by the PFI, the SEF operation team will prepare an inception report. This report will cover the inception phase; the SEF operation team will report to the EBRD SEF manager their activities including the starting up phases of establishing the country or region specific marketing strategy, establishing processes and procedures in consultation with the PFI, and identifying potential suppliers, installers, materials and equipment, estimating the market size, including the market specific list of Best Available Technologies (BAT).
- Facility monitoring report: Once the Facility is officially launched, the SEF operation team will consult with the EBRD SEF manager to prepare a facility monitoring report. This report will cover the first 12 months on a weekly, monthly and quarterly basis throughout the Facility lifetime. These reports will include (i) Facility and PFI status; and (ii) end beneficiary level monitoring.
- Portfolio reporting: disbursed sub-loan amount by PFI, list of projects and sectors by PFI, project values, etc.
- Quantitative target monitoring: expected energy savings, expected emission reduction and specific and additional benefits.
- Qualitative target monitoring: market development, technology transfer, knowledge exchange, updating the list of Best Available Technologies including eligible suppliers, installers, materials and equipment.
