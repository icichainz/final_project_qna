## 2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

### A.2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)
1. Due to the complex mountainous terrain and climate, Georgia is subject to both geological and hydro-meteorological hazards.
2. The frequency, intensity and geographical spread of extreme hydro-meteorological hazards will increase. Georgia's INDC estimates economic losses from climate-induced hazards without adaptation measures for the period 2021-2030 to be USD 5.1 billion (USD 487 million per year).
3. To date, hydro-meteorological hazard risk management has relied on the limited and expensive hard structural protection measures; emergency response with limited reliance on forecasts and early warning of the population; post event compensation for damages; and a lack of institutional capacity to plan and prepare for climate change.
4. Georgia needs to adopt a proactive integrated climate risk management (CRM) approach that includes the development of an effective multi-hazard warning system, an enhanced use of climate information in planning and decision-making across all sectors. This CIF project will support the development of a Multi-Hazard Warning System (MHWS) and all other aspects of a priori climate risk management required to support an effective MHWS.
5. The project will achieve transformative change in climate risk reduction and management in Georgia by development of a fully integrated impact-based MHWS system. In doing so, it will introduce a standard approach for vulnerability assessment and adaptation planning that can be used to manage climate risk information to enable the implementation of nation-wide risk reduction policies. Importantly, it will develop long-term institutional and community capacity to plan and prepare for climate change impacts in Georgia.
6. The project is expected to contribute to a paradigm shift towards climate-risk informed and resilient development and will directly benefit up to 1.7 million people (40% of the population) currently at risk from hydro-meteorological hazards.

## (a) Financing Structure

The project is financed by a combination of resources from the following sources: (i) public and public-good nature of the proposed climate risk reduction investments targeting enhanced resilience of around 17 million people, including the most vulnerable and poor rural communities located in remote mountainous areas. It is unique that nearly all streams of revenue from these investments will be used to reduce risks in this project. A federal level project will develop cost information products which may benefit the population at risk as it line with the statutory obligations of the GoP to provide public goods and services. The project will also contribute to reducing the vulnerability of communities exposed to extreme hydro-meteorological risks. The majority of recipients of climate risk information (i.e., the public at large) is unable to pay for such information products, but the project aims to ensure that this activity does not lead to full cost recovery for these essential statutory services to be provided to the public.

C1. Strategic Context

Climate Change impacts and vulnerability
- Due to complex mountainous terrain and climate, Georgia is subject both geological and hydro-meteorological hazards including landslides, floods, avalanches, floods and flash floods, droughts, and strong winds.
- There is evidence that frequency of these climate-induced disasters and associated damages have been increasing over the past decades. Climate change studies indicate that these hazards will further increase in frequency, intensity and geographical distribution.
- Georgia’s vulnerability to climate change impacts is high due to its dependence on agriculture for livelihood, health, critical infrastructure, tourism and protection of culture heritage, environment, natural resources and ecosystems. Georgia has a relatively low adaptive capacity as it is already facing multiple stressors such as poverty, conflict, and weak governance.
- The escalation of geological and hydrological processes is expected until 2050. The climate change scenarios indicate more extreme weather events in the future, which will increase the frequency and intensity of these hazards. These phenomena during these short periods, thereby increasing the potential for flash flooding (due to high peak river flows), mudflows and landslides.
- In addition, the climate change scenario indicates that Georgia is likely to experience a decrease in precipitation during dry periods, which will persist until 2050 in already dry areas will further increase the risk of droughts. Currently, the number of days with precipitation more than 50 mm and no rain fall will be reduced in almost entire East Georgia by 2030.
- The climate change scenario indicates that the water demand for irrigation will increase due to prolonged warmer, drier periods but will be offset to some extent by greater evaporation. This will lead to a decrease of available water resources in the country and an increase in competition between agriculture and other sectors (e.g., energy, industry).
- In addition, the climate change scenario indicates that Georgia is likely to experience more frequent droughts than towards wet periods therefore, dry areas of the country already affected by the climate change will become more vulnerable. The most vulnerable regions are the mountainous parts of Eastern Georgia (Kakheti, Shida Kartli), Western Georgia (Sagarejo, Dedoplistsikaro, etc.) Kakheti region and Shida Kartli, which are also prone to droughts will continue to be susceptible to this phenomenon.

### Background

The overall objective of the GCF project is to reduce disaster risk and enhance climate resilience in Georgia. The project will address the following key challenges:

* **Limited financial resources:** The current budget for disaster risk management (DRM) is insufficient, with a total annual average value of 8.6 million GEL in 2014 and approximately 3.7 million GEL in 2005.
* **Inadequate DRM capacity:** There are significant gaps in the national DRM capacity, including limited institutional coordination, fragmented budgeting, and lack of risk information data.

### Project Description

The project will address these challenges by:

* **Increasing financial resources for DRM:** The project aims to increase the annual average value of the DRM budget from 8.6 million GEL in 2014 to approximately 23-26 times that amount.
* **Improving institutional coordination and capacity building:** The project will strengthen national DRM institutions, improve risk information data management, and enhance disaster preparedness and response.

### Project Activities

The project activities are organized into three main components:

#### Component 1: Strengthening National Disaster Risk Management Institutions
- **Activity 1.1:** Develop a comprehensive DRM strategy.
- **Activity 1.2:** Improve institutional coordination and capacity building for DRM.

#### Component 2: Enhancing Climate Resilience in Agriculture
- **Activity 2.1:** Scale up climate-resilient agricultural practices.
- **Activity 2.2:** Strengthen the national DRM capacity to address climate change impacts on agriculture.

#### Component 3: Improving Disaster Risk Information Management and Early Warning Systems
- **Activity 3.1:** Develop a comprehensive disaster risk information management system.
- **Activity 3.2:** Enhance early warning systems for natural hazards in Georgia.

### Project Outputs

The project will achieve the following outputs:

* **Improved institutional coordination and capacity building for DRM.**
* **Enhanced climate resilience in agriculture through scaled-up climate-resilient agricultural practices.**
* **Strengthened disaster risk information management and early warning systems to address climate change impacts on Georgia's economy, people, and environment.**

**Risk Management Framework**

The risk score is based on consideration of the following categories of risk:

- Risk to people (risk to life, disruption to community and disruption to daily life), using people as a metric
- Risk to infrastructure, also using people as a metric
- Risk to agriculture, using hectares as a metric
- Risk to the environment, using both objects at risk and hectares as a metric

The activity will include the following:

- Introduction and implementation of methods and tools for the systematic gender-sensitive socio-economic vulnerability assessment for decision-making for prioritization of resilience investments.
- Development and implementation of a GIS-based socio-economic risk and vulnerability model which integrates vulnerability assessments with hazard maps, using both objects at risk and vulnerability metrics. This will include economic losses and damages and loss of life estimates.
- Introduction of gender-sensitive community based socio-economic survey techniques and technologies.

## Activity 1.4: Centralized multi-hazard risk information and knowledge system

The project will develop a centralized multi-hazard risk information systems and knowledge portal (web knowledge portal) to increase awareness, provide interactive hazard maps, with integration with social media and possible mobile app to increase community engagement and allow two-way flow of information. The hazard and risk assessment data will be shared among the various stakeholders including government departments and agencies. To enable access and sharing of this information, a centralised information system and knowledge portal will be developed. This will provide an integrated platform for the exchange of information and provide the information access and sharing platform for geospatial information on hazards. The system will represent a single point of access to all hazard data and metadata, with a common user interface that allows users to share data, sharing protocols and importantly by external training and capacity building to ensure sustainability. While the Hazard-related part of the database will be hosted and managed by NEA, as it is now, Metadatabase with socio-economic parameters will be developed and maintained by NIDA. The system will provide access to all relevant data on disaster statistics, losses and damages and socio-economic vulnerability. Both metadatabases will be interconnected through a common interface using EMA's GEOData portal. Data access protocols for various concerned government agencies will be developed.

## Output 2: Multi-hazard early warning systems and new climate information products supporting effective weather, climate and hydrological services and institutional capacities

Under this output the project will address gaps in institutional capacity and institutional set-up so as to be effective EWS resulting in a functioning coordination mechanism and communication protocols for early warning. Capacities of various stakeholders involved in generating, processing, communicating and using the warnings and other climate information will be enhanced. National and local Integrated Early Warning Systems by hazard and sectors will be developed and implemented.

### C.4. Background Information on Project / Programme Sponsor (Executing Entity)

The Ministry of Environment Protection and Agriculture of Georgia (MoEPA) has a broad mandate in the field of environment and natural resources planning, management and protection that includes monitoring and forecasting natural hazards, climate change adaptation, and sustainable development of agricultural sector. MoEPA is a responsible body for climate adaptation action planning and coordination at national level. The Climate Change Unit is part of the Department of Environment Protection and Management whose head is also a focal point to the UNFCCC. The unit coordinates development of climate-related strategies and policies required by the UNFCCC. The Climate Change Unit monitors and assesses the impacts of climate change impacts on the sectors, economy and environment and prepares relevant predictions, develops the national plan for adaptation to climate change, coordinates the national communications to the UNFCCC and provides an inventory of greenhouse gas emissions.

The National Environmental Agency (NEA), under the MoEPA is mandated to monitor ongoing hydro-meteorological, geodetic and geological events, as well as to provide monitoring of environmental pollution, to issue permits for industrial activities and to ensure compliance with environmental legislation. The NEA is a key component in the agencies of the MoEPA. NEA is responsible for hydro-meteorological and geological services and provides data on climate change impacts. The NEA has a mandate to monitor ongoing events and provide input into the National Communications, there are national working groups representing all related ministries and state agencies, academic, private sector and NGOs. ### Community-Based Early Warning System (CB-EWS) Overview

A community-based early warning system (CB-EWS) is a locally based operational forecasting and warning activities of natural hazards that mitigating the effects of disasters in an area. This is a relatively cheap, easy, capacity building system enhanced by the direct and active participation of the community and its leaders. It is suggested to establish CB-EWSs at the community level as part of the overall early warning system. The CB-EWS will be a stand-alone system and will consist of automatic rain gauges, radar sensor type water level monitoring and post-facto community water level monitoring. Three different levels of warning will be defined depending on the time to the event and on the associated risk. These levels are: (1) Early Warning; (2) Immediate Warning; and (3) Urgent Warning.

Community warden mechanism will be introduced in selected communities. Nominated wardens will be persons who will operate CB-EWSs at community level, and will be responsible for disseminating early warnings to the community members who will receive warnings from JOC/EMA through various communications means, including SMS, HF radio, email, internet, mobile phones, etc.

### H.1.1 Paradigm Shift Objectives and Impacts at the Fund level**

#### Paradigm shift objectives

The project objective is to reduce exposure of Georgia’s communities, livelihoods and infrastructure to climate-induced natural hazards through a paradigm shift in policy and practice towards climate-resilient development.

The GCF project will support Georgia in developing and implementing a national adaptation plan (NAP) that includes transformative policies and actions for reducing exposure and vulnerability of the population to climate-related hazards. The project will also contribute to the implementation of the NAP through the introduction of innovative approaches.

The project innovation and transformative change will be made by participating “Last Mile” community solution development, including the use of participatory tools such as Participatory Rural Appraisal (PRA) and Community Based Natural Resource Management (CBNRM). The project will also support the implementation of climate-resilient land-use planning, natural resource management, and community resilience measures through such innovative approaches as watershed-based restoration.

The project will contribute to the achievement of the following transformative change: improved effectiveness of early warning systems; combining best available science and local knowledge for vulnerability assessment; hazard risk reduction and community resilience measures through such innovative approaches as watershed-based restoration. The project will also contribute to the achievement of the following paradigm shift objectives: increased awareness raising, education and capacity development activities on multi-hazard risk reduction, including preparedness, response and ERFs.
