### Context

Antigua and Barbuda forms part of the Leeward Islands in the eastern Caribbean. Most of the population—94,600 people—is concentrated on Antigua Island, with a further 5,000 residents on Barbuda. As a small island developing state (SIDS), Antigua and Barbuda is among the most vulnerable countries to extreme climate events such as hurricanes and tropical storms. The country has experienced several major hurricane events in recent years, including Hurricane Ivan (2004) with extreme climate events having significant impacts on the lives and livelihoods of local communities, as well as on critical infrastructure such as housing, water supply systems, roads, hospitals, clinics, emergency services, telecommunications and schools—leads not only to disruptions to economic activity but also to considerable recovery costs over a long period. Moreover, it often leaves considerable hurdles for the country to recover from such disruptions, which leads to considerable declines in economic productivity and quality of life.

### Climate Rationale

Historically, Antigua and Barbuda has only been hit by relatively low-intensity tropical storms, with those reaching hurricane status seldom strengthening beyond Category 3. The return rate of Category 4 hurricanes in the first half of the 20th century was approximately one per decade (Figure 1a). Consequently, building codes in Antigua and Barbuda did not prescribe the construction of buildings to withstand high-intensity tropical storms. However, since the mid-1980s, the frequency of Category 4 hurricanes has increased significantly; the increasing intensity of hurricanes hitting the country (Figure 1b) is having a significant impact on infrastructure in Antigua and Barbuda.

The most recent hurricane to hit Antigua and Barbuda was Hurricane Ivan in September 2004. The storm resulted in the destruction of >95% of all infrastructure on Barbuda, with total recovery costs estimated at US$222 million. Although the impact of this event was felt across both islands, the damage sustained by Barbuda prior to Antigua and Barbuda, this is true even if the event does not make landfall in the country. This trend of increasing intensity of hurricanes has been observed for other countries in the Caribbean region as well.

Urgent adaptation measures for the building sector are therefore needed to address the impacts of climate change on the country. Further details on the climate change risks and impacts for Antigua and Barbuda are described below.

### Rationale for the use of the Saffi-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale (SSHWS) as the operational meteorology measure for the project

The current assessment for the project utilizes the Saffi-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale (SSHWS). There are, however, some potential limitations with the SSHWS as a robust and granular measure of a tropical cyclone's potential impact both in observed records and in future climate scenarios. These include: the lack of consideration of other factors that can influence ground impacts within a given hurricane category as a result of being classified primarily by maximum sustained wind speed; the inability to distinguish between different types of tropical cyclones (e.g., hurricanes, tropical storms, depressions); wind speed threshold) of alternative hurricane impact indices, the weakness of climate models to accurately predict future changes in hurricane intensity and frequency; and limited updates of these scales in academia as well as by the IPCC. The use of the SSHWS remains more feasible.®

The proposed projects are an integral part of Antigua and Barbuda’s GCF Country Programme. The current portfolio under the Country Programme has been specifically designed to take a holistic approach to climate change adaptation and mitigation, with a focus on complementarity. This means that each project is designed to build upon and reinforce the concept of complementarity underpinning the entire portfolio. These projects are focused on inter alia climate-proofing measures in public critical service buildings and community buildings, particularly with regard to the impacts of sea level rise and coastal flooding.

Although hurricanes are considered the most urgent climate hazard to Antigua and Barbuda, the impacts of hurricanes are multi-faceted and include storm surges. The proposed projects under the GCF Country Programme are directed toward immediate adaptation needs for critical public service and community buildings, particularly with regard to the impacts of sea level rise and coastal flooding. These projects will be implemented in conjunction with and/or complemented by other projects under the Country Programme which will address the additional climate hazards associated with hurricanes (e.g., wind damage). The proposed projects are also aligned with Antigua and Barbuda’s development that will: i) increase the climate resilience of road infrastructure, including adaptation measures for reducing runoff from roads; ii) reduce vulnerability to coastal flooding through the construction of sea walls and other barriers, with specific focus on storm surges. Of particular relevance within the context of the proposed project is the GCF project currently under development by the Council focused on increasing the resiliency of road infrastructure and adjacent utilities to climate change impacts.

The projects under this country programme include a combination of grey and green infrastructure through: i) increasing the thickness of key sea walls and other barriers; ii) constructing new sea walls in areas where existing sea walls are inadequate or non-existent, particularly in key areas upstream of the target road infrastructure to attenuate water flow from heavy rainfall events, therefore, reducing vulnerability to coastal flooding. iii) constructing a series of stormwater management systems and drainage paths that are situated along the coast and consequently vulnerable to sea level rise.

The proposed project, combined with the GCF project focused on climate-proofing key road and utilities infrastructure will complement other projects under Antigua and Barbuda’s GCF Country Programme which have been implemented. Climate change is causing an increase in the frequency of high intensity hurricanes making landfall on the small island developing states (SIDS) of Antigua and Barbuda. Historically, the majority of tropical depressions impacting the islands were of relatively low intensity, remaining as tropical storms or Category 1–3 hurricanes. Only nine Category 4 and five Category 5 hurricanes have made landfall in the Caribbean since 1970, with only two Category 5 hurricanes in the last 15 years alone. Given the previous return rate of major hurricanes, the country’s current infrastructure is only designed to withstand Category 3 or lower intensity hurricanes. However, climate change and warming of the Atlantic Ocean are resulting in an increasing intensity of hurricanes in the Caribbean, with Antigua and Barbuda experiencing their first Category 4 hurricane in 2017 (Hurricane Irma) followed by a second Category 5 hurricane in 2018 (Hurricane Maria). Both have not accounted for Category 4 and 5 hurricanes; the impacts of such events have been severe, causing significant damage to infrastructure including buildings, roads, bridges, water supply systems, health facilities, telecommunications, electricity, water, sewage and waste systems. Moreover, the disruption of critical services significantly limits the capacity of the National Office of Disaster Services (NODS) to respond during and after an extreme event. This problem is exacerbated by the fact that the NODS has limited resources and a small staff in this country which reduced the ability of communities and government agencies to prepare for an extreme climate event.

The challenge of upgrading existing buildings to Category 4 and 5 is estimated to cost over US$6.4 billion. While Category 4 and 5 standards can be incorporated into new buildings much easier than existing structures, the economy of Antigua and Barbuda is not strong enough to allow for such a large investment in infrastructure at this time; it will take too long to generate the funds needed. Taking into consideration that climate change is now considered an emergency situation, the government has decided to focus on the adaptation solution which involves strengthening the building of resilience in 54 of the 254 buildings in the Government sector. This will allow for the Government to provide services and continue operations during and after a Category 3 or higher intensity hurricane without significant disruption over time.

Preferred adaptation solution
For Antigua and Barbuda to adapt to the impact of extreme climate events, it is necessary to strengthen the climate resilience of the building sector. Existing infrastructure needs to be upgraded to withstand the impacts of Category 4 and 5 hurricanes. This will include: a) increasing the structural integrity of buildings to withstand the wind shear forces; as well as by decentralizing power and water supplies in order to reduce the risk of damage from flooding and storm surge; b) improving access to critical services such as on critical services such as police, fire, and health, as well as community buildings that act as shelters during a storm; increasing the capacity of these facilities to accommodate displaced persons for up to 14 days following an extreme event; and f) more rapid recovery, including restoring water supply, power and communication services. Along with strengthening the building sector, it is also necessary to strengthen the resilience of critical infrastructure which includes both the public and private sectors needs to be created to facilitate the upscaling of climate-proofing interventions in the country. The capacity of the National Office of Disaster Services (NODS) will need to be strengthened to improve the dissemination of early warnings and facilitate early action for disaster preparedness.

Barriers to the adaptation solution
Several barriers to the preferred adaptation solution have been identified for Antigua and Barbuda. These are summarized in detail with examples in Section 8 of Annex 2, Feasibility Study.

# B.3 Project/programme description (max: 2000 words, approximately 4 pages)

At present, most development efforts to address the impacts of climate change primarily focus on the deployment of mitigation measures—primarily clean energy solutions and energy-efficiency measures. Adaptation measures for climate change are generally limited in scope and scale, and often not well integrated into building codes. This proposal builds upon the experience on the ground and practically no calibrated engineering solutions for integrated building codes which enhance both resilience against extreme weather events (climate-proofing) as well as energy efficiency. The focus relates to energy-efficiency measures and the preservation of natural resources but do not incorporate customized climate-proofing solutions.

With existing successful examples of climate-proofing against wind and water loads, it can be preliminarily assessed that climate-proofing solutions are much more disruptive for the structure of buildings than mitigation measures. Consequently, in the cases where mitigation measures are not yet introduced, it is advisable to reinforce structures to achieve a certain level of resilience (e.g., by using reinforced concrete or steel) before introducing energy-efficiency measures (EE measures). This is because EE solutions are related to the building structure directly and therefore depend strongly on the building's structural integrity.

The proposed project is one of first projects in the Caribbean region to introduce building codes that account for the impacts of Category 4 and 5 hurricanes. The successful accomplishment of this task, as well as lessons learned from the introduction of these codes, will be documented and shared with other countries in the region. A key objective is to be assessed to determine which solutions are the most efficient and compatible with the improved structural integrity of these buildings. This exercise can become an important market test to benchmark the scope of integrated building codes for future projects.

The combined intervention will consist of a series of climate-proofing interventions, energy-efficiency measures, and all EE measures will be part of the design of critical buildings, along with the climate-proofing elements. The combined intervention will also include capacity-building activities in the public sector to ensure that both mitigation and adaptation measures to be introduced into building codes. A comprehensive report on this matter will be developed upon the project completion.

**B.5 Justification for GCF funding request (max. 1000 words, approximately 2 pages)**

As a Caribbean SIDS, Antigua and Barbuda is one of the most vulnerable countries to extreme climate events such as hurricanes. To address the impact of extreme climate events on the people and economy of Antigua and Barbuda, it is critical to integrate climate change considerations into development planning and to invest in building the resilience of the country’s built environment. The Government of Antigua and Barbuda (GoAB) has identified a US$27 million GCF grant funding proposal for the sector, focusing on climate change adaptation through public investment in buildings. This document provides an overview of how the project will support the GoAB to implement its climate change finance climate change adaptation interventions through domestic sources of financing – including both private and public entities.

Although Antigua and Barbuda is considered a middle-income country on a per-capita basis, its size and small population means that it lacks the scale required to invest adequately in adaptation. The country has a high public debt burden, with the total public debt estimated at 64% of GDP as of December 2019, and with consideration the cost of upgrading all buildings in the country to Cat-5 it is conservatively estimated about US$4 billion. This represents approximately 30% of the country’s annual budget. The high level of public debt also limits the ability of the country to repay public debt. Although this debt was expected to decline by 2019, long-standing problems in the fiscal and banking sectors remain unresolved, including the unsustainable public debt as well as persistent large financing gaps for infrastructure projects. As a result, the Government has been unable to meet its obligations on fiscal and debt sustainability has therefore been recommended by the Executive Directors of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) as part of the 2019 Article IV Consultation. The IMF has also recommended that the country overcome fiscal imbalances over investing in climate change adaptation. The recent 2020 global response to the COVID-19 pandemic has further exacerbated the situation, with the Government having to urgently address the crisis and by expending the limited resilience of the country’s building sector.

Several alternative financing options have been considered during project development, including government funding. However, given the high level of public debt, it was not possible for the Government to finance this project within its maximum feasible contribution of US$13 million (~35% of the total project cost) as co-financing. The threat of bankruptcy has however led the Government to commit to this project regardless of the impact of the COVID-19 response.

GCF funding will be used to overcome the barriers to increasing the resilience of Antigua and Barbuda’s building sector to extreme climate events, including hurricanes, tropical storms, and other severe weather. The proposed change with government co-financing being used to cover all baseline costs – including business as usual requirements for project implementation, such as land acquisition, environmental assessments, etc., as well as the support of GCF grant funding, the proposed project will deliver several adaptation benefits that will contribute to achieving a paradigm shift in the country towards climate-resilient sustainable development.

Without GCF support, and with the recent COVID-19 pandemic, the public and private sectors in Antigua and Barbuda will continue to face significant challenges. The Government of Antigua and Barbuda is increasingly vulnerable to hurricanes, with national and local-level decision-makers being limited in their capacity to cope for, and respond to, the increasing impacts of climate change. This vulnerability is expected to increase as the country’s population continues to grow and become increasingly vulnerable to extreme climate events such as hurricanes and tropical storms. This will result in: continued destruction of infrastructure; increased costs associated with responding to these events, during and following these events; and increased damages and loss of life under future climate change conditions.

3.2 Paradigm shift potential

At the current stage of development in climate change efforts, we are considering the deployment of a mix of adaptation measures, comprising mainly clean energy solutions and energy efficiency measures. Adaptation options for climate-proofing structures against climate change impacts at this present developmental stage are still largely based on experience on the ground. There are a limited number of engineering solutions for integrated building codes that embrace both climate change streams. This situation is apparent in the broad adoption of "green" buildings, which incorporate clean energy and energy efficiency measures related to renewable energy or energy efficiency measures. 3. Baseline Gender Situation for Antigua and Barbuda

3.1 In general, gender plays an important role in determining the adaptive capacities of individuals to climate change. However, it affects men and women differently. Gender considerations can take on board the specific needs of women and men, the ability of the individual to withstand and recover from disasters, their access to resources relative to others, and the policy and other interventions that can be undertaken by the state in general and specifically for building resilience and reduce long-term vulnerability.

3.2 Gender policy development and implementation in Antigua and Barbuda is led by the Director of Gender Affairs in the Ministry of Social Transformation, Community Development, Youth and Gender Affairs. The Republic of Antigua and Barbuda’s approach is focused specifically on issues related to the impact of climate change and environmental policy on gender and the specific policy and programmatic measures that can be used to respond to these gender differences. This approach is believed to improve access to services, reduce risk from climate change and support from the most beneficiaries.

3.3 The proposed GCF project design seeks to increase the adaptability of men and women and their families to have continued access to free government services. These services include health, education, security and key social services to groups of persons with very limited ability to adapt. The project seeks to achieve this via building resilience in the community and through strengthening the capacity of the public sector to plan and plan to deliver these services. The project will seek to focus mainly on the impact of hurricanes on building infrastructure that facilitate the delivery of these services to men, women and their families.

# Project Background

## 5.1 To increase the resilience of the population in Antigua and Barbuda to extreme climate events, the proposed project will implement climate change technologies and interventions within public and commercial buildings (hereafter referred to as “public buildings”) that will strengthen institutional, technical and financial capacity within the GoAB to enable climate-resilient building development. The project will also support long-term post-disaster recovery in buildings providing critical services – including disaster services, healthcare, fire services and police – as well as those buildings providing public and community goods - including schools, libraries, markets, and other essential facilities. Climate change adaptation and island-specific best practices will be used to climate-proof public buildings to ensure critical service delivery during and following an extreme climate event, ultimately improving disaster risk management under changing climatic conditions. This will be accomplished through inter alia:

(i) supporting the mainstreaming of adaptation for the building sector into relevant national policies and standards;

(ii) building institutional and technical capacity to identify, implement, maintain and upscale climate-proofing interventions; and

(iii)climate-proofing existing public buildings to ensure that critical services remain operational during an extreme climate event, with reduced damages to buildings resulting in reduced maintenance costs in the long term.

The project will increase the resilience of the building sector and strengthen disaster risk reduction (DRR) capacity within the GoAB. The project is expected to reduce the vulnerability of the population to extreme climate events by 20%.

## 5.2 This project also seeks to use some of its lessons learned to inform the enhancement of the Sustainable Island Resource Framework (SIRF) Fund. The SIRF Fund is a financing mechanism established under the Environmental Protection and Management Act (EPMA).18

## Methodology

The NDC GBA is currently in development; however, the GCF Readiness 4 GBA was designed using the GCF gender analysis template and the data needs of the department's climate change project pipeline.

The GCF Readiness 4 GBA was deployed on three levels with the aim of obtaining at least 1,100 responses:

- A national survey targeted to reach 500 persons on mainland Antigua
- Community surveys targeting 500 residents in both rural and urban communities that have been identified areas as most vulnerable to sea level rise, storm surges related hazards and flooding. These communities were McKee's Corners, York, Callow Hill, Urtinga, Wilkins and Bethelands.
- A survey targeting 100 residents in Barbuda

The GCF Readiness 4 Survey was developed using Kobo Toolbox software, which will allow for data collection. The collections exercises are being administered by trained enumerators using the Kobo Toolbox software to facilitate ease of data collection and analysis.

All interviews are conducted for 30-60 minute telephone calls and adopt mixed sampling methodologies:

- Simple random sampling is being used with the telephone director as the sampling frame.
- This method was chosen because it allows for a more representative sample in response to COVID 19
- However, stratified sampling was also adopted to ensure equal representation by parish, age and gender. As a quality check, the gender proportionality of respondents was checked against the population every 50 surveys that were completed, so as to avoid skewing data.
- To continue for isolated islands, a snowball method was developed to acquire contact information and referrals to other respondents. The sampling unit was based on households in these areas.

The assessments will also make use of secondary data generated by national statistics and reports as well as other studies conducted by the DOE and other national agencies.
