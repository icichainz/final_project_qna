### 2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max summary (300 words))
Ecuador is finalizing its REDD+ readiness process. A Forest Reference Emission Level (FREL) was submitted and technical assistance provided to support the development of a National Action Plan (NAP). The project document presents the policies and measures prioritized to address the drivers of deforestation? It has a national scope and includes the 5 REDD+ activities. It is designed to support the national objective of achieving zero net deforestation by 2020.

The GCF project will co-finance the REDD+ AP by providing targeted investment to control agricultural expansion into forest areas, optimize existing financial, economic mechanisms to implement agricultural and livestock production practices that are less harmful to forests, promote sustainable land use management in vulnerable watersheds, restore, conservation and sustainable production in vulnerable watersheds, release public credit lines to orient them towards the promotion of agroforestry systems, support the development of forest-based enterprises activities, and strengthen purchasing policies for deforestation-free commodities, their certification and traceability.

The emission reductions that Ecuador will achieve by implementing its REDD+ AP during the GCF project's lifetime (2017-2022) are assessed in 2018, 2020, and 2022, through the Biennial Update Reports to the UNFCCC with respect to the FREL.

## B.1 Description of Financial Elements of the Project / Program

### The cost of implementing the REDD+ AP in Ecuador is estimated at USD 450 million by 2030.

The GCF input of USD 181.7 million will cover only a portion of the total costs, with co-financing leveraged by the GCF project stands at USD 62-84 million, while other complementary financing sources under joint programming stand at USD 74.76 million, and additional funding from the Ecuadorian government stands at USD 159.77 million, and contribute approximately 63% of the total estimated cost of implementation of the REDD+ AP.

### The justification for requesting a grant and not loan from the GCF for this proposal is based on the following elements: (i) the project has been subject to an extensive due diligence process as part of the Environmental and Social Impact Analysis, for the full financial evaluation of the project. The simulated project results under the conservative scenario show that the project will be economically positive with an economic net present value (ENPV) of USD 181.7 million; (ii) the project is financially feasible, with a debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) of 2.05 and an internal rate of return (IRR) of 9.3%; (iii) the project will not be adversely affected by the inclusion of REDD+ Results-Based Payments (RBP’s) since there are uncertainties associated with this mechanism, notably regarding prices, availability of funds and conditions of payments, among others.

### Second, all project components are targeting risk and information barriers via thematic framework expenditures. These include: (i) strengthening the capacity of public sector institutions to engage in dialogue with private actors/knowledge barriers (components 1 and 2 as well as sub-component 3.2); developing, implementing and monitoring climate policies to remove market barriers (sub-components 1.2 and 4.1); providing technical assistance to support the transition from RMB to commercial oil-cutting (sub-component 2.1); law enforcement (sub-component 1.2 and 1.3); and strengthening the capacity of public sector institutions to engage in dialogue with private actors/knowledge barriers (sub-components 5.4 and 1.1). Detailed information on the baseline scenario, including the business as usual scenario, the project scenario, and the alternative scenarios, along with the level of support requested of the GCF, are provided for each component and sub-component in Section C.3 of this proposal.

### Third, given the current state of Ecuador’s public finances, these public expenditures can only come from improved efficiency in the use of existing resources. The country is currently facing a fiscal deficit of USD 10 billion due to an earthquake, placing serious constraints on the Government's ability to finance environmental programmes. This combination of factors makes it impossible for the Government to provide any co-financing or other support that would not be an option to finance this project. Furthermore, given current strong budgetary restraints, this grant financing will not displace “business as usual” expenditures.

### The GCF investment will strengthen Land Use and Zoning Plans (PDZTs) to include climate change criteria, and will identify land uses appropriate for reducing deforestation and for reforestation. Including these considerations into the PDZTs will reduce on-site resources through mainstreaming of climate change criteria in the use of the PDZTs as a basis. This, together with the project activities that will enhance the uptake of land uses that can help to reduce deforestation, promote the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions and catalyze strong socio-economic co-benefits.

### To facilitate the update of these land uses in the short term, project investment will support producers to transition to more sustainable agricultural practices. This will be achieved by providing technical assistance for the transition during the transition / reforestation stage and reduce any adverse economic displacement that may occur. This support will be provided through a combination of direct financial support (sub-component 2.1), provision of deforestation-free products, thereby increasing income through access to markets for certified produce in the medium-term, and providing technical assistance to producers to help them manage their land more effectively in order to avoid loss of profit due to land-use change, and the high costs associated with certification and traceability of deforestation-free products.

### In non-forest areas identified as critical, and where agricultural production is not considered appropriate in land-use plans, there will be investment in restoration processes to enhance carbon stocks, and in conservation and forest management activities. # C1. Strategic Context

## General Background on Climate Change

### 14. The Government of Ecuador (GoE) fully acknowledges the impacts of climate change and has adopted its policy and institutional framework to comply with the goals and principles of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) and the Kyoto Protocol.

### 15. In 2008, the Government declared climate change mitigation and adaptation as a State Policy, committing to reducing greenhouse gas emissions by at least 36% below business-as-usual levels by 2020. The GoE has established mechanisms to direct national climate change policy and action under the Ministry of Environment (MAI). In 2010, the International Climate Change Fund (FCC) was created as a mechanism for financing projects in the country. The National Action Plan (NAP) is being developed to facilitate the execution of national policies on climate change and UNFCCC commitments.

### 16. In 2012, MAI launched the National Biodiversity Change Strategy (SNBC) 2012-2025.

## UNFCCC Decisions

### 17. Ecuador submitted its Second National Communication in 2011 and is preparing its third. The process of developing this document has been accompanied by a series of workshops with stakeholders to strengthen the national climate change endeavors, one of greater institutional coordination within a detailed agenda based on the National Development Plan ("Plan Nacional para el Buen Vivir").

### 18. Ecuador's Forest Reference Emission Level (FREL) was submitted to the Convention in December 2014 for inclusion in the LULUCF sector of the national inventory system. The FREL is based on the most accurate information available for estimating GHG emissions in the LULUCF sector and covers all Ecuador's continental territory. It only includes deforestation, which will be updated in the future. Following the decision of COP21 (Decision 1/CP.21).

### 19. According to Ecuador’s National FREL, on average 30,000 hectares/year were deforested in Ecuador between 2000 and 2008, and an additional 50,000 hectares/year from 2006 and 2008. During this period, 99% of the deforested areas were transformed into agricultural areas and less than 0.6% into infrastructure and other uses. The expansion of the agricultural frontier has been accompanied by a significant increase in emissions per hectare for most products; commodity production, notably beef, palm oil, cocoa and coffee. Average annual emissions from deforestation at the national level were 52.7 MtCO2eq between 1990 and 2000, and 43.4 MtCO2eq for the period 2000-2008.

## National REDD+ Action Plan

### 20. Data from the deforestation process (2012-2014), the direct and indirect drivers of deforestation in Ecuador were analyzed from economic, financial, political, social and technical perspectives. Historical patterns of deforestation were identified as well as the main drivers of deforestation. The results show that 95% of deforestation is due to agricultural expansion. Seven have been prioritized in the REDD+ Action Plan (AP), based on a multi-criteria analysis, including forest coverage, risks of deforestation, potential for increasing carbon stocks, and other non-carbon benefits.

### Land use planning policy

22. Decentralized governments (GADs) are responsible for formulating Development and Land Use Plans (PDUTAs), and ensuring their complementarity with one another and with existing Plans in the various levels of government. The PDUTA is a strategic document that sets out the vision, objectives, strategies, policies, and measures on human settlements, economic and production activities, and natural resource management with the characteristics of land use zoning and institutional arrangements. It also includes the identification of areas for conservation and regulation of land use, land-use change and occupation at a cantonal and/or district level, and are required to prepare public participation plans (PPPs) in accordance with the PPP Law. The PDUTA is a strategic document on land use and management tool, as they are formulated by indigenous people located in important areas of forest. Life Plans must be incorporated into the PDUTA.

### Transforming agricultural production

23. The agriculture, livestock and fishing sector is a key element of the national economy, accounting for 17% of total GDP (excluding the informal sector according to data from Anuario XIII). Permanent crops represent 20% of the total agricultural surface. Sugarcane, banana and African oil palm are the principal crops at the national level. This represents an increase in production over the last decade, but this has been achieved through expansion into new areas rather than increased yields. If the expansion continues, environmental pressure will increase.

24. The livestock region accounts for 17% of total agricultural surface, and occupies grounds (cultivated and natural) cover one million hectares (ha), with a herd of cattle (350,000 head), sheep (131,400 ha). Beef cattle are dominated by beef cattle (with 5.1 million head) and swine (with 1.2 million head); 15% of the national total of beef cattle is in the Amazon. Despite having opportunities under UNEP/CICOSA, the livestock sector has registered an increase in grassland surface cover in the last 10 years.

### Low productivity

25. Low productivity is the main challenge to the production of dairy and meat. Large areas of land are occupied; pastures are poorly utilized, and CO2 emissions per milk or meat unit are inversely proportional to the level of productivity.

### Product Certification

#### 29. 54. Component 2 supports the Agenda for Transforming Production in the Amazon (ATPA). The objective of sub-component 2.1 is to provide an economic incentive to support farmers through a credit guarantee scheme that will allow them to obtain financing for investments expected during the transition from a conventional to sustainable production process, thus allowing the new production system to grow at a faster rate and generate revenue more quickly than would otherwise be possible. The objective of sub-component 2.1 is also to provide an economic incentive to support farmers through a credit guarantee scheme that will allow them to obtain financing for investments expected during the transition from a conventional to sustainable production process, thus allowing the new production system to grow at a faster rate and generate revenue more quickly than would otherwise be possible.

**157.** Ecuador uses a stepwise approach for the development of its FREL in accordance with Decision 12/CP.17, paragraph 10. The stepwise approach allows Ecuador to improve the FREL by incorporating better data, methodologies and assumptions as more information becomes available. The three main steps are: (a) emission factors per forest type (taken from the National Forest Inventory); and (c) carbon content by forest-type. It also includes a reference level calculation based on historical deforestation rates. The methodologies used to calculate the emission factors, the annual averages of activity data and the FREL calculation per year.

**158.** The deforestation activity data used for the construction of the FREL were drawn from a historical time-series of land cover maps (1970–2000) developed by the National Forest Inventory. This approach is consistent with Approach 3 as described in the IPCC Good Practice Guidelines for the LULUCF Sector (IPCC, 2003). This approach takes into account geographic location and forest type to estimate change data for estimating activity data. Following this approach, land-use/land-cover maps were generated for the entire country using a grid of cells with representative categories of land use for the reference years 1990, 2000 and 2005.

**159.** To estimate historical emissions, Ecuador proposes to multiply the gross deforestation of each forest type by the specific emission factors per forest type. The emission factors are based on information about the carbon stocks for the 7 types of natural forests were obtained from the results of the National Forest Inventory. The emission factors also assume that the entire area of a given forest type is converted to agricultural land use, and the soil and vegetation in the forest and the leaf litter is assumed at the time of conversion. These assumptions are used since the information available on the carbon stocks for each forest type does not allow for more detailed estimates.

**160.** The stratification by forest-type, activity data and emission factors is consistent with the stratification used in the National Greenhouse Gases Inventory (GHG.I), which will be presented in the First Biennial Update Report Update (BUR) and the Second BUR (see Annex I of this Chapter).

#### E.6.4. Application of best practices

6. Expansion of agriculture and ranching is the main driver of deforestation in Ecuador. If farmers livestock are repressed (e.g., by reducing cattle head per hectare), and if forests are created to avoid expansion of the agricultural frontier (land zoning, social control, traceability, etc.), then farmers will have much less incentive to clear new land for agriculture or ranching. The project will support the development of best practices in livestock extension services, support to associations, and access to markets). In all cases, the project will ensure that best practices are applied in order to reduce deforestation: production, cattle ranching, reforestation of grasslands, silvo-pastoral and agricultural systems are summarized below:

- **Cacao produced using modern techniques integrated within the traditional 'chaaca' system.** Chaaca (the local word for a smallholder farm system) combines a number of crops as well as non-timber forest products (NTFPs). It is a realistic option to increase income from cacao production, and it can be used in combination with other activities that generate income to fit them out of poverty. The best practice is to integrate cacao into the chaaca system, which will increase productivity and profitability. This will also allow for better management of soil fertility and water resources, which can be complemented by other crops. The introduction of timber shade trees can also benefit the chaaca, growing into a significant source of income.
- **Improved traditional cattle ranching practices.** Livestock production is usually very inefficient, both in the case of large and small producers. Many farmers have only one cattle head per hectare, and cattle typically compact lands and consume a lot of feed. The best practice will be to improve livestock management by increasing the stocking rate. This practice that will be supported by the project is to divide the participating farmers into many (20 or 30) small lots and control their access to pasture. In this way, each farmer can have more cattle heads per hectare. At the same time, way, cattle will not overgraze and compact land, and will gain weight more rapidly and produce better quality milk. Ranchers can sustain up to 4-5 head of cattle per hectare in this system.
- **Sheep production as a best practice is to turn non-reproductive cattle producers into much more productive livestock producers, where cattle revenues provide farmers with a constant cash-flow while the trees grow.** Trees Provide shade to the cattle, reduce heat stress, maintain soil moisture and allow grass to grow faster in some instances. Sheep can be introduced on pasture that has been abandoned by cattle or is not suitable for cattle raising efficiency on existing farms, and also to reforest production landscapes
