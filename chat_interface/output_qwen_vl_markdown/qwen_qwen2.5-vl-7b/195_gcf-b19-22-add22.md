# B.1. Description of Financial Elements of the Project / Programme

The Government of the Republic of Zambia (GRZ) aims at improving the conditions for renewable energy (RE) independent power producers (IPPs) to invest in and operate projects in the mini-grid and off-grid energy sector. The GETFIT (Global Energy Transfer Feed-in-Tariff) Zambia program, whose design has been supported by the KfW Development Bank, is a feed-in-tariff (FiT) scheme for renewable energy projects launched in October 2017. The REFIT strategy has an overall goal of promoting diversification of the power sector by adding new generation capacity and reducing the share of coal-fired power plants in the national electricity mix to meet the country’s NDC targets. The initial objective is to bring 200 megawatts (MW) of renewable energy onto the grid within the next 3-5 years, through the development of small-to-medium-scale IPP projects between 1 MW and 50 MW.

The REFIT program will be implemented in two phases: phase one will cover up to five small hydro projects with a combined capacity of 20 megawatts (MW) that will be based on competitive bidding. A total 100 MW solar PV will be procured through a competitive tender call for small hydro projects (scheduled in mid-2018). Successful bidders will sign a Standardized Power Purchase Agreement (SPPA) with the Zambia Electricity Supply Corporation (ZESCO), in line with the Ministry of Energy and Water Development and the Ministry of Finance of the GRZ. In addition the GETFIT program is supported by capacity enhancement sector regulations, licensing procedures and grid planning through technical assistance (TA) funded by KfW.

This framework targets to mobilize a financing package for upcoming RE IPP projects in Zambia identified under the GET FIT Zambia program. The framework will be implemented in two phases: phase one will cover up to five small-scale (up to 20 MW) solar PV projects will be selected through a competitive bidding process by Q2 2018 with the expected financial close by Q3 2018. It is targeted that most of them will be financed by the framework. The funding package for phase one will cover up to 75% of the project costs, subject to availability of funds. The framework may finance a limited number of small hydro projects from the second phase (i.e. up to 20% of the total).

Lack of sufficient private financing and short tenor (5-7 years from local commercial investors) are pointed out as key barriers for RE project financing in many Sub-Saharan African countries including Zambia. Participation of local commercial banks is limited due to their lack of experience with renewable energy projects, limited knowledge about these projects, little experience in the sector, and high risk perception. Under this framework, the ADB financing scheme will primarily bridge the gap between the long-term nature of RE projects and the short tenor of loans from local commercial banks.

An important feature of the scheme is that it leverages a tenor extension instrument (standby loans) from the ADB and the Green Climate Fund (GCF). The GCF standby loan will be provided to the ADB as an equity contribution. The amount of commercial banks to the loans for each sub-project will be determined on the basis of their hard currency liquidity availability and the terms offered, while the final arrangement will be up to the decision of selected project sponsors.

Further, the framework proposes that the ADB will provide a senior debt facility (up to 30% of total project cost) with long-term financing for development projects, which form paradigm-shifting in the context of the region. This financial structure is expected to attract private sector participation and reduce the risk perception associated with RE projects. The loans under this scheme will be offered to eligible banks for subsequent rounds, and it enhance the country ownership as it passes on the responsibility of financing to local commercial banks.

Under this scheme, it is proposed that the ADB will provide senior debt and standby loans to finance each of the selected and interested IPPs (the ADB exposure is to be limited up to one-third (33%) of the total project cost — USD 50 million for a 150 MW solar PV project). The loan terms are as follows: interest rate at LIBOR + 2.75%, maturity period of ten years, a pool of commercial banks (backed by a standby loan) and the national pension fund managed by the Zambian National Pension Scheme Fund (NAPFUND) (proposed). The GCF’s contribution will be passed on to the ADB, therefore its total exposure from this scheme is expected to be equal to one-third (33%) of the total project cost.

In addition, as a separate intervention, technical assistance (TA) grants up to USD 4 million, from the GCF and the ADB, will be provided in order to assist the public sector Ministry of Energy and Water Development; Rural Electrification Authority, etc.) to create enabling environment for private investments in the mini-grid and off-grid sector; and 2) to build the capacity of selected local financial institutions and the entire Zambian financial industry for its enhanced

## Economic situation: Zambia has a population of 16.5 million and a GDP per capita stands at USD $1,798 (in 2013 current US dollars). The country’s real GDP growth rate was estimated to be around 4% in 2011 (real per capita growth at 4.2%), and as a result the country has been upgraded to a Lower Middle Income Country status in 2011 (World Bank classification). In recent years, however, the public debt level has risen as borrowings for infrastructure projects have increased. The government’s fiscal deficit is estimated to be around 5% of GDP, which is financed by several additional factors: steep commodity price drop (Zambia is Africa's second-biggest copper producer); rising cost of imported inputs; and a decline in the performance of the agricultural sector. Inflation has also risen sharply (Keacha) in 2015 increasing the level of external debt services in kwacha terms to an estimated 17% of domestic resources available for servicing these debts. The country’s current account deficit is projected at USD $3 billion, and Zambia's economic outlook from negative to positive with a stable outlook). (August 2017). Access to finance therefore remains limited as the government has been unable to access international markets due to its high debt levels. The IMF facility for up to $1.3 billion to help strengthen the foreign reserves.

Despite these challenges, Zambia’s economic growth is still constrained on the economic and social front (ranked 139th in the Human Development Index). As described above, Zambia is facing an economic challenge with substantial public debt (60% of the GDP) and budget deficit (8.5% of the GDP), which sustains a primary fiscal imbalance. The country’s economy has been hit by the effects of the 2014-2015 regional drought crisis caused huge payment burdens from electricity imports, worsening the financial position of the state-owned utility ZESCO. In addition, the government is facing increasing pressure to increase spending on social services and electricity will add more pressure to the utility to expand the power supply base. When the country struggles with low electricity access (30% in urban areas and 15% in rural areas) and high energy prices, it is important that a mix of terms of investment and policy support are imperative in order to address the systemic weakness and unlock the potential for sustainable development. The GETF will thus be critical to guide the country’s development toward a low-carbon path rather than business-as-usual.

# H.1. Logic Framework

## H.1.1. Paradigm Shift Objectives and Impacts at the Fund Level[1]

### Paradigm shift objectives

The ARBSCGF framework aims to deliver 100 MW of power thereby reducing the deficit in Zambia with sustainable energy options that fossil fuel-based generation currently used to bridge the gap. Deployment of additional generation capacity, coupled with the TA to provide technical assistance and financial support for the development of renewable energy projects excluded from the grid. The diversification of the energy mix will improve the resilience of the energy system against shocks and stresses. The project is expected to make a critical contribution in fast-tracking Zambia’s transition toward a low-emission sustainable development pathway.

### Shift to low emission sustainable development pathways

Zambia is one of the highest GHGs emitter in Sub-Saharan Africa and as such intervention is timely and the expected climate mitigation impact is high. Given the ambitious targets set by the Paris Agreement, Zambia has committed to reducing its emissions intensity by 40% below business-as-usual levels by 2030. The project is expected to make a critical contribution in fast-tracking Zambia’s transition toward a low-emission sustainable development pathway. The project will also contribute towards curtail the use of charcoal and firewood for cooking, reducing emission from deforestation and land use change which is a major source of GHGs emissions in Zambia.

The framework will be set as a best case for a RE financing model that creates local commercial investment opportunities, supports private sector, and sustains itself within the local financial sector. By doing so, local financial institutions will gain the opportunity to build their own renewable energy portfolio. The project will also demonstrate the impact of a well-managed demonstration effect; the sector would be able to gradually rely on local financial institutions for financing. The project will also contribute towards the development and implementation by the national pension fund (NAPFES) will demonstrate how pension funds and institutional investors can support the development of renewable energy projects through blended financing, and set the bar for the community. Strengthening conditions for private investment in the sector is expected to lead to a significant increase in the deployment of decentralized energy solutions across the country. This paradigm-shifting experience will be scalable across Zambia and replicable in other countries in Africa.
