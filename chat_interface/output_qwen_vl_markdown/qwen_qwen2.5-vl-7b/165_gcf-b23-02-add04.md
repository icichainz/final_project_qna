# Box 1: Estimates of non-CO2 emissions of greenhouse gases

The emission of the non-CO2 gases Methane (CH4) and Nitrous oxide (N2O) has been calculated assuming that all deforestation processes in Ecuador are accompanied by slash-and-burn. The emissions were estimated by applying the equation described in the IPCC Good Practice Guidance for Land Use, Land-Use Change and Forestry.

## Estimation of GHGs directly released in fires

| Lfire | = A × B × C × D × 10^-6 |
|---|---|
| Where: | |
| Lfire | quantity of GHG released due to fire, tons of GHG |
| A. | area burned, ha |
| B. | mass of available fuel, kg d.m./ha |
| C. | combustion efficiency, dimensionless |
| D. | emission factor, g CO2-e/g |

The values applied for combustion efficiency and emission factors were obtained from the 2006 IPCC Guidelines for National Greenhouse Gas Inventories since they are the most updated available data. The default value of CH4 and N2O to CO2 in the global warming potential (GWP) was used.

### Methodology

The methodology applied for registration in the registry of Emission Reductions for Payments by Results is described below (see Annex XVIII for a view of the excel based tool).

Data on the Available Emission Reduction (ICD) eq column for the period 2009-2014 have been published and correspond to the following equation:

ERx = NREF_Dwcorr_i * L_i - Ecorr_i

Where

* **ERx:** Emission reductions available for national-level-based payments following UNFCCC processes in year t, expressed in ktCO2 eq.
* **NREF_Dwcorr_i:** National Forest Reference Emission Level for Deforestation, technically assessed through UNFCCC processes and applied to the country in year t, expressed in kgCO2 eq.
* **Ecorr_i:** Gross emissions from deforestation reported to the UNFCCC at national level, estimated in year t, expressed in ktCO2 eq.

t = Year of the period of application of these rules.

By applying this equation, we can determine the volume of results available for any given year. Each of these tonnes is then assigned a serial number (e.g., EC-RERemu-Year-0001). The serial numbers function as follows:

* **Each serial number characterizes the status for each tonne of carbon per year for registration:** The Status may be "Available" (A), "Renumerated" (RERem) or "Deactivated/Retired" (REDesc).
    * i) EC-RERemu-Year-000X
    * ii) EC-RERem-Yr-000X
    * iii) EC-REDesp-Year-000X

* **Month / Year is the serial number is the date of ER registration recognized by payment for results:** This date corresponds to the month and year in which the payment of the total or partial volume of ER is registered.
* **EC corresponds to the first initials of the country that has generated the reduction of emissions.**

# D.1 Impact potential

Describe the potential of the programme to contribute to the achievement of the Fund’s objectives and results areas.

All the policies and measures of the National REDD+ AP have been identified through studies and consultations during the readiness phase for their potential to address the drivers of deforestation and forest degradation, as well as to support the national objective of achieving zero net deforestation. The implementation of these policies has already led to emission reductions on 28,990 tCO2e (over a period from January 1st, 2015 until December 31st, 2017). This is equivalent to the emissions avoided by planting approximately 46.4 million trees. The policies and measures of the National REDD+ AP are aligned with the objectives of the Action Plan are very significant. As such it is clear that the project offers great potential to contribute towards the achievement of the Fund’s objectives.

It is important to note that attribution of reduced emissions from deforestation to a single policy or measure is feasible only if the policy or measure has been implemented at national scale and can be linked directly between a specific project component and emission reductions. Further, emission reductions result from a series of measures and policies that are implemented in different sectors and across the landscape. Therefore, it is important for countries to consider how they will attribute emissions reductions within the field (e.g. sub-tenders) to farmers. Furthermore, individual policies and measures can pose a risk of double counting if they are not properly designed and implemented at national scale. For example, emission reductions from forest restoration activities across the landscape could simply displace emissions outside of the areas covered by the program canceling off the mitigation effect. Therefore, it is important to ensure that the project design and implementation align with the FCPF methodological framework. Furthermore, it is very challenging to achieve for national scale projects to be aligned with the FCPF methodology as the project design and intensity usually differ at national versus project scale. For example, an emission factor based on national scale data may not be appropriate when applied in a sub-tender or project scale given that the statistical data to gather that date was designed to ensure significance at the national scale.

Ecuador deals with these conceptual and technical issues by implementing REDD+ plus on a national scale. Having a single policy and measure implemented at national scale allows for the attribution of emission reductions within the national territory and avoids consistency issues across scale by focusing on ensuring that REDD-plus results are consistent, verified and certified at the national scale in line with UNFCCC requirements outlined in the Warsaw Framework.

The REDD-plus Action Plan is a multi-faceted initiative to achieve results at the national scale. Ecuador will use one major policy measure (the National Forest Law) as well as several other measures that are complementary to it, and which address some of the challenges mentioned above. It is not possible to directly attribute emission reductions to any single action or policy measure in a consistent manner across all sub-tenders. Therefore, the results achieved by Ecuador will be reported and verified at the national scale only.

The REDD-plus Action Plan is aligned with the UNFCCC Warsaw Framework for REDD-plus. The amount of CO2eq reduced or avoided (including increased removals) as a result of Funded-Readiness/Implementation measures should be reported in accordance with the UNFCCC Warsaw Framework for REDD-plus, which is based on the Forest Carbon Partnership Facility Methodological Framework (Dec 2013). UN RED and emerging United Nations member states are also aligning their national REDD+ strategies with this framework.

As per the UNFCCC Warsaw Framework for REDD-plus, the exact amount of emission reductions that Ecuador will achieve by implementing its REDD+ AP at the national scale, during the implementation of these processes (2015-2030) is not known. The results achieved in 2018 and beyond are expected to be published on the Lima REDD-plus Platform (www.limaredplus.org) for the years 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2024. These results will be compared with FREL-2. This information will be published on the Lima REDD-plus Platform (www.limaredplus.org) and will be available for download.

The UNFCCC Warsaw framework for REDD-plus does not require, nor provide a methodology for attribution of emission reductions to a specific measure or action or donor. Furthermore, attribution of national emission reductions to a single measure, a single project or a single funding source can be flawed because of the risk of:

1) Double counting: This is when an emission reduction is attributed to more than one policy or measure.
2) Incomplete accounting: This occurs if the full extent of the impact of a policy or measure on emissions reductions is not captured. For example, if a project only accounts for the direct impacts and does not account for indirect effects (e.g., displacement).
3) Misattribution: This happens when an emission reduction is attributed to the wrong policy or measure.
4) Incomplete deployment outside the Accounting Area: The extent of the impact on emissions reductions that are deployed outside the Accounting Area can be difficult to estimate.

### 2.5 Land tenure and rights

In Ecuador, agrarian reform was initiated in 1964, and aimed at the expropriation of unexploited lands and the colonization of wastelands. Under this reform, land was granted in usufruct to cooperatives or individuals who were not landowners but had a minimum level of experience as agriculture and who did not own land or had very small enclaves. In 1973, the Law of Integral Agrarian Reform maintained these provisions, with which women were, in most cases, excluded from the abolition of feudalism and the distribution of lands to peasants.

This was supported by Civil Code provisions at that time stipulating that the husband was the administrator of the marital property. However, over time the land rights for women have slowly improved. Under the current Constitution (1998), women are considered equal in all matters related to civil law and, as such, men and are equal and shall enjoy the same rights, duties and opportunities in access to property. It is noted that a woman can now inherit her husband's land without losing her own privileges and inequalities in access to land, so it will avoid concentration and hoarding of land.

Nevertheless, there are discrepancies between the provisions of the Civil Code and customary rules. In practice, lands titled in the husband’s name remain at the husband’s disposal, even if they were purchased during the term of the conjugal union. An it can often be the case that women do not know their rights to property or land. This is due to the fact that in Ecuador, the law does not recognize the names of both spouses as not registered in the title of the land. And although the sale of real estate requires the signature of the spouse, this provision often gets ignored. As a consequence, women lose access to their own property and rights. In addition, it is noted that women are more likely to be owners of land by women and joint ownership by couples is much more frequent in the sierra, with a greater indigenous population than on the coast, where the land belongs mostly to men.

In terms of community ownership of land, the mechanisms of participation and representation of the land does not promote the participation of women, but rather only of male leaders. Therefore, the exercise of land ownership by women is visible in only very few cases.
