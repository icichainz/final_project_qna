### A.2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

Climate change is a major driver of water scarcity in the Salis basin, driven by a combination of population growth, economic development, a strong decline in precipitation due to climate change and unsustainable groundwater use. Water availability in the Salis Plain has fallen from an estimated 125 million cubic meters (m³) per year in 2003 to approximately 68 m³ per year today, falling to 490 m³ in 2020, below the United Nations' "absolute water scarcity" level. In this context of scarcity, the gaps between offer and demand are expected to increase further. Currently, the water demand for irrigation is estimated at 13.7 m³, from both surface water mobilized (11.8 m³) and groundwater (1.9 m³). The total available water supply in the Salis basin is estimated to be 36.2 m³, and therefore, if no measures are taken, the gap will be 45.5 m³. This poses a severe threat to agricultural production and food security in the Salis Plain, where irrigation is used for agricultural purposes.

In response, the main objective of this Project is to improve climate resilience in irrigation infrastructure and services by (i) increasing water availability and unpredictability in rainfall resulting in unsustainable groundwater use; and (ii) low adoption of water efficient technologies. The Project will address these challenges through a multi-faceted approach that includes: (1) shifting from the paradigm of water provision for the Salis irrigation system, starting with highly unsustainable groundwater use to sustainable surface water resources; (2) building a bulk water transfer scheme from the M'Dez dam to the Salis Plain in Northern Morocco; and (3) strengthening institutional capacity at all levels. This will be achieved by improving water management in governance networks. This critical infrastructure will facilitate transfer of water from a river basin with a surplus of water to the Sebou-Salis basin, which is currently experiencing a deficit. The Project will also improve community involvement in water governance by scale up technical skills and institutional capacity at all levels.

This will increase the efficiency of water use and services, promoting drip irrigation and modern water demand management methods, thus strengthening capacity for adaptation to climate change in the Sebou-Salis basin

The specific outcomes of this Project are:

1. To increase climate resilience of irrigation infrastructure through developing and strengthening the technical and institutional capacities needed to undertake infrastructure investments and improve irrigation system management in a strategic manner that will lead to improved water use efficiency, increased productivity and income generation, building long-term climate resilience;
2. To transfer bulk water from the Sebou basin to the Salis Plain in water management in governance networks at all levels of water users; and
3. To strengthen institutional capacity for adaptation in the design, implementation, operation and maintenance of irrigation infrastructure and the introduction and promotion of best-practice, efficient irrigation techniques with a significant demonstration impact across sector and region.

The specific outcomes of the Project will be:

1. Strengthening technical and regulatory systems for climate-responsive planning and development in the irrigation sector in the Salis Plain;
2. Strengthening water supply capacity and reduced exposure to climate risks related to water use for irrigation amongst farmers (both commercial and subsistence) and communities in the Salis plain; and
3. Strengthening institutional capacity at all levels of governance networks, including technical and regulatory processes amongst farmers, communities and the wider population in the Salis Plain.

The Project is expected to result in improved and more climate-resilient irrigation services for 2,849 farms, and to increase the extent of irrigated agricultural land in the Salis plain from 145,400 ha to 216,000 ha.

## Description of Financial Elements of the Project / Programme

The Salinas de Sebo Irrigation Conservation Project will change the paradigm of water provisions for the Salas irrigation system, switching from highly unsustainable groundwater (the Sebas/Salos basin) to more sustainable surface water resources (made available from the M'Der dam). It will strengthen the climate resilience of agricultural production and safeguard livelihoods in the Salas Plain (located in the northwestern part of Morocco), which is currently under threat due to a combination of climate change, population growth, and infrastructure delivery mechanisms, including preparations for a Public Private Partnership (PPP) and promoting irrigation governance and community participation. The project will be implemented over 10 years from 2018-2027.

The Salinas de Sebo Irrigation System has experienced unsustainable levels of water exploitation with a net loss of 100 million m³ per year, with over 82% of the water being extracted from groundwater. At current extraction rates, the Salas aquifer will be completely depleted within 25 years resulting in severe social and economic consequences.

In response to these severe challenges, this Project aims to enable water users and agricultural production in the Salas Plain to make the transformational shift away from current irrigation practices which are based on an unsustainable dependence on groundwater. The project will implement a new surface water supply scheme for the Salas Plain, thus making possible the annual transfer of 90-120 million m³ of surface water from the M'Der dam to the Salas Plain (including the construction of a new canal). This will be achieved through the implementation of a series of complementary and co-investment infrastructure investments to support the transition towards more sustainable and climate resilient water service delivery, and modernizing irrigation infrastructure in the Salas Plain. The project is aligned with the objectives set out by the Plan Maroc Vert (PMV) from 2008 to 2030, a major initiative and a guiding framework for addressing impacts of climate change on agriculture and rural development.

The project will be implemented under the leadership of the Ministry of Water Resources, in line with national priorities and with other financing initiatives in the irrigation sector, and of the National Water Conservation Programme (PNEE).

The indicative total financial package of the Project amounts to EUR 203.8 million, of which EUR 120 million loan finance will be provided by the EBRD and a further EUR 0.8 million grant finance from EBRD Donor Funds. An additional EUR 13.97 million will be provided as co-investment from the Ministry of Water Resources, and EUR 65.4 million will be provided in co-financing from the Kingdom of Morocco. The level of grant for the Project directly impacts the size of the investment from private sector financing partners (EUR 0.8 million). This is because the project has been designed to meet the criteria set out by the EBRD and its Donor Funds.

It is also justified by the avoided externalities related to the depletion of the Salas aquifer, such as economic slowdowns, migration to cities, job destruction, total depopulation of the Sabas aquifer, environmental consequences etc.

Experience of irrigation projects in Morocco confirms that an appropriate blend of loan and grant resources is needed due to the different financial instruments required for each type of project. The grant will be used to cover the co-investment. Bending the financial instruments (GCF grant resources, EBRD loans, and private sector finance) will guarantee the affordability of water for farmers as demand increases in the future. This will also ensure that the project is financially sustainable over time by addressing financial needs to face infrastructure projects, and in which its debt is growing; the Salas project, given the size (almost half a billion euros), will be one of the largest irrigation projects ever implemented in Morocco.

The financing gap for this climate resilient Project would not allow its implementation in due time to avoid the complete depletion of the Sebas-Salos basin, posing a severe threat to agricultural production and rural livelihoods in the Salas Plain.


## Technical assistance for capacity development and policy advice

The proposed Project will also include a technical assistance component for capacity development and policy advice. It is anticipated that EUR 10 million will be provided as grant resources amounting to EUR 2.85 million, which will be provided by the GCF (EUR 1.97 million) and the EBRD Donor Funds (EUR 0.88 million). These activities will cover skills transfer; capacity building and development of activities that will deliver improved public good services in irrigation governance and improved management for the sustainable use of water resources, including infrastructure, including the integration of climate resilience into the management and delivery of irrigation infrastructure and services. As these elements are not revenue generating, the use of grant resources is highly appropriate.

### Investment in transformational infrastructure

The elements of the Project that support the integration of approaches, technologies and design standards that promote sustainable water resource management and climate resilience in the planning and management of irrigation infrastructure will be drawn on detailed technical guidance on structuring irrigation systems investment that has been developed by the EBRD and the World Bank. The financing for this project is expected to come from a combination of grant resources from the GCF, EBRD and the Kingdom of Morocco amounting to a total of EUR 203.8 million.

This Project component is anticipated to include grant resources of EUR 10.0 million from the GCF which will be used to co-finance the development of an irrigation infrastructure in the Sebou-Saïs basin, which will provide water for agriculture and infrastructure enabling introduction of new irrigation networks by the private sector. This infrastructure is essential for improving water resource management and climate resilience in the Sebou-Saïs water basin, where chronic water scarcity is very high. The project will also include a component to support the development of an irrigation system that will be critical for the climate change and unsustainable groundwater use. This poses a severe threat to agricultural production and rural livelihoods, as well as the environment in general. The GCF funding resources from the World Bank/IFC will be used to co-finance the Project which will support conservation of the endangered aquifer and water resources of the Sebou-Saïs basin, and will help prepare a Public- Private Partnership (PPP) framework for the project. This PPP framework is critical in order to ensure that the private sector can generate revenues; it would be extremely difficult to finance it using non-grant resources, especially considering country’s budget constraints.

The Project directly impacts the size of the investment from the private sector, and consequently the final tariff, which should be affordable to farmers but also allow the private sector to make profits. The GCF funding is not foreseen to directly impact the level of tariffs as it will be used for co-financing the development of an irrigation infrastructure in the Sebou-Saïs basin under a Contract. Furthermore, it should be noted that the level of tariffs will be impacted by the level of public subsidies (EUR 112 million) which are foreseen to be provided by the Moroccan government as part of the financing for this project through a power contract with the support of international advisors. The feasibility Study included a financial analysis which assessed in particular two scenarios: (i) a scenario where the private sector would not have access to any public subsidies and (ii) a scenario where it worked both on principles: (a) it is tariffable affordable to the farmers and (b) it is contract economically and financially viable with the Moroccan government. The feasibility study concluded that the level of tariffs which are foreseen under the financing described above is the total amount of that the private sector would invest in the project, and consequently the total public subsidy needed for the project to take place. These calculations were done by the MAMIF and its advisers and are not available as new information was provided during the feasibility study.

It is important to note that this approach was successfully designed and implemented with the support of the World Bank/ IFC for another project in Morocco, and a corresponding financing structure has been agreed upon. The same approach will be used here.

The total amount of the investment component of the Project will be financed from the EBRD loan finance of EUR 120 million and the Kingdom of Morocco co-financing of EUR 53.82 million. The loan finance resources will be used to finance the section of the main distribution pipeline that is required for the project, which includes the construction of a new pumping station and supplying 12 primary pipes and potentially part of the main transfer pipeline starting from the intake and conveying water to the main distribution pipeline. This infrastructure is critical as it is a required condition for private sector participation in the original PPP framework. The EBRD loan finance will be used to finance this section only if the project is not delayed. The Project will also support MAMIF to structure a public-private partnership (PPP) scheme for the maintenance and operation of the infrastructure, which will be critical in order to ensure that the private sector can generate revenues from it and ultimately the end-users of the water. This additional infrastructure, costed at approximately EUR 140 million, is to be financed by public subsidies provided to farmers under the PPP scheme formula upon the completion of this Project. These costs are therefore not included in this Project.

# C1. Strategic Context

Water stress and climate change vulnerability: Morocco is a dry country characterised by limited and uneven rainfall. Morocco experiences chronic water scarcity, with only 879 m³ total annual renewable water resources per capita, but with regional variations from less than 200 to over 1300 m³ per person. The majority of the population (65%) depends on groundwater as a water resource is withdrawn, with the bulk (88%) being used for agricultural irrigation. The biggest challenge is not the average quantity, but the uneven distribution and the lack of infrastructure to manage it efficiently. This has led to significant environmental degradation and production impacts. Morocco's extreme water scarcity is being further exacerbated by the impacts of climate change and by unsustainable use of groundwater resources. The country faces a serious risk of food insecurity due to its dependence on agriculture with inefficient water use and management. The extreme vulnerability of Morocco’s agriculture sector to climate change impacts and the need for adaptation are highlighted in the National Adaptation Plan (NAP) of Morocco, as well as in the Second Communication to the UNFCCC (2010 and 2015 respectively) and the Independently Determined National Contribution (2015).

## Legal framework protecting water resources: Morocco is often regarded as a progressive example of the MENA region in its water policy and irrigation investments. The central legislation for water is the Law on Water (1995), which is in need of revision, to provide more flexibility and adaptability to the changing needs of society and the environment while promoting sustainable groundwater management.

Since 2009 there has been a dedicated Water Strategy in place including quantitative targets and Programmes for Wastewater and Irrigation Management. An important element of this strategy is the creation of water user associations (WUA) at river basin authorities. Implementation of some of the provisions is slower than expected, for example the polluter/user pays principle.

## Institutions managing and protecting scarce water resources: The Ministry of Energy, Mines, Water and Environment with a dedicated Department for Water is responsible for planning and coordinating the protection of water resources with a focus on pollution prevention and control. It also manages the national water fund (NWF) which finances projects in the public interest and principal water user. River basin authorities (in total 5) responsible for water abstraction and discharge authorization have been introduced and should be financially independent through water taxes from the local water service providers, but the financial arrangements are still under discussion. The High Council of Water and Climate coordinates inter-ministerial consultation and stakeholder dialogue.

### Irrigation tariffs: The regional bodies of the Ministry for Agriculture and Fishing are carrying out the infrastructure investments and are setting the irrigation tariffs, an arrangement dating back to 1960. Agricultural water users pay 40% of the total costs in irrigated areas (the remaining 60% is covered by the state). The tariff is based on volume and is calculated as a volumetric fee for abstraction. The volumetric fee was last increased in 2009 and is regularly adjusted for inflation.

## Plan Maroc Vert – a strategy aimed at protecting scarce water resources for agriculture sector: In order to address the serious threats of climate change, Morocco has developed a national strategy called "Plan Maroc Vert" (PMV). The PMV was adopted the Green Moroccan Plan ("Plan Maroc Vert", the "PMV"), a strategic plan for the period 2008-2020 with a focus on improving productivity, quality, sanitary security, and product competitiveness within the agriculture sector. The PMV aims at building a sustainable agricultural sector that is competitive in terms of production costs and environmental performance. The overall contribution of agriculture to Morocco's GDP from EUR 6.4 billion to EUR 12 billion, double the agriculture sector value added and more than triple its share in total employment. The PMV was divided into two phases: Phase I (2008-2015) and Phase II (2016-2020). Measures to increase the agricultural productivity by up to 59% till 2030 were identified as: intensification of production, extension of cultivated areas, diversification of crops, and improvement in the quality of products. The PMV also sets out a series of measures with the objective of promoting increase in the productivity of the agriculture; the PMV addresses climate change, overexploitation of groundwater, and alleviation of poverty. The strategy is divided into two pillars:

### Project Overview

The proposed Project is part of the SMAS project located in the Sebou/Saïs basin which represents 12% of Morocco's total annual water endowment. Over 87% of the water of the Sebou/Saïs basin has been used for agriculture and it is estimated that in the last 15 years, the Saïs water table has been depleted by around 100 million m³ per year. This depletion is the result of a sustainable use of groundwater for irrigation purposes.

The unsustainable use of groundwater for irrigation purposes leads to the unsuitable use of groundwater for irrigation purposes. The overexploitation of the aquifer, in a country severely impacted by climate change and desertification, has led to significant environmental impacts. To address these issues, the government developed as part of the Morocco Green Plan (TMAY, 2008-2020) a strategy aimed at mobilizing surface water and at protecting water resources.

The proposed Project is part of a staged programme designed to transfer annually between 90 and 110 million m³ of surface water from the M'der Basin to the Saïs plain for irrigation purposes. The following three key phases can be distinguished:

- Phase 1: Preparation (2017–2021) – Beneficiaries will regain EBRD loan financing.
- Phase 2: Repayment (2017–2031) – Beneficiaries will repay EBRD loan financing.
- Phase 3: Operation (2022–2052) – The project will be fully operational, initially under a PPP arrangement lasting 30 years.

The water ultimately will be distributed through a PPP that which MAMF will select a private actor (co-to finance, design, construct and operate the infrastructure). The Project will also include capacity development and investment in a strategic water transfer scheme in the Saïs plain to enable sustainable use of surface water resources. The project will contribute to the resilience of the basin by enabling the transition from unsustainable groundwater use to the use of sustainable and climate resilient surface water resources (made available from the M'der Basin). It will allow the restoration of the situation of the Saïs aquifer and contribute to the climate resilience of the Saïs plain.

The proposed Project will have three comprehensive components, as described below:

- Component 1: Strengthening institutional resilience in the original basin through technical and institutional capacities development and investment in a strategic water transfer scheme in the Saïs Plain.
- Component 2: Promoting private sector involvement in water governance and improved awareness of climate resilience issues among users of water resources.
- Component 3: Scale up private sector involvement in the design, implementation, operation and maintenance of irrigation infrastructure with a significant demonstration impact across sector and region.

On top of its main purpose, which is to provide water for irrigation, the Souss Dam will also serve as a flood control dam. It will be located between the cities of Essaouira and Agadir.

The Sidi El Othman Dam was constructed in two phases: phase I (the construction of the dam) and phase II (the construction of the reservoir). The reservoir is expected to be completed by 2018, but it may not be fully operational until 2023. The main purpose of this dam will be dedicated to flood control, and the regulated volumes will be used for Saliou Dam mitigation. This means irrigation will be the only use of the regulated water.

### D1. Value Added for GCF Involvement

The GCF’s involvement in the proposed Project is critical and is expected to make an important contribution towards improving Morocco's preparedness for climate change and reducing climate-induced social, economic and environmental vulnerabilities. The GCF will provide technical support and financial resources to Morocco to implement appropriate measures. Given the scale of the finance required, the GCF is the only climate financing mechanism that is able to mobilise the scale of funding needed to address the irrigation infrastructure investment of this kind. There are no other financing mechanisms accessible to Morocco that could achieve this.

More general reasons for the need for GCF involvement in the project include the following:

(i) **Additional:** The construction of new irrigation infrastructure is crucial for Morocco’s economy and to conserve water resources. The lack of adequate irrigation infrastructure has led to a significant change in climate putting additional burden on the country. A long-term perspective needs to be taken to address these challenges. The GCF involvement will ensure that the project is implemented with the best practices and lessons learned.

(ii) **Scale:** After initial pilot projects undertaken by the Government to promote climate resilience irrigation in selected areas, it has become clear that a large-scale intervention is required to make an impact on the country as a whole. The GCF is the only fund able to address these at the scale needed to significantly contribute to the climate resilience of the Moroccan economy and society’s well-being.

(iii) **Importance of sector:** The centrality of the agricultural sector for the economy of Morocco in this case and other countries more generally make the involvement of the GCF even more important. This Project can show how an integrated approach to irrigation infrastructure development can be used as a means of promoting climate resilience in the sector and in the whole community. Stronger climate resilience translates into higher economic well-being and more resilient communities.

(iv) **Knowledge sharing:** Involvement of the GCF will facilitate the development and sharing of best practice across projects, countries and regions. The Project can be used as a model for other irrigation infrastructure projects that the country will embark on. It will also enable the comparison of different approaches and development of lessons learned regarding particular successes or shortcomings to enable future projects to draw on these. This is the case in particular in stabilising water resources and ensuring their sustainable use.

With the GCF’s involvement the Project can demonstrate a successful implementation and can add in scaling up climate resilience in the sector in Morocco and beyond. The GCF’s resources on technical support will ensure the stakeholder participation and long-term sustainability of the project, which is critical for the success of this intervention. Specifically:

- Through the construction of a new water transfer scheme as well as implementation of it through PPP, the GCF will be able to maximise the impact of its investment in Morocco.

# G.1 Risk Assessment Summary

The level of key risks that will affect the Project’s performance are generally moderate and expected to be mitigated to a substantial degree by the project's structure and capacity building initiatives. Key risks to this Project are:

## Sovereign risk

The risk is mitigated by (i) small size of loan relative to country outstanding debt and the composition of Morocco’s sovereign debt which is largely comprised of either domestic MAD denominated borrowing or long-term concessional IFI loans; and (ii) Morocco’s track record in implementing projects under the MAMMF.

## Implementation of the PPP structure

One risk to achieving the objective of the Project is a potential reluctance to follow through or implement the PPP. To mitigate this, the Bank will monitor the PPP tender process by 31st December 2017 and signing the PPP contract with the selected private operator by 31st December 2020 will be convened. In addition, the risk is mitigated by Morocco’s track record in implementing projects under the MAMMF, including the recent Guadiana irrigation project (100,00 ha).

## Dewatering of the Salis aquifer

If estimates suggest that the Project will provide enough water to replenish the Salis aquifer, the exact impact of climate change on the Salis basin aquifer as well as the evolution of the aquifer’s water balance remains a risk. This risk is first order and could be mitigated by ensuring that the project does not exceed its designed capacity and that groundwater extraction is kept under control. The Bank will cover the development and implementation of these tools. EBD will also cover the implementation of the required measures by the Kingdom of Morocco to prevent the depletion of the Salis water table.

## Affordability and tariff risk

Affordability risk is the cost ability of farmers to tolerate water tariff increases, and consequently create challenges for ensuring sufficient tariff revenues to cover operational and maintenance costs. The GCF’s investment in the project will be used to support the implementation of a tariff structure that reflects the true cost of providing irrigation services and ensures affordability under the PPP scheme. The financial structure of the proposed Project is based on a well-tested EBD model of combining grant and loan finance in order to reach an appropriate level of concessionality that overcomes climate change-induced social, economic, and environmental vulnerabilities while addressing Morocco’s affordability concerns of farmers, communities and utilities.

## Implementation risk

This is MAMMF first project with the Bank and therefore it is not yet familiar with the Bank's PPPB. To ensure smooth procurement implementation, the Bank has mobilised TC donor funds for procurement support to assist the PU in the procurement process. The Bank also mobilised MAMMF experience in implementing similar water transfer projects, such as the recent Guadiana irrigation project (100,00 ha).
