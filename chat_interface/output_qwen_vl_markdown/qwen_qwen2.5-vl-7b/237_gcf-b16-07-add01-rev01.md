## A.2: Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

Public sector resources are scarce and insufficient to cover the renewable energy and energy efficiency project financing needed for the Green Climate Fund (GCF) projects in the three countries.

Against this context, the EIB has designed GEEREF Nekt to catalyse private sector capital at scale for the development of RE/E projects across the GCF eligible countries, as well as build capacity at the local and national level and contribute to the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) by providing a platform for the creation of enabling ecosystems for clean energy in these countries.

The project will be implemented through two vehicles: USD 250 m from the GCF to the GEEREF Nekt investment vehicle and USD 15 m for a technical assistance (“TA”) facility (“TAF”).

### GEEREF Nekt

GEEREF Nekt is an innovative financing vehicle, structured as a fund-of-funds (“FoF”), which will finance the development of RE/E projects in the GCF eligible countries. The FoF will be invested by private investors from countries, either indirectly via specialised funds or directly into investments to the beneficiary projects themselves.

It is expected that, over its lifetime, GEEREF Nekt will finance more than 200 RE/E/energy efficiency projects.

# B.1. Description of Financial Elements of the Project / Programme

## GEF3 NCF is a USD 750 m target investment pooling mechanism aiming to develop, build and operate RE/EE beneficiary projects across the GCF eligible countries.

GEF3 NCF will be structured as a two-tier capital structure in order to mobilize additional capital and build local capacity. It will provide high efficiency to the scarce resources of the public sector (in terms of mobilization effect as well as project and programme development) by leveraging private sector capital, which is more readily available than public funds for projects, technologies and countries. It is expected that the GEF3F and GEF3 NCF model and track record serve as a template for long-term financing of RE/EE infrastructure assets to the private sector in developing countries.

A Full Commitment (FC) will be made by the GCF Board to provide USD 750 m to GEF3 NCF's core, following on the precedent of GEF3, the aim is to be among the first investors in such investment funds (i.e. an anchor investor) and to lead for all other financial institutions.

GEF3 NCF is proposed to be a Luxembourg-based entity. All investors will contribute capital in the form of shares in the collective investment vehicle.

## GEF3 NCF Structure

In order to mobilize private sector capital, GEF3 NCF will have a two-tier capital structure that will include USD 250 m of A-tier share ("A-shares"), a public sector seed-in capital from the GCF, and up to USD 500 m of B-tier capital ("B-shares") from other private investors. The A-shares are expected to be subscribed by the GCF Board. The B-shares will catalyse private sector capital towards beneficiary projects in sectors and countries that may otherwise not be attractive to private investors.

To overcome the risk aversion and regulatory constraints of private investors concerning these types of investments (i.e. long-term, low-yield, high-risk), the investment return structure needs to offer private investors the following features:

1. First preferred returns: The returns structure (also referred to as "return on capital") is structured in a way that all risk for the loss of principal is borne by the A-shares and B-shares. If there are losses, no capital back hair to the A-shares. Based on GEF3's experience, this is critical to overcome procedural regulatory restrictions that other private money managers may face when investing in such types of investments. The returns structure also allows for a "high water mark" by supervisory authorities, or make such investments prohibitively expensive for investors because of the risk aversion and regulatory constraints.
2. Preferred return: GEF3 NCF is expected to provide returns to its private investors which are commensurate with the level of risk assumed.

In order to mobilize private capital at scale, the GCF FC commitment will be in place unconditionally as from the first close of A-shares and B-shares. The GCF FC commitment will be made available to all investors on a pro-rata basis.

Therefore, GEF3 NCF will be allowed to commit and call on the GCF commitment at its as first close, in order to speed up the deployment of capital into beneficiary projects as well as enable the EBRF to fundraise from the private sector.


## GEF3 NCF Investment Structure

As an investment vehicle, GEF3 NCF will provide financing for development, build and operate RE/EE beneficiary projects across the GCF eligible countries during its envisaged five-year investment period.

GEF3 NCF will be structured as a two-tier capital structure in order to mobilize additional capital and build local capacity (i.e. those where beneficiary RE/EE projects will be financed), and the split of GEF3 NCF funding per country, will be determined by: (i) the flow of private sector capital into the vehicle; (ii) the availability of RE/EE project development opportunities; (iii) the appeal of such opportunities by the GEF3 NCF IC; and (iv) portfolio considerations such as country diversification and potential risk mitigation for the GEF3 NCF lead.

It can be expected that, over the course of time, GEF3 NCF will be most active in those offer:

* a. A minimum enabling regulatory environment;
* b. A sufficient number of RE/EE project proposals; and
* c. An investable portfolio opportunity.

### Technical Assistance Facility

The EBRD Group will establish a USD 15 m TAF, to be funded through a grant contribution from the GCF. The TAF will support GEEREF Net's investment strategy aimed at creating a sustainable RE/EE landscape in the target countries, by focusing on:

    - The TAF will address the needs and skills gap of the investor funds, ensuring they have the capacity to execute projects that cannot be funded out of the fund managers' budgets.

- This model has already been successfully used by GEF, which created an EUR 5 m TAF from the European Commission (EC). GEF has extended 10 TAF grants to its investor or prospective investor funds. Investments into new markets and sectors that are perceived as aggressive and high-risk such as GEEREF Next are often required to receive regulatory approval from their respective supervisory authorities to be allowed to proceed with such investments. In addition, the risk profile of these investments is likely to be more volatile than those in established markets and fit more comfortably within their asset allocation strategy.

The historical volatility of RE/EE projects has historically been significantly higher that any usually experienced by established emerging market investors. The notion of risk is not merely one of perception. Over the last 20 years, regulatory authorities (e.g., Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and Basel III regulations) have imposed higher capital requirements or risk-weighted reserves for institutional investors.

Investment policy statements and regulatory frameworks of pension funds and other institutions require that the risk of an equity investment be measured in terms of its volatility. The historical volatility of RE/EE projects is often correlated against a risk-free benchmark. For example, in many cases, the only quantitative measure of risk is the "historic" standard deviation of returns over some time period (e.g., 10 years). This makes it difficult for investors investing in a new asset class, such as emerging market RE/EE infrastructure, is clearly impossible until a time series of historical data has been established. In addition, the lack of liquidity and the relatively small size of the market means that there are few institutional investors who have building the "track-record" which will eventually turn RE/EE investments in emerging markets into mainstream assets.

### Adequacy of Returns

A traditional FV investing in infrastructure assets in developed markets would target an annual internal rate of return (IRR) of 8% or more. This is achieved by investing in assets on highly stable "core" infrastructure (such as regional utilities, toll roads or public private initiative assets) and seek a heavy weighting of "broaderwidth" infrastructure (i.e., operating assets that are subject to greater market risk).

The GEEREF Next financial model shows it no portfolio consensus with the above. Accordingly, without the preferred return mechanism, GEEREF Next would be offering private investors the higher risk profile of emerging market RE/EE projects in exchange for a lower expected return than is typically offered by investments into established infrastructure assets.

* Project Preparation (2-12 months overlap): Including environmental and social impact assessment (“ESIA”), feed in tariffs (“FIT”), PPA agreements, licensing.
* Design Development (6-9 month overlap): Including final project design, equipment supply, and equipment delivery to site:

During the project development and construction process, the key parties involved and their respective roles and responsibilities are as follows:

i) Local engineers: These are typically local civil, local engineers are part of the local developer team or may be hired separately by the fund manager. Local engineers are on-site throughout the whole construction process and will provide weekly updates to the fund manager.
ii) Fund manager engineers: GEF/EEF’s fund managers ensure that members of their engineering teams are dedicated to monitor progress, providing weekly updates to the fund manager.

iii) Engineering, procurement and construction (“EPC”) contractor: GEF/EEF’s fund managers have an EPC contractor who is responsible for the design and construction of the project. The EPC contract includes a performance guarantee signed with the project owner. The EPC contract is thus the key contractual agreement within the project development process. A significant number of EPC contractors come from local countries, providing an excellent opportunity to boost local employment in the power sector.
iv) Third-party consultants: These are typically hired by the fund manager and provide technical support on project design, before Notice to Proceed is given, to ensure that the project complies with the requirements of the relevant international standards. They also provide advice on the construction schedule and other matters related to the commencement of commercial operations.

v) Tendering process: The banks providing the finance project have a technical consultant to approve the project design specifications and to confirm the project milestones are appropriately met.

The number of projects and their respective capacities are also reported quarterly on a project-by-project basis.
  
- **Electricity Output:** Refers to the total annual electricity output estimated to be delivered by beneficiary projects that are in the portfolio at the end of the reported financial year or are expected to be in the portfolio by the time the funds are fully deployed. Actual electricity sales on monitored daily or weekly by the investor funds and published by the fund managers in their quarterly, annual, and impact reports.
  
- **Electricity Savings:** Refers to the total annual energy savings estimated to be delivered by beneficiary projects that are in the portfolio at the end of the reported financial year or are expected to be in the portfolio by the time the funds are fully deployed. Energy savings calculations are executed by each fund manager on an individual project basis, ensuing the approval of the GPF. The data is collected annually as part of impact reporting and the annual report. Approval for this data is required if it is provided in the EER Green House Gas (GHG) Footprint Methodology Report.
  
- **Emissions Reduced:** Refers to the total annual carbon emissions estimated to be reduced by beneficiary projects that are in the portfolio at the end of the reported financial year or are expected to be in the portfolio by the time the funds are fully deployed. The data is collected annually as part of impact reporting and the annual report. Approval for this data is required if it is provided in the EER Green House Gas (GHG) Footprint Methodology Report.
  
- **Beneficiaries:** Refers to the number of beneficiary households potentially gaining electricity access or improved electricity access for the projects executed by GEEREF's funds. * The GEF-SCC/NeXt quarterly report will be issued on the quarterly reporting cycle.
* Annual reports to investors that will provide audited financial statements and performance data, financial information or performance of each of the underlying investee funds.
* Annual impact report: Data for each of the indicators outlined in sections above as well as an impact data set for each of the investee funds. The report will be based on data collected from each of the investee funds.
* Annual impact report published online that will provide aggregate impact data for each of the indicators outlined.

GEF-SCC/NeXt will also report to the GCF through processes defined in the accreditation master agreement to be signed with the GCF as part of the investment agreement.

### Investor Funds Reporting

GEF-SCC/NeXt is committed to the highest possible standards of monitoring and supervision and adheres there at the fund level. The GEF-SCC/NeXt will report to the GCF on a quarterly basis (45 days after the end of the month) through a monitoring register that includes formal reporting on a quarterly and annual basis, from which it produces its own impact report.

The formal reporting regime includes submission of:

* Annual fund financial report (audited);
* Annual fund financial report (unaudited);
* Annual GEF-SCC/NeXt fund performance data;
* Annual GEF-SCC/NeXt fund impact-data collection tool; and
* Written notification of any serious ESG incidents within 3 business days, among others.

### Impact Reporting

One of GEF-SCC's key reporting instruments will be its annual impact report which will draw on its existing impact reporting programme under GEF6. GEF-SCC publishes its annual impact report, which includes all impact-related information for the portfolio and each individual project in it, at least once a year.

In 2015, the GEF impact report included data for the ten funds in the GEF portfolio at time and presented the total number of households served, beneficiaries, capacity installed, electricity generated annually, and the amount of GHG emissions avoided. The report also provided an estimate of how much additional impact could be achieved by the beneficiary projects in the portfolio (and to be added to the portfolio) once they are operational.

All impact data is collected annually from GEF-SCC's fund managers and are aggregated by the GCF. Data is collected for the entire GEF portfolio, including funds that have not yet been invested or are still under development. Should GEF-SCC/NeXt not be able to provide a full set of impact data as required in this report, it will be dependent upon the final size of GEF-SCC and its relative participation in each of the funds and beneficiary projects.

### Independent Evaluation

GEF-SCC/NeXt will provide a mid-term and final performance report to the GCF, to be conducted by an independent evaluator, at a time requested by the GCF.
