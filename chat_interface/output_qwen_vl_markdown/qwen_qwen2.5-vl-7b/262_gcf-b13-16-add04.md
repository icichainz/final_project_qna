# A.2 Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

## Project Description

Mal is vulnerable to several climate-related hazards, including flooding and drought. It is already experiencing the impacts of climate change, with increasing frequency and intensity of extreme weather events. This project will provide the scientific base for generating climate and weather information to inform climate-resilient development pathways.

### The Project Development Objective (PDO)

To strengthen the adaptive capacity and climate resilience of vulnerable communities in Mal by providing a national-scale climate monitoring system and early warning services, which will in turn support adaptation planning for public and private sector users.

Mal already experiences extreme weather and climate variability, resulting in high exposure to floods and droughts. The impacts of these hazards are felt across all sectors, including agriculture, health, energy, water resources management, and infrastructure. Reliable and timely information on current and future climate conditions and climate information is required from the national level down to the household level so that government, communities, and the private sector can make informed decisions about adaptation strategies.

The PDO will provide a national-scale climate monitoring system and early warning services which act as a key enabler for a broad range of adaptation decisions, ranging from the agriculture sector, infrastructure, disaster risk management, and others. For example, projected changes in climate are expected to result in increased rainfall over certain areas, leading to an increase in flooding events. The PDO will provide information on where and when to monitor, prepare for, respond to extreme rainfall events and flooding, thus building adaptive capacity and reducing the vulnerability of communities.

The PDO will also support adaptation planning by providing a national-scale climate monitoring system with relevant works such as bridges, culverts, and erosion protection. In terms of agriculture and food security, reliable hydroclimate information is required at all levels for crop selection, irrigation scheduling, and other agricultural practices in responding to weather variability and climate change. Private companies and businesses also need and rely on the hydroclimate information provided by the PDO.

By ensuring delivery of services to communities, the project will benefit highly vulnerable groups, including the 80% of the country's population whose livelihoods are dependent on predominantly rain-fed agriculture and about 5.3 million people directly affected by climate change.

The project will consist of the following components:

- **1 - Capacity building and institutional development**
This will include: (i) training and capacity-building programs for agencies staff and management, (ii) enhancing existing national hydroclimate monitoring systems, and (iii) providing support for desired design and system integration of project activities.

- **2 - Improvement of hydromet and early warning infrastructure**
This will include: (i) expanding and upgrading hydroclimate observation networks, (ii) enhancing data collection & transmission, forecasting and decision-support systems, and (iii) strengthening preparedness and emergency response readiness.

- **3 - Enhancement of service delivery and warnings to communities**
This component includes: (i) improving flood and drought forecasting and warnings, (ii) developing new products for sector-specific needs (agriculture, health, energy, water resources management, etc.), and (iii) strengthening "last mile" connectivity to ensure appropriate understanding and use of information, and mobilization and sensitization of community and establishing effective feedback mechanisms for communities at risk.

- **4 - Project management**
This component will include support to the project management unit providing assistance to executing entities and overall project oversight.

### Primary benefits:

The causes of the conflict are diverse and multi-layered and can be traced to a breakdown in the social contract, growing competition over resources (public funds as well as illicit rents), wealth inequalities, and underlying social tensions. Climate change effects are increasingly felt in Mali, in the form of higher temperatures, reduced precipitation and a growing desertification front. Records from Mali Meteor indicate that the country has become hotter and drier over the last several decades. Since 1960, temperatures have increased by 0.7°C across most of the country, with typical rates of warming being between 0.5-1.2°C per decade in the north and south. There is also evidence for inter-decadal changes; rainfall in Mali declined rapidly between 1950 and the mid-1980s, partially recovered in the 1990s, then fell again from the late 1990s to present day. The most recent period of drought was particularly severe, with winter records from 1960–2005 show that overall annual precipitation trends are decreasing slightly, with a significant reduction since 1987. This has been accompanied by an increase in the frequency and intensity of extreme events.

The persistent drought in 1979 has resulted in fairly significant rainfall deficits and a continuous shift of the isohyets to the south. The average annual precipitation for the period from 1950–2000 was approximately 430 mm, while the average for the period 1980-2000 was only about 360 mm.

Projections in future climate change are expected to increase mean annual variability and the occurrence of extreme climatic events. According to climate scenarios developed for Mali, the average annual temperature is likely to increase between 4–7°C by 2100 (with a range from +2-9°C). The frequency and intensity of extreme weather events in Mali are projected to increase across Mali by 23-40% by 2060, and this rate of increase is projected to be more rapid in the South. There will also likely be an increase in the number of days with temperatures exceeding 45°C per year for most parts of the country (indicating an increase in heavy rainfall events), and decrease in the North**. More frequent El Niño events could lead to increased precipitation in the north, while droughts are expected to become more severe.

The climate change scenario is exposed to an uncertain and inadequate rainfall; however, it simultaneously causes an increasing frequency of floods. Water resources and agriculture are among the most vulnerable sectors to climate change, with projected overall yields declining by 10-25% in Mali by 2030 under a high emissions scenario.

### Poor Meteorological and Hydrological Observation Networks and Inefficient Information Exchange

## H.1.1. Paradigm Shift Objectives and Impacts at the Fund Level™

### Paradigm shift objective:

The underlying paradigm shift for the proposed project is based on a systems-based causal chain of inputs, outputs, intermediate outcomes, final project outcomes and long term project impacts.

**Output Level:** The improvement and modernization of its hydromet system and services, using technological breakthroughs in the industry over the years, will enable Mali to provide continuous and reliable climate information for decision making. This will be based on weather, climate and hydrological information. The expected output level results include:

- Climate data collection, processing, dissemination, monitoring and impact forecasting services;
- Enhanced food security early warning system;
- New flood early warning services; and
- Enhanced civil protection response capacities.

### Intermediate Outcome Level: Taking advantage of these improvements, Météo Mali, DIRE, DGPC and SAP will more systematically and efficiently consider the demands of stakeholders in their climate information dissemination activities. This will lead to:

- Increased generation and use of climate information in decision making;
- Strengthened adaptive capacity and reduced exposure to climate risks; and
- Strengthened institutional capacities for climate change adaptation.

### Outcome Level: Strengthened and modernized hydrometeorological institutions and services will increase the use of timely and accurate weather, climate and hydrological information by stakeholders in decision making. This will lead to:

- Increased resilience of the agricultural sector in Mali and thereby strengthen their awareness and adaptive capacity.
- Better management of water resources in Mali;
- Strengthened institutional capacities for climate change adaptation.

### Impact Level: Strengthened and modernized hydrometeorological services in Mali will increase the use of timely and accurate weather, climate and hydrological information by stakeholders in decision making. This will lead to:

- Increased resilience of the agricultural sector in Mali and thereby strengthen their awareness and adaptive capacity;
- Better management of water resources in Mali; and
- Strengthened institutional capacities for climate change adaptation.

We refer to the Africa Hydromet Program - Strengthening Climate Resilience in Sub-Saharan Africa Phase I: Multi-Project submitted by The World Bank on or as of 03/15/2016.

The authorities of Mali, represented by the Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development, the National Designated Authority of Mali, Pursuant to Article 8.10.b of the content of which we acknowledge to have reviewed, we hereby communicate our no-objection to the program as included in the funding proposal.

By government decision No. 2016-035 of March 9th, 2016, the Government of Mali has no objection to the program as included in the funding proposal:

(1) The content of the funding proposal is in conformity with Mali's national priorities, strategies and plans;

(2) The activities proposed are consistent with the country's strategic objectives and the goals set out for the program. The program as included in the funding proposal is in conformity with relevant national laws and regulations.

We also confirm that our national process for ascertaining no-objection to the program as included in the funding proposal has been duly followed.

We acknowledge that this letter will be made publicly available on the GCF website.

Kind regards,

**LE DIRECTEUR GENERAL**

Abdoulaye DIABATE

Agence de l'Environnement et du Développement Soutenu (AEDS)
