### A2 Project / Programme Executive Summary

The Poverty Reduction, Energy and Climate Change Project (PROZEA) will improve the resilience of 17,000 households that are highly vulnerable to the impacts of climate change (CC) in 64 municipal districts located in eight administrative regions of Madagascar. These municipalities have been selected because their environmental vulnerability is rated as extremely high. For IPCC scenario calculations and projections on the impacts of CC on people’s livelihoods and ecosystems (see Section C.2.), it is estimated that climatic variations would have significant effects on food security, water availability, and human health in these areas.

The project will address climate change through three main components: (i) increasing agricultural productivity; (ii) reducing emissions from deforestation and forest degradation; and (iii) promoting adaptation to the impacts of CC. The project will also promote a landscape approach to ensure CC resilience in target areas, with the aim of establishing 13,500 ha of CC Forest/Agroforestry systems through this innovative incentive system that blends social, environmental, economic, and technical aspects.

The project will be implemented over a period of five years. The Government plans for a ten-year transition phase to ensure institutionalization and sustainability after the project ends (see Section C.2.). During this period, the Government will establish a national CC strategy and policy framework, as well as an institutional and legal and law enforcement support system for forest land use, environment, and energy regulation entities to ensure compliance with project safeguards, environmental, land use and bioenergy regulations. After Year 10 (forward), the Government will be responsible for the implementation of the project’s interventions through a national CC service regime to secure project institutionalization and sustainability.

The project will provide the grounds for this transformative change through innovative approaches in three main areas: (i) promoting landscape-based land use planning, following indigenous peoples’ knowledge; (ii) integrating energy and agriculture; and (iii) developing an innovative project governance/implementation structure that ensures inter-sectoral coordination, swift execution and transparent management of the project’s interventions. These aspects will be supported by a Technical Support Team (TST), led by the National Designated Authority (NDA) and the Project Committee (PC), chaired by the NDA, the Technical Secretariat of Planning (STP). FAO will provide the EC with technical assistance, project supervision, implementation and fiduciary support.

# C1. Strategic Context

## Country Context

Paraguay finds itself in a vicious cycle of poverty, unsustainable agriculture, forestry and energy practices that exacerbate vulnerability to CC.

### Socioeconomic Context

While poverty rates in Paraguay grew 22 percent between 2003 and 2011, extreme poverty remained persistently high. In 2011, three out of ten people were living below the poverty line and 48% could not afford the basic food basket (the extra poor), placing the country among the poorest in Latin America. Since 2011, there have been substantial welfare improvements, with poverty rates falling to 35 percent by 2016 and extreme poverty dropping to below 27%. However, two main challenges to the sustainability of poverty reduction are still present: (i) Paraguay is one of the most unequal countries in Latin America; and (ii) although opportunities have expanded, a large share of the population still faces a volatile economic environment. In rural areas, most of those living below the poverty line are poorly self-employed in agriculture, a highly volatile sector. A sizeable proportion of these households live on their land, but they lack access to credit or other financial services and are vulnerable if the economy or climate shocks could push them back into poverty.

Note: Data from 2016 refer to the social protection program "Semitransbordo Operatividad", which includes Telefácil, Teletransfer, and Adolfo Mejía accounts for one third of the decrease in rural poverty. The conditional cash transfer program, Telefácil, targets poor families with school-aged children and offers a sizeable resource providing them with financial support to improve their living conditions. As of 2016, Telefácil had reached 345,789 households, representing 141,360 households, approximately 10% of the country's population. To stabilize vulnerable households economically and help them escape poverty, Telefácil provides financial resources to improve their living conditions and increase resilience of these households by increasing their productive, financial, human and social assets. These cash transfers may not be a major driver behind the change in the incidence of poverty but still serve an important role in protecting households from falling into extreme poverty.

Paraguay's indigenous people and female-headed households continue to be the most vulnerable social groups in Paraguay. There are about 20 different indigenous ethnic groups comprising around 15,000 people (1.7% of the country's total population). Indigenous peoples live mainly in the Chaco region and in the northern and southern territories, only 2.5% have access to potable water and 31-32% electricity. To address this, Paraguay’s National Development Fund (FONDEP) has been working on a number of projects aimed at improving access to basic services (water, sanitation and health, decent housing and electrification), and ensuring legal titling of indigenous lands. Many of Paraguay's indigenous people live in remote areas with poor infrastructure and limited access to public services, relying heavily on their natural environment. It is these households that derive most of their economic and social benefits from the forests and land that are most vulnerable to environmental degradation and in need of sustainable use options regarding the natural resources they depend on.

### Sectoral Context

Despite Paraguay's substantial production of hydroelectricity, nearly half (45%) of national energy consumption comes from fossil fuels. Paraguay’s expanding agricultural frontier and its heavy dependence on biofuels have contributed to globally high rates of deforestation, reducing native Atlantic Forests in Paraguay by 95% (see Map F-7). Deforestation risk in Areas II, III, Firewood and charcoal are used to dry for grains, to produce cacao, and as the primary energy source in the South. The forest sector is also a major employer of indigenous people who depend on their natural environment for their livelihoods. Paraguay’s reliance on fossil fuels represents both an opportunity and a challenge. Paraguay will continue to rely heavily on renewable energy sources, but it must move away from one that depletes native forests so as not to relies exclusively on sustainable sources of wood from planted forests and sustainable management of natural forests. Paraguay is poised to become the world’s largest producer of biofuels by 2015, with a target of producing 3 billion liters per year. The sustainability of Paraguay's growth model is under threat from environmental degradation. Agricultural expansion and forest conversion have been the main drivers of deforestation in the country since the late 1970s, with a peak between 2001-2015 according to the National Forest Inventory (2015). These official estimates are roughly corroborated by satellite imagery such as GIEWS Paraguay, which shows that forest cover decreased from 48% in 2003 and 40.9% in 2014 respectively. Land use change is also a major loss of unique and valuable biodiversity from the Atlantic Forest Complex. Forests in Paraguay play a valuable role in stabilizing local hydrology (important for the substantial hydropower generation capacity) and carbon sequestration, which has been increasingly recognized by national authorities. Increased public attention and effort to decrease deforestation and improve water resources management in recent years, progress has been slow due to lack of institutional coordination, limited financial resources, weak enforcement capacities, with overlapping and at times conflicting institutional responsibilities and weak governance. Land use change and forest degradation are the largest contributors to greenhouse gas emissions in the country. Agriculture is highly vulnerable to climate change impacts such as increased frequency and intensity of droughts and floods, which will have a large impact on yields, largely particularly through improved agricultural practices and reforestation. Carbon trading options are also worth exploring through mechanisms offered by the authorizing the NDC/REDD+ programs.

## C3: Project / Programme Objectives against Learning

The project's objective is to reduce vulnerability of poor households vulnerable to the impacts of CC and to increase the forest cover in environmentally sensitive areas of Eastern Paraguay. The National Program to Tackle Extreme Poverty "Sustancias Operativas" has surveyed 260,012 households nationally, of which 453,897 are located in Eastern Paraguay selected by PROEZA for their high social and environmental vulnerability (see Section C1 Map in Annex F). The vulnerability of the ecosystems and resources in this area is very high. According to the National Forest Inventory (2015), 60% of the surveyed project area are classified as "poor" or "extreme poor", totaling 8,299,000 ha. On average, 42% of these are female-headed households and 37% live in municipalities with a high percentage of indigenous populations (see Annex D: Departments). The Project's target beneficiaries consist of 17,130 of these poor and extremely poor rural households that are highly vulnerable to the impacts of CC in 64 municipal districts.

The data shows that 95.2% of the surveyed households in the project area are highly dependent on the natural environment for their daily domestic needs and food security. Currently, 22% of the rural poor and extreme poor households depend on water wells, and 30% depend on other open water sources including rainwater for all domestic purposes. This makes them vulnerable to climate change impacts such as increased frequency and intensity of droughts and floods, micro-impacts from the impacts of CC through afforestation, and by doing so improve local biodiversity.

An average of 95% of the poor and extreme poor households in the targeted departments are fuelwood to cook their food, and firewood for heating. The average annual expenditure on firewood is US$120 per household. It has been estimated that each family consumes around 12.5 t of firewood per year and that poor households spend up to US$270/year to buy charcoal and firewood in the local markets, which is a significant financial burden for these families. The project will reduce this by bringing about positive environmental and economic effects. ### C3. Project / Programme Description

The project objective is to improve the resilience of poor and extremely poor households vulnerable to the impacts of climate change and to increase the forest cover in environmentally sensitive areas of Eastern Paraguay. Building on preparatory diagnostic work undertaken by INFOFNA and SEAM with support from the UN REDD Program, the project area comprises 46 Municipal Districts (MD) covering approximately 130,857 ha located within the departments of Alto Paraná, Canindeyú, Guairá, Alto Paraná, Caazapá and Itapúa. These 46 Municipal Districts were selected due to their high environmental and social vulnerability.

PROEZA's objective will be achieved through the following complementary and mutually reinforcing components:

1. Planning for the future
2. Sustainable landscape and ecosystem markets
3. Good governance and law enforcement

For PROEZA to be successful, the connection and interactions among these components is key. Component 1 targets poor and extremely poor households while component 2 triggers a change in practices by medium size landholders and will trigger a framework for sustainable forest management and conservation of natural resources through smallholder farmers. Strengthened governance, regulatory and law enforcement supported through Component 3 will ensure the sustainability of activities under Components 1 and 2.

Components 1-3 are mutually reinforcing as they all enhance the capacity of smallholder farmers to forest activities beyond the project scope. Together, these respond to the national strategy and contribute to the national effort on mitigation and adaptation.

**Component "Planning for the Future" (US $75 million):**

This component focuses on vulnerable households living in poverty and extreme poverty that receive support through the Government's social protection program "Sombreado Oportunidades" (see Section C.1). Given the extremely high vulnerability of these households, the project will provide them with a safety net to ensure their survival during the transition period.

The intervention will fortify Telepoya with agro-ecological and climate-smart components through training, capacity building, investment in infrastructure, and access to services. The project will also support other initiatives implemented by an environmental cash transfer (ECC) payment. ECC is an environmental service incentive payments program that acts as a bridge until the farming models are financially sustainable and to prevent ad-sorption.

PROEZA will be implemented through 46 Municipal Districts in the project area, comprised of approximately 872,100 men, women, and children (5.1 persons/household). The price range is US $50 to US $70 per ton for charcoal.

There is considerable demand for citrus fruits, both for export and national consumption, and the market varies greatly in terms of price according to the place and the buyer. Though PROREA, a highly innovative development concept is being created in Paraguay by which cash transfers conditioned to social indicators (Telapoy) would be tipped up by environmental conditional cash transfers: This creates a new program to be implemented by PROREA through which rural households highly vulnerable to the impacts of climate change will be provided with financial incentives to adopt multifunctional forest land management models for climate resilience and produce public benefits. #### a. Accelerate Paraguay’s planned shift towards a low-emissions, sustainable development path

Paraguay is poised to shift rapidly from depletion of native forests to a reliance on sustainable sources of fuelwood from planted trees and other forest products. This transition will be supported by the GCF through financing for substantial payment and clear definition of authority to launch this fundamental transformation. With GCF support, Paraguay can achieve its goal of reducing deforestation in the Chaco region by 50% within five years and climate-resilient development. GCF support will catalyze this shift by supporting a change in incentives for market participants by reducing costs and risks, eliminating barriers to the deployment of climate-resilient solutions, thereby enabling Paraguay’s transition.

#### b. Larger scale of impact

GCF investments will enable Paraguay to implement this project (i) more efficiently through the proposed public/private execution and implementation arrangements, and (ii) on a larger scale than would be possible with existing resources. The project is expected to benefit 105,729 families in the establishment of 13,540 ha of multifunctional climate resilient planted forests (0.8 ha per family). The inclusion of concessional loan financing for medium and large landowners will allow the project to engage innovative approaches such as “climate-resilient agroforestry” or “climate-resilient forested plantations” that protect both riparian zones and remnant native forests by shifting fuelwood demand toward sustainable sources. This approach will also provide proof of concept for a second much larger phase on investment in growing forests for bioenergy.

The GCF grant resources will be directed to financing afforestation activities in areas of high social and environmental vulnerability. The GCF grant financing will be derived from the GCF’s core trust fund, which is specifically designed to support projects that target vulnerable groups who are more likely to reside (including indigenous and women-headed households). The GCF contribution will therefore help to ensure that the project has a strong focus on poverty alleviation through climate change mitigation measures through one single operation. GCF resources will also be used to mainstream the use of evaluation in poverty alleviation as the program of conditional cash transfers will be subject to a rigorous evaluation process (including an independent evaluation by the World Bank and a separate evaluation using the Proxy Mean Testing).

#### d. Greater innovation and multiple co-benefits

GCF grant funding will support the establishment of multifunctional forests that provide both economic and multiple co-benefits (climate resilience, diversified income sources, increased well-being of beneficiaries, including gender and social inclusion, among others). By supporting the rapid adoption of multifunctional forest management models in land belonging to small, medium and large landowners, the project will help to ensure that the benefits are widely shared. The strong focus of Component 1 on the poor, extreme poor (including women-headed households) and indigenous communities with these investments will help improve both livelihoods and ability to confront climate impacts in these particularly vulnerable groups.

# E.1 Impact Potential

## E.1.1 Mitigation impact potential

Building resilience to CC is a priority for poverty reduction and shared prosperity in Paraguay. CC projections point to higher temperatures, increased frequency and intensity of rainfall and associated flooding, along with more frequent and prolonged droughts. These changes will have significant impacts on the livelihoods of vulnerable communities, particularly indigenous communities. Projected impacts threaten many of the country's development priorities (food security energy security in rural areas).

While the population is vulnerable as a whole, indigenous communities and WHH living in poverty and extreme poverty are particularly affected as they depend more directly on depleted natural capital (land, water, and forest resources). Diversification of livelihoods through sustainable land use management practices can help to reduce vulnerability and to reducing the risk of over-exploitation of natural resources. However, especially in the case of remote communities with limited access to markets, this is not always possible or feasible. The most vulnerable are those who depend on agriculture as a means to confront periodic climate shocks. PROEZA investments are targeted to benefit the poorest and most vulnerable rural districts in Paraguay. The selection of districts to be included in PROEZA Component 1 has taken into account the degree of vulnerability, based on the results of the vulnerability assessment and deforestation prepared by IYDDA and SEAM with support of the UNREDD Programme in 2016 (see maps in AnnexF).

### Mitigation Benefits

One of the main objectives of GCF-funded activities is to mitigate CC by reducing carbon emissions from land use, deforestation and through sustainable management of forests. The GCF-financed activities under PROEZA are expected to contribute to climate change mitigation by: a) increasing access to clean cooking fuels and technologies for cooking and cooking; and c) rapidly shifting toward sustainable production of wood-based bioenergy and the substitution of un-sustainable energy sources (e.g., charcoal, kerosene). The project will also provide benefits through monitoring and reporting on mitigation in the Logical Framework (Section II.1.2) and M&E (Section II.2). Mitigation benefits have been estimated and analyzed ex-ante, and will be assessed and reported in the Project's Completion Report.

A second primary objective of GCF-funded activities is to foster adaptation to CC by increasing resilience and enhancing livelihoods of the most vulnerable people, communities and regions. CTFP are forest plantations generally established with more than one tree species, often native, adapted to the site and its ecological conditions. They can be managed for a variety of purposes such as timber production, firewood or fuel wood[6] unequally used. While conventional tree plantations have primarily a "productive function", CTFP are more complex systems that can provide multiple functions in addition to their primary role: they fulfill economic, social and environmental values and functions. An adequate diversity of functions in an ecosystem is considered a fundamental characteristic for the sustainability of forest ecosystems. The combination of timber production with other functions, combined with good forest management contribute to increased resilience and stability since they balance productivity and biodiversity[8]. In this context, CTFP are more complex systems that can provide multiple functions, it is very important that CTFP are not seen as only fulfilling environmental functions. # F.1. Economic and Financial Analysis

## Economic Analysis: This economic analysis considers the public goods the project will generate that are not yet recognized by the local communities, markets, or broader climate benefits, carbon sequestration and storage benefits, watershed values, and existence values. This analysis separates the benefits of carbon sequestration and storage from other broader climate benefits. The costs associated with the project, including those related to the institutional arrangements of governance and forest management, have not been accounted for as benefit attribution is challenging. The following assumptions were made for quantifying costs and benefits:

### Carbon Sequestration and Storage

The analysis assumes that carbon sequestered will be modeled over a period of 30 years although it is expected that project impacts would endure longer. A shadow value of US $70/CO2 was used, per the official guidance for the social value of carbon15. Carbon sequestration and storage values of forest ecosystems are different from climate regulation benefits because they represent the direct benefit to society of storing carbon in forests rather than the indirect benefits provided by forest ecosystems. According to a case study in Cameroon16, associated values ranged from US $42 to US $73 per tonne of CO2 sequestered. The analysis assumes that the project would sequester 50 million tonnes of CO2 over 30 years, which equates to an annual sequestration rate of approximately 1.6 million tonnes of CO2. This results in a total carbon sequestration and storage value of US $280 million or US $9.3 per tonne of CO2 sequestered.

### Water Values

Pierce (2001) values watershed benefits for tropical forests between US$15 and US$40 per ha/year, while the World Bank (2016) estimates that the value of water stored in tropical forests is US$72/ha/year. Watershed values by Pierce (2001) associated with tropical and sub-tropical forests show a wide variety of values between US $35 to US $48 per ha/year, while the World Bank (2016) estimates that watershed values in developing countries are US$72/ha/year. This analysis assumed a conservative baseline value of US $27/hay/ha.

### Existence Values

Estimates associated with short preservation value of tropical and sub-tropical forests show a wide variety of values between US $35 to US $48 per ha/year, while the World Bank (2016) estimates that watershed values in developing countries are US$72/ha/year. The results put the average value at US $43 per ha/year for tropical and sub-tropical forests. A sensitivity analysis was performed where the question of inverted (first 20%, followed by 5%) the results were different, at US $36 per hay/ha for 5% and US$89 per hay/ha, 20%. TEER (2009) estimated existence values at US $43 per ha/year, while Pierce et al. (2001) provided a range of existence values between US $35 to US $48 per ha/year.

### Methodology

A threshold analysis identifying the break-even point where the project's net benefits equal net costs was performed. In addition to applying conservative values for the quantitative assessment, a sensitivity analysis was undertaken where the question of inverted (first 20%, followed by 5%) the results were different, at US $36 per hay/ha for 5% and 20%; and used, and the results were tested against benefit reductions of 20% and 50%. Quantitative results were also tested using a range of existence values between US $35 to US $48 per ha/year.

### Economic Cost/Benefit Analysis

The economic cost/benefit analysis was based on the incremental flow of net benefits from the "with-" and "without" project scenarios. The "with-" scenario assumed that the baseline area for re-afforestation was zero (no forest ecosystem prior to project implementation). The "without" scenario assumed that no project would be implemented, but all other assumptions of analysis were used. While Project costs are spread over 10 years, economic benefits would be generated beyond the Project.

### H.2. Arrangements for Monitoring, Reporting and Evaluation:

PROEZA will apply F&A's standard M&E procedures and will be compliant with the GCF performance measurement framework (PMF) and reporting requirements. The project will implement a comprehensive monitoring system, PROEZA M&E comprises 3 key steps:
- **Execution level**: ML&H at the execution level is coordinated by the Executive Committee. Data will be stored in a database accessible to all partners involved in GAF. Georeferencing will enhance F&A and EO monitoring and evaluation of project activities. The system will allow national services INOFMA/SEMA/LNSP/SVNP/GIF (BEF Section H) to closely monitor outcomes' development and assess impacts and contribution towards agreed targets at midterm and completion.
- **Supervision, Support and Evaluation level**: F&A will support the EC in reviewing and analyzing progress reports and in preparing annual project implementation reports. The EC will be responsible for ensuring that the project is on track financially and administratively. At the end of each financial control and mid-term evaluation (year 3) and final evaluation phase via an independent and external evaluation expert.
- **Strategic level**: Annual results and related analysis, jointly prepared by F&A and the EC, will form the base for each annual planning exercise via the AWPE. These will be presented to the EC in order to support its strategic role and to secure transparency and result-based strategy development.

Additionally, in line with GCF requirements, the project will ensure a constant monitoring of the following:
- Forest cover and carbon monitoring: The National Forest Monitoring System (NFSM) of Paraguay has been developed under Paraguay's UN REDD+ National Joint Programme and is administered by INOPA and SEAM. INOFMA is mainly responsible for the collection of data on forest cover change, deforestation, and forest degradation using Satellite Landscapes (SLMs) in collaboration with SEAM. SEAM is the main responsible party for the GGI Inventory within INOFMA being the main generator of information for the LULUCF sector. The NFSM has generated information for Paraguay's Biennial Update Report (BUR) and will continue to do so.
- The NFSM's main objective is to monitor deforestation by generating activity data (SLMs) and emission intensity factor (NFI) for the GGI Inventory. The NFSM provides a basis for monitoring forest cover change, deforestation, and forest degradation in the country under a common methodology developed following IPCC guidelines®. One of the sites is Forest Plantations and the methodology developed can be applied to measure forests targeted by Components 1 and 2 of PROEZA.
- Additionally, INOFMA collects information on forest plantations' growth using remote sensing and field surveys
