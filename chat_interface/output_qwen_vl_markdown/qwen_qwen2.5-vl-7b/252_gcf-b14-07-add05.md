### A.2 Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

The recognition of the natural argan forest (Argania spinosa L.) (Swee), by UNESCO since 1996, as a Biosphere Reserve (RBA) highlighted its rich biodiversity and the economic opportunities. The argan oil "market boom" and its rising demand improved household income but generated serious pressure on the forest resources. This project aims to address this issue through sustainable management practices that will help mitigate and exacerbate such negative trend.

Morocco committed itself under NDC, to reduce its Greenhouse Gases (GHG) emission by 32% by 2030, through NAMAs. Arganiculture, aiming to plant 43,000 hectares of argan tree orchards, is a priority NAMA.

Through a large stakeholder consultation process involving 8 provinces and 31 rural communes, spread over six years and will require funding of 9.6 MUSD, the project aims at reducing GHG emissions by 25% through sustainable management practices that will help mitigate and exacerbate such negative trend. The project will also contribute to the conservation of biodiversity in the arganese biosphere reserve through planting 10,000 ha of argan tree orchards with soil conservation and rain water harvesting capabilities, and supporting the development of a local community-based forest management system that includes land use planning, seedling sharing and natural forest co-management. The activities once achieved pursue to contribute to relieve the anthropic pressure on the natural forest, improve biodiversity in the Biosphere Reserve, reduce GHG emissions by 25% through sustainable management practices (CO2 eq.), and will contribute to set off a new business paradigm that stimulates change in "doing business as usual" from fuel collection from natural forest towards private investments to address the issues of Climate change and unleash the full business potential of the biosphere value chain.

### C.1. Strategic Context

#### 1. NATIONAL CONTEXT
##### (i) Argan Biosphere Reserve (RBA) in Morocco

Morocco is the universal depositary of the Argan tree and UNESCO had listed the argan tree's geographic range as the Argan Biosphere Reserve (RBA) in 1988. This Biosphere covers an area of approximately 2.5 million hectares where the Argan tree holds first place with a total coverage rate of about 70% to 80%.

Several studies and research tried to measure degradation trends of the argan natural forest by monitoring changes in the density of trees, diameter at breast height (DBH), and crown cover using aerial photos and satellite images. The results showed that the Argan tree is under pressure from overgrazing and deforestation.

The study of the state of degradation of the Megalouga forest at the frontier of Agadir city, between 1980-2011 (Pérez et al., 1996) showed a relative density decrease of about 7.5% in the plain area and an increase of about 3.4% in the mountainous area.

The study of the state of degradation of the Megalouga forest at the frontier of Agadir city, between 1980-2011 (Pérez et al., 1996) showed a relative density decrease of about 7.5%. Major differences are noted from mountain areas with 3% (on annual basis of 2.2%) and 41% in the plain area (on annual rate of 18.4%).

## 1.3. Arganiculture and climate change

It is obvious that the success of the regeneration of the natural forest is closely dependent on its capacity to regenerate and effective participation of the local users is also important. At this level, the capacity for action and the availability of productive land are essential factors in the overall success of its artificial regeneration. This is considered to be a long process requiring a lot of time (10-20 years) depending on the initial state of the forest (Fig.3).

## 1.4. Arganiculture to reduce pressure on the natural forest

There is a serious mismatch between local conservation intentions and the long term sustainable use of the forest. The economic and market prices are the most important motivations for locals.

The project, offering additional fuel resources, will contribute to solving this mismatch through the emergence of a high value kernel market from sustainable production practices. This will allow users to sell higher quantities of harvested kernels and is the best way to solving real rural poverty in Morocco.

## 1.5. Sensitivity to desertification and land degradation

The most decisive factor of sensitivity to desertification and land degradation in the argane woodland is the pressure to overgrazing, which is also related to climate change. Low levels of productivity and frequent use by herds outside the argan forest range worsen land degradation of the biosphere.

## CLIMATE VULNERABILITY

Most of the area is covered by natural forest grown on shallow, rocky and poor soils. The vegetation land cover in the argan forest decreases because of soil water erosion, particularly in watersheds. Some studies estimate of soil erosion rates in the Sousa-Messaou reached critical levels of 10 tons per hectare per year. In addition, the main causes of degradation are overgrazing and deforestation. The most vulnerable areas to mountainous and hilly regions, are caused by severe erosion and concomitant. This situation is aggravated by the slow rate of soil recovery due to the use of inappropriate farming techniques and continual overgrazing.

The climate in the region is characterized by a Mediterranean type showing significant variations with very dry seasons and rainfall below 100 mm. The alternation of dry periods and short rainy periods is one of the characteristics of the climate of the region why both dry periods contribute to soil erosion and the rainy periods contribute to the development of vegetation. ### Climate vulnerability

Most of the argan tree in the natural forest grows on shallow, rocky and poor soils. The vegetation cover in the argan forest decreases is favoured by wind and water erosion, particularly in wadisomes. Some studies estimate the rate of erosion in the Sous massif about 4000m3/year with a mean annual rainfall of around 500mm. This high rate of erosion has led to the degradation of the argan forest and the increase of significant erosion. This situation is aggravated by the low rate of soil recovery due to the use of inappropriate farming methods and overgrazing.

The climate conditions of southwestern Morocco are highly arid. Annual rainfall displays significant fluctuations descending in some years below 100 mm. The high evaporation rates, coupled with a very short rainy season, are characteristic of the climate of the region and promote drought periods contributing to degradation of the prevailing vulnerable ecosystems.

### Economic Analysis

In economic analysis, the first is the individual contributions (individual earnings) and the second relates to the profitability of a project with differences between the two analyses:

- In financial analysis: taxes are treated as costs and subsidies as compensation, while in economic analysis taxes and subsidies are classified as transfers.
- In financial analysis, market prices are used; these prices include taxes, subsidies, and distortions. In economic analysis, prices must be modified to reflect the social and economic values (reference price or hypothetical price).
- In financial analysis: interest is a cost and it is deducted. Whereas in economic analysis, interest is a share of the profitability of capital.

Today given a value greater than the same value tomorrow and quick recipe is better than a distant recipe.
