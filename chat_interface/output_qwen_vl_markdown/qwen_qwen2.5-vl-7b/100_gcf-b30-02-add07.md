# Altii River Catchment Area (Project Location) baseline scenario

The Altii River Catchment Area (ARCA) located in South and West Kenya is one of the major water resources for a number of towns and villages in the region. The catchment area, from the highland in the Aberdare Ranges of around 2,600M above mean sea level (amsl) to the coastal area at the sea level. Even though the water resource is abundant, it is not evenly distributed within the catchment and will be even larger (see figure 1). According to the current monitoring situation of water resources, the quality of water in the Altii River Catchment Area is good and the water demand is relatively low and water stress is insignificant. The ARCA had present water demands of 1,145 BCM/year which represents 76% of the available 2010 water resources of 1,503 BCM/year.

## Figure 1: Water basins and water balance in Kenya

Water basins are divided into the Nandi Upper Altii, and Coastal Altii sub-regions. Most wetlands and riparian land zones are rapidly being reclaimed and converted into residential estates or commercial/industrial centres. These watersheds have been fragmented by human activities such as agriculture, livestock rearing, urbanization, and deforestation. Croplands and rangelands constitute the largest land use area in the catchment. Rangelands are found on the upper altitudes of the Altii River Catchment Area. The lower altitudes are increasingly being converted to agro-pastoralism and urbanization with consequent loss of the protective vegetation cover. This has led to soil erosion, sedimentation, and water pollution in the catchments. Agriculture and pastoralism: The Upper Altii consist of the areas of Kiambu and some parts of Nyandarua where the main crops grown are maize, beans, sorghum, millet, wheat, potatoes, tomatoes, onions, garlic, and legumes. Maize and fruits are grown in up as well as middle Altii (Nanyuki, Kajiado, Kiambu, Makueni, Machakos County). The main crops grown in the lower Altii River Catchment Area include maize, beans, sorghum, millet, wheat, potatoes, tomatoes, onions, garlic, and legumes. Cashew nuts, coconut and mangroves are grown in the coastal region of Kwale, Kilifi and Mombasa. Bananas are grown in the lower Altii River Catchment Area.

The ratio of 76% of water demand to water resources, which is called a water stress ratio, shows a very right balance between water resources and demands compared with the ratio of 40% regarded to indicate severe water stress. It is projected that the water demands for 2035 are expected to increase to about 891 BCM/year.

# G.2 Gender assessment and action plan (max. 500 words, approximately 1 page)

The gender analysis identified and proposes remedies to gender-based inequalities that could exacerbate existing vulnerabilities of women and girls in relation to water resource management and other vulnerable populations within the catchment areas. A cross-sectional gender analysis using qualitative methods was employed and a participatory approach was used for stakeholder consultations with communities, service providers and key institutions.

The assessment focused on various elements of gender equality and equity that include: Access to resources; Level of participation in decision-making processes; Gender-based power relations; Gendered division of labor and decision making roles; Gendered access to information and knowledge; Gender rights and status; and Time availability and space concerning location of water points.

The planning and design of this program adhered to the constitutional principle of public participation that requires that all projects must be designed in a participatory manner. The gender action plan has not yet been finalized as it is still under development, but it will include the following:

1. **Stakeholder Consultations:** These consultations were conducted among community participants, service delivery officers and policy makers to identify existing inequalities and barriers to women’s participation in water resource management institutions.
2. **Gender Action Plan:** The gender action plan will ensure that 50% of all project activities are gender-sensitive and will include specific indicators that will be tracked during project implementation. The accredited entity (NEMA) through GCF national focal point will provide technical support to the NEMA in developing a gender action plan for this program.
3. **Gender Training:** A gender training workshop was held on 20th October, 2016 with all key stakeholders including service providers and policy makers. The training focused on the importance of gender equality and equity in water resource management.

The gender analysis established that women and girls are the vulnerable gender majority because they are responsible for fetching water resources that their families need to survive - for drinking, cooking, sanitation, and other daily activities. Women also spend a lot of time collecting water from distant sources such as rivers and lakes; they also brave long queues in water collection points and in drought seasons they are forced to pay exorbitant prices for water. The gender analysis further established that women and girls are more vulnerable to climate change that results in water shortage exacerbates the vulnerability of women and girls leading to exploitation and abuse.

The gender action assessment, a gender action plan was developed highlighting gender responsive actions that will be implemented against each project activity such as Constitution of a gender inclusive committee to gather information on the status of women and girls in relation to water resource management; development of an integrated database for weather and water resources related information by ensuring that men and women have equal access to this information; establishment of a gender focal person at all levels within the organization; provision of training on gender equality and equity, including climate change adaptation; technical training on operation of monitoring and information systems, ensuring public awareness and involvement in the project implementation process; and sensitization of service providers and policy makers on the importance of women’s participation in water resource management.

The outcomes to be achieved from implementation of the gender action plan is reduced gender inequality and beneficial contribution of men and women in the project leading to reduced vulnerability, especially of women and girls.

### Vulnerability of the country and climate rationale

About 75 per cent of the population depends directly on land and natural resources for their livelihoods. The economy is highly dependent on agriculture. Therefore, Kenya is vulnerable to the variability of rainfall patterns.

The AID indicates that the funding proposal is mainly focused on addressing drought impacts. However, the analysis carried out in the previous submission has experienced frequent floods and droughts resulting from climate change impacts? and that "climate change has adversely affected water availability and distribution in the country". The proposed project does not address the link between climate change and drought risk as demonstrated in the proposal, failing to prove the observed decrease in rainfall.

The analysis of historical data in the previous submission was carried out using gridded data (GR) TS 3.21). Under the independent TAP's request, the current submission includes a trend analysis of rainfall projections for the Upper Athi River Basin and the Lower Athi River Basin at a national scale, and only 3 of the 33 stations are representative of the project area. This indicates that the proposed project is not representative of the entire project area.

The analysis in annex 29 is limited to paragraph 23 of the funding proposal, which states that "Despite the projections on the Global Climate Model (GCM) and the results from the rainfall trend analysis for the counties show an overall decreasing trend". The inference is drawn from analyses involving gridded datasets, largely due to the non-availability of longitudinal time series meteorological data. This is further supported by the fact that the GCM projections are not validated against time series charts of historical data or projections at basins.

The rainfall trends in the Upper Athi River Basin and Lower Athi River Basins are assessed in annex 29. ### 2.1 The Athi Catchment Area

ACA is located in the western part of the country as shown in Figure 1.3.1 and borders on the Tana Catchment Area in the north, the Indian Ocean in the east, Tanzania in the south and the Rift Valley Catchment Area in the west. The Aberdare Range, one of the Five Water Towers, lies in the northern edge of ACA. Total area of ACA is 584 km², corresponding to 10.2% of Kenya's total land area. Based on the Census 2019 population of the area in 2010 is estimated as 7.97 million, or 25.4% of the total population of Kenya. Population density in ACA is approximately 13,680 people per km².

The topography of ACA varies, from the highland in the Aberdare Range at around 2,600 m asl to the lowlands in the south and east of the catchment area at about 500-700 m asl. The upper zone at 3,400–3,800 m asl; upper mid-zone at 2,600–1500 m asl; middle zone at 1300–500 m asl; and coastal zone at 500 m or less. The Athi River flows from the southeast of Nairobi north-eastward in the upstream reaches and then southward to the Indian Ocean. The river is fed by several tributaries, mainly along the catchment area boundary with Tana Catchment Area and pours into the Indian Ocean at Mombasa. The Athi River is one of Kenya's major rivers and drains most of the ACA Catchment Area. The Lumi River, Lake Jipe, and Lake Chala flow into Tanzania and the Umba River flows from Tanzania to Kenya. Other rivers such as the Raro, Mwechi, Pemba, and Rusinga also drain into the Indian Ocean. There are several springs in ACA such as Mitma, Kikuyu, Ngiro Klwa, Nol Turesh spring, etc. ACA is located within the Eastern Arc Mountains (EAM) which are classified as non-ASAL. The mean annual rainfall ranges between 600 mm in the central part of the area to 1200 mm in the upstream area of the Athi River. The catchment area average mean annual precipitation minus evapotranspiration is estimated at 434 BCM/year for 2010 and ACA's per capita renewable water resources is calculated as 464 m³/capita/year.

2.3 Enhancing the resilience of communities and ecosystems in the Athi River Catchment Area

The National Environment Management Authority (NEMA), Kenya’s nationally accredited entity to the Green Climate Fund (GCF), in collaboration with the Korea Environmental Industry and Technology Institute (KEITI), is developing a $10 million project titled "Enhancing the resilience of communities and ecosystems in the Athi River Catchment Area" for submission to the GCF.

The ARCA is classified as semi-arid land and suffers from frequent drought and flooding events exacerbated by climate change. Through the gender analysis carried out in the four counties on building resilience of communities in the arid river catchment area it was noted that women and girls are mainly responsible for fetching water from shallow wells, roof catchments and rivers. The discussions of the findings in this study are based on the outputs from the analyses of the data obtained from the study in the four counties as follows:

#### Nyandarua County

##### Water Access and Sources

Most of the households depend on water from shallow wells, roof catchments and rivers. The average distance to the nearest water point is 1.3km. Some of the households have access to piped water while a few households have access to portable water.

##### Water Resources and quality

The county has limited water resources and the situation has been aggravated by the degradation of water catchments leading to reduced ground water recharge. As a result, boreholes have medium to low yields. Main source of water for domestic use is dams, roof catchments and rivers. The poor quality of water in these sources poses health risks to the population. Abandon forest providing water resource with high quality has been destroyed by illegal logging and deforestation activities. Consequently, the quantity and quality of water from this forest has also deteriorated.

##### Climate Risks

The county has witnessed a change in climate over the years. Cases of crop failure e.g Irish potatoes have been common due to extremely low temperatures at night leading to frost bite. Livestock farming has also been affected leading to reduced productivity especially for dairy and beef products. The county has also experienced climate risks due to increase in unregulated quarrying activities (especially in Olukolou, Kipkari and Kinangop) and sand harvesting in Mbaratii silting of dams and rivers and deforestation of the catchment area.

### Glossary of terms

**Gender:** refers to the roles, behaviors, activities, and attributes that a given society at a given time considers appropriate for men and women. It also refers to the opportunities associated with being male and female and the relationships between women and men in a given context. These are learned through socialization processes. They are not fixed, singular, or changeable. Gender dynamics is what is expected, allowed and valued in a woman or a man in a given context.

**Gender analysis:** is a critical examination of how differences in gender roles, activities, norms, opportunities, and constraints affect men, women, girls and boys in certain situation or contexts. Gender analysis examines the relationships between females and males and the distribution and control of resources and the constraints they face relative to each other. A gender analysis should be integrated into all sector assessments or situational analyses to ensure that gender-based inequalities are identified and exacerbated by interventions, and that where possible, greater equality and justice in gender relations are promoted.

**Gender mainstreaming:** refers to a strategy for ensuring greater equity for women and girls in relation to men and boys. Mainstreaming is a gender perspective is the process of assessing the implications for women and men of any planned action, including legislation, policies or programs, in all areas and at all levels. It involves making both women's as well as men's concerns and experiences an integral dimension of the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of policies and programs so that women and men benefit equally and inequality is not perpetuated. The ultimate goal is to achieve gender equality.

**Gender roles:** These often determine the traditional responsibilities and tasks assigned to men, women, boys and girls (see gender division of labor). Gender-specific roles are often conditioned by household structure, access to resources, specific impacts of the global economy such as economic globalization or disaster, and other locally relevant factors such


---
**Page 166**
---

as ecological conditions. Like gender itself,  
gender roles can evolve over time, in particular  
through the empowerment of women and transformation of social norms.

Gender-responsive budgeting or GRB is a method of determining the extent to which government expenditure has detracted from or come nearer to the goal of gender equality. A gender-responsive budget is not a separate budget for women but rather an analysis that analyzes budget allocations, public spending and taxation from a gender perspective and can be subsequently used to advocate for reallocation of budgets in order to better respond to women's priorities as well as men's making them, as the name suggests, gender responsive.

Gender disaggregated data refers to sex on individuals broken down by sex. Gender analysis relies on these un-disaggregated data and reflect the realities of the lives of women and men and policy issues relating to gender. Data collected and tabulated separately for women and men. They allow for the measurement of differences between women and men on various social and economic dimensions, and are one of the key tools in obtaining programme results.

Gender action plan (GAP) is a key gender mainstreaming tool and mechanism for ensuring gender-inclusive design and implementation of projects. Gender Action Plan (GAP) forms the basis for developing strategies and recommendations of the gender analysis. It contains specific gender elements to be considered in the project design and during the implementation of projects, programmes and activities.
