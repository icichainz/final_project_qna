The first phase of TERM are under implementation. When these phases are complete, approximately 27% of Tonga’s electricity will be generated from renewable energies. Building on this, the proposed project—the Tonga Renewable Energy Project (TREP)—to be funded in part by the Green Climate Fund (GCF) —will be the major force to implement the remaining 73% of TERM.

To also improve the demand side management of electricity, GCF has aimed to improve energy efficiency through reduction of electricity lines losses to 5 percent by 2020 (from a baseline of 18 percent in 2010) under its NDC. To achieve this target, Tonga will implement and continue implementing the following completed and planned energy efficiency activities (see Figures 1 and 4):

- Power distribution network upgrades under the Tonga Village Network Upgrade Project on Tongatapu (completed)
- Power distribution network upgrades under the Outer Islands Renewable Energy Project (DREP) on Ha'apai (completed), Eua (completed) and Vava'u (on-going)
- Power distribution network upgrades under the Tonga Village Network Upgrade Project on Tongatapu (planned); and
- Others (e.g. Smart meter installation, Interconnection Upgrades and Energy Efficiency Programmes for residential and commercial customers, etc.) (on-going).

Impact potential: The impact of TREP will be a transformational shift away from the traditional reliance on fossil fuels toward a more sustainable energy system that integrates renewable energy systems coupled with battery energy storage systems (BESS) and reduced GHG emissions as well as promotion of more private sector investment into renewable energy development.

- Install multiple units of BESS—with preliminary capacity of 10 MW/16 MWh in Tonga to overcome the technical barrier to greater renewable energy integration to the grid and therefore unlock private sector investment into renewable energy systems. This will be complemented by a further 3.8 MW of grid-connected renewable energy (4 MW solar PV and 3.8 MW wind power) generation capacity on Tongatapu than is then to be financed by Independent power producers (IPP). (see Output 1 of Chapter C.3.)
- Install about 1.5 MW of renewable energy generation capacity in the outer islands coupled with the associated BESS—this will be complemented by a further 2.8 MW of grid-connected renewable energy (2.4 MW solar PV and 0.4 MW wind power) generation capacity on the outer islands than is then to be financed by Independent power producers (IPP). (see Output 1 of Chapter C.3.)
- Build a mini-grid system in the outer islands to bring electricity generated from renewable-energy-based hybrid system including the associated BESS with a preliminary capacity of 9.50 MWh to consumers.(see Output 3 of Section C.3.)

- Improve capacity of OGT and TPL on (i) developing renewable energy systems including BESS; regarding operation and maintenance, (ii) setting due tariffs for power purchase agreements for private sector funded investment, and (iii) providing technical assistance in both areas.

- Make a major contribution to Tonga’s NDC target of generating 50% of electricity from renewables by 2030 and help Tonga build momentum to reach 70% by 2030. TREP-financed generation technologies in the outer islands will bring down the cost of renewable energy generation, which is currently high. This will also lead to a further increase in renewable energy generation by about 7.8 MW of solar PV and 3.8 MW wind power to be funded by the private sector), which will enable Tonga to increase its renewable energy generation by 24% without negatively impacting on the cost of electricity. The project will also contribute significantly towards Tonga’s NDC target of reducing electricity lines losses to 5 percent by 2020 (from a baseline of 18 percent in 2010).

## Paradigm shift potential: TREP has been designed to:

(i) Help Tonga transition from a fossil fuel pathway that is almost entirely (about 90%) dependent on imported fossil fuels for power generation towards a pathway using clean and renewable energy sources that is low-carbon and climate resilient;

(ii) Provide enabling technical solutions (e.g., BESS, etc.) and capacity building for promoting more private sector investments in renewable energy projects which will help Tonga to meet its 50% renewable energy target by 2030 and build momentum to reach 70% by 2030. Without the proposed BESS under TREP, planned solar pv and wind power generation capacity would be insufficient to meet Tonga's electricity demand in the future. Moreover, the electricity generated from these renewable energy generation facilities will be more reliable than that currently being supplied.

(iii) Provide enabling technical solutions (e.g., BESS, etc.) and capacity building for promoting more private sector investments in renewable energy projects which will help Tonga to meet its 50% renewable energy target by 2030 and build momentum to reach 70% by 2030. Tonga is a small island developing state (SIDS) and is highly vulnerable to external economic shocks and climate change. The small size of the economy means that it has limited capacity for diversification. Without significant diversification measures it cannot access commercial credit on favorable terms. Considering this, GCF requests grant financing from ADB to support TREP.

### Vulnerability
In the 2016 update of the World Risk Index, Tonga is again ranked as second most vulnerable country worldwide. Further, although not considered to be in a fragile and conflict affected situation (FCAS), Tonga has been identified by the World Bank as being at high risk for FCAS.

### Foreign exchange risk
As of June 2016, over 95% of the total public debt stock was denominated in foreign currency, leaving Tonga highly exposed to exchange rate risks.

### Need for TREP
Tonga’s debt service ratio is currently very high (73%) and government social policy precludes increasing them. Based on analysis undertaken by Tonga Power Limited (TPL) - the sole state owned power utility - it was concluded that the most cost-effective way of providing electricity to customers, thereby helping to meet social objectives.

### External Debt Sustainability Analysis (EDSA)
The EDSA is a debt sustainability analysis—Press Release and the Staff Report for Tonga released by the International Monetary Fund (IMF) in January 2018. Tonga’s Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA) raises concerns about the country’s ability to service its public debt, which is highly vulnerable to natural disasters. As a result, Tonga is only eligible to receive 100% grants from many development partners, including ADB.

### Debt-to-equity ratio
TPL has a profound 40% debt-to-equity ratio (debt/equity) threshold. Current projections for total debt at the end of June 2017 were T$32.2 million. TPL’s debt ratio has already exceeded this 40% threshold. Further borrowing for TREP would push TPL’s debt ratio well beyond its debt sustainability levels (up to about 67%).

## C.1 Strategic Context

Please describe relevant national, sub-national, regional, global, political, and/or economic factors that help to contextualize the project/programme.

The Kingdom of Tonga is a small island developing state (SIDS) consisting of 177 islands with a total area of 748 km² divided into four island groups: Tongatapu, Ha'apai, Vava'u and Niuas. 96% of Tonga's islands are inhabited by approximately 105,000 people. The largest group is the Tongatapu Group which includes Nuku'alofa, the main island and the location of the capital, Nuku'alofa. The entire country is considered remote from most markets, lying in the Pacific approximately 3,000 km from Fiji and over 4,500 km from New Zealand.

In Tonga, 89% of households have access to electricity, rising to 97% in urban areas. Like many other small Pacific islands, Tonga's electricity production relied almost exclusively on diesel generation. As illustrated throughout this report, this has the following negative implications: high greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, high electricity production costs and tariffs, high input price volatility, high dependency on imports, and localized pollution. For example, the electricity tariff peaked in September 2008 at $0.413/kWh, and again in July 2011 at $0.435/kWh and then dropped to approximately $0.30/ kWh in March 2017 due to a fall in global fuel prices.

Recognizing this contribution to GHG emissions, Tonga's Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC) includes the following targets:

- By 2020, 50% of all electricity to be generated from renewables. Since around 2010, GQT and TPL have focused on new renewable energy projects with high upfront system costs and takes longer for implementation, most entirely supported by various development partners.
- By 2030, 70% of all electricity to be generated from renewables. GQT and TPL will focus a few main islands, mainly Niuatoputapu, Tongatapu, Ha'apai, and Vava'u. To achieve this target, given that RE penetration is above 50%, a comprehensive storage plan would be of the essence, which is currently being prepared by TPL (operating as the sole grid operator). The plan will need to address issues such as balancing generation and demand. Supporting equipment (to be financed by public funding) must able to absorb these fluctuations without penalizing damage to the grid, while increasing the RE penetration (to be funded by private sector) in addition 20%. GQT will also focus on new island and new diesel operations after 2030.

Moreover, as part of GQT's demand side management efforts electricity line losses are to be reduced to 9% (from a baseline of 18 percent in 2010).

To address the challenges of (i) climate change, (ii) energy security and (iii) energy access, GQT issued the Renewable Energy Act in 2008. A transition to renewable energy has been a stated national priority ever since. Further, in 2009, GQT issued the Tonga Energy Road Map: 2010 - 2021/TEFMA. ### C.5 Market Overview (if applicable)

The Electricity Act of 2007 is the legislation governing the electricity sector and establishes the Electricity Commission as the independent electricity sector regulator, consisting of not less than three and not more than five members.

The Electricity Act of 2007 outlines the important role of the Commission in regulating the generation and selling of electricity and establishing a framework for the development of the sector. In addition to its regulatory functions, the Commission has authority for the Ministry of Finance and National Planning (MFPN) to be a party of the concession contract between the Commission and the private sector.

The Electricity Act also provides for the establishment of a Concession Agreement with the private sector. The Concession agreement outlines a utility's operations in comprehensive detail, including how the tariffs are calculated. The Government of Tonga also issued the Renewable Energy Act in 2008. Transition to renewable energy has been a national priority and is reflected in the National Energy Road Map, 2015 – 2025 (TERM) established the target of generating 50% of all electricity from renewables by 2020.

The responsibility for providing Alternating Current (AC) electrical services rests solely with TPL. TPL has legal rights under the Electricity Act to operate and maintain its generation, transmission and distribution assets in Tonga. The non-fuel tariffs cover operational costs, business overheads, interest payments, depreciation and an allowed return on investment, which provides retained earnings for future capital expenditure and/or shareholder dividend payment.

For mini-grid and off-grid systems, EPDC is the primary electricity supplier. It is not regulated by the government but has a mandate to provide and plan for the supply of electricity within its territory. In addition, the Ha'apai, and Vava'u island groups have individual mini-grid and specific off-grid project management structures. Ha'apai (its latter-day name) was once a utility or cooperative societies (EPCOS) to provide the day-to-day operations of the mini-grid system. The other islands in the group (e.g., Niuatoputapu, Nukufetau and Nukutavake) have specific arrangements for managing their solar home systems (SHS) and small diesel generators. The term SHS or CDM is expected to be made between GOA and TPL as those outer islands are to be funded under TREP.

All assets under Output 1 (BESS) and 2 (Grid REI) will be operated and maintained by TPL. Mini-grid system under Output 3 (MGS) will be operated and maintained by the respective utility or cooperative societies in each island administration. For Output 3, GOA will contract TPL under the government's budget for periodic and preventive O&M to ensure a permanent and durable commitment to the long-term maintenance of the outer island energy assets.

Market Size

As of early 2017, system capacity had increased to 25.25 MW, of which 3.7 MW was solar photovoltaic (PV), 1.2 MW of battery energy storage systems (BESS) has also been installed; mainly for grid stability. In 2016 (the last year for which information is available), Tonga's total electricity generation capacity was approximately 54 GWh. For the year ending June 2016, total generation (diesel plus renewables) in all four islands groups was approximately 60 GWh, an increase from 53.4 GWh over the previous 12-month period. Out of total power generated, 53.3 GWh were billed to consumers and 7 GWh were used for system operation (e.g., 1.4 MW and 4.9 MW respectively). The overall system losses were reduced to 11.0% at end of June 2016.
