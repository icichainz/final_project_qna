#### A.2 Project / Program Executive Summary (max 300 words)
The proposed project titled "XeBank's Energy Efficiency and Renewable Energy Solutions" will be a $60 million facility aimed at promoting the use of energy efficient and renewable energy solutions in the Mongolian MSME market.
MSME’s make up more than 95% of the businesses in Mongolia, the majority of which are currently using extremely inefficient technologies. The project's main objective is to promote both the use and production of EE/RE products in the domestic market.

For more description on the specific types of eligible sub-projects, see the table titled "MSME Eligibility". XeBank will be able to provide MSMEs with a 5-year loan facility through its subsidiary, XeBank Finance, which allows XeBank to improve its lending terms (longer loan tenor and lower interest rate) through blending with current EE/RE funds, including the GCF’s GHG emission reduction program, which this program will be an extension of.

Currently, XeBank has roughly 200 active corporate clients, more than 3,400 active SME clients (over 22,000 SMEs that have conducted business with the Bank over the years), and many more micro-sized clients at hand. As such, XeBank is well positioned to provide MSMEs in Mongolia with energy efficient products and services.
The rest of roughly 60 registered MSMEs in Mongolia will reap the benefits of a low cost, high efficiency loan product that is tailored specifically for their needs.

This project has an extremely high impact, including working in concert with the NDA and engaging local stakeholders to ensure that the program will continue to align with Mongolian’s INDCs, NAMAs, as well as other relevant legislation, such as the Forest Law and the Water Law.

Additionally, the program will look to support women-led MSMEs as much as possible by offering more concessional loans terms to such entities. The definition of women-led MSMEs can be found in the Gender Action Plan as attached in annex 8.
By the end of the third year of the program, the facility’s portfolio will be made up entirely of women-led MSMEs.

By implementing this program, it is estimated that the Bank would be able to finance projects to reduce carbon emissions by more than 1.2 million tons over its lifespan of 8 years.

## B.3 Financial Markets Overview (if applicable)

Financial barriers are one of the primary reasons for the Mongolian market to not adopt new, energy efficient or renewable energy solutions. Currently, many Mongolian businesses or individuals operate heavily under the short-term cost savings method; and as such, usually opt to purchase implement the cheapest possible solution. This is often a suboptimal choice in terms of long term costs, as they are often of poor quality or the cost saving potential of high quality, energy efficient or renewable energy solutions. To compound this problem, the Mongolian market has extremely high financing costs, which come with relatively short loan tenures; and as such, many factors that would normally be considered in a financial decision are not readily available adequately. The lack of available and accessible commercial finance on offer by local banks impedes the ability of MSMEs to invest in energy efficiency and renewable energy solutions.

### Overview of Available MNT Loans

Typically, most Mongolian banks in Mongolia offer loans in the local currency, Mongolian National Tugrik (MNT) as well as U.S. Dollars (USD); however, as the Mongolian economy has been in recession since early 2015, and the foreign exchange risk continues to rise due to the depreciation of the MNT, most banks, including XacBank, have stopped offering loans denominated in USD. ## C3 Strategic Context

### National Regulatory

In order to achieve the Mongolian government’s stated goal of a 14% reduction in total national GHG emissions, the NDC outlined a number of policies across the energy, industrial, agricultural, and waste sectors. The MSME program falls under the following INDC goal: “increase the share of renewable electricity capacity to 30% of total electricity generation capacity from 7.62% in 2014.” For information on other national priorities, such as NDCs, NAMAs, etc., see section E.5.

With regards to the Mongolian national context, the MSME program is entering the market at precisely the right time. The Energy Conservation Law has been in effect for years now but finally been passed and are beginning to be implemented. Primary among these regulations is the Energy Conservation Law. The law mandates that large energy users in Mongolia reduce energy consumption by 15%, as audited by experts. For more information on this regulation, see E.2.4.

### Inroads

In mid-2016, Mongolia held national elections and the incumbent Mongolian Democratic Party was overwhelmingly replaced by the Mongolian People’s Party. The incoming government has pledged to introduce more consistent and favorable policies to encourage foreign investment. Other than this, there is no material political difference that will affect this proposal.

### Economic Context

Mongolia is currently in the midst of an economic crisis with ballooning government debt and a rapidly depreciating local currency. Thus, it is difficult to convince businesses to invest in anything that costs more than the market minimum. However, in the recession, saving costs has become ever more important. The government has introduced a number of measures to encourage savings, including a program to support entrepreneurs in the cutting of costs to increase profits. The government has raised Central Bank interest rates 5 points to 10%. With hard money policy in place and forecasted to continue into the near future, future availability of funding will become even more crucial to MSMEs wishing to obtain a loan from commercial banks.

### Sub-National Municipal

Rural and non-capital city MSME outreach are crucial components of this proposal; all major energy-intensive MSMEs are outside the city. That being said, the key geographic focus of the program remains Ulaanbaatar, home to half the Mongolia population and base of the overwhelming majority of Mongolian MSMEs. Thus, it is relevant to discuss the context of Ulaanbaatar municipality and how this current economic crisis has affected its business environment. In 2015, the Ulaanbaatar government passed the Green Development Strategic Plan (GDSP) for Ulaanbaatar 2020. The GDSP highlights air pollution as the primary environmental challenge faced by Ulaanbaatar, writing “as long as we are dependent on burning fossil fuels and inefficient energy use, we will continue to have poor air quality.” The plan is focused on increasing efficient utilization of energy, which includes as a sub-clause: "promote use of energy efficient technologies in the private sector." The MSME program would directly support the Ulaanbaatar government in achieving this goal by providing energy efficiency training and technical assistance for its citizens. Another stated priority is increasing the supply of clean energy in the Mongolian market. By supporting both energy efficient and renewable energy technologies, the MSME program also helps the municipal

# Barrier Analysis

Mongolia faces challenges in mobilizing its full potential to achieve energy efficiency in these sectors. Barrier analysis in the UNEP’s TNA considers financial, economic and technical challenges faced by different prioritized technologies in the energy industry sub-sector. The most common barriers include the following:

## Type of Common Barriers: Banners

- Economic and financial barriers:
  - Inadequate financial incentives; high cost of capital; high transaction cost;
  - Lack or inadequate access to financial resources and uncertain macroeconomic environment.

- Policy, legal and regulatory barriers:
  - Weak institutional commitment and uncertain government policies (political risks for investors); lack of government control for implementation of laws and regulations; government or utility monopoly of energy sector.
  - Underdeveloped institutions and inefficient coordination between relevant ministries and other stakeholders.

- Market and network barriers
  - Lack of knowledge on available loans, EE and RE benefits and technologies: potential borrowers are not familiar with the terms and conditions of a loan from a bank. Also, potential customers don't know the positive impact of new EE and RE investments toward the environment.
  - Additional barriers for renewable energy technology: renewable carry additional barriers via-a-vis energy efficiency upgrades. The primary barrier is the additional costs of installing renewable technology (e.g., re-wiring, land flattening for solar), compared to the direct one-to-one replacement of energy sources. These costs are often perceived as being significantly higher, even though the eventual cost savings are likely lower, due to dependency on natural elements.

With all of these energy usage and greenhouse gas barrier contributing in the energy and carbon intensity in Mongolia, XacBank sees potential and opportunity in making a positive impact to these barriers. At the same time, the bank will be supporting the sustainable energy market in public and private sectors through our program.

# Regulatory

This program is entering the market at an optimal time to have an impact on the implementation of new national regulations on energy usage. # F.1 Economic and Financial Analysis

## GCF Concessionally

Section B.3 outlines the existing interest rates and loan tenors typical to the Mongolian commercial banking market.

Blending concessional funding from the GCF with XacBank's existing EE/RE financing facilities, which offer below market rates, will bring impactful benefits to the loan recipients: incentives to establish and/or expand on new business models for integrating EE/RE solutions; capacity building of Mongolian MSMEs as mentioned in other sections of this document; and the promotion of gender equality through support of women-led MSMEs.

Concessionally will consist of reduced pricing compared to Mongolian prices and the international market finance available thus far via XacBank. The provision of reduced pricing is the most crucial element in attracting MSMEs to EE/RE solutions, as it allows them to meet their financial obligations while enabling the Bank's clients to accept the enhanced reporting and monitoring requirements, as well as the expenditure in setting up new lines of business to incorporate EE/RE measures.

Without concessional funding from the GCF, XacBank will not be able to offer the loans on an as-is basis under the current market conditions. EE/RE financing has not been able to achieve anywhere near its potential on the Mongolian market. Since the inception of the GHG Emissions Reduction Program, XacBank has provided 10 loans for projects that have reduced CO2 emissions by approximately 76,769 tons. These loans have mainly been distributed to MSMEs on small scale projects; however, many opportunities to finance larger scale projects have been lost due to the high interest rates.

As can be seen in the financial model, the current rates are at 18.0% and 15.12%, with tenors up to only 5 years for such projects. Even with the Bank not making any profits with the EE/RE loans (only covering costs), with concessional funding from the GCF, XacBank will be able to offer a loan at below market rates. With XacBank, the opportunity will have the opportunity to reduce this interest rate down to as little as 14-15%, with loan tenors of up to 8 years. In this case, the EE/RE financing product would have a much wider appeal, thus, allowing the Bank to better compete in the market and attract larger client implementations, which is currently the most produced capital city in the world, Ulaanbaatar.

## Environmental and Social Responsibility Risk Evaluation

Risk evaluations are carried out for all Bank loans that are more than MNT 50,000,000 (approximately US$25,000) and have tenors more than 12 months, based on a comprehensive scorecard, including sections on environmental issues, social labor issues, and compliance issues. Each risk category is rated from low to high with the highest rating being "high." The SEMS has been designed to demonstrate the utility of its SEMS to its stakeholders, including clients, and closely monitors the environmental performance of these borrowers and actively manages any instances of non-compliance to rectify such situations.

Each sub-project under the MSME program will be considered on a case-by-case basis. When evaluating the environmental and social risks associated with a sub-project, the Bank will consider a wide variety of factors, including:

1. Nature of the sub-borrower's business (e.g., micro, small, or medium-sized business sector).
2. Does the sub-borrower have any current exposures that represent high environmental and social risk?
3. Does the sub-borrower have appropriate waste management solutions?
4. Does the sub-borrower have any water pollution management?
5. Does the sub-borrower's activities cause any damage or contamination to the local soil or causes any adverse effect on the environment?
6. Does the sub-borrower's activities cause any undue air pollution?

Also taken into consideration are the sub-borrower’s employee work place safety, health and hygiene practices and whether or not they cause any adverse effect to the local areas and community.

### H.2. Arrangements for Monitoring, Reporting and Evaluation

#### Monitoring
For information on monitoring, including what will be monitored and how monitoring will be conducted please see the implementation arrangements document as well as the tables in section C.3 titled "MSME Eligibility". To explicitly clarify, XacBank is the entity responsible for monitoring, and this includes the cost of monitoring and verifying emissions savings, as well as keeping evidence/invoices. XacBank calculates the emissions savings based on the methodology provided by GCF and verifies the data with verified GHGs.

Monitoring, reporting, and evaluation arrangements will comply with the relevant GCF policies as appointed in the MAV, AMA etc., XacBank will report to the GCF on a periodic basis as agreed with the GCF on the status of GCF funded activities throughout the relevant reporting period. These reports will include the disbursements made during the relevant period, the implementation status of the sub-projects and the monitoring of results and impacts of sub-projects.

#### Milestones/Reports
| Milestone/Report | Expected Dates |

| Interim Evaluation report | 1 month after the effectiveness of the FAA |
| Completion report | 1 month after the end of the Tenor or Extended Tenor, as applicable |

#### Reporting
