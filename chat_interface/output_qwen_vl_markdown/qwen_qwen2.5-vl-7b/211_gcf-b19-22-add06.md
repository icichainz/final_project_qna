## A2. Project Executive Summary (max 300 words)

1. The proposed Project aims at mobilizing concessional finance from the Green Climate Fund (GCF) to promote the efficiency in the production and use of energy in Argentina, in order to contribute to the reduction of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions and to increase the resilience of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in the long term.

2. GCF funding will be channeled through a sovereign loan to the Republic of Argentina and will be executed by the Banco de Inversión y Comercio Exterior S.A.I.C.E. (Argentina’s national development bank, NDB), and will be used for the financing of projects in the energy sector, including investments in renewable energy (RE) and enterprise (SME) + sub-projects in specific SE technologies (biomass, biogas and energy efficiency). This concept is based on the idea that a large-scale project alone may not be sufficient to achieve the desired impact. An adequate direct sub-project funding to jumpstart the evolving market, specifically where LFs do not take senior political risk, will be essential for the success of the Project. The GCF loan will be used as a financial instrument to support the structuring of various mechanisms that will complement the provision of loans and improve LF and SME technical capacity and knowledge of the Project and the type of investments it promotes*. GCF grant funding will be complemented by an investment from BGE (USD 600,000), including staff hours, logistics and office space, and...

*Hereinafter, renewable energy (RE) and energy efficiency (EE) investments are encompassed by the collective term “Sustainable Energy Investments” (SEI). The SEI will be defined as investments in projects that fall within the sub-tem of biomass, biogas and demand side EE investments (sub-projects).

*The IFC will provide a loan agreement with BGE to finance the project. BGE will enter into a loan agreement with the Republic of Argentina for Component 2 of the Program. The sovereign loan contract determines the responsibility of the Republic of Argentina as borrower, the Bank Group as lender and the Project as the implementing entity. And as such will sign the sovereign loan contract. BGE and the Republic of Argentina will enter into an agreement to provide a guarantee to the IFC for the loan provided by BGE to BGE.

*For the purpose of this Project, the definition of SME will follow Argentina’s government classification of SMEs, as per Resolution 102/2018 of the Ministry of Production. Under this resolution, firms are classified by size based on their level of sales, with distinctions between micro-enterprises (ME), small enterprises (SE) and medium-sized enterprises (MSE). Micro-enterprises are those with sales up to $64 million pesos or $21 million Pesos, for the industrial and services sectors, respectively.

*It is expected that the largest LFs will be MSEs. These firms have annual sales larger than USD 8 million. Only biomass projects are expected to be particularly this large (see Table F2.1).

*These investments will be used in the following areas: energy efficiency and renewable energy services and technology providers (ESTP) and loan discussions by LF to target bankable SMEs. The promotion thereof is based on two main pillars: 1) BGE’s strategy to target existing and new clients and 2) LFs actively search their existing client base for financing opportunities, conscious about their debt capacity.

# B.1. Description of Financial Elements of the Project

The project involves investments in biomass, biogas and EE sub projects (see Section F.2) for a total amount of USD 430.64 million. This includes USD 100 million of GCF financing from the Project in a revolving account, thus financing multiple sub-projects over time. The remaining USD 330.64 million will be provided by other sources as described below.

The project is designed with the objective of providing adequate financing to viable SE projects, in order to help fund the expansion and replication of these projects across the region. This can be achieved through two main mechanisms: (i) by generating a track record of successful projects and building capacities among agents of the industry to address barriers to scaling up; and (ii) by leveraging resources from other sources.

GCF funding will contribute to lowering the cost of financing resulting from high perceived risks of these relatively new technologies, as well as providing technical assistance for project development. The Project will also provide a platform for participating sub-projects. By working via the BDS NICE, the Project will also be able to leverage funding from this initiative and provide additional training and support mechanisms and skills to assess and implement these projects in the future.

The GCF financing will be structured as follows: USD 100 million will be provided by IGF in a revolving account for investment in SE projects, either first through tier one operations (for sub loans larger than USD 5 million) or via LFsAs. A grant element, to be executed by the IGF in co-ordination with BICE is requested to support the implementation of the proposed project. The Project will also seek to leverage other sources of financing for SE projects, including through bankable project structuring and enable dissemination and capacity building activities to address non-financial barriers.

## C1. Strategic Context

The energy sector is essential to the proper functioning of any modern economy. As economic activity grows, more and better availability of energy is required. Governments in all emerging economies are currently facing the complex challenge of balancing economic growth with environmental sustainability.

Factoring sustainability into this equation has acquired an increasingly important role in the face of global climate change, as energy generation and consumption are major contributors to growth. Two of the main sources of CO2 emissions are electricity production and transport. As a global GHG emitter, Argentina is taking on a critical part of any solution aimed at addressing the threat of climate change.

The key lies in making efforts to decouple economic development from greenhouse gas emissions. In this sense, the energy sector plays an important role: in power generation from clean sources plays a large role in this process, contributing to diversifying the countries' energy mix and reducing their dependence on fossil fuels. At the same time, improvements in energy efficiency and consumption patterns that allow for the use of energy in more efficient ways can also have a significant impact in reducing emissions.

According to the results of Argentina's Third National Communication on Climate Change, GHG emissions are estimated at 409 million tons CO2e (megatons) in 2015. This is an increase of approximately 38% from the 298.7 million Ton CO2e reported in the Second National Communication in 2006; 43% of those emissions come from energy production.

Argentina's goal, set forth in their first revision of the NDC 2015, is to reduce GHG emissions by 15% by 2030 with respect to a business-as-usual scenario. This target can be achieved through various actions such as the promotion of sustainable forest management, nuclear power biocells and transport modal shift. Furthermore, it will also require that adequate and predictable international financing becomes available; (ii) support for transfer, innovation and technology development; and (iii) capacity building to ensure the creation or capacities to spread good practices and effectively implement the proposed measures are provided.

D.1 Value Added for GCF Involvement

As long as there remains a lack of a larger and more sophisticated banking system in the country, and given the levels of risks associated with Sustainable Energy (SE) projects, as well as the existing lack of knowledge that prevails among private sector investors about SE projects, it is unlikely that they will be able to finance large-scale beneficiary projects viable, influencing the characteristics and evolution of private sector participation. The GCF contribution will help to overcome these barriers.

As described in Sections C.1 and E.3, Argentina is already committed to the transformation of its energy sector based on a combination of public and private investment. In this context, the GCF contribution will be timely as the reforms underway are expected to improve conditions for investment in the sector; this process will likely take time before it becomes fully effective.

The availability of concessional financing at low interest rates and with long-term maturity is considered meaningful to jumpstart the market. Without such a facility, private investors would not be able to finance large-scale projects, contributions by Lifs of LFs and RE investments will continue to be limited in Argentina.

The Project's objective is to provide concessional financial resources strictly relate to the objective of the Project itself, namely to scale up investment in RE and EE. Without this incentive, the volume and pace of investments in these sectors would remain low. The GCF contribution will also help to overcome barriers for structuring a financing package that is essential to overcome the barriers faced by private sector investors through the provision of concessional financing at low interest rates and with long-term maturity.

SE investments and increasing private actors' access to finance per se.

GCF's involvement in Argentina is critical for sub-projects to effectively offset the additional cost associated to the described risks and barriers and benefit from the financial effect of increased leverage that is fundamental for their competitiveness and financial viability.

The following mechanisms are considered sufficient to mitigate the risk that the benefits of the concessionality can be lost:

1. First, Operational Regulations will be developed and enforced to ensure that the spread charged by Lifs is aligned with the interest rate on concessional financing. Competition between Lifs will further ensure that rates from BICE and Lifs to beneficiary SMEs are competitive.

2. Second, semi-annual reporting and verification of the beneficiary SME portfolio and activities financed with GCF funds will be carried out. The reporting and verification will specifically consider the appropriate application of concessionality; and

3. Third, corrective measures in cases of non-compliance with the previous provisions will be defined in the Operational Regulations.

The above mentioned risk mitigation mechanisms are considered sufficient to ensure that concessionality of funds from BICE and Lifs is not reduced or lost as a result of their being fully capitalized by BICE or Lifs.

As they develop a long-term financing market, the concessionality in the form of interest rate and tenor will enable the financing of sub-projects that currently are not being served. The interest rates are subject to monitoring by the IBS via reporting of specific financial data on each beneficiary sub-project; by transferring concessionality to the local system, it is expected that the interest rates charged by Lifs will be competitive with those available without long-term finance.

In the medium term, grant money will provide the necessary technical assistance to develop support mechanisms, build capacities and support sub-projects, while concessional loans will help lower the cost of long-term financing. The combination of these two instruments will make it possible for a greater number of projects present at market rates, the financing terms of the blend will represent an enhancement of the financing available in terms of interest rate and tenor.

The GCF contribution will be provided through a NDB as a loan with concessional terms and long-term maturity. The funding available through the local financial system, under conditions that make it possible for these institutions to structure projects at market rates, is expected to cover the remaining financing needs of sub-projects.

### RATIONALE FOR GCF INVOLVEMENT

The way in which the Project is designed promotes leverage of additional sources, as each beneficiary sub-project would require a certain level of equity financing (in general: between 20% and 30% of total investment costs), depending on the size of the sub-project. The rationale for this is that NDBs complement program funding with their own resources and may bring other financial institutions into the transaction (commercial banks who would not be willing to finance the projects by themselves in the absence of the Program). This will allow the GCF to leverage its resources, as it will only need to provide financing to other banks or others along through Tier 1 operations if the size of the sub-project is large enough (> USD 5 Million) to allow so.

### H.1.1. Paradigm Shift Objectives and Impacts at the Fund level
#### Shift to low-emission sustainable development pathways
The Project is expected to shift the current paradigm by altering the risk perceptions of the local investors, banks, and other stakeholders in Argentina, thereby creating a more enabling environment for investment into high-standardization, providing a track record of profitable and sustainable business in the energy services market, and thereby supporting an evolving SE market. The Project will also provide a replicable model that can be used by other projects. The Project aims to build a track record of profitable business in the selected technologies with a focus on Argentina. The Project will also provide lessons learned from existing RE and EE financing initiatives. Achieving such a paradigm shift would reduce national energy related emissions in Argentina by 9.1 million tCO2e over the project period, while creating new opportunities for women and men in the biomass, biogas and EE industries, as well as improved access to finance for women-led/owned SMEs investing in biomass, biogas and EE.
