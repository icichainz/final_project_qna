## A.2 Project Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

Please provide a brief description of the proposed project/programme, including the objectives and primary measurable benefits (see investment criteria in section B). The detailed description can be elaborated in section C.

For more than two decades now, Senegal, as an underdeveloped Sahelian country has been facing climate change with all its adverse effects. This is a complex phenomenon that affects all sectors of society and the environment at ecological, social and economic consequences. The main causes of land salinization related to climate factors include:

- (a) Increase in temperature leading to higher evaporation rates and drainage by mean water.
- (b) Low infiltration of fresh water leading to salt water intrusion on land;
- (c) Rising sea level causing saltwater encroachment on land; and
- (d) The combined effect of reduced rainfall and increasing temperatures accelerating evaporation which contributes to the salinization process.

Land salinization and the contamination of water tables that result from it have significantly increased the vulnerability of ecosystems and communities, leading particularly to: reduced arable lands, lower soil fertility, decline in yields and productivity, loss of biodiversity, reduction in livestock production, and a general deterioration of human health. At all levels, institutional, legal, social and economic levels leading to the development of policies, strategies and laws as well as the extent of the phenomenon and the persistence of some constraints and barriers, including: (a) a poorly defined problem; (b) lack of knowledge on the phenomenon; (c) limited access to information and data; (d) inadequate climate change in planning and programming; (e) appropriate technical and technological tools are poorly disseminated and not adapted to local conditions; and (f) weak institutional capacity and poor coordination among all stakeholders.

This project is therefore a response, in the short and long terms, to the impacts of climate change specifically on the issue of land salinization in the area of intervention which is a delta area and estuaries. To address the problem, the project proposes to: (a) improve knowledge on the phenomenon; (b) promote the adoption and dissemination of appropriate technologies; (c) encourage the broad participation of beneficiaries (populations and local authorities); it aims to: (d) improve knowledge on the phenomenon; (e) promote the adoption and dissemination of appropriate technologies, and (f) improve the living conditions of the worst-hit local communities.

Here is a summary of expected results:

- Strengthened institutional and technical capacities of all stakeholders (State, local communities, technical structures, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), the private sector, local communities and their organizations, vulnerable groups women and youth, etc.).
- Reduced salinity of water tables.

#### 1.1. The physical context:

Senegal is located between latitudes 12°46' and 18°41' north and longitudes 17°05' and 17°39' west. It covers an area of 196,722 km² (Figure C.1). The Senegalese climate is of tropical semi-arid type, with two distinct seasons: dry season from May to October and rainy season from June to September. It is characterized by high inter-annual rainfall variability in the district and duration of the wet season. The country has a strong dependence on rainfed agriculture influenced with over 700 km² of cropland. Like other Sahelian countries, Senegal is marked by a strong degradation of soil resources due to climate change and human activities. This degradation is characterized by desertification, salinization, variation in seasonal cycles, and low rainfall among others that all impact on production systems. However, the situation varies from one region to another depending on the type of soils, topography, and climatic conditions as well as their magnitude and impacts at the ecological, social and economic levels for local communities and the country as a whole.

# Detailed Project / Programme Description

## I. Biophysical and environmental baseline conditions

The project operates in the ecoregion of the Groundnut Basin (Bassin arachidier; figure 1) with a high density of population comprising mainly agropastoralists growing the country's major food and cash crops such as millet and groundnuts. The project area is characterized by its vulnerability to climate change impacts, particularly in terms of agro-sylvo-pastoral production systems in order to enhance the resilience of ecosystems and ensure food security of communities.

Land salinization in the project area is caused by natural (e.g., climate) and/or anthropogenic (e.g., human) activities:

- Natural causes: (a) seawater intrusions; (b) capillary action from drought and a declining rainfall; and (c) wind transport.
- Anthropogenic causes: (a) bad farming practices such as the irrational use of chemical inputs; (b) the poor quality of development; (c) overuse of groundwater fostering salt water intrusion; (d) the lack of a drainage system; and (e) the absence of an efficient irrigation system.

### Climate variability and man-induced activities have caused increased salinization of land in Senegal, particularly within the project intervention area due to its coastal location, intensive farming and high population density. In the country as a whole, this has led to reduced yields for crops such as groundnuts, millet, sorghum, rice, maize, etc., poor livestock production, and the pollution of freshwater aquifers causing food insecurity and lowering population incomes. This leads to increased poverty in rural areas.

### Faced with a nationalization salinization issue, several measures have been taken by the State, many dating from before the country's independence in 1960, by technical and financial partners, local communities and NGOs. These included the construction of dams and dikes along rivers to control water flow, the creation of irrigation schemes (e.g., the project of Sénégal et Casamance) (on the southwest of Senegal).

However, these measures have not been very effective in controlling salinization as they are often proven ineffective over time and their maintenance costs have been high, while their buy-in by local populations has been weak. More recent experiences promote the use of small-scale community developed infrastructure. These buys-in from the local populations which are more likely to use and maintain them.

### Two major types of actions can be taken out to fight land salinization. They include mechanical and biological actions.
- **Mechanical actions** are under way mainly in Casamance and Sine-Saloum with the construction of anti-salt dikes and embankments particularly in the valleys, especially through the PAPII and Project d'Aménagement et de Développement Rural (PADER) projects to curb seawater intrusion. Other additional actions are under way or planned such as the creation of small-scale irrigation schemes for water flow with support from research (Institut Sénégalais des Recherches Agricoles - ISRA), Institut de Recherche pour le Développement (IRD) ou Centre de coopération Internationale en recherche.

### Socioeconomic baseline: the resilience and coping capacities of local populations to salinization and climate change

The loss of land and productivity in this coastal area is contributing to the increased vulnerability of the population by reducing their access to food, water and other resources. This situation leads to poverty which is concentrated in rural-urban migration, impacting the country as a whole. The project seeks to reduce this phenomenon through the development of local coping strategies that are adapted to the socio-economic context of communities to protect them from increasingly harsh climate conditions.
