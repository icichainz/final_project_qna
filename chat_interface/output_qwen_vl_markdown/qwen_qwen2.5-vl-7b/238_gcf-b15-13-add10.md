## A.2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

1. As a Small Island Developing State (SIDS) in the Pacific, Samoa has been heavily impacted by increasing sea levels and more frequent extreme weather events such as cyclones, droughts, and floods. These have resulted in flooding of lowland areas. Recent tropical events such as Cyclone Evan have caused significant damage to both public and private infrastructure and property, with a heavy toll on the population. The vulnerability of Samoa is exacerbated by the recency of flooding and is unable to cope as climate-related events are expected to become more frequent.
2. In response, the Government of Samoa (GoS) has adopted a programmatic approach to address the issue of climate change impacts in Samoa. This project will contribute towards reducing the vulnerability of communities to the impact of recurrent flood related impacts in the Vaigai River catchment, which flows through the Apia Urban Area (AUA). Recent extreme events have resulted in approximately US$320m worth of damages during each event. Climate projections suggest that this trend is likely to continue and will be exacerbated by urbanization and population growth within the urban AUA where the majority of the population and economic activity is located.
3. The project will contribute towards reducing vulnerability of communities, infrastructure, and the built environment in the Vaigai River Catchment.

### Expected key fund level impacts
- Increased resilience of infrastructure and the built environment to climate change.
- Primary direct beneficiaries include approximately 26,000 people in the Vaigai river catchment who will benefit from improved flood management solutions. Secondary beneficiaries are the wider population exposed to extreme events; flood mitigation measures especially riverside ecosystems and solutions in the Vaigai River Catchment, upstream areas of the Apia Urban Area (AUA) and downstream areas of the Apia Urban Area (AUA). These will help reduce exposure for downstream areas for improved regulation and rapid discharge of water flows during periods of extreme events. Overall, 31,000 people in total are expected to benefit from this project.

### Project represents GoS's initial steps in operationalizing a comprehensive flood management solution to the likely consequences of extreme events in Apia, with capital with about 80,000 people.
- This project, three interlinked components:
    - (i) Capacities and information base strengthened for GoS to pursue an integrated approach to reduce vulnerability towards flood-related risks
    - (ii) Infrastructure in the Vaigai River Catchment is flood-proofed to increase resilience to negative effects of excessive water; and
    - (iii) Upgraded drainage in downstream areas to increase capacity and allow for more rapid outflow of flood waters.

# C1 Strategic Context

## Samoa

### Background

- **Location:** Samoa is a SIDS located in the Polynesian region of the South Pacific.
- **Economic Structure:**
  - GDP: US$663.7 million with a growth rate of 1.2%.
  - Economy relies strongly on agriculture, fisheries, and tourism.
  - Agriculture contributes ~10% of the GDP; however, the sector accounts for ~48% of the labor force.

### Projected Climate Change Scenarios

- More frequent and intense rainfall events.
- Extreme wind events with an extreme daily rain rate of 400 mm currently. A one-in-50-year event will likely become a one-in-10 year event by 2030, while the one-in-100-year event is projected to occur every 7 years and may become a one-in-10-year event by 2050.
- The CSIRO model projects an 8% increase in wind speed for extreme events.

### Settlements

- Concentrated on coastal areas with approximately 70% of the population living within one kilometer of the coast.
- Coastal infrastructure such as hospitals, government buildings, schools, places of worship, and other critical facilities are at risk from flooding caused by extreme rainfall events and coastal inundation. Roads and other key transport infrastructure are also vulnerable to damage or destruction.

### Key Transport Infrastructure

- Key transport infrastructure includes roads and bridges that connect communities.
- These are particularly vulnerable to damage due to flooding, which can lead to accelerated deterioration of road surfaces owing to extreme weather and rising water tables.
- Key transport infrastructure is critical for promoting connectivity and access of communities to inter alia economic growth.

19. A particularly important baseline project for the proposed GCF project is the “Economy Wide Adaptation to Climate Change in Samoa” (EWACCS) funded by the LDCF (Least Developed Country Fund). The LDCF recognizes, among others, the mainstreaming of adaptation efforts into sector policies is critical to address long-term climate change impacts. In this context, a baseline study for the proposed GCF project in the Greater Apia Catchments was developed to identify the root causes of climate vulnerability and outline strategies for reducing these risks. The study identified that the lack of flood protection infrastructure in the catchment area is an approach to building climate resilience and protecting community livelihoods.

20. Although the efforts of the GoS under projects such as the EWACCS and its sectoral sister projects are contributing towards addressing climate change impacts, there remains a need for further action. The GoS recognizes that the necessary transformative process to induce game-changing flood-proofing of the Samoan climate change response mechanism, particularly for the economically vital AUA. By definition, the LDCF financed projects address only urgent and immediate needs and do not provide long-term solutions. In this context, the proposed GCF project will allow for the focus on the highest priority risk-prone geographical areas and/or focus on a subset of critical infrastructure within these catchments. The GoS recognizes that the proposed GCF project is an opportunity to address the risks at hand (and expected) in a holistic manner.

21. In addition, the GoS faces other climate change related hazards that Samoa is and will more frequently be facing in the future as a result of climate change prompted the GoS to request support from UNDP in developing a programme for integrated ridge-to-ridge flood management. As such, a priority focus area within this programme is to reel programme covering water catchments, reservoirs, drainage, river hydro generation, over wall, road network, bridges etc as well as the Matutu port.

22. Specifically, the GoS has aspirations to pursue an integrated ridge-to-ridge approach that encompasses the following:

* Integrated catchment management for the protection of the AUA;
* Construction of a resilient flood protection network including detention storage to manage river flows and the drainage network;
* Establishment of hydropower system for climate smart economic development;
* Sustainable water supply for ensured availability of agricultural and potable water during climatic dures; and
* Rehabilitation of existing infrastructure.

226. Samoa is no stranger to the impacts of hyper-tropicalmonsoon hazards, as it has been affected by cyclones numerous times in recent decades (1989 and 1991). Following Cyclone Ofa, GDP fell by 27% of Samoa's economy (corresponding to the size of Samoa's economy (estimated at the time of Cyclone Evan) to be approximately USD$275 million).
