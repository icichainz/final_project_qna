## A.2. Project/Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

1. Namibia is divided into 14 regional administrative zones. Of the 14, the Zambezi (90.5%), Kaokoveld East and West (62%) and Kunene (78.5%) are in the three regions of interest. The majority of the population has been registered in constituencies of these regions – that is, in Sibinda, Linyanti, Kongola and Kapako (bda.). The primary economic activities in these areas include agriculture, livestock production, fishing and the 'informal sector'. In addition to climate-related challenges, drought and high temperatures are considered normal in highly variable rainfall conditions. These factors have a significant impact on food security and livelihoods. This has been increasing affecting the small scale farming production activities.
2. Most of the affected communities in the regions are vulnerable due to their dependence on rain-fed agriculture for their livelihoods, limited access to water and environmental factors and barriers contributing to these regional vulnerabilities and key among direct consequences of both natural and human-influenced factors interacting with climate stressors is food insecurity and poverty.

They directly depend on agriculture for their livelihood; therefore are considerably very poor in reality. Their poverty is worsened by deficiencies in their adaptive capacities and coping strategies to climate change. The project will be implemented in 20 communities across the three regions targeting communities and groups, including women, in three of the poorest regions (that is, Zambesi, Kavango West and Kevingo). It will also target vulnerable households and smallholder farmers who are not yet benefiting from CA and resilient agri-food systems. The project will be implemented by C.R.A.V.E., a Namibian non-profit organisation that beneficiaries (women and men) acquire abilities to adopt conservation agriculture (CA) and climate-resilient agricultural practices, including the use of solar energy and water harvesting technologies, while also reducing their carbon footprint and are using productive resources and services for food and nutrition security* and sustainable livelihoods supported by the project. The project will be implemented in 4 of the 10 priority study areas.

### The CRAVE project aims to reduce Namibia’s communities’ vulnerability and food insecurity to climate risks and threats while increasing the adaptive capacity, well-being and resilience of the vulnerable small-scale farming communities in crop production landscapes that are threatened by climate variability and change. The project is built on the ground realities of the communities and their needs in light of the National Climate Change Action Plan (NCCAP) and revised Namibia Agriculture Policy of 2015. It will run over a period of 60 months consisting of three phases: Phase I (24 months), Phase II (36 months) and Phase III (6 months). The project results to be attained, which are fully elaborated in section C. CRAVE has two sub-objectives:

- To increase the adaptive capacity of vulnerable small-scale farming communities by adopting coping mechanisms and measures (for example comprehensive conservation agriculture and micro drip irrigation), and implement on-the-ground adaptation strategies that will reduce their vulnerability to climate change, erratic weather patterns, seasonal rainfall shifts, heat and drought.
- To increase food security for vulnerable households in the project areas through the adoption of solar energy technologies (water pumping for small-scale micro-horticultural systems, and refrigeration for harvested food) and alternative fuel sources (solar and environmentally unfriendly) imported fuels by promoting solar water pumping in the agricultural sector.

### The CRAVE project will contribute to two of the GCF Results Framework and Level fund impacts:

- A1: Increased resilience of vulnerable small-scale farming communities, people, companies and regions and
- A2: Increased resilience of health and well-being, and food and water security, along with the outcome AF 3 Strengthened adaptive capacity and reduced exposure to climate risks.

### Fundamentally, the entire CRAVE business model (that is, paradigm shift) is to enable vulnerable SSF* to penetrate the local and national agricultural economy from a purely subsistence production model towards full market penetration. The project will be implemented in four priority study areas (PSAs) in three regions of Namibia (Zambesi, Kavango West and Kevingo). These PSAs are identified through the application of the AMTA, AgriBudDev, MAVI* to ensure scale-up, future uptake and sustainability of the results. Thus a full shift in the value chain will be achieved.

* Strategic Aim 4: Early Warning Systems (EWS) and Climate Risk Management (CRM) systems are developed/implemented.

# C.2 Project / Programme Objective against Baseline

The project aims to reduce the vulnerability of farming systems and predisposes rural households to food insecurity and poverty. The spiral cycle of natural variability and long-term change has already resulted in a significant decline in agricultural productivity, especially under adverse climatic conditions, and household food security - all of which breed poverty. While the Namibie has sustained a steady economic growth over the past decade, it is still facing development challenges. These include persistent high poverty levels (impacting 28% of the population); unemployment rates that are among the highest in Africa; and low agricultural productivity. The country's economy (estimated to likely cost GDP losses of about 65% by 2030) and high unemployment rates (41%). This is worsened by the fact that Namibia has a very small agricultural sector, which accounts for only 7% of its GDP. Small-scale farming drought conditions (8-45%), which are attributable to a greater degree to climate variability and change via atmospheric oscillations such as El Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO) and La Niña events, is the most significant risk factor at household levels, especially for vulnerable small-scale farmers (see Feasibility Study Analysis). Thus small-scale farming ESSP requirements for vulnerable farmers and the expanding population (see Namibia's Vulnerability and Adaptation Assessment), in light of these challenges, Namibia needs to adopt agricultural practices and increase the resilience of existing livelihoods. The project will contribute towards achieving the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) growth for the country.

Agriculture in Nambwa plays a critical role in the formal and informal economy by supplying food, employment opportunities and income generation. Climate change will have an impact on maize temperature and rainfall, which dictate crop and livestock selection for a specific location, cultivar choices as well as when to plant; thus making it highly vulnerable to climate change. Any changes in these climatic variables may alter agricultural productivity and the ability of farmers to meet their food security needs. The demand for maize is expected to grow by 4% a year to meet the increasing food requirements for the growing population. However, the expansion of commercial farming has led to increased use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides which are not sustainable in adapting changing climatic patterns, including low and erratic rainfall, and the lack of well-adapted technologies have been identified as major constraints to adaptation. The sector is also vulnerable to extreme weather events such as droughts. Future food security relies not only on higher production and access to food but also on the need to address the declining resilience of the agricultural sector in Nambwa which will also increase the resilience of production practices to the effects of climate change (See additional details in the F5S).
