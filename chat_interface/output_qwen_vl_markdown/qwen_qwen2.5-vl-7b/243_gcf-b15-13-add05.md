### A.2 Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

The coastal areas of Bangladesh are highly susceptible to the impacts of climate change including from cyclones and tidal flooding. The project will target women, adolescent girls and their families who are extremely poor and vulnerable.

#### Objectives:

- **A1:** The proposed GCF funded project will intentionally targets and benefits women and adolescent girls living below the extreme poverty line in six districts.
- **A2:** The project objective is to enhance the adaptive capacity of women and adolescent girls (and by extension, their families), who are in extreme poverty, in six districts that are constantly exposed to cyclones, tidal flooding and salinity. Specifically, GCF financing will be used to roll out a social protection programme that provides cash transfers to vulnerable households.
- **A3:** The project objective is also to enhance the adaptive capacity of women and adolescent girls by strengthening their livelihoods through the activities in the context of a national initiative that will empower targeted vulnerable women and girls to rise above their current poverty levels. Specifically, GCF financing will be used to roll out a social protection programme that provides cash transfers to vulnerable households.
- **A4:** The project objective is also to enhance the adaptive capacity of women and adolescent girls by strengthening their livelihoods through the activities in the context of a national initiative that will empower targeted vulnerable women and girls to rise above their current poverty levels. Specifically, GCF financing will be used to roll out a social protection programme that provides cash transfers to vulnerable households.

#### The primary measurable benefits that will be realized as a result of the GCF investment includes:

- **A5:** Expansion of safe drinking water supply for 125000 people where the current access to clean safe drinking water is limited.
- **A6:** Provide assistance to 40,000 women and 17,000 adolescent girls in six districts to pursue climate resilient livelihoods.
- **A7:** Strengthen community participation in the provision of early warning services directly to benefit 1.2 million women and girls, and indirectly to 2.0 million people.
- **A8:** Strengthen the capacity of communities to address the needs of women and girls as forecasted climate change impacts worsen.
- **A9:** Empower women and adolescent girls in fragile coastal regions to address climate shocks through training and skill building in climate resilient livelihood options that will complement ongoing efforts to help them rise above current poverty levels.

#### The project supports and strengthens Gob’s priority actions to build resilience to climate change as enshrined in the Bangladesh Climate Change Strategy and Action Plan 2013 and Climate Change and Gender Action Plan 2013. The project is aligned to the priority areas of promoting climate resilient livelihoods, early warning systems and water security as it focuses on providing safe drinking water supply for women and girls living below extreme poverty line through climate change adaptation projects and disaster management and livelihood projects implemented by Gob, UNDP and other implementing partners.

#### The project relies on grant finance as the proposed intervention(s) (a) will benefit the extremely poor and climate vulnerable women and adolescent girls (and their families) who are in no position to accept non-grant finance; (b) are focused on providing safe drinking water supply for women and girls living below extreme poverty line, which do not generate significant revenue that lends itself to providing refunds at scale. The project is fully aligned with the Government of Bangladesh’s National Climate Change Strategy and Action Plan 2013 and other relevant policies following extensive stakeholder consultations including participation of civil society. The NDA and his office have been closely involved in the preparation of this proposal, are currently on process and has issued a no objection letter for the project after reviewing and vetting the final design of the project.

C1. Strategic Context

Bangladesh is one of the most densely populated countries with around 158 million people living in 147,070km².

Since independence in 1971, Bangladesh has made significant development progress. The poverty rate has reduced from 60% in 1990 to 31.5% in 2010 and 24.8% in 2015. Economic growth has averaged 6.5% since 2010 and over 7% between 2010-2015.

In disaster risk reduction, Bangladesh has made significant strides in address loss of life as a result of cyclones. Compared to 1991 when more than 150,000 people perished, Cyclone Sidr in 2007 left a relatively smaller number of deaths (around 300). The country has also made progress in reducing the economic impact of disasters.

As a low-lying deltaic ecosystem, Bangladesh ranks sixth amongst the ten most climate vulnerable countries in the world (CRED, 2015). It is highly exposed to natural hazards such as cyclones, floods, droughts, storms, and other hazards. The GoB currently allocates 2.2% of its GDP on social safety net programmes, of which 40% is delivered through conditional cash transfers (CCT) targeting the poor and vulnerable population.

The aim of these social protection schemes is to address chronic food and营养 insecurity and personal asset loss, and to provide a safety net for those who are most at risk. The GoB has also been implementing various disaster risk reduction measures in recent years.

Climate data clearly indicates that surface temperature in Bangladesh has increased gradually since the 1950s (Choudhury et al., 2003; Islam et al., 2006). Early modelling indicated an increasing trend in surface temperature, with higher temperatures expected to continue into the future (Hulme and Jenkins, 2004; Hulme et al., 2011).

Although there has not been any significant change in average annual rainfall across the country, an initiation of a broad-scale increase in extreme precipitation events is evident from the early 1980s to mid-2000s (Choudhury et al., 2007), with an increase in mean rainfall episodes throughout the monsoon period. The frequency and intensity of heavy rainfall has increased significantly since the late 1990s (Hulme et al., 2006), which has been associated with increasing frequency of occurrences of high-intensity floods. Moreover, the reduction of rainfall during the other months (November to March) threatens to impact livelihoods (especially those based on agriculture and processing medium crops), especially in the north-western part of Bangladesh (Hulme et al., 1994; Hug et al., 1996).

Bolstering these hydro-meteorological developments, demographic factors also increase the susceptibility of common community-based livelihoods that affect livelihoods and opportunities for prosperity. These include high population densities, high poverty rates, income inequality, gender inequalities, regional disparity, fragile infrastructure, and poor access to services. The combination of these factors in a context where people feel land itself to depressing human development. In a bid to reduce household and community level vulnerability, the GoB has been implementing various social safety net programmes targeting the poor and vulnerable population.

A long history of resistance to natural hazards has shaped the culture and various practices of communities living in Bangladesh. However, climate change is expected to exacerbate these practices and may overwhelm existing capacities. Support is therefore essential to capacitate vulnerable populations to become resilient to the changing environment.


Over the past four decades, Bangladesh has been able to reduce mortality from disasters significantly. Yet exposure of its people and economy to climate induced losses continues to grow given the high frequency of extreme events in recent years (Hulme et al., 2011). The country is estimated to be among the most vulnerable countries by a conservative estimate, at least five major climate related disasters since 1998 have caused damage equivalent to approximately 5% of GDP. ## Gender Equality in Disasters and Climate Change

### 19. The impacts of climate change and disasters affect women and men differently. Climate change impacts can be seen as a threat multiplier for existing vulnerabilities, particularly those related to gender inequality, as well as societal inequality such as unequal power, information asymmetry and constraints in making abilities (Alam et al., 2013). Women are disproportionately affected by climate change because of their roles in the household. In rural Bangladesh, women are most severely affected because of their multiple roles as drinking water collectors; food producers; and caregivers for children and the elderly. The impacts of climate change on women’s health and changes in circumstances as a result of a climate change tip the balance against them succeeding. As access to basic assets such as land, education, and income opportunities are limited, women’s vulnerability to disasters is significantly increased. The lack of natural resources, caused by flooding, drought and erratic rainfall cause women to work longer hours for less pay than men. Women also have a higher risk of falling into poverty or out of poverty particularly when they have, for example, lost their land due to the impacts of flooding during cyclone events (Dhaka Tribune).

### Selection of Beneficiaries

#### Criteria for Women and Girls:

  - Health Education (including reproductive health)
  - Financial Literacy
  - Disaster Risk Reduction

1. **Training Curriculum**:
   - **Gender Equity Modules**: Incorporate gender equality into the curriculum.
   - **Life Skills Training**: Focus on sexual and reproductive health, leadership skills, and financial literacy.

   - **Government Training Institutes**: Provide training in collaboration with government institutions.

4. **Training Content**:
   - **Curricula Development**: Develop curricula tailored to adolescent girls' needs by women and adolescent girls themselves.

   - **Two-Phase Approach**:
     1. **Behavior Change**: Focus on changing attitudes, behaviors, and practices.

   - **Randomized Control Trials (RCT)**: Use RCTs to assess the impact of the training program in terms of climate resilience benefits for women and girls.

- **Enhanced Decision-Making**: Increase women's voices and decision-making power within their communities.

# G.1 Risk Assessment Summary

The project design has carefully considered all social, financial, operational and environmental risks. The environmental risk associated with the project is that the proposed rainwater harvesting tanks may not be adequately maintained. Financial risk may arise at the procurement and beneficiary selection stage, and the project design includes adequate safeguards to mitigate this risk. For example, the probability of coverage of beneficiaries due to currency exchange loss or domestic inflation is low.

- **Description:** Extraction of resources from sensitive and protected natural ecosystems.
- **Risk category:** Environmental.
- **Level of impact:** Low (20% of environmental risk).
- **Probability of occurring:** Low.
- **Mitigation Measure(s):** MoiKFC's financial support system to beneficiaries will consider site specific environmental sensitivity, and sources of funds for the project. ### Selected Risk Factor 2
- **Description:** Poor management of rainwater harvesting tanks.
- **Risk category:** Social and environmental.
- **Level of impact:** Low (<5% of social risk).
- **Probability of occurring:** Low.
- **Mitigation Measure(s):** There is an extremely low risk that rainwater harvesting tanks may not be adequately maintained. It is assumed that the only maintenance required will be to replace any damaged parts, and that all tanks will be sited in such a way as to keep free from leaves. The first flush diverters will ensure public safety along with the proposed sterilization and purification of harvested water. Importantly, all tanks will be tied to the ground, to reduce impacts on them during significant climate events.

- **Risk category:** Social and environmental.

   - Monitoring and evaluation roles and responsibilities and finalize the M&E plan;
