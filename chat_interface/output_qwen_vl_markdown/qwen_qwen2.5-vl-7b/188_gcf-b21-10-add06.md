## A.2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

The project's overarching objective is to reduce the impacts of climate change on the hydrological cycle in target watersheds through improved land use practices. This will lead to improved water recharge and productivity and contribute to the population’s and ecosystem’s increased resilience to climate change. Period: implementation of the project will be from 2018-2023.

Total project area is 146,500ha of which 22,500 will be directly restored. This area includes agroforestry with annual crops, silvopastoral systems, and agroforestry with permanent crops or forest plantations and protection areas. The expected results are: improved water recharge; increased productivity; and enhanced resilience to climate change.

GCF financial support (US$25M) is requested with additional funding from the Korean Cooperation Agency for Development (KOICA) (US$4.5M). Cash co-financing from the Government of Guatemala (GGF) (US$3MM has been committed by the Ministry of Environment and Natural Resources, MINEG), with an additional in-kind co-financing of US$8MM.

Project will achieve its objective addressing three result areas:
1. Integrated climate-smart watershed management adapted to the local context of the Highlands.
2. Community-led watershed management systems promoted through grant facilities. This result is oriented toward community-led implementation of climate actions in priority areas through funding from the grant, and has two main activities: i) Strengthening the capacity of GCF grantees for second level GCFs; ii) Awarding and implementation of small grants for grassroots organizations.
3. Climate-related information provided to farmers and target stakeholders. This result will improve multi-level and multi-sectoral climate change adaptation planning, policy development, and implementation through tools and programs. Main activities are: i) Strengthened meteorological and hydrological information systems through investment in equipment for data collection, modeling, forecasting, and archiving; and ii) Design and implement a participatory early warning system for agricultural practices and water management.

## C1. Strategic Context

### 1. The Impacts of Climate Change in Guatemala are twofold: In the short term, an increase in extreme events (e.g., hurricanes, droughts) is expected for the country. For example, a significant increase in temperature and precipitation is estimated for the region. While extreme events have negative impacts on population and infrastructure, they also have positive effects such as increased biodiversity of flora and fauna and impact ecosystems. Both types of impacts are detrimental to agricultural productivity.

### 2. Regarding short-term events, several studies argue that global warming will increase the incidence of natural hazards in Guatemala. For example, a significant increase in temperature is expected for the country, with an incidence of hurricanes, droughts or floods can be expected in the region (Lavel 2013). There is scientific evidence showing that the frequency of this event has increased from two events in the period 1950-1970 to seven events per year between 2010 and 2014 (UNDP et al. 2011).

### 3. Climate events are already affecting population in Guatemala. During the period 1990-2016, more severe extreme events took place, particularly in 2009 (drought, affecting 2.5 million people); 2011 (flooding, 1.6 million people; and 2013 (floods, 487,000 people). The most recent event was the eruption of Pacaya volcano in 2010.

### 4. High temperatures along with periods of increasing humidity (variables affected by climate change) create conditions for the reproduction of mosquitoes and other vectors that transmit diseases such as dengue fever. Authorities were on alert for a dengue epidemic in the region, disease transmitted by the Aedes aegypti mosquito that spreads through water accumulation.

### 5. Regarding mortality losses due to extreme events during the period 1990-2016, three years have had the highest number of deaths: 2005, 1998 and 1996. Due to Tropical Storm Agatha, along with the eruption of the Pacaya volcano in 2010 became the year with the greatest economic losses due to natural events.

### 6. During the period 2010-2012, the Government of Guatemala (GoG) allocated a total of Q.294 million (approximately US$800 million at current prices) for reconstruction activities, which represented up to 5% of the annual GDP. The GoG has stated that the economic impact of natural disasters in the country continues to rise; the cost of damages and the budget required for reconstruction can be expected to increase for the next decade (GoG 2013). This represents a significant burden on the economy, and the cumulative cost for 2010 is expected to be equivalent to 38-64% of the GDP (Berta et al. 2014).

### 7. Adaptation to climate change requires the adequate provision of ecosystem services, which in Guatemala are already under pressure due to population growth and urbanization. The cost per hectare is estimated at Q500 per year (UGSI et al. 2011). In gross terms, the country lost 103,867 hectares per year during the period 2001-2006 (UGSI et al., 2011).

11. Regarding public policies and/or legislation that allow the mainstreaming of flexibility mechanisms for climate change, the most consistent ones include those related to forest conservation and sustainable development.

12. For conservation, the main policy instrument is the Community System of Protected Areas (CSA) in terms of promoting sustainable use of natural resources available. Instruments included are provided through the new PROBOSQUE law (focused on commercial plantations, natural forests for production or protection, restoration and agroforestry systems). The CSA law has a duration of 30 years while PROBOSQUE has a duration of 50 years; differences between these mechanisms: i) PROBOSQUE has a finite duration of 30 years while PINPEF stems from a permanent agreement with the community; ii) CSA is applied to all types of forests, while PROBOSQUE only applies under PINPEF (it can be as small as 0.1 ha). Natural forest management is promoted through command-and-control mechanisms and/or market-based instruments such as payments for environmental services. Other recent planning tools, such as the RBAP Institutional Strategy, the National Forest Agenda and more than three recent policies on biodiversity conservation and climate change.

13. The low effectiveness of these policies is due to the lack of institutional capacity for their implementation, inefficiency in the application of environmental legislation, deficiencies in the allocation of public budgets for environmental conservation and climate change adaptation.

14. Regarding institutional and policy frameworks for water, there are several government organizations with competencies in water use and management. The municipal water companies (the municipalities can measure this impact by looking at the change in nephrometric turbidity units (NTU))12.

# F1. Economics and Financial Analysis

The project will restore the strategic ecosystems of the Guatemalan Highlands and the environmental services they provide, through sustainable land management and reforestation, as well as agricultural forestry and agroforestry practices. Additionally it will help establish more resilient livelihoods that will reduce pressures on ecosystem services.

The total benefits from field investments, as described in the project proposal, can be classified as follows:

* **Direct Benefits:** The direct benefits of the proposed project will generally through the implementation of agroforestry systems with permanent crops, agroforestry with annual crops, silvopastoral systems, and tree nurseries.
* **Protection and restoration of ecosystem services from the restoration of targeted areas.

The actions described above as well as the protection and restoration of forests will generate a series of ecosystem services including regulation of water flows and erosion reduction, which will have a direct influence on water availability. These services also contribute to climate change mitigation by reducing carbon sequestration, soil formation and aesthetic and spiritual values, amongst others. In the present analysis the social benefits are generated by:

* **Agroforestry systems with coffee, and fruit systems producing peaches and apples**
* **Silvopastoral systems of sheep with scattered alder trees**

The following environmental services were assessed in the economic analysis: regulation of water flows, erosion reduction, and carbon sequestration. These services are provided by the above-described systems as well as by other forested areas within the project area.

The assessment of water regulation was based on comparative data on water supply (INAB-Marinera Tuna, 2014). The analysis is based on the assumption that the reference value for the first five years will be 50% in the fourth year (25% of the reference value in the fourth year, 50% in the fifth year, 75% in the eighth and 100% in the tenth year). The same assumption was applied to estimate the benefit of water regulation in agroforestry systems with permanent crops.

Erosion control was estimated only for agroforestry systems as current cropping practices cause agricultural systems to be more susceptible to erosion. Erosion control is provided by the retention of soil, and replenish lost nutrients with the use of fertilizers. Estimates of the erosion volumes that were used those developed in De Groodt (2012) for the Guatemalan Highlands. The estimates are based on the rotation of traditional corn crops with the losses of the agroforestry systems of maize and alder. Because the practices to be implemented in the green beans and pine trees as well as the maize and pine systems are the same, it was assumed that the erosion volumes would be similar for these two types of agroforestry systems.

The allocation of monetary values for the ecosystem service (erosion control) was made based on data generated for the Guatemalan Highlands. The estimated value per hectare is US$ 105.42, which corresponds to US$ 368 per hectare of eroded soil based on the loss of elements (phosphorus, potassium, calcium, magnesium, copper, zinc). This value was obtained by multiplying the monetary values for each element by their respective weight.

It should be noted that the estimates of the values of environmental services are quite conservative when compared with data generated for other regions, such as those in De Groodt (2012) that exceed several times the values used here.
