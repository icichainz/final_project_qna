Please provide a brief description of the proposed project/programme, including the objectives and primary measurable benefit (see investment criteria in section A). The detailed description can be elaborated in section C.

* **The project aims to reduce climate change vulnerability and greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions from four agricultural value chains: rice, rubber, oil palm, and cassava.** This will enhance competitiveness and household incomes in the project areas. The project will operate in four vulnerable provinces, which are located along the Mekong River corridor. These provinces have a high level of poverty and many farmers already have link production to markets, namely Kampang Cham and Thong Khum provinces along the Green Climate Fund (GCF) Mekong River Corridor Project, and Kampong Speu province along the Mekong River corridor (please refer to map in Annex B). The project aims to enhance agricultural resilience to climate change and build a sustainable rural economy.

* **The GCF resources, along with the Asian Development Bank (ADB) and the government funds, will be used to invest in:** (i) climate smart agriculture value chain infrastructure; (ii) capacity strengthening in climate friendly agriculture, with an emphasis on women’s empowerment; (iii) institutional capacity building for sustainable management of the value chain; by investing in critical infrastructure / and support intensification of production and commercialization of climate-smart agricultural practices (CSA); and (iv) creating a conducive policy and regulatory environment that addresses climate risks to promote long-term sustainability and enhance profitability for farmers and producers.

* **The primary measurable benefits, corresponding to the GCF’s set investment criteria, are as follows:**

    * First, the project will contribute to poverty reduction in Cambodia through promotion of renewable energy and introduction of biocharges, while increasing household incomes from 390,000 USD per year (in 2015) to 480,000 USD per year by 2024. This is achieved through the deployment of climate smart agriculture (CSA) practices such as reduced tillage, laser land leveling, water and nutrient management, and biochar application.
    * Second, the project will contribute to a transformative impact through: (i) introduction of policies and standards for CSA; (ii) institutional capacity to support climate-resilient agriculture and maintain critical proof infrastructure; (iii) the deployment of CSA practices in the four agricultural value chains; and (iv) changes in behaviour and/or changes in behaviour of stakeholders regarding potential benefits of private and public partnerships (PPPs).
    * Third, the project will create economic, social and environmental benefits such as a 10% increase in yields, improved livelihoods for farmers (by >10%), increased water savings (>20%) of irrigation systems, and an increase in income from agricultural production by 35%. The project will also reduce GHG emissions by up to 40%, with a 10% reduction in post-harvest losses; investing in climate-proof infrastructure to provide more sustainable access to markets for farmers and producers. This will lead to increased productivity, improved livelihoods, and benefiting productivity while reducing GHG emissions from fuel wood and avoiding methane release to the atmosphere through the use of biochar. The project will also increase the resilience of smallholder farmers to climate change and CSA practices along the whole value chain.

This project is targeted at vulnerable populations, including those residing in flood-prone or drought-prone areas, women (at least 150,000) and rural communities. The project targets the agricultural sector, which has sufficient potential for adaptation and mitigation to climate change. The project is fully aligned with the country’s national priorities in climate change adaptation and mitigation and will lead to significant economic benefits. The project is economically viable with economic internal rates of return (EIRRs) of at least 14% for various subprojects.

### Cambodia's Agricultural Sector Development Strategy (2014 - 2030)

#### Background and Context

Cambodia's economy grew consistently during the past 15 years due to improved macroeconomic stability and public financial management. However, the country remains highly vulnerable to climate change impacts on agriculture, which accounts for approximately 85% of GDP and employs about 60% of the population. The agricultural sector contributes significantly to national economic growth, with a value addition of $12 billion in 2013.

#### Challenges

Despite these achievements, Cambodia faces several challenges:
- **Climate Change Impacts**: Climate change is affecting crop yields and quality, leading to lower harvests.
- **Low Productivity**: Agricultural productivity remains low due to limited access to modern inputs such as seeds, fertilizers, and pesticides.
- **Limited Access to Markets**: Farmers often lack access to markets for their produce, especially in rural areas.

#### Opportunities

Recognizing these challenges, the government's agriculture sector strategic development plan (2014 - 2030) aims to address them through a series of interventions:
1. **Agricultural Sector Transformation and Diversification**: This includes expansion of extension services, improvement of seed quality, and adoption of climate-smart agricultural practices.
2. **Market Access and Value Chain Development**: Strengthening the private sector's role in value chain development to improve product quality standards (e.g., sustainable rice platforms), enhancing access to markets for smallholder farmers, and improving post-harvest handling.

#### Government Priorities

The government has prioritized several key areas:
1. **Agricultural Sector Transformation**: Improving agricultural productivity through the adoption of climate-smart agriculture practices.
2. **Diversification and Commercialization**: Expanding crop production to meet domestic demand and increase exports, with a focus on high-value crops like cassava and rubber.
3. **Market Access and Value Chain Development**: Strengthening the private sector's role in value chain development to improve product quality standards (e.g., sustainable rice platforms), enhancing access to markets for smallholder farmers, and improving post-harvest handling.

#### Conclusion

The government aims to transform Cambodia into a competitive agricultural economy by 2030. This will require significant investments in transport infrastructure and trade facilitation to ensure that the country can compete on global markets. Investing in climate smart and sustainable infrastructure is imperative for Cambodia's long-term competitiveness, as it helps reduce post-harvest losses substantially and supports farmers better cope with climate change while also reducing GHG emissions.

## C2. Project / Programme Objective against Baseline

This project is part of an overall program designed to reduce climate change vulnerability and GHG emissions of Cambodia’s agricultural and agribusinesses sectors, which are among the most vulnerable in Southeast Asia and low-income countries (Cambodia, Laos PDR and Myanmar) of the Greater Mekong Subregion (GMS). Each project will work in areas connected by the GMS transport corridor, and is designed to achieve the objective of enhanced climate resilience while also contributing towards achieving key objectives of the government’s agriculture sector strategic development plan in terms of climate smart production capacity, infrastructure, and policy, with a view to leading to the professionalization of agricultural practices.

### Baseline scenario

In the baseline scenario, Cambodia faces increasing climate change vulnerability and its effects on increases in production, increasing food insecurity, and losses from the agriculture sector. Low capacity and insufficient access to technologies, compounded by an inadequate policy environment, maintain producers in a situation of high vulnerability to climate risks and leads to a probable increase in poverty.

### Climate vulnerability

Given that 80% of the population in Cambodia derives its livelihood from agriculture, the degree of exposure to climate extremes and long-term climate change is high. In recent years, Cambodia experienced severe losses in agricultural production due to climate-related disasters. The most significant were floods (2014) which impacted 18 of the 24 provinces of Cambodia and destroyed crops and communal infrastructure, including national provincial roads, bridges, and schools. The total economic loss was estimated at USD $35 million with an additional USD $6 million in damages and losses. Droughts are also recurring, with a trend towards accentuation of the severity of water shortages. In 2014, Cambodia experienced its worst drought since independence (1975), which was mainly due to flooding (about 62%) and drought (38%). 2015-2016 saw the country suffering from a 1 in 35 year drought, with disastrous consequences for agriculture. The agricultural sector is also vulnerable because it is highly dependent on rainfall, coupled with natural disasters and a lack of access to water during dry periods, contributing to temporary food shortages. A major challenge facing Cambodia’s agricultural sector is its low adaptive capacity, which is further exacerbated by additional seven as moderately insecure. Adaptive capacity among rural populations and national institutions remains low due to limited resources, poor infrastructure, and weak institutional capacities in the face of climate change-related natural calamities and emergencies. It has been estimated that the negative impacts of climate change led to a 10% loss in the GGR of agriculture.

There are several institutional, technical, financial, informational and policy-related barriers to address climate change vulnerability. However, efforts to address climate vulnerability have been stepped up with the launch of the National Adaptation Plan (NAP) process. The NAP is an important mechanism for countries to identify their needs and develop the National Adaptation Plan (NAP). The Government of Cambodia is implementing a number of projects designed specifically to address climate change, including the GEF-funded project on Climate Change in Cambodia, the Cambodia Climate Change Alliance (CCCA) with EU funding, whose objectives are to strengthen governance of climate change and enhance capacity for adaptation. The CCCA has been instrumental in supporting the GCF project to strengthen climate information and early warning systems (UNDP) and Pilot Program for Climate Resilience (PFCR). The Government of Cambodia has also identified several priority actions to reduce vulnerability in its recent Intended Nationally Determined Contribution (INDC).

The RGC is also working with the Makong River Commission (MRC) to address transboundary climate change issues. ### Activity 1.1: Rehabilitating water management infrastructure to climate resilient conditions

(i) enhanced storage capacity;

## 0.1 Value Added for GCF Involvement

**Green Climate Fund (GCF) is one of the most vulnerable regions in Southeast Asia**, affected by floods and droughts on a seasonal basis, which reduce agricultural capacity, poor infrastructure, and limited resources underpin the region's vulnerability to climate variability and change. Floods and droughts have been recognized by the government as some of the main drivers of poverty and food insecurity in the region. The frequency and intensity of extreme weather events associated with climate change indicate more severe variability, higher levels of risk and exposure, and potentially significant losses in agricultural productivity and livelihoods.

The GCF will make a real contribution to move the traditional agriculture development efforts towards a more climate-resilient and sustainable pathway, with a comprehensive package of support to build in resilience across all smart features in the agriculture value chain development. With some of the project outputs bearing considerable potential for scaling up, the project will also provide an opportunity to demonstrate how GCF can be used as a platform that would only consider current weather conditions, leading to a missed opportunity for increased productivity and resilience, and climate change adaptation. The project will ensure that the benefits from the investments in agriculture and climate change climate would remain wide, and the targeted regions, rather than benefiting from a growth opportunity, would become more vulnerable.

To make this necessary shift towards a more sustainable pathway of agricultural development among project beneficiaries, a number of additional activities are required that can only be financed through GCF support. The value added by GCF support includes the following:

- Extending the performance and lifespan of water, transport, post-harvest and agro-processing infrastructure investments in the agriculture sector to ensure more sustainable use of resources. A combination of climate-proofed infrastructure investments will be a stronger agro-economic activity and improved livelihoods throughout the year.

### Safeguards monitoring - Involuntary Resettlement Category B
The project is classified as category B for involuntary resettlement and/or involuntary land acquisition.
