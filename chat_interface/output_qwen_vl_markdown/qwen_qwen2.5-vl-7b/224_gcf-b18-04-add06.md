### A2 Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

1. Due to a long period of neglect and under-investment during and after the Bosnian war (1992-1995), public infrastructure, in particular buildings, in Bosnia and Herzegovina (BiH) is now in a dire state and in urgent need of upgrading and renovation. The project proposal for the BiH Public Buildings Renovation Project (PBRP) explicitly recognizes the potential of public sector buildings for GHG emission reduction and emphasizes that to increase the amount of funding available and develop a sustainable system for public building renovation, international financial support is required.
2. The project seeks a total of US$ 1346 million of GCF grant resources to finance identified needs for investment in new construction and renovation of public buildings in BiH. The project will leverage additional sources of funds such as the Environmental Funds, entity and municipal budgets, and international organizations (UNDP, GEF, World Bank, etc.). The project is structured into four components, as follows:
   - **Component 1:** Provides technical assistance (TA) to public and private sector stakeholders at municipal, cantonal, entity and national level in BiH to help address non-financial barriers, and to mainstream climate change considerations. This will be achieved by implementing the National Investment Framework for Low-Carbon Public Buildings.
   - **Component 2:** Provides financial support under the National Investment Framework for Low-Carbon Public Buildings, including the required investment support to improve risk-return profiles and bring forward prospective low-carbon building projects to financial close.

3. Overall, the project is expected to reduce CO2 equivalent greenhouse gas emissions of 2.02 million (tCO2e) over the lifetime of the investments enabled at a cost to the GCF of US$ 9/CO2e. Additionally, significant indirect emission reductions can be expected: -71.61 million tonnes of CO2 reduction due to the project enabling market transformation effects in the building sector and related energy efficiency measures. The project will directly benefit approximately 305,000 people – occupants and users of public buildings (4% of the total population), including 80,000 women, and will lead to creation of over 5360 full-time equivalent FTE jobs.

## C1. Strategic Context

### 17. Bosnia and Herzegovina (BiH)’s administrative and political structure is extremely complex. It includes two largely autonomous entities: the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina (FBiH), and Republica Srpska (RS). It also includes a self-governing district, Brcko, under the direct authority of the central state government. The central state-level BiH government was granted limited responsibilities under the Dayton Peace Accords in 1995, which established a multi-ethnic presidency with the Bosnian Serb leader as the country’s de facto prime minister. The entities (FBiH and RS), the ten cantons within FBiH, also have their own governments.

### 18. Due to a long period of neglect and under-investment during and after the Bosnian war (1992-1995), public infrastructure, in particular buildings, is in now a dire state and in urgent need of upgrade and modernization. Over 70% of BiH’s public buildings were designed and built over 30 years ago with no consideration for their energy performance, let alone carbon footprint (Figure 3).

### Public buildings have been identified as the sector with the largest potential for cost-effective energy saving in BiH (20-60%). Detailed energy audits (see Annex C) conducted in public facilities by UNDP confirm that average energy use in a building is 1.5 times higher than the national average, and that there are significant opportunities to reduce energy consumption and after retrofitting. In addition to energy efficiency, significant potential for GHG emissions reduction lies in fuel switch measures (e.g., from coal to natural gas or district heating systems), as well as in the use of renewable energy sources – biomass (biomass/biofuels), solar and other sources – combined with investments in energy efficiency, thereby reducing both energy consumption and CO2 emissions. The total cost-effective energy savings potential for public buildings is currently amounting to approximately 10% of BiH’s annual governmental budget. In total, the cost-effective energy savings potential for all sectors (public buildings, residential buildings, industry, transport) amounts to an estimated 35-40 million €/year in GHG emissions reduction over the lifetime cycle for both energy efficiency (EE) and renewable energy (RE).

## 20. In its Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) under the Paris Agreement, BiH explicitly recognizes the potential of public sector buildings to reduce energy consumption and greenhouse gas emissions. The NDC’s unconditional mitigation scenario foresees implementation of minimal energy performance requirements related to increased energy efficiency (EE) within this sector, which are primarily expected to be achieved through renovation and modernization of existing buildings. In addition, BiH has developed approaches and plans for implementation of EE measures in the building sector, in particular related to expected EE potential in public buildings. The Government is committed to developing a sustainable system for public building renovation, international financial support is required. Provided that Bosnia and Herzegovina (BiH) commits to reduce energy consumption by 30% within its public buildings by 2030, which include, inter alia, "systemic energy rehabilitation of existing buildings [focus on public sector]", BiH commits to reduce emissions from the building sector by 15%.

## 21. BH has also signed the International Energy Charter (IEC) in 2016 and the Energy Community Treaty (2009), indicating the Government's recognition of the need to improve energy efficiency in order to ensure sustainable, low-carbon economic growth. In line with the provisions of the Energy Community Treaty, BiH has developed the draft National Energy Efficiency Action Plan (NEEAP) – expected to be adopted imminently and which is currently under public consultation. The NEEAP sets out a series of measures that are expected to make the single greatest contribution to achieving this target, with an annual reduction in energy consumption of 1,900 GWh.

## 22. The new Law on Spatial Planning and Construction in Republic of Srpska (TBO Official Gazette no 46/13) provides the legal framework for the corresponding secondary legislation, regulations and guidelines including energy auditing regulations, building certification systems and equipment standards defining the maximum energy consumption in buildings and their components. This law is currently under public consultation. The Law on Energy Efficiency of FBiH (under consideration by the Parliament) and its RIS adopted in 2013 recognize the importance of EE measures for public buildings. They also provide a framework for implementation of such measures, including such as quantitative decision making for EE investments, monitoring, verification and reporting with support of information systems and databases, energy performance contracting, energy management and strategic EE documents, regulation of energy services with respect to EE and financial incentives.

### C2. Project / Programme Objective against Baseline

#### 23. The Climate Change Adaptation and Low Emission Development Strategy of BH features four priority sectors for climate change mitigation, of which energy efficiency in buildings is highlighted as having the strongest potential for emission reduction and is presented as a key priority at national level. The Strategy clearly indicates that fuel switch measures in buildings are not considered feasible.

#### 24. Finally, the Second National Communication to the UNFCCC (2013) also emphasizes the potential for considerable GHG emission reductions (up to 80%) from improving the thermal performance of building envelopes (thermal insulation of roofs, external walls and windows). The Strategy clearly indicates that fuel switch measures in buildings are not considered feasible (coal to biomass) in buildings. More detailed analysis of building sector’s GHG emissions and mitigation potential has been presented in the Energy Efficiency Action Plan for Buildings (EEAP), which is currently under preparation.

#### C2. Project / Programme Objective against Baseline

The reduction of GHG emissions in BH's public sector will cost a lot as it will require significant upfront investment on energy efficiency measures, such as thermal insulation and replacement of boilers in BH’s public buildings sector that embark upon a low-carbon pathway. These investments are very slow to materialize under baseline conditions due to a number of institutional and financial constraints, as described below.

#### Fragmented structures and weak capacities

The cost of energy efficiency measures for use given building is often a significant share of its total costs which needs to reflect the specifics of a particular building. Such an approach represents significant upfront transaction costs.

### Section 40: Current Financing Paradigm

In all, the current financing paradigm for investment in low-carbon retrofits of public buildings in Bhutan can be summarized as follows:

- The existence of seemingly numerous, but cumulatively insignificant, grant-based funding sources/projects from international donors.
- The lack of a coordinated and integrated approach to public building retrofits that leads to inefficient and sub-optimal allocation of public funds.
- The absence of market readiness and interest in market-based finance, including the lack of a developed market for ESCO business models and energy performance contracts.

### UNDP's Lessons Learned

1. UNDP’s own experience with promoting and implementing low-carbon projects in the public sector offers valuable lessons for addressing the structural imbalances.

## The Situation in BiH

The size and scale of energy service providers in BiH are both limited, with about 10 energy audit firms and a few companies providing services such as boiler maintenance and continuous data. There is currently a growing interest from ESOs from neighboring Croatia, Slovenia, and other EU countries, where the market segment is already quite advanced.

While the developed market for energy service providers represents an important challenge for scaling-up energy efficiency projects in BiH, it also offers opportunities to attract investors interested in the demand for energy efficiency services and goods.

## The Situation with RE Adoption

The situation with RE adoption is slightly different: fuel switch projects in public buildings (hospitals, prisons etc.) and commercial buildings such as hotels are gaining momentum, but only for a certain category of fuel switch, i.e. from heating oil, LPG or natural gas to biomass, especially pellets. These projects are attractive for investors. Private companies (acting as boiler suppliers) usually have sister company(ies) dealing with pellet production and/or heat supply operation.

Heat supply companies usually have sister company(ies) dealing with pellet production and/or heat supply operation. The contracting period in implemented projects is from 5-10 years. However, the downside of such projects is that essential EE measures are not always included (e.g., insulation).

## Other Renewable Energy Resources

There is enough biomass, as well as other renewable energy resources available to ensure full switching away from fossil fuels in BiH public buildings; however, their financial viability varies significantly and depends on the type of baseline fuel supply. Biomass projects can be financially viable if they replace coal with wood pellets for heating purposes, electricity and much cheaper domestic coal and firewood: therefore, only certain types of fuel switch projects are financially viable (e.g., LPG/coal to pellets). However, for most public buildings with coal-based heating systems, the economic rationale of fossil fuel switch is not apparent.
