### A.2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

In the past 50 years the Buenos Aires Province (PBA for its acronym in Spanish) has suffered rainfalls of great intensity mostly concentrated during the summer months. The Luján River Basin, which covers an area of approximately 14,287 km² and needs rainfall (a proportion larger than that of the Buenos Aires Province, which is 8.2%). During the last years the extent of flooding has increased resulting in waterlogging of soils in urban and rural areas as well as roads and trails. The general objective of this project is to develop an integrated flood risk management plan for the Luján River Basin by creating streamflow screens and mitigating the impact of floods in the Luján River Basin, thus causing more resilience vis-à-vis climate change.

Flooding in the Luján River Basin are mainly caused by (i) meteorological and climate factors resulting in increases in rainfall frequency and intensity; (ii) physical characteristics of the basin such as slope, contour, water-storage of the soil and water conveyance of the river; and (iii) anthropogenic factors such as changes in land use, growing urbanization and the naturalization of rivers.

To address the issue, the Buenos Aires Province has structured the Luján River Comprehensive Plan (LRCP) with an estimated cost of USD $315.3 MM to solve the aforementioned problems. The plan envisages the development of an integral governance model for flood risk management in the basin and the implementation of a series of actions such as: (i) early warning system (EWS), actions aiming at implementing land use management and the strengthening of institutions.

The scope of this project that defined fully complies with the priorities set out by Argentina under the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB) and the Global Environment Facility (GEF). The project will implement a series of structural and non-structural measures to increase the conveyance capacity of the river, construction of retention basins and substitution of inflatable dams for lock gates.

Funding of the project has been structured as follows: 29.13% PBA; 50.23% GCF and 18.65% GCF. This funding structure enables PBA to have the required cash flow and ensures that the project may be fully and successfully implemented within the timeframe agreed upon, ensuring the early implementation of structural and non-structural adaptation measures.

## C1. Strategic Context

The Republic of Argentina is made up of 23 Provinces and the autonomous city of Buenos Aires. Argentina has a total land area of approximately 2.76 million km², with an estimated population of around 40 million people. The City of Buenos Aires, which includes the city of Buenos Aires and 26 municipal jurisdictions, accounts for 5% of the national territory and concentrates 31% of the population.

Between 2008 and 2009, Argentina experienced a period in poverty reduction, but since 2008 this trend has slowed down. In 2014, 12.7% of the population was considered poor (living with less than USD $4 a day). One third of the population of Argentina lives with an income between USD $4 and USD $10 a day, and are at risk of falling into poverty.

In the last decade the main concern of the Federal Government was economic recovery and economic growth. However, this policy has been changed in 2015 when the new government took office. The National Development Plan (NDP), launched in March 2015, is introducing adjustments to eliminate economic distortions responsible for hindering economic growth and for increasing the competitiveness of the economy. These measures are expected to bring about results in the mid-term with the reduction of fiscal deficit and increases of debt capacity later.

### The Republic of Argentina and the Climate Change

In 1993 Argentina ratified the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) and, in 2001 the Swiss Protocol. In 2005, the Government created the Secretariat of Environment and Natural Resources with the Secretary of Environment and Sustainable Development under authority of the Head of the Cabinet of Ministers. By December 2014, the new government established a new Ministry for Environment and Sustainable Development duties. Its core purpose is to provide assistance to the President and the relevant Ministries, as well as "coordinating all public policies related to environmental protection and sustainable development". The new Ministry will develop policies and programmes adopted by the national government-. The New Minister relies on the Secretary for Environment and Sustainable Development to carry out his duties. The National Environment Council (NEC) is responsible for the adoption and coordination of policies and actions relating to climate change. Additionally, there is a government committee for climate change with the involvement of both Federal and Provincial governments.

In the Third National Communication to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (November 2015), Argentina presented its Intended Nationally Determined Contributions (INDC). The INDC sets out a series of Greenhouse Gas Emissions Reduction Goals (GHG) and put forward a set of planned mitigation measures and the need for adaptation measures to address problems created by the impacts of climate change. It also includes information on the country's capacity to implement its commitments.

Furthermore, prior to the Paris negotiations in 2015, Argentina submitted to the UNFCCC its Intended Nationally Determined Contributions (INDC), where it made clear that adaptation to climate change is a major concern, whereby it has implemented and will continue implementing policies and programmes. It also stated that for the implementation of technology transfer and institutional strengthening are required. Two out of the main seven adaptation measures considered in Argentina's INDC include early warning systems and implementation of structural and non-structural measures to face extreme events.

Additionally, the Inter-jurisdictional Cabinet on Climate Change was established on July 25 2016 for the purposes of articulating a common position between Federal and Provincial governments with regard to actions on mitigation and adaptation to climate change. The Cabinet is also responsible for coordinating national policies in this area as an effort to discuss a core comprehensive national strategy and contribute to capacity building in the field of prevention and handling of emergency and disaster situations related to extreme climate events, among others.

## Climatic Factors

Rainfall and temperature are the main variables influencing the region's climatic characteristics. In wet years, the increase in rainfall results in a disproportionate increase in the streamflow because water infiltration (absorption capacity of soil) is reduced. On the other hand, in dry years, the reduction in rainfall results in a proportionally reduced streamflow.

In short, rainfall variations in the zone result in a negative basin response. The less water brought to restrictions in water absorption capacity, the more severe and prolonged are floods, where the magnitude of events increases in floods.

Streamflow is also affected by the spatial distribution of rainfall. The larger the rainfall intensity and frequency over a zone of the river basin with low interception capacity and/or over a zone with good drainage capacity, the larger the magnitude of flow from this area.

### Physical Factors

During 1898-1994 conventional farming in the river basin was based on terrace farming. Thereafter, the region changed the modality of land preparation for direct seeding (cornfields, oilseeds). This type of land preparation for cultivation modifies the hydrological cycle and increases runoff. The increase in runoff is due to the decrease in interception capacity and the more use of land for agricultural purposes gives rise to soils with sparse vegetation and low level of interception resulting in an acceleration of runoff and a reduction of temporary retentions. As a result, the volume of water in the river becomes larger in shorter spans of time.

On the other hand, channelization in the river basin should rely on a technical justification and the subsequent approval from provincial or municipal authorities. Clandestine channelization generally lacks of technical planning, and adversely affects the drainage network of creeks, channels and rivers.

### Anthropogenic Factors

Urban growth brings about a decrease interception capacity and an increase of soil impermeabilisation, which increases the volume of runoff at urban areas and in the river courses. In addition, the increase is residential density calls for more infrastructures work, more roads, more public utilities etc., in other words, changes in land-use increase the levels of interception of runoff and also, increase vulnerability in the region.

### Objective of the Project

The objective of this project is to prevent flooding, control stream flows and mitigate the adverse effects from floods in fifteen municipalities of the Luhan River Basin. In order to achieve this objective it is required to solve problems in the basin derived from physical and anthropogenic factors and, implement other non-structural measures.

The Ministry of Infrastructure of the Buenos Aires Province through the Provincial Directorate of Water Infrastructure (DPHI) conducted the "Study of the Integrated Plan and Project for the Construction of Infrastructures for the Regulation and Sanitation of Luján River". This study provided the planning of structural and non-structural measures that, together with other actions, will contribute to the achievement of the objectives.

The study analyzed 8 structural measures from environmental, social, economical and financial point of views, by calculating such measures cost-effectiveness and choosing the necessary works for protecting and ensuring the quality of life in the river basin and its surroundings. The non-structural measures consist of building an institutional framework to: ensure the coordination and sustainability of flood risk management; benefit from technological developments in climate forecasting in order to improve prevention actions, early warning systems and awareness campaigns through civil society and to obtain prompt responses in case of disasters through groups of first responders.

Likewise, the objective of the Project is consistent with the purpose of Law N° 7.170 "COMMITTEE" Committee of the Luján River Basin Law, which was enacted on June 23rd, 2015. The COMILU is an autonomous entity with legal capacity to act under private and public law and whose objective is to improve the quality of life in the river basin and environmental conditions in the Luján River Basin.

### Components of the Project

The Project has 6 components. Components 1, 2, 3 and 4 seek to address problems derived from physical factors; component 5 seeks to address problems derived from anthropogenic factors adversely affecting the river basin and component 6 seeks to mitigate the issues relating to abrupt occurrence of floods.

#### Component 1: Engineering studies and others

- Performance of executive projects and preparation of tender documents for civil works, hydraulic structures and other related works.
- Preparation of tender documents for engineering works considered in Components 2, 3 and 4.

#### Component 2: Water conveyance works and enlargement of the river channels. These will improve the conveyance capacity of the Luján River by means of:

- **Santa María channel**: Communicating Luján River with Parana de las Palmas River, it will increase its cross-section over a distance of 10 km.
- **Enlargement of Luján River channel with trapezoidal sections** over a distance of 12 km, from provincial route (PR) U-6 to the railway bridge in the locality of Mercedes.
- **Enlargement of Luján River channel with trapezoidal sections** over a distance of 9.5 km, from PR-U until the railway bridge in the locality of Mercedes.

#### Component 3: Construction of retention basins and replacement of lock gates

- Creation of areas for temporary retention of water (ARTEN) in the following creeks: Los Leones, Mayan, Lizaguirre, Granados, San Martín, and Luján.
- Substitution of ineffective dams for locks in Luján and Mercedes.

#### Component 4: Works for bridges replacement and enlargement

Works for replacement and enlargement of bridges to improve current runoff conditions, removing river obstructions and detritus. These works will be in addition to the construction of retention basins complementary to Component 2 and needs to be implemented in order to fully obtain the benefits of enlarging the channels.

The work will be performed over 15 bridges across the river basin. Bridges will be enlarged or replaced depending on the need of enlargement and their structural conditions.

### Component: Environmental and land-use issues.
Establishment of the riverbank to define boundaries on private and public areas and identification of flooding zones that will be subject to administrative restrictions or easements. ### Component 6: Early Warning System.
The EWS will be integrated into the Provincial Early Warning System of the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation. The EWS is composed by four systems:
- Monitoring System: responsible for monitoring extreme events, which are transmitted to the EWS operator who is responsible for the measurement and transmission network; responsible for data processing and follow-up to extreme events.
- Warning System: responsible for issuing alerts in case of triggering any alert or warning, thus alerting the authorities and the community.
- Communication System: responsible for communications with the National Meteorological Service, the measurement and transmission network, and the EWS operator to transmit alerts to the authorities and the community.
- Evacuation System: EWS operator; efficacy to transmit alerts of extreme events to those persons responsible, skills of personnel in charge of evacuation, and availability of means for evacuation, including the possibility of car evacuation.

This is one of the non-structural measures necessary to develop an integrated project that responds to adaptation needs.

### Component 7: Strengthening, supervision and auditing.
This component includes strengthening all institutions dealing with the Project implementation, mainly COMLUI, through consulting services, installation of furniture and IT equipment, and personnel training. The supervision included in this component relates to internal supervision by COMLUI and the external audit for the Project. The decrease in runoff will have a negative impact on water resources. These phenomena adversely affect agriculture that accounts for a large percentage of exports from Argentina and also affects the hydroelectric generation capacity in the country.

- **Increased frequency of extreme climate events:** More frequent rainfalls and floods are expected, adversely affecting the Northeast and central zones of the country, including Buenos Aires.
  
- **Rise in sea level:** A rise in sea levels is expected to occur. In the Patagonian area, mainly in the Southern part of Patagonia, Buenos Aires may also be affected.

- **Retreat of glaciers:** The retreat of glaciers is expected and it will take 20 years for the disappearance of glaciers in Patagonia, thus adversely affecting consumption, agriculture and hydroelectric energy. In particular: it is estimated that hydroelectric energy generation in Provinces of Mendoza, Rio Negro and Neuquén will decrease by up to 50%. The same applies to the hydropower generation in the region of Comahue, South of Argentina, that currently generates almost one fourth of total hydroelectricity. It is expected that this region will be affected by a decrease in runoff and an increase in temperatures which will lead to a decrease in river flows.

- **Though poverty and human and social development have substantially improved after the 2002 crises, levels of poverty and inequality in Argentina are similar to those prevailing during 1990s. There are significant differences between regions with high poverty rates, for instance rural Northeast and Northwest regions, where the economy is mainly based on agriculture. These inequalities have significant impact with regard to the adaptation capacity of people.

### Current Vulnerability of the Luján River Basin

The Luján River Basin has a population of approximately 2.8%, accounting for 17.4% of the population of the Province, with 100 inhabitants per km² on average. The population density in the basin is 227 inhabitants per km²: approximately four times higher than the population density of the Buenos Aires Province (50.8 inhabitants per km²).

Regarding levels of poverty, 11.4% households in the basin have at least one indicator of basic unsatisfied needs, a larger proportion compared to the national average.

Across the river basin there is a mixed and varied land-use. Land use in some regions is agriculture, manufacture and urban, whereas in others such land-use is basically urban.

The Luján River Basin has a rich historic, archaeological and paleontological heritage, threatened by anthropogenic pressure, mainly urban pressure. This heritage is at risk.

The first step will confirm the Project Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP), described in this document as the "Community Relations Plan", that will include spaces and methodologies in which, and through which, each person that feels affected, or that may want to be part of some work in particular, will be able to approach before, during and after its construction period and lay out their opinions, desires, and observations, without regard to their status.

To begin any work, the Contractor will have to possess a document in which the held consultations are explained. In case it is not possible to include all of them, or if they are not included for some reason that is not the case (in case it were included, the reasons would have to be justified).

Alternatively, in a similar fashion, the contractor will have to look after implementing and maintaining active a process of consultation with the community, which will permit the affected population or any interested party to be informed on the progress of the work, the progress of the implementation of those affected measures that were established as part of the ESMP, and the existence of an effective, transparent, and traceable mechanism for making complaints and resolving conflicts, including proposing one so that it be employed, after the completion of each phase.

The Community Relations Plan, from the start of the work, until its Operation and Maintenance stage, is the only thing that must be elaborated for any work that comes to form part of the Project to be financed, since the rest of the plans, depending on their nature (whether which it is developed) and on the environmental sensitivity of the staging site among others, may be necessary or not.

c. Establishment of commitments Plan, with a preponderance of this last option.
d. Prevention of water and soil contamination
