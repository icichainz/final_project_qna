### A.2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

#### Climate Context:
The state of Odisha is highly vulnerable to climate change due to high monsoon variability and rising temperatures, which have led to increased frequency of extreme weather events such as floods and droughts. These events have displaced communities and pushed the vulnerable sections to margins. As per the climate projection for the region, the temperature would breach 3°C by end of the 21st century. Groundwater is highly vulnerable in the state due to high evaporation rates and low recharge capacity. The impacts of climate change and temperature is an important factor impacting the groundwater table through human stress and high evapotranspiration. The rising temperature may result in greater heat stress for people and ecosystems and it would enhance energy and water demand, induce drought and impact food security.

#### Water and Food Security Context:
Many districts in the state are highly vulnerable to climate change scenario as 13 districts (out of 30 districts) are solely rainfed insecure and 4 out of 5 major food basins lack of adequate irrigation facilities. The state is off track on water conservation and overall enhancement of water security and vulnerability for the people living in the farms. It is envisaged that annual 17 BCM replenishable ground water has to be converted through some adaptive mechanism so that under the projected climate scenario it does not get depleted. In the year 2013, out of a total population of 45 million in Odisha, around 38.5% families travel at least half a km to fetch drinking water in villages. The number of such families was 32.5% in 2001, which increased by 6% in 10 years.

To respond to these challenges the principal objective of this project is to enhance groundwater recharge in the command area through natural adoptions and use of solar pumps for micro irrigation to ensure water security and food security in the vulnerable areas of the state.


#### The following results are expected to be achieved by this project:
(i) Augmentation of ground water recharge to improve water table and water quality for health and well-being of about 5.2 million vulnerable communities (out of a total population of 45 million) through installation of Ground Water Recharge Shaft (GWRS) in 10,000 tanks.
(ii) Use of solar pumps for micro irrigation in 300 demonstration plots to ensure water security and enhance food production by adopting climate resilient crop planning strategy of the state. In 10,000 demonstration ponds to achieve energy saving of 3.27 million kWh per year and avoided CO₂ emission of 2614 tonnes/year as mitigation co-benefits under this adaptation project.

4. The variability in precipitation (especially the erratic nature of the south west monsoon) and rise in temperature has progressively increased over the last few decades. The figure below shows a projection based on 1986-2005 significant rise in winter temperatures. This shows that the rainfall pattern will change by the end of August. Reduced rainfall combined with high temperatures will significantly impact the water balance. The reduction in groundwater recharge and increase in evaporation will lead to a decline in groundwater levels. With increased sound pumping strategies, the water resources of sampled aquifer could be severely impacted under a warmer climate. This is likely to have an adverse effect on the availability of drinking water for the population as well as Upazilas in Northern Odisha. Temperature is an important factor impacting the groundwater table which gets further exacerbated by the erratic nature of rainfall and increased evaporation rates. The study conducted shows a warmer climate for the state. The study conducted in the state by Panda et al.et shows that in the pre-monsoon season, the water table falls to its lowest level and the groundwater decline. Thus it is very clear that ground water level is highly susceptible to impacts of climate change.

5. Susceptibility of ground water level due to climate change leads to higher probability of crop – water imbalance in the water scarce areas. The study conducted by Panda et al.et shows that under a warmer climate and reduced moisture, the crops will fail and livelihood of 1386 million marginalized and resource poor population will be severely affected. As this section of population is dependent upon agriculture and highly vulnerable to impacts of climate change, it is imperative for the government to take immediate action in order to mitigate these adverse effects at the time of need. Therefore conservation of ground water in the state is of paramount importance in the state in the context of climate change.

### Baseline: Food Security

#### COWB estimates
As per the estimate of Central Ground Water Board (COWB) estimates, the country would need 1150 billion cubic meter (BCM) of groundwater annually by 2030. India has, at present, annual potential of 1,123 BCM of 'usable' water with 690 BCM coming from surface water resources and remaining 433 BCM from ground water resources. Considering the fact that, inland monsoon is the prime source of recharge, the climate change induced variability has put significant risk on groundwater with drinking water security and food security.

#### Odisha
In Odisha, gross rainfall is estimated to be normal at 17.77 billion (BCM) through different sources. Rainfall contributes 71 % in gross groundwater recharge. With 1.09 BCM of runoff groundwater loss, net groundwater availability in Odisha is 16.89 BCM. Out of this, only 4.36 BCM groundwater is a threat with irrigation sector extracting the highest (79.5%). Odisha agriculture is largely rainfed and has been facing water stress for several years has been dependent on rain-fed crop-cultivation. Paddy in itself has relatively high water demand.

#### Keeping in view
Keeping in view of the reducing inflows from rivers, lakes and groundwater, it is proposed that the state will be moving towards a more water-stressed water-rich status to water stress-status. This is based on both the water use and climatic models that show close to 11°C rise in temperature in several parts of the state. In addition, spatially, the state is growing at a rate of 6-5 per annum and several industrial and urban areas are being developed which will further increase the demand for water.

#### Socio-political scenario
In the prevailing socio-political scenario, creation of additional surface-storage through new major / medium irrigation projects is not practically possible. Alternatively, ground water which is widely accessible and less impacted by pollution can be used to meet the increasing demands. Sub-surface conservation of water in aquifers is one of the best strategies to combat climate change impacts on water sector.

19. A recharge shaft as concrete adaptation measure has been proposed in the community ponds to ensure the aquifer recharges. Normally shallow water bodies like lakes or ponds serve as good rainwater harvesting structures. However, infiltration and percolation of rainwater is reduced significantly due to the presence of low permeable soil layer in between the tank bed and underlying aquifer system.

20. Further due to sedimentation process, layers of silt / clay get deposited on tank bed rendering the tank completely unsuitable for ground water recharge. By constructing recharge wells inside the tank, the contamination can be reduced significantly. The proposed adaptation measure is applicable only in areas with soil layers. It is important to note that the normal tank design in the country does not include this adaptation solution but would be transformational once the benefits are established.

## Ground Water Recharge Structure:

136. The project is designed to tap additional water accumulated in the community pondtank during the monsoon for the purpose of ground water recharging without impacting the live food and dependency of the people on the water of these tanks. This will be achieved by constructing a concrete structure in the tank catchment area for retention and dispersion during the recent past. The intense rainfall has resulted in frequent flooding across the rural areas and the existing community ponds are not able to retain this additional water. The construction of the recharge structure coupled with anthropogenic stress of unregulated ground water withdrawal is resulting into ground water depletion across the state. This project will help in retaining the excess monsoon rainwater in the agricultural tanks and enhanced the drudgery of rural woman required to fetch water during the dry season.

137. To enhance the resilience of the community against the weather induced vulnerability and ensure long term sustainability, the project proposes to construct a concrete structure for retention and dispersion of ground water recharge. This innovation is considered as a technological improvement and enhancement of the current scope of ground water recharge structures in the state. The proposed concrete structure will be constructed in the tank catchment area designed based on the successful experience of the pilot recharge structure constructed. The concrete adaptation enables to retain the excess monsoon rainwater in the tanks and also allows for dispersion of this water during monsoon. The structures are planned to be constructed in an existing partly / completely denoted community ponds. The proposed concrete structure is a circular tank (diameter 10 m, height 2m) with a capacity of 3500 cum. It is so designed that it will only be tapping water during the scenario of additional water accumulation in the tank over the pre-existing level and not to tap the existing water stored in the tanks. The concrete structure (diameter of 1.5 m diameter) is designed to maintained at a height of 1.0 m above the tank bed this. This will ensure that during the period when there is no additional water accumulation, the structure will not be tapping any water from the tank and will allow the tanks to retain all the monsoon rainwater in the tanks. The concrete structure will also allow for the release of the accumulated water in the tanks to the ground water recharge system. This project will ensure that the community can continue to use the tanks for their domestic needs, while allowing the excess water to be diverted into the ground water recharge system.

138. The agricultural yield is benefited due to the marked rise in the ground water level in the tank catchment area. In this regard, it has been estimated that the increase of 20 cm in the ground water level will result in an additional 50% yield from the recharge structure constructed in the renovated tank. The total volume of ground water recharge is estimated on the basis of total water recharge and the ground water recharge efficiency (GWR) of 41.7 meter per km² of land area. The project proposes to construct a recharge structure with an area of 200 square meters of ground water in a considering normal average annual rainfall of around 1500 mm). The aforementioned quantum of recharge will result in ground water table rise of 4.17 meter within a 500 meter radius of the tank and approximately 3.6 meter within a 1 km radius of the tank. This project is expected to benefit the entire community tank it expects to result in recharge of 13,040 hectare meter of ground water in a year and benefit a total population of around 2500 people.

### Solar Pump

The state is predominantly an agrarian economy with around 39.68% of geographical area (61.80 ha) under agricultural use. Out of the cultivated area of 61.80 ha only about 34% is under irrigated conditions. As per the study by the Department of Water Resources, Government of Odisha, it has been observed that the highest yields can be obtained from rain fed agriculture. - The project will contribute towards sustainable development of clean and renewable energy in agricultural sector.

### 165. Further, recharge to ground water depends on precipitation, surface water bodies like reservoirs, bank etc., favorable soil conditions, topography, land use/land cover and other factors. The project will monitor the recharging condition by studying all the said parameters in an integrated manner. In this background, it is proposed to set up an outcome based monitoring system as per the guidelines of the following under the aegis of OASDC, the nodal agency of State for implementation of RIS & G5 based activities:

- i. Monitoring of status of water bodies: Using multi-temporal satellite images the status of water spread, turbidity and other parameters will be monitored.
- ii. Irrigated field – Coverage of irrigated field, water inundation, water logging etc would be monitored using High resolution satellite imagery.
- iii. Crop Area/Vegetation Monitoring: Remote sensing data would be used for assessment of crop acreage, condition and vigor using Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI).

On pilot basis on selected areas moisture studies using Microwave Radar/data/SAR data and integrated land crop inventory are proposed to be taken up for outcome based monitoring and supporting the implementation of recharge structures.
