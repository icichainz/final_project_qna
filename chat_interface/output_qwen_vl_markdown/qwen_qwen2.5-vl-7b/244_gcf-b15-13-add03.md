### A.2: Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

Argentina has embarked on an ambitious transformation process, enhancing the efficiency and sustainability of its energy sector, which has struggled in the last few years as a result of poor policies. Its key role is to help restore energy security, competitiveness, local jobs, and mitigate its environmental impact. The Government of Argentina's decision in 2012 to focus on this sector from the outset has led to significant progress: renewable energy capacity increased by 64% in three years. One of the goals of Argentina’s new strategy for the sector lies on aggressively supporting and promoting development of renewable energies, including solar photovoltaic (PV) technologies.

The objective of the hereby proposed program (the "Program") is to help the Government of Argentina achieve the ambitious renewable energy targets set out above by providing a framework for the rapid deployment of large-scale projects. The Program will be implemented in two phases: the first phase will focus on the development and implementation of the new regulatory environment, while the second phase will focus on the financial structuring and execution of the projects.

The Program is expected to be approved within 120 days from the IDB signature of the AAA. Most projects are expected to close by Q1 2018, based on timeline requirements of the first RenovAr® tenders. The Program will reinforce the regulatory framework for the sector and enhance its capacity to develop and implement renewable energy projects in Argentina. It is expected that the demonstration effect IDB expects to help the development of other investors and lenders in subsequent tenders.

# B.3 Description of Financial Elements of the Project / Programme

Please provide a description of how the choice of financial instruments will overcome barriers and achieve project objectives, and leverage public and/or private finance.

The use of GCF investment resources will be aimed at helping structure and complete the long-term debt packages required for projects to be financially viable at competitive prices. For this, GCF resources will be made available in two forms:

1) Long-term project debt, matching the tenor that the IDBG will provide.
2) Refinancing guarantees, which would help commercial banks and commercial debt investors extend the tenor they can offer on those terms (which are currently restricted to 5-7 years). The guarantees would allow sponsors to accept financing that gives these lenders the possibility to exercise early repayment options. By qualifying for such a guarantee, sponsors could also attract other types of institutional investors, including pension funds and investors that would otherwise not be able to extend suitable terms and participate in the financing to deliver the volumes needed by the ambitious targets of the Renewo public tendering program.

Through these two GCF instruments, IDBG expects help leverage investment for adequately termed debt and timely close deals. This will allow sponsors to avoid the risk premium on projects that are never built that has been so high in previous tenders (just about 15% of the awarded capacity built by the previous renewable energy tenderer in 2016).

The proposed USD 130m from the GCF would help mobilize investment for a total of USD 650m, and support about 400 MW of new renewable energy power projects. This is equivalent to about 7% of the capacity that was tendered under Renewo. If achieved, the GCF Program will have helped provide a meaningful contribution to the success of this government-led initiative for the development of the building blocks. This would be the way for the significant replication and scale-up required in the mid- and long term to achieve Law 27/19's ambitious renewable generation goals.

### C.1 - Strategic Context

In spite of various efforts during the last decade to promote renewable development in Argentina, renewables penetration remains at 2% (or 1.9% if nuclear is considered) with only 0.5% of small hydropower (not considered) and 2.6% of installed capacity (or only 1% without small hydro) by the end of 2014. ### C.3.1 Project / Programme Description

Describe the main activities and the planned measures of the project/programme according to each of its components.

The objective of the program is to help Gokh achieve the ambitious renewable energy penetration goals established by Renewable Energy Law 27.191, by contributing to (i) the successful financial close of the first few projects to be built under the new regulatory regime, and (ii) the development of capacities of relevant public and private entities. For this purpose, the program will provide a combination of technical assistance and financial support.

#### Component 1 - Financing of Renewable Energy Power Projects

This component will be executed by IIC and will be focused on providing and mobilizing long-term debt financing for at least five projects that are expected to achieve financial close and help projects timely reach financial close. For this purpose IIC will consider projects awarded a PPA as part of the RenovAr tender (or those contracted between private parties) in accordance with Law 27.191's renewable penetration target, by eligibility and credit criteria agreed with the GCF for this Program.

#### Component 2 - Enhancing Policy, Regulation, Planning, and Project Financing capacities

This component will be executed by GSB and will be focused on providing targeted technical cooperation to both public and private entities in order to build their capacity and mobilize the financial resources needed to ensure successful implementation of RenovAr and other government renewable energy development programs aimed at delivering on the goals set by Law 27.191. Activities include:

- a. Strategic environmental and social studies and training activities at national level that will help enhance regulatory frameworks, policies, and strategies for the environment and social aspects for the development of renewable energy projects.
- b. Support to the development of a robust and transparent renewable energy dispatch.
- c. Capacity building for private contracting and financing of renewable energy projects, including development of project preparation and financial modeling tools.
- d. Capacity building activities to enhance gender equality in supported projects and companies.
- e. Conducting studies on market conditions and opportunities.
- a. Support for development of various renewable energy technologies (e.g., solar, biogas, marine, geothermal), including development of resources/potential studies, strategic plans, incentive schemes, among other.

### F.1 Economic and Financial Analysis

#### I. Introduction

As described in previous sections, the proposed GCF program is aimed at helping renewable energy awardee projects secure long-term debt financing, to allow them to timely reach financial close and advance to construction and operation phases. The main reason for this approach is that the cost of renewable energy technologies and volume of debt financing is key for the competitiveness of renewables, so projects aiming to prevail in the tender have built high debt leverage into their business models. However, the GCF program will not be able to provide sufficient volumes of debt financing to successfully carry projects forward. Per IDB’s market sounding, limitations in the available volumes of debt financing are expected to persist for at least 2015 and possibly beyond. Therefore, the investment needs associated to the 1,300 MW initially targeted by the first RenovAdi tender and any additional capacity that may be added through the second round of tenders will likely exceed the available volumes of debt financing in limited number of DFS. Appetite from the range of commercial banks consulted by IDB seems to be far much shorter than the investment needs, and the current market conditions do not seem conducive for a large number of projects to succeed in the tenders. The economic and financial analysis hereby assesses the effect on tariffs and project economics (costs, revenues, and costs of returns) of projects financed under these different tenor scenarios, to evaluate the impact that the proposed GCF’s financial support and IDB’s additional mobilization efforts to secure sufficient long-term project debt could have.

#### II. Methodology

The application in this economic analysis is the stakeholder approach, in which the ERR (Economic Rate of Return) is calculated on the basis of two sample1 projects FRR (Financial Rate of Return), flow, by performing adjustments for the differences between the financial and economic cash flows. The ERR is defined as the rate at which the net present value of a project equals zero. It is also known as the internal rate of return. The ERR reflects the project’s financial and economic costs and benefits flow’s Internal Rate of Return over the period of time that the project is expected to generate positive cash flows, i.e., the point in time when the project’s net present value (NPV) equals zero. The ERR is calculated as the discount rate at which the NPV of a project’s cash flows equal to the cost. While estimating some stakeholder benefits is readily possible based on sample projects’ financial projections and other relevant data, the limited immediacy availability especially of more indirect and generally hard-to-attribute effects (such as long-term environmental impacts) makes it impossible to estimate these effects with any degree of certainty on the quantitative analysis that can be performed. Nevertheless, this economic analysis intends to capture some of the medium-term financial impacts, with the resulting ERR believed to be a representative lower-bound measure for the project’s economic impact.

The ERR calculation simultaneously captures:

- Project costs to the sponsors and financiers
- Project revenues to the sponsors and financiers
- Differences between the price of energy paid to the projects and estimates of the energy sources (and their prices) displaced by these projects

## Mitigation Measure 1

Please describe how the identified risk will be mitigated or managed. Do the mitigation measures lower the probability of risk occurring? If so, to what level?

Until the reforms underway in Argentina's electricity sector (competitive processes to reduce cost of supply, adjustments to subsidy regime to enhance the cost recovery capacity of tariffs, etc.) are fully implemented, enhanced creditworthiness of the off-taker will be a key factor for project success. The public tender program will depend to some extent on the monetary transfers it continues to receive from GoA.

In order to mitigate this risk, GoA has established a Renewable Energy Trust Fund (FODER) in which it will deposit financial resources with a face value equivalent to the obligations of one year of PPA payments that the off-taker needs to pay to the Borrower. The FODER is structured as a public trust fund and is required by law to pay on behalf of the off-taker, effectively mitigating the risk of not occurring in delay in payment. In addition to the support of the FODER, the financial plan will include a Debt Service Reserve Account of no less than 6 months of Project debt service, which will be used to pay debt in the event of any cash short fall at the Borrower.

## Migration Measure(s)

The Projects to be financed by the program will be exposed to risks related to the potential underperformance of its different components, in particular the solar panels and inverters and the wind turbines which could lower the capacity of the Project to generate revenues. This risk will be mitigated through (i) the selection of commercially proven technology with a track record of reliability; (ii) the requirement for the supplier to provide a minimum plant availability guarantee; (iii) solar panel degradation guarantee and defects guarantee provided by manufacturers of all panels and wind turbines, if any; and (iv), appropriate, a major maintenance reserve accounts to perform scheduled maintenance or replacements.

In addition, this risk will be mitigated by contracting an experienced operation and maintenance company, which will ensure proper maintenance of all equipment in accordance with best industry practices.

### Migration Measure(s)

The PPA is denominated in US Dollars, but payments will be made by the off-taker in Argentine Pesos at the exchange rate of the invoice date. In the event that Argentina faces serious restrictions on hard currency, the Borrower may face difficulties converting Argentine Pesos into US Dollars and to transfer them abroad to service debt.

This risk is mitigated given that the FODER gives the right to the Borrower to terminate the PPA and sell the project to FODER upon occurrence of a convertibility and transferability event.
