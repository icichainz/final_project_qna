### A.2 Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 200 words)

The SA Government is seeking to achieve a 34% percent reduction in domestic Greenhouse Gas Emissions by 2020 and 50% by 2030, compared with the 1990 baseline level. This will require an investment of approximately R28 billion or US$2.2 billion over this period. The SA economy is estimated to lose R46 billion (US$3.7 million) in economic terms if it fails to meet its targets. In South Africa, the green economy suffers from chronic lack of access to finance. This is mostly due to lack of track record and collateral, thus resulting in high risk for financial institutions. The SA Government has therefore established a Green Climate Fund (GCF), which aims at mobilizing resources and biodiversity positions, the green economy sector is seen as strategic to solving the problem. Renewable energy is one such area where the GCF can play an important role by providing funding for small-scale renewable energy projects through a programme called “green and energy-saving industries”. The latter was established to offer opportunities for small-scale renewable energy projects in South Africa, which are often not able to access finance due to lack of track record and collateral. The GCF will provide the necessary funds to support this initiative.

This application is for the GCF to contribute 36 units (US$21.2 million) of the required funding of US$34.15 million (R481 million at the exchange rate of 13.5 per US$). The fund will be set up to finance MSMEs. The fund will deploy Early Credit Risk Mitigation (ECRM) tools, such as supply chain financing and invoice discounting, to provide working capital for MSMEs. Supply chain financing is a form of trade credit that allows the buyer to purchase goods or services on an open account basis. Supply chain credit approval decisions are made based on the credit worthiness of the buyer (not the MSMEs) and the overall strength of the supply chain.

* **We will target MSMEs which supply green services to off-takers for lower risk.**
* **We will focus on returning supply chains to optimize operational efficiency and cut off operation by stable amounts.**

Risk per transaction is minimum because individual transactional values are small, volumes are high and transactions are frequent.

SCF has already developed specific eligibility criteria for a pilot programme that is currently being used to extend credit; this criteria is in-line with the South Africa’s National Green fund. The current qualifying criteria is also aligned with the eligibility requirements of the GCF as well. The DBSA will ensure these criteria in enhanced for complete alignment with GCF.

### C.1. Strategic Context

Please describe relevant national, sub-national, regional, global, political, and/or economic factors that help to contextualize the proposed project within the broader regional and sector policies and strategies.

Climate change is already a measurable reality and along with other developing countries, South Africa is especially vulnerable to climate change impacts. The country has therefore developed an integrated National Climate Change Response (NCCR) which is risk-based in scope to identify and prioritize short- and medium-term adaptation needs that need to be addressed in sector plans. The process will also identify the adaptation responses that require coordination between sectors and departments and it will be reviewed every five years. For the implementation of these adaptation responses, South Africa has developed a National Climate Change Adaptation Strategy for settlements. Resilience to climate variability and climate change-related extreme weather events will be the basis for South African’s approach to mitigation.

South Africa's approach to mitigation, which is addressed by section 6 of the response policy, balances the country's contribution as a responsible global citizen to the international effort to curb global emissions with the economic and social opportunities that can arise from the transition to a lower carbon economy as well as with the requirement that the country successfully tackles the domestic challenges.

The key elements in the overall approach to mitigation are:

- Using a National GHG Emissions Trajectory Range, against which the collective outcome of all mitigation actions will be measured.
- Defining desired emission reduction outcomes for each significant sector and sub-sector of the economy based on an in-depth assessment of the mitigation potential, best available mitigation options, science, evidence and a full assessment of the costs.
- Adopting a carbon budget approach to provide for flexibility and least-cost mechanisms for companies in relevant sectors and/or sub-sectors; and where applicable, translating carbon budgets into company level desired emission reduction outcomes.
- Requiring companies and economic sectors or sub-sectors for which desired emission reduction outcomes have been established to prepare and submit mitigation plans that set out how they intend to achieve the desired emission reduction outcomes.
- Developing a range of policy instruments and other enabling measures that optimize the mitigation outcomes as well as job creation and other sustainable developmental benefits. This applies in all sectors.
- Of migration actions will be developed to achieve the defined desired emission reduction outcomes for each sector and sub-sector of the economy by ensuring that actions are specially tailored to the potential, best available solutions and other relevant requirements of each sector and sub-sector.

The deployment of a range of economic instruments to support the system of desired emission reduction outcomes, including carbon budgets, will be based on the principles of least-cost and cost-effectiveness. The instruments include: (i) emission reduction trading mechanisms for those relevant sectors, sub-sectors, companies or entities where a carbon budget approach is appropriate; (ii) national data collection to provide detailed, complete, accurate up-to-date emissions data at the level of a Group 1 company, as well as primary and Monitory data on the System in support of the implementation of the above.

## The South African Environmental Challenges in the Context of Economic Growth

The Europe 2030 Strategy recognises the central role of the transition towards a green, low carbon and resource efficient economy. This is because South Africa is heavily dependent on the exploitation of resources, the unsustainable pressure on the environment, and climate change, as well as social exclusion and inequalities pose long term economic growth and development challenges. The country’s environmental challenges are not unique in this regard; it is an exception in this regard. In fact, the country's current economic growth mode is heavily resource and energy-intensive, aggravating its already high levels of greenhouse gas emissions. South Africa is one of the top 20 countries with highest greenhouse gases emitter while only ranking 29th and 70th in terms of gross domestic product (GDP) and GDP per capita.

On the other hand South Africa is a unique position to exploit the emergence of green economic development in the world. The country’s renewable resources abundance (solar and wind predominantly) and biodiversity positions it to play a leading role in the global green economy. This paper argues that South Africa has an opportunity to use its natural assets with the potential to foster South African shift to low carbon and sustainable development, which would also contribute to employment creation.
