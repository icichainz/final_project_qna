A.2 Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

Please provide a brief description of the proposed programme, including the objectives and primary measurable benefits (see investment criteria in section E). The detailed description can be elaborated in section C.

The objective of the programme is to promote community-based adaptation and low-carbon development in micro-regions that are vulnerable to climate variability. This objective will be attained through: (i) awareness raising on the impact of climate change, and the generation of capacity to design and implement local adaptive and mitigation strategies; (ii) the development of resilient infrastructure, natural resource management, and carbon sequestration; (iii) the promotion of climate-resilient agriculture, and the transformation of the generation and access to energy from renewable sources. This programme will build on the best practices and lessons learned from the execution of rural development programmes executed by UCA/UNDP, which will contribute to transforming climate change into investment to increase the resilience of rural communities while reducing greenhouse gas emissions.

The Programme will be executed in a five-year period considering a direct target population of 5,750 families (that is to say, 28,750 beneficiaries).

In order to promote sustainable, inclusive and resilient development of the micro-regions, the Programme plans to develop Mitigation and Adaptation Plans that integrate adaptation and mitigation measures in the form of projects. These projects will be implemented through a participatory approach involving local communities, with the aim of creating climate-resilient infrastructure, sustainable natural resource management, low-carbon and climate-resilient agriculture, and the transformation of the generation and access to energy from renewable sources.

The Programme will work on 5 vulnerable micro-regions to climate change:

- Yavi-Micro-region (Luján Province)
- West of Formosa Micro-region (Formosa Province)
- Chaco Province
- Los Llanos Micro-region (La Rioja Province)
- Valles de Punilla Micro-region (Salta Province)

Component 1 - Capacity building and community-based planning: The necessary capabilities will be developed for the planning of mitigation and adaptation strategies to climate change and for the developing of proposals to access the grant facility. Such component is essential to create awareness on climate change impacts, generate capacity at local and sub-national levels, and foster the development of comprehensive ambitious plans on climate change at a local level.

# B.1. Description of Financial Elements of the Project / Programme

How the choice of financial instruments will overcome barriers and achieve project objectives, and leverage public and/or private finance

The financial tools proposed in the Programme will allow to promote the use of technologies and practices that have a positive impact on adaptation and mitigation and which are currently not implemented due to lack of knowledge or resources.

In the same way, at present local institutions do not have the capacity to face threats arising from the intensification in climate variability without the help of external funds. This is why the grant facility windows will provide financial tools strategically designed to enhance adaptation and mitigation on various scales of beneficiaries.

One of the major innovations of the Programme is to reduce the obstacles to investment in climate change actions: the project will finance the acquisition, installation, and operation of climate change mitigation technologies, and in parallel to establishing revolving funds in order to ensure sustainability and overcome the barrier of the lack of access to credit.

## I. Window 2.1 – Systemic projects of public investments: Non-reimbursable grant of up to 80% of the investment for public works will allow overcoming the limitations of the micro-regions regarding financial constraints and deficiencies in respect to structure and sustainable management of natural resources that contribute to both adaptation and mitigation of GHG. In this window, the beneficiaries are the public institutions responsible for service.

The beneficiaries must co-finance at least 20% of the total cost of the project, and their contribution may be in money or kind. The amount of co-financing may be requested based on a cost-benefit analysis, as appropriate, according to the sub-project in question.

## II. Window 2.2 – Adaptation and mitigation projects for organizations and associations of smallholders: Small producers are particularly vulnerable because they have limited access to credit and financial services, and are extremely sensitive to climate change because their livelihoods depend on climate related activities such as agriculture, livestock production, fishing or forestry. They also lack the necessary skills and adaptive capacities, and they are unaware of certain tools and practices for adaptation and mitigation to climate change.

Non-reimbursable grants are intended for small rural producers (whether they are on-farm or associative investments) who have a limited access to credit and financial services. The beneficiaries must co-finance at least 10% of the total cost of the project, and their contribution may be performed in money or kind.

Self-managed Revolving Funds (SMRF) associated to the adaptation and mitigation projects financed by the previously mentioned windows will contribute to the sustainability of these investments. These funds are organized by an organization of rural producers with a degree of institutional maturity, previously evaluated and trained, with the objective of financing their own projects in order to improve their adaptive capacities.

The use of successful revolving funds will contribute to the sustainability of the projects, helps the organization to strengthen its organizational capacities and therefore empowers the community.

Funds are UCAR to be used for investment. The funds from self-managed revolving funds will be transferred as non-reimbursable grants. This means that the funds will be allocated to the organizations for the settlement of their own projects in order to improve their adaptive capacities, and/or to finance revolving funds. On the other hand, funds from the organizations to the borrowers will be transferred as loans at low interest (in money or in kind). The members of the organization, the borrowers, will return the sum received according to previously established

# C.1. Strategic Context

## Argentina’s socio-economic context: Argentina is a federal country comprising 23 provinces and the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires, covering a total of 2,780,469 km² with an estimated total of 43,590,368 inhabitants for year 2016 according to INDEC, of which 23,746,470 are men and 22,223,898 are women.

### Regional Development: Development in Argentina is measured by the HDI with a value of 0.836, life expectancy at birth is 78.1 years, and the population inhabiting urban centers is 95%.

#### Rural population: Rural population reached a total of 3,599,764 in 2010. Over the last sixty years Argentina’s rural population has shown a decrease due to three factors: i) the technological progress of the agricultural activity requires less labor; ii) the migration from rural areas to urban centers; iii) the increase in the number of people with an increase in the scale of agricultural production and a reduction of the number of agricultural producers; 3) an increasing migration to urban centres in search of basic services, such as potable water, electricity, schools, housing, etc.

#### During 2010: During 2010, indigenous population or descendants of indigenous people added up to a total of 955,032 people who represent around 2.4% of the population.

### Great North of Argentina: The Great North of Argentina is composed by the North West (NEA) and North East (NEA) regions in Argentina. This territory has been given priority on account of i) the high levels of rural poverty, ii) a strong presence of small-scale producers and/or indigenous communities, iii) high vulnerability to climate change.

#### The Gross Domestic Product per capita: The Gross Domestic Product per capita in the Great North barely exceeds half of the national average. This region accounts for 20% of the country’s population and accounts with 54% of the country's households with Unsatisfied Basic Needs (UBN). Likewise, 57% of the country’s literates are in this region, whose provinces show levels ranging from 39.5% to 61%.

#### The region presents: The region presents the highest national level regarding unemployment (NOA: 14.9%; NEA: 12.8%; national average: 9%, 2011), as well as very significant rates of precarious employment and underemployment (NOA: 35.7%; NEA: 36.5%; national average: 25%). The region also presents the highest levels regarding road infrastructure and communications is deficient relative to the rest of the country.

#### The country has: The country has 251,000 smallholder family farmers, of which many are poor and live most in villages in the countryside. In 2010, 18% of the rural population and 32% of scattered rural households lived with Unsatisfied Basic Needs.

### Valles Calchaquíes Saltillenos Micro-region (Salta Province): Geographically, the "Valle Calchaquí" region is inside the great region of Argentine Northwest (NOA) and it is recognized for being a chaining of valleys and water courses. The region has an area of 102,547 km² and its main river is the Salado River, which is a tributary crossed by rivers Calchaquí and Santa María, in the provinces of Salta, Tucumán and Catamarca. In the Province of Salta, the region of Valles Calchaquíes Saltillenos or Province of Salta is composed of the following departments: Cafayate, San Carlos, Cachi, Cafay y LaPoma.

The MR features an arid climate, with concentrated rainfall monsoon-like (in summer), high thermal amplitude, strong sunlight, low atmospheric humidity and thus, evaporation intensity. The soils are not very much developed and their fertility is poor. The soil texture is sandy loam and the mineral composition of these soils varies very slightly. Furthermore, there are soils with substantial salt and sodium concentrations. These soils are very fragile and susceptible to erosion. This characteristic makes them a limiting factor for agricultural production in this region, being one of the main environmental issues of the region. Natural vegetation is shrubtype soil, low and with bushes, woody and scarce foliage, with minor species usually with aromatic and medicinal properties. In areas with higher water availability, there are some patches of grassland with a predominance of gramineous plants. The flora is very diminished and impoverished by the indiscriminate logging suffered in the area in order to get firewood and soil for agricultural purposes.

The MR population represents 2% of total province's population and it presents 49% of rural population within the MR (INDEC 2010 Census). Family Agriculture (FA) is a fundamental component in agri-food industry of Salta. In the region, FA accounts for 85% of total agricultural production, representing an average contribution value of the total farm production in Salta. Regarding Unsatisfied Basic Needs (NBI), the MR presents 19.4% of total households in the MR with NBI and an 8% of total provincial rural households with NBI. The main productive activity is livestock farming, which represents a significant part of the family income. Livestock production is a significant resource in the life strategy of the family, which provides with the prevailing portion of food for consumption and sale. In addition to this, it provides with other resources such as milk, wool, hides, etc. The great aridity in the area makes water resource the biggest limitation for agricultural production. The surface water is very scarce and stationary. There is a severe lack of water harvesting, pipes and distribution infrastructure, with low levels of irrigation. In addition to this, there are no significant investments on modernization or adoption of modern technology. Chemical fertilizers and plant protection products are rarely used. Farming is extensive type, focusing mainly in cattle breeding. The main crops grown by the farmers are wheat and corn. Most of these farming are carried away from homesteads, along the hills most of the year; and they constitute the greatest capital reserve for farming families.

### Climate Change in NOA and NEA Regions in Argentina: According to the information provided in the Third National Communication on Climate Change, the NOA and NEA regions are the regions in Argentina with greatest climate change-related impact. The study identifies the following changes in climate tendencies regarding the changes in climatic variables from 1980 to 2015:

- Increase in mean annual precipitation in all of Argentina, being the NEA region the region with most increase in annual precipitation, registering an increase of up to 200mm in some areas.
- In both NOA and NEA regions events of extreme precipitation are on the rise. Occurrence of heavier precipitation in fewer events.

- Especially in NOA, increase in the length of dry spells and little or no rain during winter, thus intensifying the aridity of the region.
- Increase in frequency and duration of floods in the NEA-region.
- Significant increase in the number and duration of heat waves.

Climate projections indicate that annual mean temperature will rise more substantially and faster than observed between 1960 and 2005. For the scenario of a 1 to 2°C increase is expected with relation the 1986–2005 period in the corona of fewer emissions (RCP 4.5) and a temperature increase of 3 to 6°C is expected by the end of the century considering the worst emission scenarios (RCP 8.5). For the scenario of a 1 to 2°C increase, it is expected in relation with the 1986–2005 period in the corona of fewer emissions (RCP 4.5) and a temperature increase of 3.5 to 7°C is expected by the end of the century considering the worst emission scenarios (RCP 8.5). Based on the above, this region will have the country's greatest projected increase.

In relation with temperature extremes in both regions for the scenario of fewer emissions: i) frost would practically disappear; ii) tropical nights would increase; iii) there will be an increase in the number of days with heat waves of 10°C or more and a significant increase in the duration of the NOA (this being the region with greatest projected increase in the duration of heat waves for the country).

According to the climate projections, in the near future the occurrence of extreme precipitation will intensified in the NEA-region. This will lead to an increase in the number of days with heavy rainfall and intense rain events in both regions, increasing between 25 mm to 150 mm in the NOA and between 25 to 100 mm in the NEA region.

These new projected climate conditions for the North region of Argentina will bring about impacts on the environment, economy and society. These impacts will be felt at different levels: i) changes in the availability of land and related desertification processes which will affect productivity and extinction of species; ii) changes in water resources and their distribution, leading to a decrease in agricultural production, as well as access to food and water production, as well as access to use of food. Impacts will also be seen through the increase of forest fires and mass weathering phenomena.

This scenario increases environmental vulnerability in the region which has been discussed previously as well as existing environmental fragility and institutional weaknesses, aggravate the capacity of the communities to respond and adapt to climate change impacts.

### Output 2.2: Adaptation and mitigation projects for organizations and associations of smallholders implemented

#### Activity 2.2.1: Awarding and implementation of grants for adaptation and mitigation projects for organizations and associations of smallholders

This activity is intended to finance comprehensive and innovative climate adaptation and mitigation projects for organizations and associations of smallholders in the micro-regions. These projects will finance climate related investments to increase the resilience of small-scale producers in the face of climate variability and extreme events that are expected to become more frequent and intense due to climate change.

Comprehensive projects are those addressing the climate issue with a systemic approach by aiming to solve more than one problem at the time instead of focusing on only one aspect. Such projects need to cover the social, environmental and economic dimensions of the problem in order to be sustainable over time. They can include restoration of ancestral forests, seeking to enhance resilience of small-scale producers in the face of climate change while preserving biodiversity.

**Beneficiary profile:** Small-scale family agriculture producers (A, B, C) who produce for their own consumption or with a surplus production for the market. They can be confirmed in organizations, groups, associations and cooperatives.


#### Type of project: These projects are very vulnerable to extreme climate events and require a Non-Residual Contribution in advance in order to improve their adaptation capacity to climate variability. ### Programme target population

The Programme is intended to generate a transformation across the entire micro-regions so as to generate a long-term impact towards a sustainable climate resilient development.

In this regard, the programme beneficiaries from the Programme will be the entire rural population of the micro-regions, including small-scale producers with high poverty levels and population from rural towns within the micro-region.

The main beneficiary group of the Programme encompasses groups of rural population with high poverty levels and vulnerability to climate change, including small-scale family producers, indigenous communities and rural women.

In general, these groups have a high sensitivity to climate variability and they have a low adaptive capacity due to the following factors: deficient assets, limited resources, isolation, limited access to services and markets, food insecurity, lack of income diversity, low levels of education and organization, lack of climate change awareness, and lack of technological capacities.

Additionally these groups lack of access to technology and financing that would allow them to become more

adapted to climate change.

The Programme is expected to benefit about 5,750 families (that is to say, 28,750 beneficiaries), distributed as follows:
- > 4,000 families (20,000 beneficiaries) living in rural areas who benefit from systemic public projects;
- > 1,750 families (8,750 beneficiaries) of small-scale family producers and rural workers.

In order to reach the target population, the calling and communications will be inclusive and promote the equal participation of men and women.

The Programme will identify and address barriers or obstacles faced by men and women, depending on their age and socio-economic condition, to access the Programme's financial windows and other benefits, and contribute to the empowerment of rural women in particular.

The diversity of current family structures will be considered, especially those where women are the head of households, households with single mothers or fathers with children or youngsters in their care. Even though the programme is not a credit programme, it is expected that women who have more control over resources and/or on ownership of the grant will be more participative in the project. That is why the Programme will aim to ensure that the decision-making process of ownership of the grants will be equal for both men and women.

D.1 Value Added for GCF Involvement

Please specify why the GCF involvement is critical for the project/programme, in consideration of other alternatives.

Climate change is gravely affecting rural areas and agriculture-based livelihoods. Building resilient livelihoods based on low carbon agro-productive activities will aid in protecting valuable ecosystems as well as provide sustainable jobs. In this regard, the Programme will contribute to the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) by supporting the development of the North Region of Argentina by promoting the incorporation of adaptation and mitigation measures in territorial planning, land use planning, and rural development plans. The Programme will also support the development of Mitigation and Adaptation Plans and in the selection and definition of projects to guarantee sustainability.

The Programme will address climate change impacts on the most vulnerable communities of Argentina, which are exposed to the most severe climate extreme events of the country. These rural communities are significantly sensitive to their environment and are highly dependent upon natural resources for their livelihoods. They have limited access to land and livestock; they have limited resources since the north of the country has the highest rural poverty. The impacts of climate change on these communities will be significant, as they lack the capacity to adapt or mitigate against them.

Regarding climate change impacts and solutions, the institutions are very weak; there are serious deficits in energy infrastructure and access to water. In addition, many of the communities have weak property rights and find it difficult to assert dominion over their territories and face climate-change impacts.

Additionally, local stakeholders as well as local institutions that support rural communities, have decided climate change is a priority issue for them. However, they are not aware of what the Programme will do or believe that the Programme will tackle throughout its implementation by generating capacities and raising awareness on these issues. Rural communities need to be involved in the decision-making process regarding adaptation action.

The Programme will allow generating capacities on the benefits associated to climate planning and investments.

The proposed Programme is built on the structure of UCAR's rural development programmes and projects and aims to incorporate new approaches that are innovative, cost-effective, and replicable. The Programme will be highly innovative and will give place to setting an example that can be replicated in other strategic plans. GCF's support will maximise the impact of existing national initiatives executed by the Ministry of Agroindustry, and will provide a platform for local-level climate action change.

The Programme will enable to generate capacities regarding climate finance at all levels: national government, provincial government, municipal government, and rural communities. It will also allow generating capacities for farmers (family agriculture smallholders with no access to credit). This will allow to replicate the successful experiences.

The Programme will address a current funding gap in the country that needs to be addressed to preserve our natural resources, promote a transformation in the development of the region and improve the livelihoods of vulnerable rural communities.

GCF's support is essential to transform micro regions by enhancing the climate resilience of the population in the area of intervention.

The low adaptive capacity of rural communities in Northern Argentina has been deeply described in this Chapter 1 and 2 of the Feasibility Study. Without this Programme, loss of food production derived from the lack of support for the strengthening of agricultural practices will be significant. This will result in a reduction of income and increase in poverty funding requested. Without Programme intervention, losses due to climate impacts are likely to keep on being dependent upon external assistance. The associated financial losses and increased costs of reaction associated with climate change indicate the need for immediate action.

The aim of the Programme is to subsequently up-scale adaptation measures and mitigation solutions to the rest of the region in order to ensure that all rural communities in Northern Argentina will be effectively respond to climate change impacts and show example of the success of having adopted these practices.

# D.2 Exit Strategy

Please explain how the project/programme sustainability will be ensured in the long run, after the project/programme is implemented, with support from the GCF and other sources, taking into consideration the long-term financial viability demonstrated in [E.3]. This should include a description of strategies for longer term maintenance of physical assets (if applicable).

Strengthening the sustainability is a goal sought from the project formulation process, but especially during the implementation.

The sustainability possibilities of the projects will depend on a framework that considers: (i) the structural constraints and the policy environment in which the projects are implemented; (ii) the capacity of the communities involved as subjects and their organizations to overcome identified constraints, and (iii) the opportunities that the public policy framework generates or promotes.

The proposal addresses aspects to ensure the sustainability of its actions in (ii) and (iii) by:

- **Strengthening of Local Ownership:** In line with the objectives of the GCF EDA modality, rural communities will be enabled through this Programme to directly access climate finance. The proposed approach of self-determined development is based on the premise that local communities are best placed to identify their needs, vulnerabilities and opportunities.

- **Empowerment of local actors:** Participation processes will ensure the effective ownership of projects and the sustainability of the results. The Climate Change Adaptation Plans (Component I) will be carried out with a participatory methodology for collective diagnosis and participatory project selection through which stakeholders from different sectors are involved in the process. This approach is consistent with the MAPs aims to empower the private sector to exercise social control over the works (speed of implementation, quality of execution, etc.) and to ensure that the results are sustainable in the long term.

- **Prevention of localism:** The programme will promote associative work in order to mitigate environmental, social and economic risks and to allow projects to prevail over time, through training for organizations and groups of producers that strengthen the entire community and non-refundable grants for organizations and groups of producers.

- **Generalization of specialized technical capacities:** Local and national technical capacities will be strengthened on climate change adaptation in order to ensure the sustainability of the results. This will allow the creation of new ones. Technical assistance is essential to develop local understanding of the relationship between climate change and development, to create greater local awareness of climate change and the appropriation of the response to climate change.

- **Permanent territorial presence of partners:** The Programme will work and strengthen capacities of national and local actors in order to ensure their permanence in the selected micro-region. This will contribute to sustain the actions promoted by the Programme.

## Monitoring and evaluation

UCAR has established procedures for the monitoring and evaluation of the different programmes. In this case, these procedures will be adjusted and up-to-date to its specific features. The monitoring and evaluation activities will include the following:

### Inception workshop

An initial workshop will be organized during the first month of the implementation of the Programme to be attended by the representatives of the regional Implementation Unit, the Steering Committee, responsible agencies, and UCAR. The workshop will establish the execution and implementation mechanisms, as well as the finalization of the necessary agreements between the different parties to ensure transparency and efficacy in carrying out the Programme. In the inception workshop, the project team will present the main objectives of the programme, the expected results, stakeholders and the indicators will also be analyzed and discussed with them to ensure appropriation of the M&E System. The participants will also be informed about the roles and responsibilities of each party involved in the Programme. Also, the Gender Action Plan will be presented with its indicators, to guarantee that all the parties involved have a clear understanding on how gender equality will be addressed during the implementation of the project.

It is expected that at the end of the project, each participant will be able to identify the MAE System requirements, the different indicators, the responsibility of each person in the process of reporting, collecting data, registering the information and monitoring the progress of the project.


### Models of agreements

Models of agreements between the relevant parties can be found in Annex I - Model Contracts. Specifically, during the inception workshops the agreements to be signed are the agreements between UCAR and the ad-hoc associations, as well as those with the public institutions which will be present in the micro-regions. In addition, an agreement has been described in Component 1. However, as the institutional framework of the micro-region, it will be assured that the ad-hoc associations will sign agreements with the relevant public institution (e.g., the Director of Agriculture or the Sub-Director of Agriculture) for the implementation of the project and to ensure coordination and cooperation; and thus these institutions will not manage or transfer funds: only expenses derived from their technical work will be covered by UCAR. The same applies in the case of the micro-regions.


### Registration of information, informal arrangements and monitoring support

The Programme will be submitted to an external and independent evaluation in the middle of its term of completion, or once half of the funds are allocated. The evaluation will also assess the effectiveness of the gender action plan implemented and potentiality to replace them. Its efficiency and performance will also be included along with transversal topics such as the impact on women, indigenous families and young people in the area. A special analysis will be based on the results achieved by the Programme in the micro-regions prioritized for funding, specifically those selected after the first year of the implementation of the Programme.

The Final Report will include the date of the results evaluation, which will be done with a household survey and will allow the assessment of the impact of the Programme on the mitigation capacity generated in the community-level and government officials along with the broader impact in micro-region sustainable development.
