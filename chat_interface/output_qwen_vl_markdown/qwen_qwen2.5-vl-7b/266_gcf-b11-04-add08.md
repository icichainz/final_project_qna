### A.2 Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

Please provide a brief description of the proposed project/programme, including the objectives and primary measurable results expected to be achieved within five years. The summary should be no more than 480 words.

1. **The project will improve access to safe water and sewerage services by building infrastructure to increase water production by 20% and wastewater treatment (WWT) by 200%, in the greater Suva area (GSA), and supporting environmental recovery of the Suva-Nuova wastewater system through improved water quality and sewerage. A capital development technical assistance was provided by the ADB to prepare the project.**

2. **The project features a strong focus on climate change adaptation and mitigation measures, critical for resilience and sustainability in the GSA. Climate-related risks that have been identified include those of the project particularly in adaptation to floods, droughts, and salinity intrusion due to sea level rise, as well as ecosystem consequences associated with increased water demand and wastewater treatment. The project will address these through use of energy efficient technologies and the increase of average coverage. Only water supply adaptation measures are included within this project.**

3. **Water Supply.** The project will design and construct a new water intake by the Rewa River, with a pumping station to provide 10 million liters per day (MLD) of potable water for Suva and surrounding areas, serving about 250,000 people daily; and will reduce non-revenue water (NRW) due to technical and non-technical losses. The project will improve resilience through appropriate sizing of the intake higher up the river catchment, away from the projected salinity wedge due to sea level rise. The new water supply system will be designed with a 20% increase in capacity over time as sources are still all reliant on surface water systems; the impacts on stream flows associated with future climate change is not yet fully understood and will need further investigation. The project will also include measures that allow flexibility for demand increases and/or changing supply source yields.

4. **Wastewater Treatment.** The project will improve the management, operation, maintenance, coverage and reliability of wastewater treatment (WWT) processes in the GSA through increasing WWTP capacity of the Kioniya plant. The project will design and build new sewerage infrastructure to connect 10% of households currently not connected to sewerage services to the Suva-Nuova sewerage network to service an additional 15% of households. This will improve overall effluent quality and minimize the need for costly bypasses, improving water quality in Laucala Bay downstream in receiving waters and reducing pollution levels affecting Laucala Bay. The project will also include measures that allow flexibility for backing up future demand within the Suva-Nuova wastewater system.

5. **Improvement of Service Delivery.** The project will improve sector management and sustainable service delivery capacity by supporting implementation of a water demand management program; strengthening the NRW reduction program; improving institutional capacity through training, technical assistance, and equipment; supporting the institutional strengthening of WAF; and supporting WAF to develop a decentralization strategy to reduce number of small decentralized systems.

6. **The proposed financing structure combines a maximum debt financing and a minimum grant financing, taking into account the country's debt burden, the project's revenue-generating capacity at completion, and the urgent need to value water as an economic good. The project will also include measures that allow flexibility for backing up future demand and environmental return to Fiji; the financial returns by GCF are limited. Grant funding by GCF will allow Fiji, a small island developing state (SIDS), to undertake adaptation measures without either reducing funding for other high priority development needs or increasing its risk of debt distress.

# C.1. Strategic Context

* Fiji has a population of approximately 868,000, of which 53% is urban. By 2030, it is estimated that two in three people will live in the country's urban centers.
* While poverty rates in Fiji are highest in rural areas, the rapid growth of peri-urban squatter settlements implies that the majority of poor people in Fiji now live in and around urban centers.
* The GSA, which consists of Suva City—the national capital—as well as the towns of Lami, Nasinu, and Nausori, is home to 50% of Fiji's population. The gross domestic product (GDP) of the GSA is expected to grow by 12.8%.
* Despite the economic importance of the country's cities and towns, urban infrastructure and services have not kept pace with rapid urbanization. This has resulted in rising costs as well as increasing pressure on the provision of these services by the Government. Irresolvably this is contributing to environmental degradation and increasing vulnerability to climate change impacts.
* The high cost of providing water supply and sanitation services in Suva City, Lami, Nasinu, and Nausori, coupled with the rapid growth of urban population, makes it essential for ongoing economic development and is a high priority area for investment. This is reflected in the Water Supply and Sewerage Master Plan 2013-2033 and the WAPF 2014 corporate plan and through consultation with Suva City Council.
* Fiji's development plan, entitled the Government of Fiji: Roadmap for Democracy and Sustainable Socio-Economic Development 2010–2014*, aligned to the Millennium Development Goals, has prioritized water and sanitation as a key sector in its national development strategy. The National Climate Change Strategy and Action Plan (NCCSAP) recognizes that climate change is an impact on efforts to achieving sustainable development.

# C.3. Project Description

The project will ensure that residents of some of Fiji’s most densely populated areas have improved access to safe water and a more environmentally friendly sewerage system. The project's impact is aligned with the government’s policy to improve delivery of water supply and sanitation services as articulated in the Roadmap for Decentralized Water Supply and Sanitation Services Development (2015-2030). The project will ensure access to sustainable water supply and sewerage services. The project has three main components: (i) increasing access to safe water supply through the construction of a new water treatment plant, Wastewater Management, and improving management and sustainable delivery of water and sewerage services.

## 17. Project Consideration of Climate Change

The project has considered climate change in the design of the project, particularly in adaptation to floods and droughts due to changes in rainfall patterns, salinity intrusion due to sea level rise, and mitigation through use of energy efficient technologies and renewable energy sources such as solar power for water treatment plants and methane gas emissions. The project will also contribute to protection of Lauca Bay and surrounding coral reefs by reducing erosion from the Wairama River.

## 18. Water Supply

The Wairama River is the only raw water source for the Wallis and Tamana water treatment plant, which supplies drinking water to Suva and Nausori. The river has a low flow during dry periods as it cannot meet peak demands. With NRW estimated at 46% by WAIF, WAF has no capacity to extend the network to person areas on the ground. This is compounded with the expected changes in rainfall patterns and increased salinity intrusion due to sea level rise. The situation is worsened during drought periods when the Wairama River flow is insufficient to operate the plants at their full production capacity and during periods of high rainfall, when the Waimana waters become too turbid for treatment. This results in a reduction in water supply reliability and increased costs.

## 19. Project will increase access to reliable and safe water supply in the OSQA sphere

The project will increase access to reliable and safe water supply in the OSQA sphere through: (i) increasing production by building a new WTP, which will be located on the River Wairama; (ii) constructing a new water treatment plant, pumping station and reservoir to serve the GSA water supply system; and (iii) reducing non-revenue water due to technical losses and non-technical losses by replacing meters, improving leak detection and repair, and increasing metering coverage. The project will also improve WTP reliability through Wastewater modeling.

## 20. New raw water intake at the River Wairama will result in a more resilient water supply system

The new raw water intake at the River Wairama will result in a more resilient water supply system by reducing the reliance on surface water based systems and increasing production capacity of the WTP. The current sources are still all reliant on surface water based systems, the River Wai is a much larger river system and any impact on stream flows associated with future climate change impacts are not likely to impact extractions for urban water supply. Subsequently, this will increase reliability of water supply in Suva and Nausori and reduce costs due to climate change on sea levels and decreased river flows during extended drought periods and provides such as the current situation.

## 21. Wastewater Management

Current planning regulations ban high-density urban development in unsewered areas, which has resulted in a large number of informal settlements being established. The lack of wastewater infrastructure has constrained high density development. This is directly contributing to urban sprawl and subsequent environmental degradation. The project will address this by improving the sewerage network in the GSA in 90% by 2023. The main WWTP facility in the GSA, the Koinya WWTP plant, is currently working at full capacity and needs to be upgraded and expanded so that all wastewater can be treated. Wastewater modeling has also been undertaken for the pump station and gravity and rising mains.

## 22. Project will increase WAF sewerage coverage and reliability of WWT processes in the GSA

## 25. Non-Revenue Water Reduction

WAF has been supporting NRW at reduce NRW since 2007. This project will finance an International NRW specialist and an international water demand management specialist (see their TDRs in Project Appraisal Link Document 1: Project Administration Manual). In addition to the support to be provided by the project, the Government of DRC has committed to providing WAF with a dedicated staff member who can provide advice in NRW and water demand management. NRW is a priority for WAF as it also is receiving training and technical assistance from ADB on this topic (see Project Appraisal Link Document 1: Project Administration Manual). The project will finance the following activities: (i) supporting WAF to develop a National Water Demand Management Strategy; (ii) supporting WAF in the development of a national water demand management plan; (iii) providing technical assistance and training on water demand management for WAF staff. This project will also support WAF in developing Document 14 - NRW Non-Revenue Water Reduction Strategy and on going Technical Assistance on NRW and Water Demand Management.

D.1. Value Added for GCF Involvement

53. GCF involvement will provide approximately 30% of the financing required to deliver Output 1, which will significantly increase water supply in the greater Suva area, the main metropolitan center of Fiji, and ensure that the project is financially viable.

54. Critically, grant funding by GCF will allow F, a small island developing country, to undertake vital adaptation work to protect its water resources from climate change impacts. This will reduce the cost of adapting to climate change from 49% to 45% of GDP. The Government is also committed to ensuring that external debt is capped at 30% of total debt service in any given year and that the country’s debt servicing ratio does not exceed 12%. The additional costs of adaptation and mitigation measures will substantially increase the cost of providing such infrastructure. While this investment will have a strong social, economic, and environmental return to Fiji, the following table shows how GCF financing can help reduce the cost of adapting to climate change impacts.

55. GCF financing for the water supply component will finance adaptation measures for building a new water intake pipeline from the river Revesa to the Suva Water Supply Scheme (SWSS) and the construction of a reservoir and a pipeline into production by 30,000 m³ per day and supplement water supply for the GSA.

56. GCF financing will cover the additional costs associated with moving the facility from 29 km to 48km from the river Revesa (i) costs of constructing the new intake pipeline; (ii) costs of construction of a reservoir (10% of site access costs); (iii) costs of bringing electricity to the site and (iv) 50% of land acquisition and resettlement costs. The GCF will also cover the additional costs associated with the construction of the Revesa water supply pumping station, WTP and balance reservoirs. These are not included as costs of adaptation. Please see cost table below for details.

57. Projected costs of adapting to climate change impacts in this project are calculated to be 15% of the base cost to take into account the high risk of currency exchange and the difficulty to predict future water demand. The project is also expected to require a significant amount of technical expertise to recruit international experts and limited number of qualified contractors with experience in similar projects in Fiji.

58. The project will provide adaptive capacity and resilience of these activities through appropriating sites of the intake higher up the river catchment, away from the projected salinity wedge moving up the river system due to evaporation and rising sea levels. This will ensure that the water supply is not affected by climate change impacts. This will provide additional security and less likely to impact extractions for urban water supply. The proposed Revesa water supply scheme will also be designed to ensure that it can be used as an emergency backup in case of any future climate change impacts.

# F.2. Technical Evaluation

118. The technical evaluation has been prepared for the overall project. The project will utilize the biogas produced in the digesters for the production of electric and/or thermal energy, in their combined form also called cogeneration. The biogas is collected from the digesters and then compressed, cleaned, dried, cooled, and purified as required. A flare is typically used to burn gas that cannot be utilized and for emergency situations.

119. Cost efficiency in the operation and maintenance (O&M) of treatment plants is essential. One important aspect of this is the choice of technology. The project will use anaerobic digestion technology because these technologies are properly chosen and designed so they do not have higher investment costs than straight forward sewage treatment systems. However, it should be noted that there may be a significant increase in O&M operational expenditure which has big impact on the long term economic sustainability of the investments at the plant level.

120. The project will introduce WAF (Wastewater Anaerobic Fermentation) technology for the stabilization of sludge and it remains to see which technology will be proposed for the extension of the plant. However, it is expected that the O&M costs associated with WAF are lower than those associated with conventional sludge technology and WAF being aware of these possibilities they will be able to consider them in case of new WWTPs are implemented.

121. The wastewater system in the GSA consists of 85 sewage pump stations, 44 of sewer rising main and trunk gravity mains, and over 330km of sewageway reticulation. In addition, there are 6 WWTP serving the area. However, the existing capacity is insufficient to meet the current demand for wastewater treatment services. Currently, Kinossa is foreseen to be further extended and be the central WWTP for GSA, since the other 4 small WWTPs will be closed down in the coming years.

122. The number of 86 sewage pump stations seems to be high for a catchment area of the size of GSA, even though it provides good coverage of the urban and peri-urban areas. However, the current system has a large number of pump stations, and eventually, increase the capacity of the remaining ones, which would improve the overall efficiency of the system. The project will consider the possibility to reduce the number of pump stations by adding more WWTP in other parts of GSA area rather than centrally treat the vast majority of wastewater in Kinossa. This will also provide a better distribution of the treatment services and will be more cost effective.
