## A.2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

1. Poverty and environmental degradation are resulting in intensely negative socio-economic effects in The Gambia. Climate change is exacerbating these problems as the frequency of droughts and floods are, for example, increasingly severe, resulting in reduced agricultural production and unsustainable extraction of resources from forest ecosystems by rural Gambians.
2. To address this problem, a paradigm shift is required to build resilience of rural Gambians by adapting agricultural practices and natural resource management to climate change challenges. The Gambia’s existing policies provide decentralization of natural resource management (including inter alia community forests, forests reserves and conservation areas); to community-managed organizations.
3. The project will comprise three components:
  - a) large-scale EBA to build a climate-resilient natural resource base based on the principles of ecosystem-based adaptation (EBA), including policy support, institutional strengthening and knowledge generation to support large-scale implementation of EBA.

#### B.1.8 Project Cost Information

The Government of The Gambia requests that 100% of the proposed GCF grant commitment is committed in the form of grant (USD). The request for grant financing is based on The Gambia's status as an LDC. The country has a low GDP per capita ($US325), and a high debt ratio (external debt to GDP > 100%). This makes it difficult for The Gambia to access equity or manage loan or reimbursable grant finance. It consequently requires significant grant financial support to adapt to the impacts of climate change, as well as to implement its Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) under the Paris Agreement (NAPAs), Second National Communication (SNC) and Intended Nationally Determined Contribution (INDC). In addition, an important part of The Gambia's NDC is to enable access for smallholder farmers in the agricultural sector. Smallholders are eligible to receive resources from the GCF that will finance aggregated full costs for activities to enable and support existing or new community-managed businesses based on natural resources.

# C.1 Detailed Project Programme Description

The proposed GCF project will use large-scale EDA to increase crop and livestock productivity, and increase the supply of resources from forest ecosystems under climate change conditions. The project is consequently aligned with a number of national policies and strategies.

## a) The Forestry sector-sub-policy period (2010-2019), which encourages the involvement of local communities and the private sector in the management of forests, as well as the development of community forest structures. To this end, we maintain and develop forest resources at least 30% of total land area to provide environmental protection services, including carbon sequestration, water supply, soil conservation, biodiversity conservation, ecosystem stability and protecting swamps; ensure that 75% of forest lands are managed and protected according to forest laws and regulations. The project will contribute towards the achievement of the following targets: (i) establishment of local structures such as community forest committees, devolving authority and management responsibilities to communities, and increasing their capacity for sustainable forest management; (ii) improving the supply of forest produce necessary to both urban and rural population through the rehabilitation of forest lands and establishment of fast growing plantations and woodlots.

## b) The Agenda 2016 document (2009-2015), which identifies needs such as decentralized management of wildlife resources, developing value chains for natural resource-based products, and improving access to markets for these products. Vision 2030, the Medium Term Development Framework, and the Gambia Environmental Action Plan, all of which highlight the need for sustainable forest management in order to reduce poverty, improve food security, and counter loss of biodiversity and climate change.

## c) Project 1000 Forests (2009-2015), which focuses on sustainable forest management, increasing agricultural production and achieving food security for the country.

## d) The National Forestry Policy (NFP) (2008-2030), which aims to improve the use of forest resources of The Gambia with a view to enhancing the contribution of forestry to socio-economic development. Of particular relevance to this project is the creation of community forests, where communities are given legal rights over forest areas and forests are formed and managed (Part VII Declaration of reserved forest; Creation of community forest). Designation of community forests is currently in the process of being enacted by the Ministry of Justice.

## e) The National Forest Policy (NFP) (2015-2030), which aims to reduce poverty and social problems by generating sustainable livelihoods while protecting the environment.

## f) The National Adaptation Programme of Action (NAPA) and the National Action Plan (NAP) for UNCCD, which promote activities such as: wetland engineering to ensure food and water security; restoration of degraded land; reforestation and afforestation; rehabilitation of forest resources; expansion of existing ones; expansion and consolidation of community forestry; and joint forest park management.

## g) National priorities in response to climate change, including:

- The Rehabilitation of Early Warning Systems for Climate-Related Natural Hazards;
- Improvement of Freshwater Availability;
- Environmental Management of Agricultural Production, Processing and Marketing;
- Expansion of Community Participation in the Management of Forests and Protected Areas;
- Expanding the Role of Women in Forestry;
- Improved Livestock and Rangeland Management of Food Security and Environmental Sustainability.

## h) The National Climate Change Strategy (NCCS) (2015-2030), which puts a strong emphasis on the need to integrate national adaptation priorities into sectoral and cross-sectoral planning and programming. The NCCS is currently being developed by the Ministry of Environment, Forestry and Rural Development with input from the private sector, civil society organizations, local communities, government staff and the general public. The proposed GCF project will contribute towards achieving the adaptation targets proposed in this strategy.

## i) The Sustainable Development Goals, to which Gambia is a signatory, particularly SDGs on Climate Change Biodiversity, Poverty, Food security, and Gender Equality (SDG 13, 15, 2, and 5, respectively).

In addition to national policies, strategies, and programmes, the project is aligned with local-level planning activities. The GCF project will contribute towards the achievement of sustainable forest management areas such as Community Forests (CFs), Jointly Managed Forest Parks (JMPFs), Jointly Managed Wildlife Parks (JMWP).

### 9. As a result of the reliance of Gambian's rural communities on natural resources and agriculture, the livelihoods and food security of these households is threatened by activities which degrade the country's ecosystems and natural resource base.

The Government has identified that the over-exploitation of natural resources in The Gambia is reducing the generation of ecosystem goods and services that support both agricultural productivity and rural livelihoods. The Government also recognizes that the widespread adoption of modern agricultural techniques on stanch-and-burn agricultural techniques has resulted in a widespread depletion of soil fertility, thereby reducing agricultural productivity.

The over-exploitation of natural resources in The Gambia is leading to the decline of small-scale businesses, and production of charcoal results in a considerable loss of vegetation cover which leads to widespread degradation of land. This degradation of land in turn leads to increased vulnerability of communities to climate change. The decline in forest cover also leads to negative effects on rural Gambian's food supply, health, nutritional status, income streams and socio-economic well-being.

### C. Market Overview (if applicable)

The proposed GCF project will develop an enabling environment for the development of climate-resilient, natural resource-based products in The Gambia. This will be achieved through the establishment of existing businesses – known as Community Forest Enterprises (CFEs) within the Department of Forestry – the project will support the development and expansion of these CFEs to include new value chains for a range of forest resources available and for which there is an established demand*. Natural products for which there is a well-established local demand in The Gambia are: honey, palm oil (Borassus aethiopum), firewood/timber, and other non-timber forest resources (numerous species), building materials (Rhino palm as well as semi-valuable and valuable timbers); and handicrafts (e.g. Kaya shea butter). The development of value chains for these products has been identified by the project. Market-based businesses have previously been assessed on a limited scale by the ongoing MAAD process, which is a stepwise process for determining the practicality of establishing a CFE in the context of specific communities.

The primary challenge to developing the various markets and value chains for the above-mentioned products is not the lack of demand but rather the lack of supply. The market potential for these products depends on the supply side. As a result of the lack of business and financial management capacity, the limited skills for marketing and sales, and the low levels of investment in infrastructure, the existing forest-based businesses are so small as to undermine the incentives for supply-side stakeholders to participate or in interest in contributing to the development of these markets. The project will support the development of value chains from natural resource-based products through investments in value addition, processing, packaging and collective marketing. Other interventions that have been identified by the project include: (i) honey production; (ii) palm oil products (such as drying or juice manufacture) have been identified as potential measures to increase the financial returns paid to honey producers.

In response to the above-mentioned constraints that prevent the realization of the commercial potential of various natural resource-based products in The Gambia, the project will take a phased approach to the development phase. This will be achieved through the following interventions: (i) the identification of natural resource-based products and business types that were identified could that be established based on the proposed EDA intervention; The technical feasibility and market potential for these products will be assessed in order to determine their commercial viability. These include:

* a) sustainable collection of fuelwood/timber;
* b) establishment of value chains for low-value logs/timber for construction: e.g. Gmelina arborea, Khaya senegalensis, Pericarpium eriocarpus, Daniella olivieri; in particular, planting tropical hardwood trees to produce timber for construction; (ii) the identification and investment that is likely to provide attractive financial returns in the long-term*;
* c) beekeeping for honey and wax production;

### D.1. Rationale for GCF Involvement

This proposed GCF project is fully aligned with the GCF's focus of promoting a paradigm shift in developing countries. The project will contribute to the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). The previous ecosystem restoration initiatives in The Gambia have been conducted in an ad hoc manner over relatively small areas (e.g., 10,000 ha) and have not had a significant impact on national-level SDG targets. This project is aligned with GCF Investment Criteria (described in Section D.6). In terms of project beneficiaries: It is estimated that the 11,550 participants will directly benefit from the proposed EBA interventions, conservatively assuming a household size of four people, or at least half of participating households.

### 68. Cumulative value of GCF's project investments

The cumulative value of the GCF's project investments will be the delivery of climate-resilient livelihood benefits through large-scale implementation of EBA that will supplement the income and increase the food security of climate-vulnerable communities in The Gambia. By targeting at least 50% of the population, with women representing at least 50%, who would benefit: That adapt climate-resilient land management practices, the project will stimulate economic growth and poverty reduction by increasing agricultural productivity and income generation from increased investments in a climate-resilient natural resource base and facilitate the development of a green Gambian economy. Excluding the direct benefits to households, the project is expected to have indirect benefits for the objectives of socio-economic and rural and urban development, climate change adaptation, and improved management of natural resources.

### 69. Design of the proposed GCF project

The design of the proposed GCF project includes multiple considerations: To increase cost-effectiveness, efficiency, and effectiveness; To ensure that the project is aligned with national priorities for addressing climate-related challenges and overcome the barriers to implementing large-scale EBA as described in Section C.

### 70. Building on a well-established baseline of past and ongoing initiatives

The proposed GCF project builds upon the existing ecosystem restoration initiatives in The Gambia, which include initiatives focused on climate change adaptation and mitigation (for example, past and ongoing GEF-funded projects). These initiatives have been implemented under the framework of the Ministry of Agriculture, Animal Industry and Fisheries (MA&F) process through the DoR. For instance, the UNEP-UNDP LDCF project "Strengthening climate services and Ecosystem-based Adaptation in The Gambia" has already established a strong baseline for this project. This will strengthen the climate monitoring capabilities, early warning systems, and available information for responding to climate change impacts. The proposed project will build on these existing initiatives by: a) Strengthening national capacities to generate and use climate information in the planning for and management of climate-induced risks; b) Enhancing the capacity of communities to access and use climate information through training, learning dissemination and advisory communications; and c) Promoting uptake of the climate information at the local level.

##### E.4.1 Vulnerability of county and beneficiary groups (Adaptation only)

The Gambia is one of the most vulnerable countries in West Africa to climate change, with more than half of the population living below the USD 2 per day poverty threshold. Most Gambians rely strongly on livelihoods from natural resources, particularly agriculture, which is highly dependent on rainfall and water availability. The Gambia experience a "hunger season" between July and September, during which food stocks are low or depleted. To compound this problem, the country has experienced severe droughts in 2011 and 2013, with devastating effects.

Such hardships have been exacerbated by the global economic downturn and the drought of 2011. In The Gambia, Rainfall is projected to decrease by up to 5% per decade over the next century, which will have a significant impact negatively on the production of food in agricultural landscapes and natural ecosystems. In particular, expected increases in temperature are likely to increase evapotranspiration rates, leading to more frequent and severe droughts, floods,108 and wildfires109. Addressing the effects of these climate change-related events have been placed at the center of GEF-5 and Second National Communication under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change.
