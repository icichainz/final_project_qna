### A.2 Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

Please provide a brief description of the proposed project/programme, including the objectives and primary measurable benefits (see investment criteria in section B). The detailed description can be elaborated in section C.

Tajikistan already faces recurring climate-related extreme weather events, particularly food and landslides. Climate change is expected to exacerbate these impacts [7]. Dairies in the Pyanj River Basin, in particular, are among poorest and most vulnerable to climate change!

However, low capacity in the country for forecasting of weather, hydrometeorological, and climatic conditions remains a critical weakness. The lack of infrastructure and human resources has severely hampered the implementation of climate change adaptation efforts, thus being hampered. The responsible forecasting entity, the State Agency for Hydrometeorology (Hydromet) is crippled by many of the socio-economic issues posed to post-Soviet states including limited budgetary allocations, lack of modern equipment and infrastructure, poor training of staff, low pay, and government and civil society stakeholders are generally unsatisfied with the forecasting services offered by Hydromet. Without investment in Hydromet’s capacity building, Tajikistan will continue to be vulnerable to climate change impacts.

The subject proposed project will support Hydromet's development to a sustainable and well-resourced institution that produces timely and accurate forecasting of extreme weather events particularly in the PRB. In doing so, the project will address the following components: (Component A) improve the institutional capacity of Hydromet by providing technical assistance and training for staff; (Component B) support legal and organizational transformation and capacity building of Hydromet into a modernized institution that is capable of delivering high-quality services to its stakeholders; (Component C) undertake capacity building for improving forecasting and warning of extreme weather events [Component (C)], and (iv) support development of long-term climate change adaptation strategies in Tajikistan.

The project’s primary measurable benefit (indicator) are: (i) Hydromet develops and disseminates long-lead forecasts of extreme weather events in the PRB and (ii) Hydromet develops a weather service product marketed to customers in the PRB.

**Impact:** The project will have direct benefits to individuals, community organizations, and government agencies in the Pyanj River Basin. It will also contribute to the achievement of the following Sustainable Development Goals:

* **Paradigm shift potential:** The project will facilitate a significant shift in the funding and provision of information and forecasting services in Tajikistan. The model is replicable in other hydrometeorology agencies around Central Asia. The project will help enable the scaling up of Hydromet’s products and services into for example agrometeorology.
* **Sustainable development potential:** The project will provide direct and indirect economic and social benefits to the PRB, with particular emphasis on: (i) improved livelihoods for farmers and pastoralists in the PRB; (ii) improved disaster preparedness and response; and (iii) increased productivity in key economic sectors.

# C.1 Strategic Context

Please describe relevant national, sub-national, regional, global, political, and/or economic factors that help contextualize the proposal, including existing national sector policies and strategies.

## Tajikistan: A Country Highly Prone to Climate-Related Extreme Weather Events

### Population and Economy

* **Population:** 8.5 million people.
* **Economy:** Primarily reliant on agriculture (90% of population lives in rural areas), with a significant share of the population living below the poverty line.

### Administrative Structure
Tajikistan is divided into 18 administrative districts across two regional adminstrations (Rahmon and Gorno-Badakhshan provinces).

### Geography and Climate

* **Geography:** Tajikistan is located in Central Asia, bordering Afghanistan to the south, Uzbekistan to the west, Kyrgyzstan to the north, and Pakistan to the east.
* **Climate:** Tajikistan experiences a wide range of weather conditions due to its diverse geography. The country has high-altitude drainage networks that deliver runoff and sedimentation to the rivers.

### Water Resources

* **Water Stress:** Tajikistan is one of the most water-stressed countries in Central Asia, with 72% of the population living in areas where water stress is a critical issue.
* **Agriculture:** Tajikistan's agriculture sector contributes significantly to its economy and employs over half of the workforce. The country has a high level of agricultural production (2.7 million tons of wheat and 774,000 tons of cotton per year), with the largest proportion of the population living in rural areas.

### Climate Vulnerability

* **Vulnerability:** Tajikistan is particularly vulnerable to climate-related extreme weather events such as flooding and droughts.
* **Flood Risk:** Tajikistan has experienced significant flood risk, especially during the 1998-2016 period. The country's economy is heavily dependent on agriculture, which makes it susceptible to climate change impacts.

### Water Resources Management

* **Water Scarcity:** Tajikistan faces water scarcity issues due to its high population density and limited water resources.
* **Agriculture:** Tajikistan's agricultural sector contributes significantly to the country's economy. The country has a high level of agricultural production, with over half of the workforce employed in agriculture.

### Climate Change Impacts

* **Climate Change:** Tajikistan is expected to experience increased frequency and intensity of extreme weather events due to climate change.
* **Water Resources:** Tajikistan's water resources are likely to be affected by climate change. The country has a high level of agricultural production, with over half of the workforce employed in agriculture.

### Conclusion

Tajikistan is highly vulnerable to climate-related extreme weather events such as flooding and droughts. The country's economy is heavily dependent on agriculture, which makes it susceptible to climate change impacts. Tajikistan faces water scarcity issues due to its high population density and limited water resources.

# Detailed Project / Programme Description

## GREEN CLIMATE FUND FUNDED PROGRAMME | PAGE 8 OF 53

Addressing the current and future climate-related extreme weather risks is a concern and priority at high levels of the government. This is reflected in the Tajikistan Nationally Determined Contribution submitted to the UNFCCC Paris Agreement, which prioritizes reducing the vulnerability to climate change in key sectors such as agriculture and water resources management. The Government has identified adaptation priorities for these sectors including increased resilience, reduction, and improved knowledge sharing. These adaptation priorities were likewise reflected in investments proposed by Tajikistan under the Green Climate Fund (GCF) and are now being implemented through water resources management including flood risk reduction in PRB and improved knowledge sharing "as well as the Tajikistan National Adaptation Plan (NAP), which was submitted to the UNFCCC in 2015."

### C.2 Project / Programme Objective and Baseline

Describe the baseline scenario (i.e., emissions baseline, climate vulnerability baseline, key barriers, challenges and/or policies) and the outcomes and that the project/programme will aim to achieve in improving the baseline scenario.

The State Agency for Hydrometorology is Tajikistan's climate and weather agency. with a mandate to among others (i) ensure protection of vital interests of the individual, society and state from natural hazards; (ii) provide hydro-meteorological services to support sustainable development of the country; (iii) improve the quality of life for citizens through the provision of reliable information on the state monitoring network and the system for collection, processing and dissemination of hydrometeorological information. This mandate is achieved by providing a wide range of weather forecasts and climate change related services. However, Hydromet's capacity against the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) Strategy for Service Delivery (SSD®) and Global Framework for Climate Services (GFCS) is limited due to lack of modern equipment, infrastructure, and ability to achieve its public service mandate to inform, warn and protect the public on weather and hydrometeorological issues.

User engagement: Only 20% of surveyed stakeholders of Hydromet services expressed they were satisfied with Hydro's forecasting services. There is a lack of two-way communication with customers to support product development and improvement. The majority (85%) of users are not aware of the existence of hydro-meteorological forecasts from Hydro. Users have limited access to weather forecasts, which are often outdated or inaccurate due to poor quality of data received from sources such as satellites and radars. There is a lack of feedback from users. Hydromet lacks a framework and capacity for the development and sale of fee-based services. There is an apparent gap between the forecasters' needs and the users' needs in terms of weather situation. Advisory services are focused on general weather warnings rather than key decision-making issues.

Relevant GFCS pillars: User interface platform; Climate Service Information System

Quality of outputs and capacity development: Hydromet has a limited and patchy meteorological and hydrological monitoring network, which is not capable of providing reliable and accurate weather forecasts, for which it depends on third party numerical weather prediction modelling and satellite imagery, and which lacks probabilistic analysis. The weather forecasting system does not provide analysis or probabilistic response such as risk of flood/forecasting its platforms and networks for rapid and broad dissemination of weather-related news and alerts to key government and civil society

As described in Section C.1. and C.2., Tajikistan is Central Asia's poorest country and among the most vulnerable countries in the region. Tajikistan is a country highly prone to climate-related extreme weather events, notably floods and droughts. The country has experienced significant changes in precipitation patterns over recent decades; climate change is expected to exacerbate these adverse events and their impacts. Higher temperatures and changes in precipitation patterns are expected to lead to increased evaporation rates and reduced water availability, leading to an overall water availability. Water stress conditions are likely to become more common, and flooding and landslides are likely to become more frequent and damaging.

Climate vulnerability is particularly acute in the districts along the Pyanj River Basin (PRB), the primary industry to the PRB is agriculture, which accounts for 40% of GDP. The PRB area is among the country's poorest and comprise a wide range of geographical and climatic conditions. PRB covers the majority of Khujand region, the province with the largest population in Tajikistan (15%), and the majority of the population lives on less than $2 per day. The PRB includes the most food insecure zone among country's irrigated area, and it is the country's poorest river basin (55% poverty rate). The PRB has a high vulnerability to climate change due to its location in the upper reaches of the Pyanj River with 4 lives lost and damage exceeding over $3 million per year. Women dominate lower paying jobs in the agriculture sector and are more vulnerable to climate change impacts, including food insecurity.

However, women have less access to resources (land, credit) and fewer are in leadership roles in WUAs and other organizations that manage water resources. The PRB is expected to experience a 10% increase in average temperatures by 2030 or 2075, due to increased melt caused by higher air temperatures. The expected change in monthly flow rates due to increased evaporation will be between -40 and +60%, with the greatest impact on water availability during flood events. A gradual shift in the river flow seasonal distribution and increase of irrigation water requirements are predicted.

## Legal analysis

Hydromet is a Government Agency transformed by a Government Resolution (decree) dated May 30, 2015 into a civil service organization, whose civil service rules apply to 64 staff while the majority fall outside the civil service. After May 2015 the Agency was subject to the same laws and regulations as other State institutions. Hydromet is a hybrid entity applying two different sets of rules to its staff: international law for foreign employees and civil service rules for local employees. Hydromet is also subject to the law that regulates a State institution. According to law and regulations applicable to State agencies, any revenue generated by Hydromet must be deposited in the state budget. Any funds received from outside sources are considered as income under an Agency status; "surrendered" first to MoF. Distribution to revenues that accrue from sources outside the State budget is subject to a separate law. The Government has provided Hydromet with project implementation through sovereign loans and grants governed by project agreements.

For Hydromet to retain and manage its revenues flexibly and provide performance-based incentives to civil servants, it must be considered as an independent legal entity under the laws of the Kyrgyz Republic governing state entities. The Government has assured in a letter issued by the Office of the First Deputy Prime Minister of the Government of Tajikistan of its principal support for the transformation of Hydromet into a State institution with flexibility towards international and foreign investments. There may be a basis to consider the legal status of Hydromet different than that of an Agency (civil service) allowing it to carry out the institutional transformation under a grant project. In this regard, Hydromet is currently in the process of applying for recognition as a State institution with its institutional transformation and is awaiting its approval as a State Institution for similar justifications may be worth considering.

### H2. Arrangements for Monitoring and Evaluation

#### Describing the arrangements (e.g., frequency of performance reports) laid out in AMA, please provide project/programme specific institutional setting and implementation arrangements for monitoring and reporting evaluation. Please indicate how the intermediate term and final evaluations will be organized, including the timing. Please provide availability of monitoring and evaluation resources.

Monitoring, evaluation, and reporting will follow ADB’s Evaluation Policy and the Accreditation Master Agreement concluded between ADB and GCF. The Hydromet PMO established for the project will be responsible for day-to-day monitoring and reporting on the implementation progress and challenges, updated procurement and implementation plan (PIP) and other relevant documents. The Project Director will serve as a member of the team at the country level for all indicators, on the implementation progress and challenges, updated procurement and implementation plan (PIP), and other relevant documents. The Project Director will be supported by a M&E specialist under the PIC.

The PMO Director will inform Hydromet and the ADB on an as-needed basis of any delays or difficulties during implementation, so that the appropriate support and corrective measures can be adopted. The Project Director will also ensure that the project is implemented in accordance with the approved PIP and other relevant documents and reporting project results.

In addition:

- A modified project administration manual will be prepared at the project outset setting out the reporting, monitoring and evaluation activities, responsibilities, and roles.
- EGP will conduct a mid-term review of progress every six months per year to assess progress of project implementation activities, compliance with covenants and project agreements, and to monitor progress in achieving project outputs and agro-climatic impacts.
- ADB will undertake a mid-term review mission through project implementation at a time that ADB and the Government of the Philippines agree is appropriate. The review will cover (i) assessing the performance against organizational, technical, environmental, social, economic, procurement, and financial aspects of the project based on the assumptions in the PIP; (ii) assessing compliance with covenants and other relevant agreements; (iii) assessing the need to restructure or reformulate the project; and (iv) update the project’s design and monitoring framework if restructuring or reformulation is necessary.
- Project completion report will be prepared by Hydromet on a rolling basis by ADB staff in Manila and Dushanbe.
- Following physical completion of the project, Hydromet will submit a project completion report to ADB.
