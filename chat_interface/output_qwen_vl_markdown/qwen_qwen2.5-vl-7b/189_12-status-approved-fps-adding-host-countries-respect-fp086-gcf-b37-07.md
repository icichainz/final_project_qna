# Status of approved funding proposals: adding host countries in respect of FP086 (Green Cities Facility)

## Summary

This document presents the request for the addition of host countries to FP086 (Green Cities Facility) originally approved at the twenty-first meeting of the Board for the consideration of the Board.


---
**Page 2**
---

## I. Introduction

1. The Board, at its twenty-first meeting, approved through Decision B2/74 funding proposal (PROPOSAL) titled “Green Climate Fund” [the “programme”] submitted by the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (“EBRD” or the “accredited entity”). The funded activity agreement (FAA) for the programme was executed on 2 July 2019 and became effective on 3 September 2019. The EBRD is responsible for implementing the programme in funding, but the Board approved EUR 87 million [EUR 65 million in reimbursable funds and EUR 22 million in non-reimbursable funds] to be disbursed by the accredited entity. The FAA provides the option for the accredited entity to request additional commitment from GCF up to the total amount of EUR 100 million. Any such additional commitment should be requested up to one year before the closing date of the programme.
2. The programme aims to address “offices” climate change challenges while building the market or enabling access to finance for low-carbon and climate-resilient investments. The programme addresses multiple climates to action through four components that: (i) deliver policy and strategic support; (ii) provide technical assistance and capacity development, including infrastructure investments; (iii) build the capacity of key stakeholders; and (iv) facilitate and provide a pathway for access to green finance and capital markets. The programme’s investment focus is on six sectors: agriculture, energy, forestry, water resources, urban transport, municipal energy systems (district heating, waste-to-energy, etc.). Underpinning the programme has been slower than envisioned in the disbursement plan of the FAA. The indicative disbursement schedule in the FAA reflects that all funds would be disbursed by 31 December 2024. However, as at 31 March 2022, only EUR 87 million has been disbursed to the programme. Drivers of the slower than expected disbursement are convoluted and include a combination of administrative and lessons learned by the accredited entity in relation to eligibility criteria for the projects.

## II. Proposed change

4. The accredited entity has submitted a restructuring proposal (contained in annex III and VI) seeking to add additional countries to the programme: Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan. The restructuring proposal also includes a further set of requests by the accredited entity to accelerate deployment of funds under the programme and increase disbursement rates. These include: (i) changes in the indicative disbursement plan for the realisation of non-reimbursable commitments; amendments to the eligibility criteria, increased currency flexibility, extension of programme timelines and inclusion of additional countries.
5. The Secretariat has reviewed the restructuring proposal and its accompanying changes with regard to the realisation of non-reimbursable commitments, amendments to the eligibility criteria, increased currency flexibility, extension of the timeline of the programme, etc., and considers that these changes are material (major). Further details on the approved changes included in annex II. The Secretariat has also evaluated the request for additional countries; is supportive of this change given the need to diversify the portfolio of the programme. However, the inclusion of a country is significant change to the scope of the programme, the Secretariat does not consider that it constitutes a material (major) change and therefore the restructuring proposal does not trigger clause 16 of the GCF Policy on Restructuring and Cancellation, specifically subclause 16(b).

### III. Assessment

The request from the accredited entity that will enable it to expand the programme is part of a set of programmes changes to allow EBRD to accelerate the deployment of the programme, while due diligence on other identified projects continues. The Secretariat is supporting the expansion of the programme by facilitating the inclusion of additional countries under the programme such that the programme can achieve its target outcomes and objectives.

The accredited entity, in consultation with the NIBs of the host countries, has sought to include additional countries as part of this process. These are the same countries with which NIBs of all current host countries (see annex II and annex V). The accredited entity has received seven NIBs from the nine existing host countries, and six additional NIBs from the Secretariat recommend that the Board take note of the inclusion and approve the request.

In line with previous instances where accredited entities requested host countries to be included in the programme, the Secretariat has prepared this document, which includes the NOIs from the NIBs from seven of the nine host countries, and the NOIs from all six additional countries. The Secretariat recommends that the Board take note of the inclusion and approve the request.

### IV. Compliance with GCF policies

In submitting this document for Board consideration, the Secretariat confirms that all relevant requirements contained in the GCF Policy on Restructuring and Cancellation have been complied with as the changes have been assessed as non-major and NDA consultation has taken place.

### V. Recommendation

In light of the above considerations, the Secretariat recommends that the Board approve the inclusion of Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Morocco, Tajikistan, and Uzbekistan as host countries for FPBHD.

Accordingly, it is recommended that the Board adopt the draft decision contained in annex I.

# A.2 SUMMARY OF PROPOSED CHANGES TO THE PROJECT/PROGRAMME (max 300 words)

Please provide a brief description of the proposed changes to the project/programme, including the rationale and anticipated benefits. Emphasize any new priorities or previously unrecognizable benefits (see standard criteria in section A1). The detailed description can be elaborated in annex.

The EBCG seeks to expand the geographical scope of countries eligible under the Funded Activity: IP086 "Green Cities Facility" (the "Facility").

The difficult global context has profoundly affected cities. Still, in the recovery phase of the lasting impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic, cities have been further impacted by the war on Ukraine and the resulting energy crisis, as well as displaced refugee communities. The EBCG is committed to supporting cities in this challenging environment. In a context of the unavoidable impact of externalities, new priorities emerge and strained budgets, it is more critical than ever.

In this context of increased needs, high uncertainty and new challenges for cities, the expansion of the geographical scope of the Facility will allow EBRD to:

- (i) support a larger group of cities; and
- (ii) accelerate the deployment of the Facility by providing timely financial support to the projects which have reached maturity, while providing critical technical assistance for those under preparation.

The EBCG seeks to increase the geographical scope of the Facility to six new countries and their cities:

* Bosnia and Herzegovina - Sarajevo
* Georgia - Tbilisi, Kutaisi, Rustavi, Gori, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Zugdidi
* Kazakhstan - Almaty, Shymkent, Ust-Kamenogorsk

## Strategic context for improving resilience and adaptation

Regardless of strained budgets, cities increasingly recognize that resilience and climate adaptation is a priority area for investment. The threat of extreme weather events has become more acute across all regions of the world, particularly in developing countries where cities grow at unprecedented speed. Both extreme weather events and climate projections indicate that urban areas will be disproportionately affected by climate change. This is especially true in Mongolia, which is extremely vulnerable to climate change-induced challenges: the devastating 2014 floods in the Western Balkans and Serbia in particular which triggered a wave of displacement and migration; the severe droughts in East Africa; the extreme heat waves in Europe; and the devastating Mongolian winter that killed livestock in large numbers, forcing rural population to migrate and lead up residence in informal urban settlements.

CMIP5’s climate data, which underpins the latest IPCC Assessment report and which has been synthesised by the World Bank into country briefs, is unequivocal: the mountainous Western Balkans are likely to suffer more severe floods and landslides due to heavy precipitation events; East Africa will experience increased frequency of extreme heat waves and droughts; Europe faces a combination of increasing water stress, wildfires and higher mean temperatures – which is expected to put pressure on agricultural production in the Mediterranean and the Central Asian nations; extreme heat events are projected to have strong knock-on effects on droughts and food security.

Climate related events threaten social and economic development, with urban poor, living in substandard housing or informal settlements, being particularly vulnerable. The Western Balkans and East Africa are among the regions most affected by climate change and high priority for cities. As a result, beneficiary cities have expressed strong demand for support for adaptation projects. A first GCF investment grant was signed in Georgia in January 2013 for the modernisation of Tbilisi metro and two additional grants have been signed in Serbia (Belgrade) and Kosovo (Pristina). The project in Ulaanbaatar Green Affordable Housing A is also being developed utilising almost all non-renewable funds available under Component 2. The subsequent commitment envisaged when the GCF Board approved the Funding Proposal (decision GC/12/06) will prove instrumental to address these pressing needs.
