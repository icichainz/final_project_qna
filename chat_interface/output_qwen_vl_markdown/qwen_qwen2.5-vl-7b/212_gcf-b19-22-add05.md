### A.2 Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)

1. In Paraguay, micro, small and medium-sized enterprises (henceforth SMEs for simplicity) have an inadequate access to medium and long-term finance to narrow technology gaps and boost productivity and growth. This inadequate financing is mainly due to the high cost of credit, which is often linked with a lack of collateral. The expansion of hydropower has restricted SMEs in key energy consuming sectors to develop energy efficiency investments projects, including the replacement of inefficient equipment (e.g., incandescent light bulbs) by more efficient ones and the use of other fossil fuels; the exchange of old equipment with new efficient equipment that replaces use of firewood, biomass and other renewable sources of energy. The lack of access to finance is also due to a high cost of credit which reduces use of firewood, biomass and other fossil fuels.
2. A major challenge for Paraguay is the increasing energy consumption from non-renewable energy source, mainly biomass, and reduce greenhouse emissions by about 4 million CO2eq over the project's lifetime, in addition to promoting increased energy efficiency in SMEs. The project will support the implementation of energy efficiency projects (EE projects) in SMEs by supporting the structuring of financing mechanisms and instruments and improve energy efficiency in SMEs through the development of a national energy efficiency policy.
3. GCF funding will be channeled through a sovereign-guaranteed loan from the Inter-American Development Bank (IADB). The IADB is a member of the Green Climate Fund (GCF) and has been designated as a trustee for the IADB-World Bank Group Trust Fund (WBGTF) for the GCF. The GCF reimbursable resources, which will be maintained in a dedicated account, will be treated as an equity investment by the IADB. The project will also support the development of national institutions (LINs) so that they can, in turn, offer financing at adequate terms and conditions to SME firms interested in adopting energy efficiency projects.
4. The project is aligned with the National Climate Change Policy and the National Determined Contribution, where improvement of energy efficiency is one of the key strategies for Paraguay's climate change mitigation efforts. The project will also contribute to the input of stakeholders, as they have been fully consulted and the NDA has issued a Letter of Non-Objection.

15. Standardized performance contract: There is virtually no E&M market in Pakistan, and there are no clear and established mechanisms for financing arrangements that assist in providing a stable revenue stream, savings, and subsequently allocate the risks to market actors best able to manage these risks related to achieving those savings. The proposed project will introduce an innovative mechanism called the Energy Savings Insurance (ESI) which is a financial services and technology provider (ESTP) in which the risks associated with achieving the future energy savings are transferred from the E&M service provider to the ESTP. This is achieved through a standardized contract mechanism through a contractual retention (around 25%) that is deducted from the total value of the project to be paid by the E&M service provider to the beneficiary client. The E&M service provider will then pay this amount to the ESTP. A standardized contract not only gives confidence to the SME that the ESTP will deliver the contracted energy savings but also provides a financial risk management instrument for the E&M service provider. This is because the retention is paid in advance and not on an ongoing basis, which means that the E&M service provider can be confident of receiving this amount even if the project does not achieve its targeted energy savings.

16. Energy savings Insurance (ESI): As highlighted in the Green Innovation Lab for Climate Finance Analysis (December 2015), the ESI is a financial risk management instrument in the form of a surety that partially covers the energy saving commitment made by the E&M service provider to the beneficiary client. The ESI is an insurance policy that provides protection against potential financials. To that effect, local insurance/surety firms will be engaged in the program, and these firms are likely to reinsure their policies with international re-insurance companies. The insurance has a similar expected positive effect on the loan as the energy performance contract (EPC) but is more flexible for the E&M service provider. The ESI provides for energy savings projects where end-beneficiary clients (SBAs) repay the project loan through the monetized energy savings and not through cash payments to the E&M service provider. This means that the SBAs are repaying their loans to an end beneficiary client, which constitutes a retention fund to be managed by an insurance company. The inclusion of all retained funds in the ESI is expected to provide a more stable revenue stream for the E&M service provider as equipment will be paid by the client to the technology service provider. A share of the project costs go into a retention fund maintained by the insurance company, which is released only after the energy savings have been achieved and thus the ESI complies with its promises. If there is a lack of energy savings, then the insurance company will not release any funds from this fund to the E&M service provider.

### 27. General Overview
Paraguay’s financial system consists of 16 banks and 13 finance companies. Bank assets (US$19.2 billion as of December 2014, accounted for 95% of total system assets. Paraguayan’s financial system is relatively small in terms of the size of its economy. The agricultural sector accounts for about 27% of GDP and 38% of employment. Agriculture and livestock receive a little bit more than a third of all bank loans (34%), given the importance of that sector as a result of the country’s dependence on agriculture and livestock production. Other sectors include: industry (15%), consumption (15%), industry (10%) and financial system (7%). Consumer lending has risen steadily in recent years at a rate of 28% per year.

Paraguay's financial system is dominated by commercial banks. The main components of the system are:
- (i) Commercial banks, which account for about 94% of total assets;
- (ii) other financial institutions, with about 4%;
- and (iv) currency exchange offices, which account for the remaining 1%.

There are no central banks in Paraguay. The commercial banks are supervised by the Central Bank of Paraguay, holding assets of about 2 percent of GDP. These institutions mainly fund their operations with transfers from the budget and foreign loans.

### 29. Lack of long term credit
The principal indicators of Paraguay’s financial system performance indicate that it has performed well in recent years. The ratio of non-performing loans to total loans is low, at about 1%. This indicates that it prevents from meeting the demand for credit among domestic production sectors. The unused demand for medium and long-term credit is estimated at around 20% of the current portfolio of SMEs. Firms require considerably more financing than the amount effectively available in the financial sector.

The structure of loans in Paraguay’s banking system is as follows: short-term loans (less than one year) account for about 18%, medium-term loans (one to three years) are around 20% and long-term loans (more than three years) account for a little over 60%. The ratio of non-performing loans has improved from previous years, as long-term credit has increased from 28.9% in 2013 to 41.5% in 2016. Despite the increase in the share of long-term loans, the structure still restricts medium and long-term lending by intermediary LFs both to individuals and businesses, as a way to support their productive activities.

The average maturity for SMEs is about three years (up from 8.5% in 2013), 24.9% for 1-3 years and 63.7% of deposits for less than 1 year. The financial system does not have a significant capacity to finance medium-term investments, as the average maturity is about three years or less than one year. The financial sector offers less attractive instruments, adjusted for inflation and with variable rates, and high interest rates.

### 31. Lack of long term credit directly undermines firms' capacity to invest in new projects.
The financing structure of Paraguay’s banking system does not allow the intermediation of medium-term loans into the financial sector. This lack of investment translates into low competitiveness, especially for SMEs, which have very limited access to finance and are therefore unable to meet their working capital requirements or long-term financing requirements. As these SMEs employ approximately 47% of the country’s labor force, the lack of credit has a direct impact on employment levels.

The financial system also does not allow firms to invest in new projects due to the high cost of equipment obsolescence and capital stock. In addition, there is usually an incompatibility between the time it takes for these investments to be amortized and the period during which they are covered by the financing system, generating maturities in loan amortization periods for those SMEs.

### 32. Lack of long term credit also affects Paraguay’s capacity to finance its public debt.
The ratio of public debt to GDP is 31% as of Paraguay’s last available data (2014). The country has a high level of external debt, with the largest share in Latin America and the Caribbean.
