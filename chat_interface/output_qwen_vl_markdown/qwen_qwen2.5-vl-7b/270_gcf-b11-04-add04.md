# C.1 Strategic Context

The project addresses the systematic institutionalization of climate change adaptation by a major public infrastructure provider in Bangladesh, the Local Government Engineering Department (LGED). Such a systematic and institutional approach to climate change adaptation is critical for ensuring that the country’s infrastructure development follows a climate change adaptation framework (see Section E.5) and is one of the few national institutions that due to its experience and capacity can be considered as a potential lead institution in this area.

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) has been instrumental in supporting such institutional changes, and it was recognized by one of the best suited institutions for piloting this case of institutional change and will have positive spill-over effects on other public infrastructure providers. The project is being implemented with co-financing from GCF and a buy-in from the Government of Bangladesh (GoB), reflected by a significant financial contribution to the project and an active role in its implementation.

The start of operations of the Green Climate Fund (GCF) has thus triggered an important positive dynamic in Bangladesh and created a unique window of opportunity for paradigm shift on an institutional level (see Section D.1).

### Project Outcomes and Key Outputs

The project outcomes will respond to these three baseline trends. Key outputs of the project will be:

1. The systematic integration of climate change adaptation into decision-making regarding infrastructure planning, supervision, and maintenance.
2. The establishment of a Centre of Excellence (CoE) for climate-resilient local public infrastructure in Bangladesh.
3. The creation of an institutional knowledge base that is fully integrated into LGED’s operations.

### Project Impact

The project will have an immediate positive impact on the increased resilience of livelihoods of the most vulnerable people and their families, as well as on the resilience of infrastructure to climate threats (impact). The CoE will provide the capacity for LGED to mainstream climate change adaptation in its operations. CRiLC will cover 10 percent of all planned LGED investments and thus, 10.4 million people (see Section D.1) in the project area would benefit directly from this investment. This means that 150 percent of LGED investments and would then benefit indirectly a total of 194.8 million people.

### C.3. Project | Programme Description

The project will consist of four closely interlinked components (see B.1): (1) Institutional Development; (2) Hot Spot Identification and Mitigation; (3) Climate Resilient Local Public Infrastructure; and (4) Knowledge Management.

#### Component 1: Institutional Development

This component establishes the Centre Resilient Local Public Infrastructure Centre (CRiLC), a Centre of Excellence, an institutional mechanism for LGED for adopting local public infrastructure to climate change. The Centre will be dedicated to gathering, analyzing and developing relevant local and national best practices, taking into account international experience in this area. It will also provide technical assistance on the development of guidelines, building material standards, training curricula and geo-information for infrastructure planning, supervision and maintenance.

#### Sub-component f.f: Knowledge Management

The sub-component aims to ensure continuous internal provision of up-to-date climate-relevant data and information through user-friendly ICT applications and the systematic application of climate impact assessment for LGED’s operations. It will also provide access to relevant national and international research and monitoring initiatives. ### C.4. Background Information on Project/Programme Sponsor

The **KfW Development Bank**, KfW is Germany’s development bank and was with a balance sheet of EUR 480 billion and a funding volume of over EUR 74 billion (2014), one of the largest development banks in the world. On behalf of the Federal Ministry for Economic Cooperation and Development (BMZ) of the German government, KfW administers Germany’s Official Financial Cooperation in more than 100 developing and transition countries in Africa, Asia, Latin America, and Eastern Europe. KfW has been active in Bangladesh since 1972, shortly after the independence of the country, and has invested over EUR 3 billion.

The **Executing Agency**, The Local Government Engineering Department (LGED) is the technical arm of the Ministry of Local Government, Rural Development and Cooperatives of the Government of Bangladesh. It is mandated with responsibilities for the provision of local public infrastructure in rural areas and small to medium-sized towns in Bangladesh. LGED’s main functions include planning, design, construction, supervision, maintenance, disposal and small scale water resources. For sustainability of these development interventions LGED strives to ensure proper institutional capacity building and financial management systems.

With an annual allocation of more than 1 billion US$ (FY2012-2013), LGED is responsible for more than ten percent of all public infrastructure spending in Bangladesh. Over the past two decades, LGED has implemented roughly two-thirds of all investments in Bangladesh in rural development, its largely decentralized manpower of over 15,000 staff members and a network of 42 district offices across the country.
