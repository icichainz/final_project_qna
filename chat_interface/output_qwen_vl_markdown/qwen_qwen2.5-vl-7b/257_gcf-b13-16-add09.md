### Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 200 words)

Please provide a brief description of the proposed project/programmes, including the objectives and primary measurable benefits (see Investment criteria in section E). The detailed description can be elaborated in annexure A.

An enabling setting should be made available by the GCF and CAF - Latin American Development Bank to set up the first Chilean national-level convergence instance between climate funding, private funding, and sector plans for adaptation and active institutional programmes towards a low-emission sustainable development. The international project will be implemented by the GCF and CAF will support a pilot Atacama Solar (being driven by Sonnepro company), expanding the potential of solar energy generation in an area where the electricity generation matrix is highly intensive in thermoelectricity (intensive to GHG emissions), making it more competitive in one of the most important sectors for Chile.

Atacama Solar consists on a 143 MW solar photovoltaic plant in the Atacama Desert region of Chile, located in an area with high levels of solar radiation and low population density. This project is expected to be a game changer in the energy matrix future. This development represents a clean, sustainable solution to Chilean energy challenges, since the country has a very limited capacity for renewable generation (mainly hydroelectric power plants becoming less reliable). With a commitment to have a 20% of non-conventional renewable power generation by 2030, key decisions and investments taking place now will shape the transition to an energy system based on renewables.

When fully completed, the site will have an installed capacity of at least 250 MW to be built-out in two phases:

- Phase I: 143 MW - operation&financing (this Project)
- Phase II: 107 MW - operation&financing (to be financed by private investors)

The Atacama Project – due to its scale, adoption of top tier technology, and favorable cost structure – provides an estimated annual CO2 reduction of approximately 183,969 tonnes/year for a total of 3,697,440 tons of CO2 over 20 years (from 2015-2035).

The Project seeks to contribute to Chile's stated and quantified policy goals of shifting towards a low emission sustainable development pathway by developing and connecting a large-scale, expandable, clean energy power generation system. The project will be implemented in the Atacama Desert region (the largest desert in South America dominated (104%) Sept 2015) by fossil fuel-based thermal power generation, and providing the lowest cost per CO2 avoided of any solar power plant in Chile.

In an innovative financial design for solar power generation projects in Chile, the proposed financial model of the Project will provide a guaranteed revenue stream to the project developer through a performance-based power purchase guarantee from any contract of sale of energy (PPA). Based on market conditions, the Project will complete and dispatch up to 100% of peak power generation plants demonstrating that solar power generation in Chile has the potential to meet near-term electricity demand while also contributing significantly towards meeting renewable power generation target.

The Project is expected to have a turning point demonstrating access to loans and investments for renewable projects with high upfront capital costs and need for long repayment terms which are currently unavailable in Chile's capital markets. In addition to the Project finance, CAF will expand its work in a strategic partnership with Chile aimed to build resilience at local and regional level vis à vis climate change impacts. This articulation seeks to permanently set in place the necessary conditions for the development of innovative financing mechanisms and low-carbon cutting programmes that will ensure a low emission and climate resilient development in the northern region of Chile.

## C1 Strategic Context

Please describe relevant national, sub-national, regional, global, political, and/or economic factors that help to contextualize the proposal, including existing national and sector policies and strategies.

The following sections highlights the global (COP21), Chile's national, CAF’s and GCF’s institutional context relevant to the Alsacana Solar Project:

### I. Global Context

On December 12th, 2015, 196 nations adopted the Paris Agreement to combat climate change and commit to limiting global end temperature increase towards a low carbon, resilient and sustainable future. The Alsacana Project provides a “shovel ready”, large scale, expandable solar PV project with peer leading CO2 offset potential.

### II. Chile’s Context

In preparation for this 2015 Agreement, Chile submitted its new climate action plan (the "Intended Nationally Determined Contribution") to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). It outlines a series of actions that will be taken in order to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and increase its non-conventional renewable capacity, and (b) that related investments will be driven by the private sector. A signature goal of the Chilean government’s 2014-18 energy agenda is the “2025” objective, which calls for 20% of Chile's electricity to come from renewable sources by 2025. In order to meet this target and scale up the scope of the Alsacana Solar development, the project will act as an enabler a paradigm shift in Chile.

Regarding other issues in Chile’s Context, we can add the following:

- **Hydro Situation in the Country**: Hydro projects are very important in the SIC. They account for around 50% of the ECO generation capacity. However, there are certain concerns for further developing these types of projects:
    - Variability of Hydrology (Hydrological Risk, see section C.3 on this issue);
    - Environmental concerns of the Chilean people and the Chilean State with regard to large scale hydro development (see section C.5 on this issue).
- **Capital costs**.

### Argentina Gas Issue

In 1997, Argentina and Chile signed a trade agreement that brought about a significant natural gas supply at convenient prices. Several investments followed this agreement, including gas pipelines to supply the SIC and SINCH. The SIC has also invested in two large-scale combined cycle power generation units. Thus, an important share of SIC and SINCH (Chilean interconnected grids – see Section C.5 for more info on SIC and SINCH networking) capacity started to depend on the Argentinian fuels market.

The linkages, outputs and outcomes are outlined from (1) GCF institutional perspective, and (2) a project implementation and project-financing perspective below:

### 1. In terms of GCF objectives and indicators, the successful completion, connection and operation of the large-scale solar power plant will result in a substantial displacement of CO2 (quantified further below) by increasing the number of large, low emission power suppliers in the SING transmission system. The Project will also enable the development of new renewable energy sources in Chile (e.g., copper intensive mining operations). The Project would also enable and support the realization of Chile's COP21-related emissions commitments to increase its energy generation mix from currently 11.4% to 20% by 2035.

The Project is expected to take a leading rank among its solar PV project peers in the Atacama Desert in terms of unit PV cost (i.e., US$786/kWp for total investment) and low specific unit costs (Phase I: $1,671/US$2 for total investment of US$50k for CO2 offset; Phase II: $3,997.442/kWp for total investment of US$18M). The Project will also be the largest solar PV project in Chile and one of the largest in South America with a capacity of 60 MWp (Phase I) and 50 MWp (Phase II), which is expected to supply electricity to approximately 24,000 households: energy use (Phase I: 198.565, based on average Chilean household power use of 2,160 kWh/year).

### 2. In terms of Project implementation, outputs include progress and completion defined against detailed performance indicators for each phase of the project as outlined in the table "Key Milestones" and the simplified Gantt "Preliminary Project Schedule" further above.
