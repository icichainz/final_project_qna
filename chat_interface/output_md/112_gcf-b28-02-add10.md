## Botswana

In rural areas, only 42 percent of households have access to proper sanitation and 22 percent to electricity. Botswana is a signatory to key international and regional agreements related to women's rights, except for the African Charter on Elections and Governance (ACDEG) and the Maputo protocol. The government has ratified several conventions and adopted national reforms to protect women's rights and promote gender equality, including:

- 1993: Amendment to the Tribal Land Act allows any citizen, including women, to apply for land.
- 1996: Mines and Quarries Act removes restrictions on women working underground.
- 1996: Employment Act guarantees equal remuneration for work of equal value.
- 1998: Penal Code Act increases penalties for sexual offenses and criminalizes the intentional spread of HIV.
- 2001: Marriage Act prohibits child marriage.
- 2003: Amendment of the Citizenship Act allows women married to foreign nationals to pass citizenship to their children.
- 2004: Amendment to the Penal Code introduces a minimum sentence of ten years for rape.
- 2004: Abolition of Marital Power Act provides equal power for men and women within common law marriages.
- 2008: Domestic Violence Act provides protection to survivors of domestic violence, excluding marital rape.

Botswana operates under a dual legal system, Common Law and Customary Law, which preserves some discriminations against women. However, the judicial system has recognized equality between men and women within the household.

## Central African Republic Profile

The Central African Republic (CAR) is a landlocked country with a population of 4.9 million, rich in natural resources. The country faces a complex conflict since 2012, leading to significant displacement and economic disruption. CAR is one of the poorest countries, ranked second to last on the 2019 Human Development Index. The government has limited authority over the territory, with armed groups controlling much of the land.

CAR has ratified several international human rights instruments but struggles with implementation. Key milestones in gender equality include:

- 1966: Ordinance prohibiting female genital mutilation.
- 1997: Family Code requires consent for marriage and bans early marriage.
- 2006: Laws protecting women against violence and promoting reproductive health.
- 2009: Labour Code guarantees equal wage for equal work.
- 2016: Constitution prohibits gender discrimination and promotes gender representation in politics.

Customary law remains widely implemented, resulting in discrimination against women and girls.

## Democratic Republic of Congo Profile

The Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) is the largest country in Sub-Saharan Africa, with a population of 84 million. Despite its natural resource wealth, DRC is one of the poorest countries and faces significant challenges in achieving the Sustainable Development Goals. The country has endured conflicts, leading to displacement and instability.

DRC has ratified many international human rights instruments but struggles with implementation. Key reforms include:

- 2002: Labor Code prohibits workplace harassment.
- 2006: Constitution bans discrimination against women and prohibits sexual violence.
- 2015: Parity Act promotes women's entrepreneurship.
- 2016: Labor Code amendment allows women to work at night in certain industries.

Customary law continues to be widely implemented, contradicting positive law and affecting women's rights.

## Kenya Profile

Kenya, with a population of 51 million, is projected to see significant population growth. The country has achieved lower-middle-income status, driven by agriculture and tourism. Despite economic growth, poverty and inequality persist, with significant regional disparities.

Kenya has made progress in electrification and is on track to meet the Sustainable Development Goal of universal electricity access by 2030. However, many still lack access to electricity and clean cooking.

Kenya has ratified international treaties related to gender equality and enacted a progressive Constitution in 2010. Key legislative measures include:

- 1882: Married Women’s Property Act provides equal property rights.
- 2006: Sexual Offense Act defines sexual offenses.
- 2011: Anti-FGM Act prohibits female genital mutilation.
- 2011: National Gender and Equality Commission Act promotes gender equality.
- 2012: Land Act grants equal land ownership rights.
- 2013: Matrimonial Property Act guarantees equality in matrimonial property.
- 2014: Marriage Act allows polygamy.

Kenya's Constitution promotes equality in marriage, employment, and education, and prohibits discriminatory customary practices.

## Gender Issues in Mali

### Education and Early Marriage
Girl’s access to education is constrained by early marriage and childbearing, especially in rural areas. Mali has one of the highest child marriage rates in Africa, with 53% of women married by age 18 compared to 4% of men. Early marriage correlates with low educational attainment and poor health outcomes, including high maternal and infant mortality rates.

### Gender-Based Violence
FGM affects 89% of women aged 15-49, with regional disparities. Mali has not legislated against FGM, and cultural norms support it. Other forms of violence are prevalent, with 2 in 5 women experiencing physical violence. Polygamy is linked to higher intimate partner violence.

### Women’s Political Participation
Women have limited access to political institutions, holding 9.5% of seats in the National Assembly in 2018. Women’s low political participation reflects societal views of men as primary decision-makers.

### Female Employment
Women predominantly work in the informal sector with low qualifications and income. They are often unpaid agricultural laborers. Social norms assign domestic work to women, contributing to a significant employment gap.

### Entrepreneurship and Access to Resources
Women play a major role in agriculture but have limited access to land and credit. Customary law excludes women from land ownership, and legal barriers hinder entrepreneurship.

## Namibia Gender Gap

### Education and Health
Namibia has achieved parity in education, with higher female enrolment in tertiary education. However, challenges remain in rural-urban educational gaps and occupational sex-segregation. Maternal mortality is high, and women are vulnerable to HIV.

### Gender-Based Violence
Women face high levels of sexual and GBV, with harmful practices prevalent in rural areas. Social tolerance for intimate partner violence has decreased, but it remains justified by some.

### Women’s Agency and Political Participation
Namibia has high female political participation, with 46% of Parliament seats held by women. However, women report lower interest and participation in politics compared to men.

### Female Employment
Women face gaps in income, vulnerable employment, and unemployment. They are more likely to work in the informal sector and earn less than men. Rural women are particularly vulnerable to unemployment.

### Entrepreneurship and Access to Resources
Women’s access to land is protected by law, but traditional customs affect property rights. Women have relatively equal access to bank accounts but face barriers in accessing formal credit.

## Uzbekistan Gender Gap

### Health and Education
Uzbekistan has improved reproductive health but faces barriers in rural healthcare access. Women have near parity in primary and secondary education but lower enrolment in higher education due to social norms and financial barriers.

### Women’s Political Participation
A quota law increased women’s representation in the National Assembly, but participation remains low. Gender stereotypes confine women to household roles, limiting their political engagement.

### Female Employment
Women have high labor market participation but are under-represented in formal employment and concentrated in lower-income sectors. Social norms assign unpaid care work to women, affecting their economic opportunities.

## 5.2 Project Level

At the project level, the following approach would be followed:

a. **Data Collection**: Document the baseline situation and identify gaps. Baseline data will be collected to set targets and measure progress during implementation, focusing on countries with weak existing baseline sex-disaggregated data. Research will build upon existing country gender diagnostics and action plans when available. Stakeholders’ consultations will involve female participants to understand men and women’s differentiated needs. Initial assessments will include an analysis of the differentiated impact of the SRMI project on men and women, involving gender and energy experts.

b. **Identifying and Implementing Relevant Actions**: Based on the initial diagnosis and inclusive stakeholders’ consultations, targeted activities will be identified to address key gender gaps. These may include actions to promote female employment in the renewables sector, financing local community projects empowering women, implementing prevention measures to mitigate GBV, and facilitating energy access for female-headed households.

c. **M&E and Knowledge Management**: The World Bank Group has strengthened its monitoring system regarding gender, focusing on results and outcomes. A gender-tag was launched to identify projects that effectively promote equality between men and women. The SRMI facility will adopt a gender perspective and aim to close key gaps in its sub-projects. Results will be monitored throughout the project and used for real-time adjustments. Projects will build on lessons learned from prior projects and regional and country-based knowledge production.

d. **Strengthening Female Participation and Voice**: Consultations and public meetings with stakeholders will be conducted throughout the project cycle in a participatory manner, focusing on engaging women. Special attention will be paid to using local dialects and including women from minority ethnic groups and other socially vulnerable groups. Consultations will be organized at convenient times for women, considering their household and childcare responsibilities. Female facilitators and local female leaders will be identified to ensure women’s voices are heard. Women-only discussion groups may be organized if appropriate. Partnerships will be created with women’s groups and NGOs to ensure the project’s sustainability.

## 6. Gender Action Plan

The Gender Action Plan proposed below is indicative and may be further amended and adapted according to knowledge gathered during the project inception phase to reflect the local context:

### Impact Statement

The program will follow a systematic approach and provide cross-support to sub-projects interventions to narrow gender gaps, focusing on policy dialogue, data collection, training, and capacity-building activities. The expected impact includes enhanced female economic participation in the energy sector, increased access to electricity, and the creation of income-generating opportunities for women in affected communities.

### Outcome Statement 1

The SRMI facility will focus on four key areas: 
1. Sector-specific and country-level gender knowledge to support the design and implementation of projects is generated.
2. An enabling policy and legal environment for women’s economic participation is promoted through policy dialogue activities.
3. Tailored project and program interventions to address identified gender gaps are designed and implemented.
4. Knowledge and learning opportunities are enhanced.

#### Output 1: Sector-specific and country-level gender knowledge is generated and shared

  - Undertake sector gender assessments to identify key gender gaps.
  - Organize multi-stakeholders workshops at the country level to present key findings.
  - Conduct gender need assessments of institutional partners.
  - Hold consultations with key stakeholders to gather knowledge on men and women’s differentiated needs.
