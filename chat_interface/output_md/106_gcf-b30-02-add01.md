# Climate Change Impacts and Projections for FSM

## Drought and ENSO Events

- Although the frequency of droughts is projected to decrease in FSM, drier conditions and droughts are linked to ENSO events, with increased intensity under El Niño conditions. The driest year on record for Micronesia was 1998 due to an El Niño phenomenon.

## Sea Level Rise

- Between 1993 and 2010, FSM experienced significant sea level rise, ranging from 8 to 12 mm per year. ENSO variations amplify mean sea level, exacerbating impacts of tidal surges, rainfall, and extreme events like typhoons.

## Extreme Climate Events

- FSM is highly susceptible to extreme weather events such as tropical storms and typhoons. Notable events include Typhoon Mitag in 2002 and Typhoon Maysak in 2015, which caused significant damage and loss of life.

## Climate Change Projections

### Temperature

- Projections suggest an increase in maximum daily temperatures for FSM. By 2030, under a very high emissions scenario, air temperature is projected to increase by 0.6–1.1°C.

### Precipitation

- FSM is expected to experience an increase in precipitation over the next century, with a decrease in drought frequency but an increase in intensity.

### Sea Level Rise

- By 2030, under a very high emissions scenario, sea level rise is projected to be 8–18 cm. The frequency of extreme high sea levels is expected to increase dramatically.

## Vulnerability of the Programme Area

- FSM is particularly vulnerable to climate change due to limited infrastructure, geographic remoteness, and economic reliance on external assistance. Approximately 60% of households live within 180 meters of the shoreline.

## Impacts on Agriculture and Fisheries

- Agricultural production is at risk due to lowland flooding and seawater inundation. Small-scale fisheries are vulnerable to climate change due to rising sea surface temperatures and ocean acidification.

## Adaptation Measures

- Proposed interventions include constructing small-scale coastal infrastructure, restoring ecosystems, developing climate-resilient crops, and improving water storage systems.

## Summary of Climatic and Non-Climatic Drivers

| Type of Drivers | Drivers | Impacts | EDA Programme Potential Sub-Grant Interventions |
|-----------------|---------|---------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Climate Change Drivers | Sea level rise and storm surges | Coastal inundation, loss of arable land, food insecurity | Coastal infrastructure, ecosystem restoration, climate-resilient crops |
| Precipitation | Increased rainfall | Landslides, flooding, damage to watersheds | Watershed reforestation, coastal infrastructure |
| Air Temperature | Increased temperatures | Physiological stress on crops, pest growth | Climate-resilient crops, water infrastructure |
| Ocean Acidification and Sea Surface Warming | Coral bleaching, reduced fish stocks | Small-scale aquaculture, fisheries management |

## Natural Climatic Drivers

- ENSO conditions are expected to result in drought episodes, impacting groundwater resources, agroecosystems, and biodiversity.

## Conclusion

- The combination of climate vulnerabilities requires locally led adaptation measures. The proposed EDA Programme aims to address these challenges through targeted interventions.

## Additional Considerations

- The ECU will prioritize interventions relating to food security in outer atoll communities.
- Organizational audits will determine the capability of LAs to manage and implement proposed sub-grants.
- Training and support will be provided to municipalities evaluated as “low capacity.”

## Sub-grant Preparation and Screening

- Invitations to submit full proposals will be issued if an EOI passes screening.
- Support for full project preparation may be requested by applicants.
- Proposals will undergo a three-stage review process involving pre-screening, technical evaluation, and final decision by the EPB.

## Awarding of Grants

- Grants will be awarded in thematic areas: food security, climate-induced disaster risk reduction, coastal protection, and water security.
- A portfolio of gender-responsive sub-grants will be developed.

## Implementation Arrangements

### Overall EDA Programme

- SPC’s Climate Change and Environmental Sustainability Division will oversee project implementation.
- SPC will be the sole Executing Entity, with various partners contributing to operational management.

### EDA Programme Coordination Unit

- A dedicated ECU will be hosted within the SPC regional office in FSM.
- The ECU will manage day-to-day operations and coordinate programme activities.

### EDA Programme Board

- The EPB will provide overall guidance and direction to the Programme.
- Responsibilities include tracking progress, approving reports, and addressing grievances.

### EDA Facility Mechanism

- The grant facility will have a separate implementation structure for reviewing and approving sub-grant proposals.
- The ECU will be responsible for initiating proposal submissions and conducting organizational audits.

### Flow of Funds

- Funds will flow from the GCF to SPC’s CCES Division and then to the ECU.
- Disbursement will follow SPC’s Finance Regulations and Policies.

### Facilitating Agents

- FAs will be selected to provide sub-grant development and implementation support.
- They will conduct site visits and support reporting and monitoring processes.
