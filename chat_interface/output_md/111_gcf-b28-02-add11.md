# Green Climate Fund Funding Proposal

## Potential for Knowledge Sharing and Learning

GGEF is structured to de-risk investors and projects by providing patient equity capital for green infrastructure projects. A complementary TA facility addresses market failures, allowing this approach to be replicated in India and across Asia. Successful fundraising and operationalization will enable further climate-focused funds and scaling of green infrastructure in India.

### Knowledge Sharing and Learning

The program focuses on expertise transfer and capacity building through equity and grant components. It encourages low-carbon technologies and skills development, creating a critical mass of expertise. This will catalyze investments and provide confidence to debt providers, leading to growth in green infrastructure sectors.

### Contribution to Regulatory Framework and Policies

GGEF supports India's climate objectives by encouraging policy dialogue to identify gaps and design regulatory frameworks. This is supported by Component II of the program.

### Contribution to Enabling Environment

The program provides finance for green infrastructure projects, addressing policy, knowledge, and capacity barriers to low-carbon technologies in India.

## Sustainable Development

GGEF aligns with the following UN SDGs:

- Climate action (SDG 13)
- Affordable clean energy (SDG 7)
- Clean water and sanitation (SDG 6)
- Sustainable cities and communities (SDG 11)
- Industry, innovation, and infrastructure (SDG 9)
- Responsible consumption and production (SDG 12)

Secondary contributions include Gender Equality (SDG 5) and Decent work and economic growth (SDG 8). Metrics for impact will be routinely measured.

### Sustainable Goals and Impact

- **Affordable and Clean Energy**: Reduces energy import bills, lowers power costs, and contributes to local development.
- **Waste Treatment**: Benefits 40+ million people annually.
- **Water Treatment**: Benefits 4.5+ million people/day.
- **Job Creation**: Generates ~20,000+ new jobs, with a focus on gender diversity.

## Needs of Recipient

### India's Vulnerabilities

India faces increased temperatures and extreme weather, affecting resources and livelihoods. Vulnerabilities include poverty, unemployment, and low electricity consumption. Climate investments are urgently needed.

### Investment Needs

India requires USD 2.5 trillion to meet climate objectives. Public budgets are insufficient, and private sector mobilization is crucial. GGEF provides timely equity financing, de-risking investments for local and international investors.

### Project Responses

- **Country Needs**: Provides capital for critical green infrastructure, complementing existing financing.
- **Population**: Gender-inclusive planning, access to clean energy, improved mobility, and waste management.
- **Project Developers**: Encourages growth in asset development, equipment supply, and service provision.

## Country Ownership

### Climate Change Policy

India's policy is articulated through the National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC) and Nationally Determined Commitments (INDCs). GGEF aligns with these policies, contributing to energy efficiency, waste management, and clean energy promotion.

### Investment Strategy

GGEF invests in green infrastructure, focusing on e-mobility, resource efficiency, energy services, and renewable energy. It aligns with India's NDCs, mobilizing co-financing and promoting low-emission public transport.

## Efficiency and Effectiveness

### Funding Multiplier Effect

GGEF's investments attract ~USD 890 million of co-investment and ~USD 2.9 billion of debt, improving efficiency. It aims to avoid ~452 million tons of CO2 emissions, with an estimated cost of USD 10.39 per tCO2e.

### GCF Mobilization Mechanism

GCF commits up to USD 132.5 million, mobilizing additional capital at a 1:3 ratio. The fund targets a total corpus of USD 940 million, with commitments from various investors.

### Target Sectors

1. **Renewable Energy Generation**: Utility and C&I RE with significant co-investment potential.
2. **E-Mobility**: Promotes low-emission transport with co-investment potential.
3. **Energy Services**: Energy efficiency and independent service providers with high IRRs.
4. **Resource Efficiency**: Waste and water management with viable business potential.

### Financial Structure

GGEF uses a SEBI registered AIF structure, allowing efficient investment across portfolios. It aims to attract ~USD 890 million of co-investment and ~USD 2,918 million of debt, enhancing social infrastructure and employment opportunities.

### Expected Financial Returns

GCF's investment catalyzes investor interest, with a target of three times the commitment from other investors. A scaled fund allows for catalytic investments in essential sectors for climate change mitigation.

# GCF POLICIES AND STANDARDS

## G.1. Environmental and Social Risk Assessment

The Executing Entity has adopted a comprehensive Environmental, Social and Governance Policy and established an Environmental, Social and Governance Management System (ESGMS) for the Green Growth Equity Fund. This system aligns with IFC Performance Standards and GCF policies, including the Environmental and Social Policy, Information Disclosure Policy, and Indigenous People Policy.

The ESGMS ensures that projects financed by the Programme are designed and implemented sustainably. It includes:

- **ESG Integration in Investment Process**
  - **Screening of Project Eligibility**: Projects are screened against the GGEF ESGMS exclusion list and categorized as high, medium, or low risk.
  - **Environmental and Social Governance Due Diligence (ESGDD)**: Assessment of potential ESG risks and development of an Environmental and Social Action Plan (ESAP).
  - **Incorporating ESG into Legal Agreements**: ESAP is included in shareholder agreements, legally binding investee companies to implement it.
  - **Post-Investment Monitoring**: Regular monitoring of ESG performance by the Programme ESG Head Officer.

The Programme will invest in projects across all risk categories (A, B, C), with a focus on stakeholder engagement and compliance with national and international standards.

## G.2. Gender Assessment and Action Plan

A gender assessment identified vulnerabilities such as non-inclusive infrastructure planning and gender gaps in employment. The Executing Entity commits to mainstreaming gender considerations at both the Fund and project levels.

Actions include:

- **Fund Level**: Adoption of a Gender Policy, updates to HR guidelines, and allocation of resources for implementing the Gender Action Plan.
- **Investee Level**: Institutionalizing gender equality, incorporating gender-inclusive approaches in stakeholder consultations, and community-based initiatives.

The Gender Action Plan is included in Annex 8 and aligns with GCF Gender Policy.

- Monthly reporting and periodic board meetings.
- Site visits and compliance checks.
- Quarterly and annual reporting to GGEF investors, including audited statements and impact reports.

## G.4. Disclosure of Funding Proposal

The Information Disclosure Policy favors transparency, with confidential information redacted. The accredited entity provides both full and redacted copies of the funding proposal.

# GCF Gender Policy Requirements

The GCF Board adopted their Gender Policy and Action Plan in March 2015 and an update to this policy was issued in February 2019 followed by another update to their Gender Action Plan in November 2019 for the years 2020-2023. The updates to the Policy and Action Plan of GCF respond to a fast-changing landscape related to gender in the context of climate change and emphasize the importance of reporting and disclosure of gender-related impacts created by its downstream partners (Accredited entities (AE) and approved projects). Its rationale is rooted in GCF’s mandate to encourage a paradigm shift towards low-emission and climate-resilient development pathways. The updated policy emphasizes gender responsiveness rather than gender sensitivity. Being gender-responsive means that instead of only identifying gender issues or ensuring a “do no harm” approach, a process will substantially help to overcome historical gender biases. Gender responsive approaches are essential to this policy, including decisions and actions by women and men.

GCF’s Gender Policy aims to:

- Achieve greater, more effective, sustainable, and equitable climate change results, outcomes and impacts, through the adoption of a gender sensitive approach;
- Build resilience to climate change equally for men and women, as well as to ensure that men and women equally contribute to and benefit from activities supported by the Fund;
- Address or mitigate risks for women and men associated with adaptation and mitigation activities financed by the Fund; and
- Reduce the gender gap of social, economic and environmental vulnerabilities exacerbated by climate change.

For the purposes of this Gender Equality Policy, GCF requires that women and men, including marginalized and vulnerable groups, be provided with an equal and equitable opportunity to be fully and effectively engaged in meaningful consultations and decision-making throughout the project cycle.

GCF’s Gender Policy is based on the following six fundamental principles.

Source: Green Climate Fund Proposal Toolkit 2017

According to GCF, “Gender assessment refers to methods used to understand relationships between men and women, their access to resources, their activities, and the constraints they face relative to each other. A comprehensive gender assessment entails the examination of the different roles, rights, needs, and opportunities of women and men, boys and girls in a given project/programme context. Gender analysis needs to take place early in the planning process so that an understanding of gender roles and power relations is built into the project.”

The main objective of the Gender Assessment exercise is to screen the gender aspects of the Investments and to make recommendations to better integrate gender responsive aspects within the Action Plan.
