## Women and Agriculture

### Female Participation in the Labour Market
- Female participation: 51.7%
- Male participation: 67.7% (Human Development Report, 2019).

### Women in Agriculture
- Women: 78% of economically active in agriculture.
- Men: 57% in agriculture.
- Women responsible for 40% of total agricultural production.
- Women face limited control over resources like land, inputs, credit, and technology.
- Horticultural production by women often sustains households.
- Gendered division of labor in crop production.
- Women primarily grow lowland rice; men grow sorghum, millet, maize, and groundnuts.
- Women manage small ruminants and poultry; men manage cattle.
- Gender roles in fisheries: men fish, women process and trade.
- Gender division is changing with women in groundnut production and men in rice production.
- Land controlled by men; women have limited access.
- Conflicts arise when men reclaim land after women invest in it.
- Economic value increase in women-dominant commodities attracts men.

## Education
- Young women have a higher illiteracy rate (20-30%).
- 30.7% of adult women have secondary education compared to 43.6% of men.
- Limited access to climate information.

## Employment
- Women: 50% of total labor force, 70% of unskilled laborers.
- Female employment in agriculture: 42%; male: 22%.
- Vulnerable employment: 84.6% women, 71% men.
- Wage and salary workers: 14% women, 29% men.

## Energy
- Urban electricity access: 76%; rural: 35.4%.
- Rural households rely on fuel wood.

## Policy Framework
- Government actions for gender and women since 1980.
- Established Women’s Bureau, Ministry of Women’s Affairs, and Federation of Gambian Women.
- Ratified CEDAW in 1992.
- Implemented National Women’s Policy and Gender and Women Empowerment Policy.
- Enacted laws like Sexual Offenses Act 2013, Domestic Violence Act 2013, and Women’s Amendment Act 2012.
- Land ownership traditionally favors men despite legal rights for women.

- Gender analyses informed by stakeholder consultations.
- Focus on understanding roles, interests, and priorities of women and men.

## Key Barriers
- Gendered division of labor limits women's economic activities.

- Lack of awareness of financial opportunities.
- Limited access to climate information.
- Lower educational levels for women.
- Financial sector biases against women.
- Gender-based violence prevalent.

## Recommendations
- Address lack of climate information.
- Promote bioenergy and compost production.
- Improve access to drinking water.
- Support women's involvement in agriculture.
- Ensure gender mainstreaming in project activities.

## Targeting of Women and Youth
- Set targets for women and youth participation in projects.
- Address constraints related to legal and social norms.
- Focus on women-led MSMEs and farmer organizations.

## Conclusion
- IFAD recognized for addressing gender inequalities.
- Program builds on experience to promote gender equality and women's empowerment.
