## Natural Hazard

The natural hazards in the Maldives include storm surge, swell wave, heavy rainfall, drought, and tsunami. These hazards are discussed in terms of severity, frequency, and regional characteristics.

### a) Storm Surge

Few cyclones occur near the Maldives. The southern parts are less affected due to their proximity to the equator. However, SST rise can increase the strength of cyclones, affecting storm surges.

### b) Swell Wave

Flooding from swell waves occurs two to three times a year. The 2007 event caused flooding in 68 islands. SLR and SST rise significantly impact swell waves.

### c) Heavy Rainfall

Flooding from heavy rain is frequent but not severe. Southern regions experience higher precipitation, increasing flood risk.

### d) Drought

No significant drought damage has been reported, but climate change may increase drought frequency.

### e) Tsunami

Tsunamis have been observed 80 times since 1816, with significant damage from the 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami. The frequency is rare, and climate change has little impact.

## Summary of Climate Projection and Natural Hazard

### a) Climate Projection

Major projections include precipitation increase, SLR, and SST rise. SLR is a major concern due to its impact on other hazards.

### b) Natural Hazard

Flooding and drought are less severe, while storm surges and swell waves cause significant damage and are sensitive to climate change.

### c) Focused Region

Southern regions are more affected by swell waves. Islands in the south should be prioritized.

## Vulnerability to Related Sectors

### a) Tourism

Tourism is affected by beach erosion and coral bleaching. Climate change impacts include increased temperatures and extreme events.

### b) Fisheries

Fisheries depend on coral reefs, which are affected by SST and ocean pH changes.

### c) Agriculture and Food Security

Agriculture faces challenges due to limited land and water. Climate change exacerbates these issues.

### d) Human Health

Health impacts from climate change include heat stress and increased disease. Lack of data hinders analysis.

### e) Water Resources

Freshwater resources are impacted by temperature rise, SLR, and rainfall changes. Groundwater and rainwater availability are key concerns.

### f) Coral Reef Biodiversity

Coral reefs are sensitive to temperature rise and SLR. Coral bleaching is expected to increase.

## Coastal Vulnerability

### a) Natural Vulnerability

The Maldives is vulnerable to high waves, storm surges, and flooding. SLR will increase these hazards.

### b) Human-induced Vulnerability

Coastal development and lack of planning increase vulnerability. Information sharing and community actions are needed.

## Selection of Target Atolls of the Project

### Item 1: Climate Impact and Vulnerability

Laamu Atoll has the highest climate impact and vulnerability.

### Item 2: Socio-economic Status

Addu Atoll is important for socio-economic activity, followed by Laamu Atoll.

### Item 3: Project Implementation

Laamu and Addu Atolls are selected for project implementation. Target islands include Fonadhoo, Maamendhoo, Meedhoo, Gan, and Ishdhoo.
