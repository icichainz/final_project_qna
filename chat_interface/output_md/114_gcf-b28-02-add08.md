- **Decreasing Trends**: Significant decrease in annual total precipitation amount. Mixed trends in precipitation intensity and consecutive dry days.

- **SST Correlation**: Changes in temperature and precipitation indices are correlated with SST increases in the western Indian Ocean.

# B.3. Project/programme description

## Project development objective and contribution to climate change adaptation

The project aims to strengthen adaptive capacity and climate resilience in Comoros, Madagascar, Mauritius, and Seychelles through:

1. Institutional strengthening and reforms, capacity building, and public-private engagement.
2. Improvement and modernization of hydro-meteorological systems and services.
3. Enhancing the use of climate services and establishing MH-IBF-EWS.

These efforts will support climate change adaptation and disaster risk reduction, aiding the implementation of NDCs, national adaptation plans, and Sendai targets.

## Scope of the project and description

The Hydromet project will scale up climate services in the target countries by:

- Improving weather and climate monitoring techniques.
- Enhancing the production and dissemination of climate services.
- Fostering collaboration for hazard monitoring and risk management.
- Improving capabilities in implementing a people-centered MH-IBF-EWS.

### Component 1: Capacity building, institutional development, regional cooperation, and public-private engagement

This component will establish a regional approach for weather and climate monitoring and risk reduction, including:

- Adoption of a Regional Climate Services Framework (RFCS).
- Establishment of an RCC-Network under IOC.
- Strengthening partnerships between NMHSs and DRM institutions.

### Component 2: High-quality climate-related data, improved multi-hazard impact-based forecasts and EWS (MH-IBF-EWS), and climate risk assessments

This component focuses on:

- Modernizing observation and monitoring networks.
- Enhancing technical capacities of NMHS staff.
- Developing hazard and vulnerability maps.
- Building MH-IBF-EWS capabilities.

The project will leverage regional collaboration and national institutional development to ensure effective climate services and risk management.

# D.3. Sustainable Development

The project fully supports the implementation of SDGs in the four countries, particularly SDG 2, SDG 3, and SDG 13. It also supports the operationalization of the Sendai Framework targets for Disaster Risk Reduction (SF-DRR).

## 1. Environmental Co-benefits

The project will reduce vulnerability to climate change, decreasing pressure on the natural environment. Agrometeorological advisories and weather warnings will enable climate-smart decision-making among farmers and fishermen, reducing losses and damages from climate variability and extreme events.

## 2. Social Co-benefits Including Health Impacts

The project will improve well-being, health, and save lives by strengthening EWS and other climate services. It will provide information on flood risks and improve early warnings, enabling communities to protect their lives and assets.

## 3. Economic Co-benefits

Significant economic gains are expected, with annual average benefits linked to EWS assessed at around USD 14 million for the SWIO region. The NPV of the project is estimated at USD 230 million at a 5% discounting rate.

## 4. Gender-sensitive Development Impacts

The project includes a Gender Assessment and Action Plan to operationalize gender-responsive adaptation actions. It will promote gender balance through capacity-building activities and establish gender-aware policy frameworks.

# D.4. Needs of Recipient

The SWIO region is a 'climate hotspot' with rising climate-related hazards. The four islands are vulnerable due to their reliance on agriculture, fisheries, and tourism. There is a lack of location-specific climate information and limited technical capacity within NMHSs.

Significant investments are required to equip or upgrade observation networks and foster the production of climate services. The project will support the harmonization of infrastructure, tools, and processes at the regional level.

# D.5. Country Ownership

The project responds to a call for support to NMHSs in the Indian Ocean region. It involves collaboration with DRM institutions and international advisors. The project supports the operationalization of the Sendai Framework and the implementation of SDGs.

The IOC Member States have developed a joint adaptation strategy to strengthen regional cooperation. The project will contribute to achieving these goals by enhancing technical capacities and regional cooperation.

The project aligns with the NDCs of each target country, supporting priority actions in sectors like agriculture, water, and disaster risk reduction.

# D.6. Efficiency and Effectiveness

The Hydromet project is the most appropriate investment for climate change adaptation objectives. Without GCF funding, the project would lose major activities and benefits. The regional approach is key to underpin improvements in CP-CS production and dissemination.

The improved agricultural profits attributable to the project have been evaluated to be significant. Without the project, NMHSs would struggle to produce tailored weather forecasts and early warnings.

The financing structure is based on investment grants and co-financing, with limited impact on public debt. The project will enable sustainable, long-term business models for hydrometeorological systems.

The budget funding analysis shows that the impact of the project will be manageable for the four countries, with potential for commercial revenue development to complement public funding.

# IV. Assessment of Consistency with GCF Safeguards and Policies

## 4.1 Environmental and Social Safeguards

The proposed project focuses on capacity-building and institutional strengthening in four target countries to improve climate services. It includes providing new hydrometeorological equipment and tools for monitoring and forecasting. The project is classified as category B under the GCF risk categorization system, indicating minimal environmental and social risks. An environmental and social management framework has been developed to manage these risks.

Environmental co-benefits include reduced pressure on wood and water extraction. Social co-benefits include improved health and nutrition due to enhanced agricultural productivity and minimized flooding risks.

The project involves installing new meteorological equipment, which may cause temporary noise, waste, and dust emissions. Occupational health and safety hazards will be mitigated by following good practices. No additional land acquisition or household relocation is expected.

The AE will oversee implementation and monitoring of safeguards. Consultations with stakeholders, including government departments and communities, have been conducted. A grievance redress mechanism will be established.

## 4.2 Gender Policy

The AE provided a gender assessment and action plan, complying with GCF Gender Policy. The assessment highlights gender disparities in climate-induced disasters and the need for gender-responsive measures. It identifies gender-based violence as a significant issue and outlines actions to reduce gender-related vulnerabilities.

The gender action plan includes promoting gender-informed capacity development, increasing gender parity in staffing, and ensuring gender-responsiveness in climate services. Separate consultations with women and girls will be held to ensure their participation.

## 4.3 Risks

### 4.3.1 Overall Programme Assessment (Medium Risk)

The project requests USD 52 million for building resilience through improved meteorological services. Co-financing will be provided by AFD and the EU, with in-kind contributions from the governments of Comoros, Madagascar, Mauritius, and Seychelles.

### 4.3.2 Accredited Entity/Executing Entity Capability to Execute the Current Programme (Medium Risk)

AFD is a public international financial institution with a strong track record. The IOC will be the EE, with experience in managing climate-related projects.

### 4.3.4 Compliance Risk (Medium Risk)

### 4.3.5 GCF Portfolio Concentration Risk (Low Risk)

### 4.3.6 Recommendation

The Board is advised to consider these factors in its decision.

## 4.4 Fiduciary

## Gender Mainstreaming, Sectoral Issues & Hydro-Meteorological Information Services

The World Economic Forum (WEF)’s report, *The Global Risks Report 2020*, identifies the top five risk factors facing the world population as environmental: extreme weather, climate action failure, natural disasters, biodiversity loss, and human-made environmental disasters. These risks are compounded by factors such as economic status and lack of political access, affecting health, education, and livelihood indicators.

### Vulnerabilities and Gender Mainstreaming

Vulnerabilities impair a social group's ability to cope with and adapt to external shocks, including climate change and natural disasters. Addressing these vulnerabilities is crucial for the IOC Hydromet project, as they hinder coping capacities against climate risks. Gender mainstreaming through the Gender and Development (GAD) approach recognizes the role of marginalized groups, allowing them to contribute traditional knowledge to coping strategies.

### The Gender-Climate Change-EWS/CS Nexus

The 25th Conference of the Parties (COP) highlighted the need for gender-responsive climate change adaptation. Despite recognition, operationalization of gender-responsive measures remains scarce. Resource mobilization and commitments by governments and local actors are crucial for grassroots gender-responsive climate action.

### Main Gender Risks and Challenges for IOC Hydromet

| **Gender-Differentiated Impacts** | The project faces gender-specific differences that create vulnerability to climate change, limiting adaptation capacities and progress towards gender equality. |
|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Gendered Access to Adaptation Solutions** | Adaptation solutions face gendered impediments, with women often lacking access to public spaces and information channels. |
| **Sidelining Local Adaptation Measures** | Local adaptation methods and information channels should be integrated into broader EWS, CS, and hydromet services. |
| **Gender-Based Violence (GBV)** | GBV is exacerbated during climate impacts and disasters, with high rates in the Indian Ocean island nations. |
| **Gendered Limitations in Decision-Making** | Decision-making institutions are skewed towards men, limiting responses to vulnerable groups' needs. |

### Mainstreaming Gender within Climate Information Services

The IOC countries are vulnerable to economic and environmental stressors. Gender-responsive CS can increase adaptability and resilience, strengthening national meteorological and hydrological institutions. Mainstreaming gender involves considering factors like division of labor, poverty levels, human development factors, access to resources, and decision-making influence.

### Tailoring Products towards End Users in Climate-Vulnerable Sectors

The IOC countries face consequences of disasters and limited meteorological capacities. Reports highlight the importance of hydromet and CS, emphasizing the need for robust EWS. CS products like impact forecasting and nowcasting address differential exposure patterns among communities.

- **Comoros**: Lost 60-80% of staple crops due to Cyclone Kenneth.
- **Madagascar**: Ranked 7th most affected by climate events in 2017.
- **Mauritius**: Experiences US$ 110 million in losses annually from natural disasters.
- **Seychelles**: Cyclone Felleng caused US$ 8.4 million in losses.

The CGIAR report emphasizes the importance of coping mechanisms, resources, and information in determining exposure to climate impacts.
