In Liberia, UNDP has led environmental protection and climate change responses through policy formulation, strategic planning, law making, coordination, and information sharing. # B.5. Justification for GCF Funding Request

GCF grants are critical for this proposed project given Liberia's limited economic resources and numerous development priorities. Liberia is classified as a Least Developed Country with a low GDP per capita and a challenging economic situation exacerbated by the Ebola epidemic and the COVID-19 pandemic.

The project includes three outputs and twelve activities, none of which result in direct and quantifiable earnings or savings. The activities result in non-attributable savings that benefit vulnerable communities. A financial modeling-based analysis has not been conducted, and a Financial Internal Rate of Return (FIRR) has not been calculated.

Considering the GCF’s minimum concession policy, the project’s activities have been analyzed from a macro-economic and government perspective. The GCF grant is recommended as the only feasible financial instrument to fund the project activities due to the nature of the benefits, vulnerability of beneficiaries, and co-financing limitations.

# B.6. Exit Strategy and Sustainability

The project has been designed with a focus on an effective exit strategy to ensure long-term sustainability. Project interventions incorporate sustainability considerations with minimal maintenance required beyond the project period. The Ministry of Mines and Energy (MME) will finance and undertake operations and maintenance (O&M) after project completion.

The sustainability of project interventions will be underpinned by an integrated approach to coastal zone management, emphasizing capacity-building, cross-sectoral coordination, and ongoing learning. Community-led co-management agreements for mangrove ecosystems will be developed with community involvement to ensure sustainability.

A participatory approach will be used to build community resilience to climate change and generate gender-responsive, sustainable livelihoods. The project will promote sustainable livelihoods as an alternative to timber extraction from mangroves, contributing to sustained gender equality and reduced gender-based vulnerability.

All project activities have been designed to incorporate sustainability considerations, including the potential for scaling up and continuation of best practice. Information generated will be shared widely to scale up project interventions across all coastal counties in Liberia.

# Component-by-component analysis

## Output 1: Protection of coastal communities and infrastructure at West Point against erosion

- **Total cost:** USD 18.1 million
- **GCF cost:** USD 13.5 million (75%)

GCF grant funding will be used to construct a revetment structure to protect the vulnerable community at West Point. This structure is expected to prevent sediments and sand from being washed away. Appropriate drainage will be included to prevent localized flooding.

## Output 2: Institutional capacity-building and policy support for ICZM

- **Total cost:** USD 4.1 million
- **GCF cost:** USD 1.6 million (40%)

The project will develop a national-scale vulnerability map and design a national risk-informed Integrated Coastal Zone Management Plan. It will support cross-sectoral coordination among government institutions for climate-resilient coast management.

## Output 3: Protecting mangroves and strengthening gender- and climate-sensitive livelihoods

- **Total cost:** USD 2.2 million
- **GCF cost:** USD 5.7 million (59%)

Measures will be implemented to reduce anthropogenic pressure on mangrove ecosystems in the Mesurado Wetland, improving their health and resilience to climate change.

## Output 4: Project management

- **Total cost:** USD 1.2 million
- **GCF cost:** USD 0.8 million (75%)

Project management costs will be financed by GCF and UNDP, complying with the GCF Policy on Fees for Accredited Entities.

# Environmental and Social Safeguards

## Natural Habitat

Direct impacts of the proposed revetment wall to the Mesurado Wetland ecosystem are not expected to be significant. However, impacts on specific areas of the coast could be locally significant if interventions are not designed according to prevailing dynamic processes. Further research is recommended to understand potential indirect impacts. Biological surveys must be conducted prior to construction, and areas of high biological density or diversity must be avoided. The ESMP will include habitat monitoring using established scientific methodologies.

## Indigenous Peoples Policy

Liberia has a multi-ethnic population, and no groups in Monrovia are identified as "indigenous." The project should ensure inclusivity of all groups. The ESMMM will include provisions for free, prior informed consent (FPIC) if indigenous peoples are encountered. Culturally appropriate consultations have been conducted and will continue during the ESIA and project implementation.

## Cultural Heritage

There are historically important sites in the project area, but none will be affected by the project. The ESMMM includes provisions to identify and protect cultural heritage sites and refers to the Chance Find Procedure for archaeological discoveries.

## Implementation Arrangements

The Environmental Protection Agency will be the Executing Entity (EE) and will establish a Project Management Unit (PMU) with specialists in various fields. The PMU will supervise the ESMP implementation. Service providers and contractors will ensure day-to-day compliance.

## Stakeholder Engagement and Grievance Redress Mechanism

The ESAR includes a stakeholder engagement plan. A detailed plan will be prepared after the ESIA. The project includes a mechanism for grievance redress, and the GCF independent Redress Mechanism will be available.

# Gender Policy

## Gender Assessment

Liberia has a strong legal framework for gender equality, but challenges remain, such as limited access to land and financial services for women. The gender action plan addresses these barriers and includes measures to ensure women's participation and empowerment.

## Gender Action Plan

The AE will hire a gender expert and ensure women's participation in project management. The project will provide training and employment opportunities for women and ensure equal access to resources and decision-making.

# Risks

## Overall Programme Assessment (Medium Risk)

GCF is requested to provide a grant for climate change adaptation in Monrovia. The project will construct a rock revetment and diversify livelihoods. The AE supports a full grant request as all income will support project sustainability.

## AE/EE Capability to Execute (Low Risk)

UNDP is the AE with a strong track record. The Environmental Protection Agency of Liberia is the EE. The AE will use direct cash transfer methods to minimize corruption risk.

## Project-Specific Risks (Medium Risk)

The project design must adhere to sound engineering practices. The CSC will manage income from cookstoves and cold storage facilities. The AE should ensure optimal design for solar power and diesel back-up generators.

## Compliance Risk Rating (Medium)

UNDP confirms that no project activities will involve entities on UNSC sanctions lists. The project will follow UNDP's risk-based due diligence requirements.

# Fiduciary

The UNDP will serve as the AE, and the Environmental Protection Agency of Liberia will be the EE. The EE is accountable for managing the project and achieving outcomes. Funds for high-risk activities will be channeled directly to service providers by UNDP.

# Results Monitoring and Reporting

The project aims to achieve climate change adaptation in Monrovia through coastal protection, effective management, and resilient livelihoods.
