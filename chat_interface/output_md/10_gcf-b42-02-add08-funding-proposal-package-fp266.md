## Responsibilities and Participation

The project aims to enhance women's participation in decision-making processes, ensuring they have access to decision-making spaces and the ability to influence and lead. This will be achieved through training for both women and men on women's leadership and the importance of their participation.

## Project Monitoring

Monitoring and evaluation methods are participatory, engaging women in the process. Gender-response data collection will measure progress, assess gender issues, and make necessary adjustments. Gender indicators will highlight differences and inequalities, informing policy-making to address gender gaps. The project will generate a gender-sensitive database to monitor gender advancement.

### Gender-Sensitive Indicators

For indicators to be gender-sensitive, they must:
- Be disaggregated by sex.
- Reflect gender issues.
- Capture the diversity of women and men.
- Consider stereotypes and socio-economic and cultural factors.

Quantitative methods will provide data on issues like labor market participation and wage rates, while qualitative methods will capture experiences and opinions. Participatory methods like focus group discussions will be used for qualitative indicators.

## Gender Considerations in Project Design

### Approach and Problem Analysis

The project addresses 'time poverty' among women in Mauritania, who perform unpaid household and care duties. By incorporating climate-smart agricultural practices, women can modify and increase their use of time. The project evaluates gender barriers and considers gender-specific concerns in agriculture and natural resource use.

### Project-Related Gender Challenges

| Main Gender Challenges | Causes | Immediate Effects | Effects |
|------------------------|--------|-------------------|---------|
| Limited property rights and access to land ownership | Women's access to land is limited, particularly in rural areas. | Land degradation and compromised agricultural yield | Reduced productivity and income from natural resources |
| Gender-blind policies and non-inclusive norms | Limited representation of women in decision-making processes. | Limited entitlements to assets outside household work | Limited access to resources reduces coping capacity |
| Gender gaps in education and skills | Women's limited literacy rates exclude them from awareness campaigns. | Limited adaptive capacity | Reduced resilience to climate change |
| Access to finance and labor force participation | Restricted participation in economic activities. | Women have limited time for income-generating activities | Women are economically disadvantaged |

### Gender Mainstreaming in Project Design

Gender mainstreaming ensures visibility and support for both women and men, aiming for gender equality. The project considers gender-specific challenges in designing ecosystem-based adaptation approaches. It avoids exposing women to risks of sexual exploitation and harassment, ensuring their empowerment while engaging male community members.

### Project Expected Benefits Promoting Gender Equality

The project will provide tangible benefits for women, promoting a shift in gender dynamics. By involving women in decision-making processes, building their capacity, and ensuring access to resources, their resilience to climate change impacts will be strengthened. The project targets 30%-50% of women in alternative livelihoods activities, enhancing their economic empowerment.

## Project Management

1. **Ensure Gender Balance in PMU**: Aim for at least 50% women in project roles.
2. **Recruit a Gender Officer**: Support the implementation of the Gender Action Plan and oversee grievances related to gender-based violence.
3. **Gender Training for PMU**: Provide training on SEAH, GBV, and strategies for equitable access to project benefits.
4. **Facilitate Women's Participation in MEL Activities**: Ensure at least 40% women participation.
5. **Enable Women's Active Participation**: Ensure women's engagement in decision-making processes affecting their lands and resources.

### Impact Statement

Using an ecosystem-based adaptation approach will strengthen the resilience of communities and ecosystems to climate change impacts. The project will benefit 85,229 people directly and 145,008 indirectly, ensuring gender-responsive outcomes and building capacity for government institutions to acknowledge gender differentials in climate risk response.
