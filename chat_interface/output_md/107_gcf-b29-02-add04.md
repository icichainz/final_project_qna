# Green Climate Fund Funding Proposal V.2.0

## Lack of Local FI Capacity and Market Data

Local financial institutions (FIs) lack the experience and capacity to finance DRE projects. This is due to the nascent market and limited data. Benchmarking studies are needed to boost investor confidence.

## Enabling Environment

Policies often favor public investments, neglecting private sector needs. Mini-grid investments face licensing and tariff challenges. Net metering regulations are inconsistent, affecting C&I investments. Regulatory inefficiencies add risks.

## Framework Objectives

The Framework aims to overcome financing and policy barriers to accelerate DRE deployment, increasing electricity access and supporting clean energy transitions.

## Key Barriers and Interventions

| Type of Barrier | Barrier | Area of Intervention | Programme Activities |
|-----------------|---------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Financial | Limited access to finance, high capital cost, tenor mismatch | Mobilize and de-risk commercial investment in SHS, mini-grids, C&I | Provide concessional guarantees, subordinated debt, structured facilities, and tenor extension guarantees |
| Covid-19 Crisis | Scarce affordable capital | Deploy COVID-19 recovery funding | Provide concessional debt through recovery platforms |
| Lack of Capacity | Lack of local FI capacity | Provide TA support to LFIs | Capacity building and structuring innovative financing |
| Regulatory | Unclear licensing, high VAT, prohibitive regulations | Support enabling policies | TA to develop policies and frameworks |
| Gender Gaps | Inequalities in DRE sector | Address gender gaps | Conduct gender assessments and develop inclusive strategies |

## LEAF Outcomes

- Establish 386MW generating capacity.
- Reduce emissions by 28.8 million tCO2.
- Increase electricity access for 5.9 million people.
- Improve capacity of 20 local FIs.
- Develop stronger DRE policies.

## Theory of Change

The Framework will facilitate private sector access to financing, build local capacity, and support policy development to enable DRE growth. Key solutions include de-risking financing instruments and capacity building.

## Project/Programme Description

### Definition

The LEAF Framework aims to accelerate clean electricity access and reduce GHG emissions by de-risking investments in DRE businesses. It seeks US$ 160 million from GCF for credit enhancement instruments and technical assistance.

### Focus Countries

The Framework targets Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, Tunisia, Ethiopia, and Guinea, chosen for their market potential and alignment with energy transition programs.

### Market Potential

Studies confirm a market potential of US$ 7.2 billion in the six countries. The Framework will enhance local markets by de-risking DRE investments and providing technical assistance.

## Framework Structure

### Component 1: De-risking

- **Output 1.1**: Deploy US$ 575 million additional capital for DRE businesses.
  - Provide concessional guarantees and subordinated debt.
  - Support receivables-backed financing structures.
  - Finance portfolios of DRE companies through aggregators.
  - Offer tenor extension guarantees.

- **Output 1.2**: Mobilize US$ 100 million local currency debt.
  - Provide guarantees and subordinated debt alongside local banks.

### Component 2: Covid Recovery Support

- **Output 2.1**: Deploy US$ 70 million for COVID-19 recovery.
  - Provide concessional debt through recovery platforms.

### Component 3: Technical Assistance

- **Output 3.1**: Increase capacity of 20 FIs to finance DRE solutions.
  - Support local lenders and intermediaries.
  - Structure innovative financing facilities.

- **Output 3.2**: Support governments in creating enabling policies.
  - Develop regulatory frameworks, leverage technology, and promote social inclusion.

- **Output 3.3**: Conduct country-level adaptation assessments.

AfDB will mobilize donor funds for technical assistance, enhancing local capacity and supporting policy development.

# Gender and Energy in Ethiopia

## Energy Access

Ethiopia has a significant urban-rural divide in energy access. 45% of the population is connected to energy sources, with 92% in urban areas and only 32.7% in rural areas.

- 65% of the population lives within 1.5 miles of medium-voltage transmission lines, but only about one-third of households, one-quarter of primary schools, and one-third of health clinics have grid electricity.
- Ethiopia aims for universal electricity access by 2025, including 100% access for schools and health centers.

The Alternative Energy Directorate is working to improve access to alternative energy sources. Efforts include mainstreaming gender through a National Gender Action Plan for the Energy Sector.

## Impact on Women

The availability of energy affects the quality of life for both men and women, with women often responsible for collecting firewood. Electrification can facilitate labor participation and economic productivity at home.

- Women make up 20% of the DRE workforce, lower than the African average of 30%.
- Inclusive HR policies are needed to support gender equality in the energy sector.

## Key Data on Gender Equality

- Women constitute 49.74% of the population.
- 27% of women aged 15-49 have never been married, while 58% are married.
- Female-headed households were 25.4% as of 2016.
- In 2019, 83.5% of the population suffered from multidimensional poverty, with an estimated 51% being women and girls.

# Political Participation

Ethiopia has made significant gains in gender equality and women's empowerment. Women occupy 50% of the cabinet and 38.8% of the House of Peoples’ Representatives.

## Education

The government has progressed in primary education provision. The Net Enrollment Rate for primary education increased from 77.5% in 2005/06 to 85.4% in 2011/12.

- Secondary education enrollment rates are lower, with gender roles and cultural attitudes affecting girls' education.

# Health

The National Health Account shows improvement in health financing. The total fertility rate decreased from 5.4 children per woman in 2005 to 4.1 in 2014.

- Maternal Mortality Ratio decreased from 673 deaths per 100,000 births in 2000-2005 to 420 in 2013.

# Division of Labour and Economic Participation

According to the 2016 Labor Force Survey, 76% of the economically active population was employed. Male employment is higher than female employment.

- Agriculture is a major sector, with women more likely to be unpaid workers.
- Only 36% of women have decision-making power over their cash earnings.

# Entrepreneurship

Government and non-governmental programs are promoting women's entrepreneurship. Women face challenges such as less access to finance and education.

# Summary

Only 32% of rural households have electricity access. LEAF aims to increase access to clean energy, benefiting rural communities and women.

# Gender and Energy in Guinea

## Energy Potential

Guinea's hydroelectric potential is estimated at 6000 MW, with less than 3% exploited. The private sector's share of energy production increased from 16% in 2000 to 31% in 2016.

- The access rate to electricity is 44%, with 20% in rural areas and 87% in urban areas.

## Gender Equality

Guinea's HDI value for 2019 is 0.477, placing it in the low human development category. The gender index is 0.439, indicating significant disparities.

- Female-headed households were 18.7% as of 2018.
- In 2019, 66.2% of the population suffered from multidimensional poverty, with an estimated 51% being women and girls.

## Political Participation

Women represented 22% in parliament and 18% of Supreme Court judges in 2018. The overall rate of women's representation in decision-making positions remains low.

## Education

Guinea has the lowest literacy rate in the region, with 23% for women and 38% for men. Primary school enrollment was 81% for boys and 69% for girls in 2015.

## Health

Guinean women struggle to access adequate health services. The maternal mortality rate is one of the highest in sub-Saharan Africa.

- Only 45% of births were attended by qualified health workers in 2015.
