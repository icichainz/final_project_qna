# EbA Measures for Climate Resilient Water Management

EbA measures for climate resilient water management and livelihood development would represent an innovative approach for Thailand, integrating both grey and green approaches to water drainage, storage, diversion, and irrigation. This hybrid approach aims to maximize effectiveness and cost-effectiveness. The project also integrates climate risk management into IWRM, tackling drought and flood by diverting floodwaters to replenish groundwater for dry periods.

Precision agriculture is in its early stages globally. Although BAAC launched a soft loan for purchasing drones in 2019, the disbursement rate was low. This may be due to the introduction stage of precision agriculture in Thailand or the economic impracticality for small farmers. The funding proposal lacks adequate justification for precision agriculture, which is costly and hinders diffusion to low-income farmers.

## Potential for Knowledge and Learning

The project involves capacity-building, technical training, and enhancement for inter-agency coordination as cross-cutting activities in all components.

### Component 1: Improving Capacity

- Provision of hardware and software for enhanced climate and environmental information.
- Technical training for 30 staff from TMD, RID, and relevant agencies.
- Development of tailored climate information products and models.
- Development of SOPs for sharing climate information.
- Workshops and meetings for collaboration.
- Development of a communication strategy.
- Development of smartphone and LINE app for agriculture households.
- Development of a complementary website.

The risk of cross-posting or poaching of trained personnel may affect capacity-building effectiveness. Safeguards like a minimum number of years in a post are needed.

## Contribution to the Creation of an Enabling Environment

RID has committed to providing necessary funds for O&M beyond project completion. Activities promoting sustainability include a financial instrument with BAAC, knowledge-sharing, training activities, and an online market platform.

## Contribution to the Regulatory Framework and Policies

Integration of climate risk knowledge into policies and planning across water and agriculture sectors is sought through technical training and development of tailored climate information products.

## Scalability and Replicability

The project has potential for scaling up activities to other regions of Thailand. Parallel financing from BAAC supports scaling up, though precision agriculture scaling without increased costs seems over-optimistic.

## Sustainable Development Potential

### Environmental Co-benefits

Implementation of EbA measures and conservation agriculture would reduce soil erosion, improve water quality, and enhance biodiversity.

### Social Co-benefits

Increased food and water security would enhance food security and health conditions of farming families.

### Economic Co-benefits

Support for climate resilient agriculture and market access would enhance income generation and possibly create jobs for young people.

### Gender-sensitive Development Impact

Gender inequality and provisions for equal gender participation are addressed in the Gender Assessment and Action Plan.

## Needs of the Recipient

### Vulnerability of the Country and Climate Rationale

The proposal highlights increased intensity of wet seasons and drier dry seasons, impacting water management. The climate rationale uses observed data and gridded data for trend analysis and model validation.

### Vulnerable Groups

The project targets farming households and communities vulnerable to flooding and drought. Selection criteria prioritize vulnerability and urgency.

### Economic and Social Development

Thailand's rural population is mostly engaged in agriculture, generating over 10% of GDP. The agricultural sector employs half of the population, dominated by smallholder farms.

### Absence of Alternative Sources of Financing

Alternative financing options for climate change adaptation in agriculture and water sectors are limited. The proposed line of credit from BAAC may address climate finance availability but not accessibility and affordability for small farms.

### Need for Strengthening Institutions and Implementation Capacity

The proposal addresses the need for strengthening institutions through capacity-building and institutional strengthening activities.

## Country Ownership

### Alignment with National Climate Strategy and Policies

The project aligns with Thailand’s development plan, Water Management Strategy, and various national climate plans.

### Capacity of Accredited Entities or Executing Entities to Deliver

UNDP, the accredited entity, has experience working with RID and other agencies on climate-informed projects.

### Engagement with Civil Society Organizations and Other Relevant Stakeholders

Stakeholder discussions from 2016 to 2019 informed project design. Engagement included subnational decision makers, private sector, farmers, and community leaders.

## Efficiency and Effectiveness

### Cost-effectiveness and Efficiency

GCF financing is unlikely to crowd out private investments. The proposed financial structure is reasonable.

### Amount of Co-financing

GCF would finance 52% of the total project cost, with the rest financed by the government of Thailand and Krungsri Bank.

### Financial Viability

The cost-benefit analysis includes scenarios of reduced agricultural production. The economic efficiency remains significant even with increased costs and decreased benefits.

The basis for calculating future flood damages was clarified by the AE, relating recorded flood damages with water levels and flow.

The replication of IoT and precision agriculture faces cost-related uncertainties, making it financially untenable for many target beneficiaries.

# Annex 8 – Gender Assessment and Action Plan

## GREEN CLIMATE FUND FUNDING PROPOSAL

### Activity 3.2 Implementation of on-farm climate resilient measures

- **Objective**: Improve drought and flood resilience, and improve access to finance for sustainable agriculture.
- **Approach**: Integrate household methodology approach (HHM) to complement EbA approach and support agricultural extension efforts to strengthen climate-resilient farm planning, including agricultural livelihoods, market access, VC development, etc.
- **Indicator**: Extension workers applying methodology in project areas incorporating HHM approach in agricultural extension for strengthening agricultural livelihoods.
- **Target**: 100% by 2025, 50,000
- **Note**: This would support gender expertise advising/working with district level adaptation plan team(s) to integrate/institutionalize HHM approaches in agricultural extension to strengthen household/community resilience.
- **Responsible Entity**: MoAC

### Activity 3.3 Capacity building for farmers

- **Objective**: Support market access for climate resilient agriculture products.
- **Approach**: Promote participation of women in training, as agents of change towards climate resilience. Make available training materials that may be of help to women in the area (e.g., financial incentives targeting women and female entrepreneurs, information related to available help related to domestic violence).
- **Indicator**: Women participating in training activities; use of mobile application for climate change information for crop planning; adoption of climate resilient practices; access to finance for investment in climate resilient agriculture resilience.
- **Target**: 50% of beneficiaries are women by 2025, 50,000
- **Responsible Entity**: MoAC
