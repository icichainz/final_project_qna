A.2. Project / Programme Executive Summary (max 300 words)
In the past 50 years the Buenos Aires Province (PBA, for its acronym in Spanish) has suffered rainfalls of great intensity
adversely impacting the Lujan River Basin where 11.4% of households fall within at least one of the unsatisfied basic
needs indicators (a proportion larger than that of the Buenos Aires Province, which is 8.2%). During the last years the
extent of flooding has increased resulting in waterlogging of soils in urban and rural areas as well as roads and trails. The
general purpose of this Project is promote measures aimed at reducing and/or preventing river flooding, as well as
controlling stream-flows and mitigating the impact of floods in the Lujan River Basin, thus causing more resilience vis-à-
vis climate change in the basin.
Floods in the Lujan River Basin are mainly caused by: (i) meteorological and climate factors resulting in increases in
rainfall frequency and intensity; (ii) physical characteristics of the basin such as: slope, contour, water-storage of the soil
and water conveyance of the river; and (iii) anthropogenic factors such as changes in land-use, growing urbanization and
the features of road and water infrastructure.
To address the issue, the Buenos Aires Province has structured the Lujan River Comprehensive Plan (LRCP) with an
estimated cost of USD $315,3 MM to solve the aforementioned problems. The plan envisages the development of an
integral project to implement structural measures together with non-structural measures such as implementation of an
early warning system (EWS), actions aiming at implementing land-use management and the strengthening of institutions.
The scope of the project thus defined fully complies with the priorities set out by Argentina under the Intended National
Determined Contributions (INDC) regarding climate change. Those priorities include implementation of early warning
systems and application of structural and non-structural measures to face extreme events. Structural measures are the
following: feasibility studies and civil works to increase the conveyance capacity of the river, construction of retention
basins and substitution of inflatable dams for lock gates.
Funding of the project has been structured as follows: 29.13% PBA; 52.3% CAF and 18.56% GCF. This funding structure
enables PBA to have the required cash flow and ensures that the project may be fully and successfully implemented
within the timeframe agreed upon, ensuring the early implementation of structural and non-structural adaptation
measures.

B.1. Description of Financial Elements of the Project / Programme
The Project and its financial sources:
a. The Project´s success is based on the integrated implementation of structural and non-structural measures as set
out in the LRCP.
b. The Project impact has been quantified as economic benefits brought about by the increasing resilience in the
Basin, and, consequently, the avoidance of damages to dwellings in the Lujan River Basin resulting from less
flooding.
c. The Buenos Aires Province has resources available for partially funding the Project; however, for an optimal
implementation, additional climate finance resources under concessional terms are needed. Further to ensure
appropriate implementation of the Project, the financial structure should take into account the Province’s future
payment capacity.
d. On 19 July 2016, CAF approved a first credit for an amount of USD $100MM and a second credit was approved on
July 4, 2017 for up to USD 120MM. The credit facility was divided into 2 stages due to limit of lending capacity from
CAF to Argentina in year 2016 nevertheless it is fundamental that the Project is carried out as an integral Project.
e. Against this backdrop, the financial structure proposed for the Project should be consistent with PBA payment
capacity. To that end, the following is proposed:
i. Loan repayment and grace periods falling preferably in periods different from due dates for the payment of the
current PBA debt.
ii. Concessional climate resources to support the PBA climate change mitigation efforts.
iii. Financing resources from the GCF that will support the PBA in its first climate change adaptation project.
f. In addition to the loan, in order to replicate the Project, a grant of USD $1.57 MM will be requested to the GCF.

C.1. Strategic Context
The Republic of Argentina is made up of 23 Provinces and the autonomous city of Buenos Aires. Argentina has a
population of nearly 40 million inhabitants, 90% of which is located in urban areas. The Metropolitan Area of Buenos
Aires, which includes the city of Buenos Aires and 26 municipal jurisdictions, accounts for 0.5% of the national territory
and concentrates 31.92% of the population.
Between 2004 and 2008, Argentina was a leader in poverty reduction, but since 2008 this trend has slowed down. In
2014, 12.7% of the population was considered poor (living with less than USD $4 a day). One third of the population of
Argentina lives with an income between USD $4 and USD $10 a day, and are at risk of falling into poverty.
In the last decade the main concern of the Federal Government was economic recovery and economic growth. However,
this policy brought about some important distortions in the economy. The new administration, inaugurated in December
2015, is introducing adjustments to eliminate economic distortions responsible for hindering economic growth and for
increasing the inflation rate. Argentina is now living in a context of changes expected to bring about results in the mid
term with the reduction of fiscal deficit and increases of debt capacity later on.
The Republic of Argentina and the Climate Change:
In 1993 Argentina ratified the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) and, in 2001 the
Kyoto Protocol. In 2002, a presidential decree replaced the Secretariat of Environment and Natural Resources with the
Secretariat of Environment and Sustainable Development under authority of the Head of the Cabinet of Ministers. By
December 2015 it became the Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development vested with significant institutional
duties. Its core purpose is to provide assistance to the President and the relevant Ministries, as well as «coordinating all
and any public policies having impact on the environmental sector, by implementing a strategic planning of environmental
policies and programmes adopted by the national government». The new Minister relies on the Secretary for
Environmental Policy, Climate Change and Sustainable Development that hosts the Directorate for Climate Change and
is responsible for the adoption and coordination of policies and actions relating to climate change. Additionally, there is a
government committee for climate change with the involvement of both Federal and Provincial governments.
In the Third National Communication to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (November 2015),
Argentina highlighted the country’s vulnerability vis-à-vis climate change, analyzed greenhouse gas emissions (GHG)
and put forward a set of planned mitigation measures and the need for adaptation measures to address problems created
by frequent heavy rains, such as floods, with subsequent harm to the population and the agricultural production.
Furthermore, prior to the Paris negotiations in 2015, Argentina submitted to the UNFCCC, its Intended Nationally
Determined Contributions (INDC), where it made clear that adaptation to climate change is a major concern, whereby it
has implemented adaptation measures. Nevertheless, to further such adaptation actions, international financing,
technology transfer and institutional strengthening are required. Two out of the main seven adaptation measures
considered in the INDC include early warning systems and implementation of structural and non-structural measures to
face extreme events.
Additionally, the Inter-jurisdictional Cabinet on Climate Change was established on July 25 2016 for the purposes of
articulating policies on climate change and increasing the society’s awareness of their relevance and significance. Among
these core duties is aligning the national and provincial governments with regard to actions on mitigation and adaptation
to climate change; propose a National Plan on Response to Climate Change, as an input to discuss a core comprehensive
national strategy and contribute to capacity-building in the field of prevention and handling of emergency and disaster
situations arising from extreme climate events, among others.

In 1994 Argentina introduced in its Constitution the obligation to protect the environment granting to its population
the fundamental right of a healthy environment. In addition, the Constitution gave the Provinces the right to
regulate and introduced the notion of “Minimal Budgets for Protection” to ensure a minimal threshold of
environmental quality across the national territory. The two laws currently enacted on “Minimal Budgets” for the
Lujan River Project are the following:
• Law 25.675, General Law on Environment (Law 25.675). This Law sets out the notion of land-use as a tool
for environmental management. It also establishes guidelines for the environmental policy and the
Congruence Principle to align the domestic legal framework with the goals and targets of sustainable
development.
• Law on Minimal Budgets for the Integrated Water Resources Management (Law 25.688). This Law is the
basis for creating agencies devoted to river basin management.
At the national level, the Federal Agreement on Water Resources and the Guiding Principles for the Integrated
Water Resources Management (Law 26.438) seek integrated management of water resources through the notion
of territorial areas in river basins. The Province of Buenos Aires has already ratified this Law.
Provincial Framework
At the Provincial level there is a large number of regulations for the integrated river basin management:
• Law 11.723 (amended by Law 13.516) is the General Law on Environment, which is the environmental
regulatory framework for the Province of Buenos Aires. This Law sets out guiding principles governing
the Province´s environmental policy. The purpose of this Law is “the protection, preservation,
improvement and restoration of natural resources and environment in the Province of Buenos Aires, to
preserve life in the broadest sense, ensuring present and future generations the preservation of biological
diversity and the quality of the environment”.
• Law 5.965, which sets out the framework for water resources protection. This Law is the basis to fight
against water pollution in the Province and to grant wasteeater discharge permits into receiving waters.
• Law 12.257 (Water Code). The Water Code of the Province of Buenos Aires, with its amendments, is the
most important piece of legislation for water regulation and water management for river basin units in the
Provincial territory.
• Law 14.710, which creates the Committee of the Lujan River Basin Committee (COMILU). This is a major
body for the implementation of the Lujan River Project.
Additionally, with regard to planning and management of water resources, the government of the Province of
Buenos Aires approved in 2007 the Provincial Water Management Plan contemplating differentiated actions for
the metropolitan area, the rural area and the coastal area. The plan calls for investments of USD $1.000 millions
which benefit 8 million of people in Lujan, Salado, Reconquista, Matanza-Riachuelo, Areco and Rio V river basins
and the region adjacent to the capital (La Plata, Berisso and Ensenada).

The Lujan River Basin is large and narrow. Water from heavy rains reaches the main river course very quickly, thus
raising abruptly the river´s water level and bursting the banks of the river. Floods in the river basin occur rather fast, on a
large-scale and unexpectedly while people become defenceless in face of such natural phenomena.
Over time this problem has become more acute due to the following reasons:
• Climatic Factors.
• Physical Factors.
• Anthropogenic Factors.
Climatic Factors
Rainfall and temperature are the main variables influencing the region´s climatic characteristics. In wet years, the increase
in rainfall results in a disproportionate increase in the streamflow because water infiltration (absorption capacity of soil
and subsoil) is low and water evaporation decreases. On the other hand, in dry years, the reduction in rainfall results in
a proportionally larger reduction of streamflow.
In short, rainfall variations in the zone result in a magnified river basin response. The lack of water brings about restrictions
in water provision for both human consumption and agriculture, whereas the excess of water results in floods.
Streamflow is also affected by the spatial distribution of rainfall. The larger the rainfall intensity and frequency over a zone
of the river basin with low interception capacity and/or over a zone with good drainage capacity, the larger the magnitude
of river flooding.

Physical Factors
During 1989-1994 conventional farming in the river basin was based on terrace farming. Thereafter, the region changed
the modality of land preparation for direct seeding (cereals, oilseeds). This type of land preparation for cultivation modifies
the runoff of the surface whereby a larger part of the rainfall infiltrates, resulting in a larger subsurface runoff. Intensive
use of land for agricultural purposes gives rise to soils with sparse vegetation and low level of interception resulting in an
acceleration of the runoff and a reduction of temporary retentions. As a result, the volume of water in the river becomes
larger in shorter spans of time.
On the other hand, channelization in the river basin should rely on a technical justification and the subsequent approval
from provincial or municipal authorities. Clandestine channelization generally lacks of technical planning, and adversely
affects the drainage network of creeks, channels and rivers.
Anthropogenic Factors
Urban growth brings about a decrease in interception capacity and an increase of soil impermeabilisation, which increases
the volume of water and the speed at which such water flows into river courses. In addition, the increases in residential
density calls for more infrastructure works, more roads, more public utilities, etc., in other words, changes in land-use
increase the levels of interception of runoff and, also, increase vulnerability in the region.

The Ministry of Infrastructure of the Buenos Aires Province through the Provincial Directorate of Water Infrastructure
(DPOH) conducted the “Study of the Integrated Plan and Project for the Construction of Infrastructure for the Regulation
and Sanitation of Lujan River”. This study provided the planning of structural and non-structural measures that, together
with the basin systematization, will serve to mitigate flood impact.
The study analyzed 8 structural measures from environmental, social, economical and financial point of views, by
calculating such measures cost-effectiveness and choosing the necessary works for protecting and ensuring the quality
of life of the population and, thus, consolidating urban and commercial development of the region.
The non-structural measures consist of building an institutional framework to: ensure the coordination and sustainability
of floods risk management; benefit from technological developments in climate forecasting in order to improve prevention
actions; involve the population into awareness campaigns through civil society and to obtain prompt responses in case
of disasters through groups of first responders.
Likewise, the objective of the Project is consistent with the purpose of Law 14.710 “COMILU” Committee of the Lujan
River Basin enacted on 5 May 2015. The COMILU is an autonomous entity with legal capacity to act both under private
and public law and which objective is to improve the quality of life and the environmental and health conditions in the
Lujan River Basin.
Components of the Project
The Project has 8 components. Components 1, 2, 3 and 4 seek to address problems derived from physical factors;
component 5 seeks to address problems derived from anthropogenic factors adversely affecting the river basin and
component 6 seeks to mitigate the issues relating to abrupt occurrence of floods.
Component 1: Engineering studies and others: Performance of executive projects and preparation of tender documents
for civil works under the Project needed in order to finalize engineering designs and to prepare the tendering process for
the engineering works considered in Components 2, 3 and 4.
Component 2: Water conveyance works and enlargement of the river channel. These will improve the conveyance
capacity of the Lujan River through construction of supplemental channels, enlargement of channels and crossing works:
• Santa María channel, communicating Lujan river with Parana de las Palmas River, will increase its cross-section over
a distance of 7.1 Km.
• Enlargement of Lujan River channel with trapezoidal sections over a distance of 12 Km, from provincial route (RP) 6
until the bridge of the national route (RN) 8 in Pilar.
• Enlargement of Lujan River channel with trapezoidal sections over a distance of 9.5 Km, from RN 8 until the railway
bridge of Belgrano North line.
• Improvement of the river conveyance capacity through enlargement of its cross-section in the locality of Mercedes,
in the zone of Olivera, and in a section corresponding to the locality of Lujan (15.5 Km).
Component 3: Construction of retention basins and replacement of lock gates. Construction of areas for temporary
retention of surplus water (ARTEH)- This component is complementary to Components 2 and 4. The retention basins,
built on the higher part of the river, are meant to remain empty, when the river floads they retain water thereby allowing
lower parts of the river to evacuate its excess water.
• Areas for temporary retention of surplus water (ARTEH) in the following creeks: Los Leones, Mayan, Leguizamón,
Grande, del Oro and other two over the Lujan River.
• Substitution of inflatable dams for lock gates in Lujan and Mercedes.
The lockgates replacement will allow adecuate waterconveyance thereby being complementary works of Component 2.
Component 4: Works for bridges replacement and enlargement. Works for replacement and enlargement of bridges to
improve current runoff conditions, removing river obstructions and detritus. These works will be in addition to the
construction of channels. This component is complementary to Component 2 and needs to be implemented in order to
fully obtain the benefits of enlarging the channels.
The work will be performed over 15 bridges across the river basin. Bridges will be enlarged or replaced depending on the
need of enlargement and their structural conditions.

Component 5: Environmental and land-use issues. Establishment of the riverbank to define boundaries on private and

basin and is one of the main non- structural components that complement the structural ones explained above.
Component 6: Early Warning System. The EWS will be integrated into the Provincial Early Warning System of the Ministry
of Infrastructure and Public Services of the Province, through the Directorate for Provincial Hydro-environmental
Monitoring, an organizational unit of the Sub-secretariat of Water Infrastructure. The EWS of the Project has four systems:
• Monitoring and Surveillance System: consolidates information relating to weather and rain forecasts; responsible for
the measurement and transmission network; responsible for data processing and follow-up to extreme events.
• Warning and Alarm System: fixes alert threshold levels and develops actions in case of triggering any alert or warning,
thus alerting the authorities and the community.
• Communication System: responsible for communications with the National Metereological Service, the measurement
and transmission network, and institutions and stakeholders linked to the alert and evacuation system.
• Evacuation System: EWS operators’ efficacy to transmit alerts of extreme events to those persons tresponsible; skills
of people involved in implementing the Evacuation Plan; community knowledge over steps to follow in case of
evacuation.
This is one of the non- structural measures necessary to develop an integrated project that responds to adaptation needs.

and operational expenditures. It also includes hiring technical, social, and environmental supervision of works, as well as
the external audit for the Project. The supervision included in this component relates to external supervision that will be
hired by the PBA in order to ensure the correct implementation of the project.
This component, also non- structural will, through the COMILU, support policy implementation and the correct land use

D.1. Value Added for GCF Involvement
PBA has included this Project among its investments priorities; however, there are budget and financial constraints.
Those constraints are the reasons why PBA has divided the Project in two stages assuming the risk of delay in the
commencement of stage 2 and the risk of failure in the full implementation of structural and non-structural measures.
GCF funding will allow full development of the Project removing the uncertainty of commencing stage 2 on time. The
Project was conceived for an integrated management of the Lujan basin which will not be possible if it is not fully
developed. Current problems derived from climate change have a root cause not only from the physical conditions of
the basin but also from inadecuate land- use, GCF funds, therefore, will provide the opportunity to implement measures
for the adaptation to climate change in the early implementation of the project.
The GCF participation in the project is key to (i) ensure the integral implementation of the project encompassing both
structural and non-structural measures such as institutional strengthening of the COMILU, the implementation of land-
use plans and expropriations and resettlements derived from such measrures. Furthermore, the availability of funds may
allow starting the construction of areas for temporary retention of surplus water (ARTEH) in the upper and lower parts
of the river basin and to substitute inflatable dams for lockgates at Mercedes and Lujan. The GCF resources ensures
the implementation of non-structural measures that without the GCF participation would be at risk of not being
incorporated, (ii) The Lujan River Basin Project is the first adaptation project in Argentina funded with reimbursable
resources and incorporated in Argentina’s budget and investment portfolio. This represents important changes in the
usual approach of addressing climate change adaptation through non-reimbursable resources. This project seeks to
combine public and climate long term investments that promote sustainable and climate-resilient development. The
concessional financial terms and conditions of the GCF are essential to make a reimbursable operation for climate
resilient infrastructure viable for the PBA.
GCF funding will provide the opportunity to rely on a financial structure in line with the timeframe for implementation of
the Project and to witness timely economic, social and environmental results from the Project as expected. GCF funds
bearing interest rates lower than current interest rates (0.75% vis-à-vis 9.125%), grace and repayment periods of 10
years (instead of 8 years) will enable PBA to invest future cash flows in the development of other priority projects. Today,
the main PBA creditors are international bondholders, domestic bondholders and the national government (more than
90% of the debt). Involvement of multilateral organizations and other financial sources make up a low percentage of the
whole PBA debt. CAF and GCF will enable PBA to reinforce the Project funding, improve the profile of its debt and
liberate resources to fund other projects.
PBA will work together with GCF’ and CAF’ environmental and social experts, thus allowing institutional strengthening
of PBA. The recently created COMILU will also benefit from the GCF and CAF know-how and expertise with long-term
benefits in terms of sustainability and management of the river basin as well as better skills to be replicated in other
priority basins in the Province.
The work on technical and environmental supervision will be enhanced with specialized external auditors and GCF
involvement in environmental issues.

In 1993 Argentina ratified the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) and in 2015 the
Kyoto Protocol. Thus far, Argentina has submitted three communications under the United Nations Framework
Convention describing Argentina’s vulnerability to climate change, providing the inventory of gas emissions and
advancing Argentina’s position regarding adaptation measures, giving high priority to the implementation of such
measures.
The following are Argentina’s priorities under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change:
- Introduce the expected climate change effects into parameters for designing infrastructure works, particularly,
those related to water management and the construction of embankments to avoid flooding.
- Update laws and land-use plans taking into account expected climate changes, introducing statutes on land-
use based on flood risk maps under current and forecasted climate conditions.
- Aim at having a land-use plans that take into account climate changes to avoid or mitigate undesired
consequences over human settlements. This is relevant for developments along maritime coastlines and
riverbanks.
Furthermore, prior to the Paris negotiations in 2015, Argentina, through the INDC, put forward its contributions on the
matter of mitigation and adaptation measures making it clear that adaptation measures are an ongoing priority. Two
out of the core seven adaptation measures to climate change are the following: a) intensifying and broadening early
warning systems for heavy rains, floods and hot weather, and systems of response to and recovery from climate
disasters; b) implementing structural and non-structural measures to address extreme events. Additionally Argentina’s
INDC calls for international financing to implement adaptation measures.
The Lujan River Basin has been structured taking into account Argentina’s INDC and, therefore, is within Argentina’s
priorities for adaptation to climate change. Moreover, this is the first project on adaptation to climate change relying on
international funding on the basis of reimbursable loans. The success of this Project would lay down the basis for
structuring future projects on adaptation to climate change, both in Argentina and other countries in the region and
worldwide, which have addressed the matter by means of non-reimbursable donations.
The Lujan River Project falls within Argentina’s regulatory framework and PBA’s field of competence. It is worth
mentioning that Law 14.710, whereby the Committee of the Lujan River Basin is created, seeks to modify existing
institutional arrangements enacted by the Water Authority more than a decade ago.
Law 14.710 vested COMILU with implementing powers for river basins management with legal authority to make and
implement general interest decisions. Prior to the enactment of Law 14.710, Committees were of consultative nature
to coordinate or arrange initiatives.
The purpose of Law 14.70 is the preservation of water resources and the sustainable management of the basin. To
fulfil this objective COMILU has been vested with powers to formulate environmental policies of the river basin together
with other competent organisms and powers for the integrated management of the river basin, including issuance of
land-use plans and recommendations on related expropriations and resettlements.
Successful implementation of the Lujan River Project under Law 14.710 would constitute a regulatory framework
replicable for projects on adaptation to climate change.

Argentina is subject to high vulnerability vis-à-vis climate change. Despite the fact that Argentina ranks in the middle
of the Index of Environmental Risk Exposure (World Bank, 2005), exposure to such risk increases because of the large
number of glaciers and the large number of people living in low-lying coastal areas. In the past 30 to 40 years, Argentina
has shown specific climatic characteristics in large part of its territory, most likely associated with climate changes.
Those characteristics were the cause of certain type of disasters in sectors such as energy and agriculture. Broadly
speaking, the most important trends are the following: (i) increase in the average annual rainfall in almost all the
country, except in the mountain range area of the West and in some areas of the extreme Southern part of the country.
This trend in the Northeast dates back from 1970s. The increase in rainfall ranged between 10% and 40% and was
sharper in two areas of the country: West of Buenos Aires and North in the Pampa, and North-eastern parts of the
country; (ii) increase in the frequency of heavy rains in the central and Eastern parts of the country. The frequency
became higher since 1990s, and in some areas the frequency of heavy rains has tripled; (iii) increase in the average
temperature in the Patagonian area since the second half of the twentieth century, reached temperatures of 1o C in
the Southern part. These phenomena gave rise to an increase in the isotherm to 0o C and retreat of glaciers, sharp in
48 out of the 50 existing glaciers. The retreat is also observed in the Cumana zone.
The main consequences of this trend are the following: changes in water balance and hydrology across the central
and North-eastern part of the country; increase in rainfall resulting in a westward shift of the isohyets corresponding to
dry land farming; more frequent flooding of the Parana river giving rise to significant losses in the agricultural sector
and the need to plan huge livestock evacuations, particularly in the Southern part of Entre Ríos; disruption of the land
transport network – rural roads become impassable about 60 days a year – complicating farming and the delivery of
products. In the North-eastern part of the country there was a generalized increase in the average annual rainfall and
a greater internal variability, adversely affecting agriculture. In the region of Cumana, since 1980s the average
riverstream flows have decreased between 50% and 60% (San Juan, de los Patos, Atuel, Mendoza and Tunuyán
rivers), increasing the risk of a water shortfall. On the other hand, an increase in average temperature brings forward
the process of snow fusion, increasing both streamflows and flooding risks and mudslides in spring. In the region of
Comahue, during the last 20 years the average streamflows have decreased about 30% adversely affecting the
generation of hydroelectric energy: the generation was 40% less than the generation in past climate conditions. The
impact of retreating glaciers on streamflows is not yet well understood, but it may entail material losses of water
reservoirs and other mechanisms on streamflow control.
According with Swiss Re (October, 2016)2 over the past 50 years, 75 major flood events have been reported in the
country, affecting around 13 million people and taking more than 500 lives. With the equivalent of USD 22.5bn lost
since 1980, floods are the most costly natural catastrophe affecting the country. Adjusting for the country’s GDP growth,
the same events today would have caused accumulated losses of USD 43.5bn. Today 1 in 3 Argentinians live in areas
highly exposed to flooding, amounting to a total of 14.2m people across the country. Most of the population living in
flood-prone areas is concentrated in the Greater Buenos Aires, Pampas and Gran Chaco regions, which together
account for 65% of highly exposed population. Looking ahead (flood-exposure in 2025), the impact of floods on the
social and economic structure of Argentina is expected to worsen. Population growth, urbanisation, economic growth
and climate change are the main drivers of this increase in flood risk exposure. Some options to close these gaps
fronm the public sector are: structural and non structural measures. Structural measures for flood control Infrastructural

infrastructure and periodic adaptation to reflect changes in flood exposure are also important. Temporary protection

The following are expected climate changes in Argentina:
• Temperature rise: It is likely that in the period 2020-2040 temperature rises 1°C in Argentina. This temperature
rise will be more significant in the North resulting in greater evaporation and, subsequently, in increases in
both aridity and desertification.
• Changes in the pattern of rainfall: An increase in rainfall is expected in the central area of Argentina, mainly in
summer and autumn. By contrast, a reduction in rainfall levels in the Andean region (Northeast of Patagonia
and Comahue) is expected. Those changes in rainfall patterns are significant bearing in mind that 20% of the
land area is already under water stress with considerable variations in the distribution of fresh water resources.
Changes in rainfall and temperature increases translate into increased evaporation and reduced runoff
(running water are not adequate for irrigation), and some areas become unsuitable for cropping. These
phenomena adversely affect agriculture that accounts for a large percentage of exports from Argentina and
also affect 54% of its land area. Agriculture should be positively affected assuming that fertilizing with CO
2
increases harvest of wheat (14%), maize (19%) and soja (67%) in the South whereas in the North a decrease
is expected.
• Increase in the frequency of extreme climate events: more frequent rainfalls and floods are expected, thus
adversely affecting the Northeast and central zones of the country, including Buenos Aires.
• Rise of the sea level: Most likely, average sea level will rise causing erosion in the coastal area, mainly in the
Southern part of Patagonia. Buenos Aires may also be affected.
• Retreating of Patagonia and Comahue glaciers may continue: In 20 years the disappearance of glaciers in
Patagonia is expected, thus adversely affecting consumption, agriculture and hydroelectric energy. In
particular, it is estimated that hydroelectric energy generation in Provinces of Mendoza, Río Negro and
Neuquén will be negatively affected by the downward trend of snows in the Andes. The hydroelectric
generation in the region of Comahe, South of Argentina, that currently generates almost one fourth of total
hydroelectric energy of Argentina, may be reduced by one third during the decade starting in 2020 because of
decreases in river levels.
• Though indicators on human and social development have substantially improved after the 2002 crisis, levels
of poverty and inequality in Argentina are similar to those prevailing during 1990s. There are significant
differences between regions with high poverty rates, for instance rural Northeast and Northwest regions, where
the economy is predominantly based on agriculture. These inequalities have significant impact with regard to
the adaptation capacity of people.

E.6.4. Application of best practices
Unlike other projects of this kind, the Lujan River Comprehensive Plan sets forth the implementation of both structural
and non-structural measures for an integrated management of the river basin, and it is not limited to the construction
of infrastructure. This comprehensive plan enables the Project follow-up beyond 5 years contemplated for
implementation of structural measures.
Non-structural measures provide implementation of EWS as part of Argentina’s strategy for addressing adaptation to
climate change, in addition to other benefits for the population in the river basin. EWS enable the collection of climate
information and early reactions in face of extreme events.
Regarding structural measures, 8 alternatives were considered and their assessment took into account technical
aspects of civil works, environmental conditions and economic and financial optimization. In other words, all relevant
aspects of any project of this kind were taken into account to select the best possible alternative.
On the other hand, works for temporary retention of water surpluses (ARTEH), which allow controlling streamflows
and reducing floods - resulting from the poor conveyance capacity of the river - were included among the structural
measures. This is part of the Project best practices.

from floods when the Lujan river burst its banks. The economic proposal uses the methodology of economic cost

H.1.1. Paradigm Shift Objectives and Impacts at the Fund level4
Paradigm shift objectives
Describe how the proposed project/ programme’s expected contributions to global
low-carbon and/or climate-resilient development pathways could be scaled-up and
replicated including a description of the steps necessary to accomplish it.
The Project will increase the resilence to climate change in the PBA by preventing flooding,
controlling stream-flows and attenuate the adverse effects from floods in fifteen municipalities
of the Lujan River Basin.
By reducing floods, the project will improve economic conditions in the region, i.e. improved
conditions for agricultural production, industrial production and commerce, as well as
improved conditions for people in the region. Additionally, the implementation of the Project
will mitigate damages in dwelling located in land with recurrent floods.
To achieve this objective, the project seeks solving problems in the river basin derived from
physical and anthropogenic factors and, implement other non-structural measures.
